Why your client's AI visibility dropped: how to find the cause and report it

When the share of AI answers that name a client or cite their site falls from one month to the next, don't change the strategy straight away. Work in three steps: first check that you measured under the same conditions and that the drop holds across repeat runs; then locate it by AI, by question, and by mention versus citation; only then look for the cause on the site, outside the site or on the AI side. Swings between single runs are normal and the cause often can't be confirmed, so the client report should keep apart what you observed, what you checked, what you don't know and what you will measure next.

Written November 14, 2026 (JST). This guide is for web agencies, SEO firms and consultants who run AI search optimization for clients on a retainer and report AI visibility every month.

In a Japanese industry survey, the challenge most often named by people already working on AI search optimization was measurement and KPI design (37.9%) (GMO NIKKO, August 2026, 1,003 respondents; TRUE MARKETING, in Japanese). A number that is hard to measure is even harder to explain when it goes down. This guide is for that moment.

AI answers change by day and by phrasing. The steps here are for finding out why a number fell and telling the client accurately. They do not guarantee that a business will appear in AI answers or that the number will recover.

First, check: single-run swings are normal

AI tools give slightly different answers to the same question every time. In a study by SparkToro and Gumshoe, around 600 volunteers ran 12 identical prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI nearly 3,000 times. The chance of getting the same list of recommendations twice was under 1 in 100, and the same list in the same order about 1 in 1,000. Yet some brands appeared in 60% to 90% of answers for a given intent (Search Engine Land's write-up). That is why you count how many runs out of how many named the client, rather than tracking a position.

The number of runs matters too. In its own study (October 2026, 30 questions, 3 AIs, 10 runs each), the Japanese vendor AI検索ドック (Stellagent) found that a single run differed from the 10-run average by 29.9 points on average, falling to 16.5 points with 3 runs and 10.7 points with 5 runs (their article, in Japanese). It is one vendor's study, but it shows that if you ask once a month and compare, a swing of 10 to 30 points is not surprising.

So before you report a drop, check two things:

  1. Does the drop hold when you ask the same questions under the same conditions, several times, on different days?
  2. Were last month's and this month's measurements taken under the same conditions (listed below)?

Step 1: did the measurement conditions change?

Quite often, the number moved because the measurement changed, not the site or the AI. Compare last month's record with this month's, item by item.

Don't line up months measured under different conditions as if they were comparable; report them as months where the setup changed.

The data itself can be the problem. Google has said that a logging issue affected Search Console from August 13 to 17, 2026, and that the data was later restored (Search Console data anomalies). When the analytics firm Promptwatch reported that reddit.com's share of ChatGPT search citations fell from 3.83% to 0.52% in August 2026, it called the figure provisional and said it could not rule out a data collection issue on its own side (Search Engine Journal). When you see a sudden cliff, suspect the measurement first.

Step 2: locate where it dropped

Once you know the drop is real, narrow down where. Compare with last month by AI, by question, and by mention (the name appeared) versus citation (the site was listed as a source). The shape of the drop tells you where to look.

Count questions that include the business name (branded) separately from those that don't (discovery). Branded questions almost always surface the name, so mixing them hides movement in the rate.

Step 3: candidate causes and how to check each

Check the candidate causes that fit where the drop happened. Start with what you can verify yourself on the site.

When the cause is on the AI side, there is usually little an agency can do quickly. In the reddit.com example above, the drop came around the same time as a change in how ChatGPT search runs its queries, but the timing does not fully match and the cause has not been explained (Search Engine Journal). If you tell a client "the AI algorithm changed" without evidence, you will have nothing left to say the next time the number falls for a different reason.

How to write the client report

In a month where the number fell, the most important thing is not to mix what you checked with what you suspect. Splitting the report into these five parts makes it easier for the client to decide, and keeps your explanation consistent from month to month.

  1. Don't promise that the number will come back or that a position will be regained.
  2. Don't decide on large changes, such as rewriting pages, from one result. Confirm that the drop persists first.
  3. If the cause was on the site, including your own agency's work, say so. Record the date you fixed it and check the effect in later measurements.

A monthly routine so the next drop is less of a scramble

  1. Fix the measurement conditions (questions, runs, AI, language) and write them down. When you change them, note the month.
  2. Ask each question several times and read a rate. More runs narrow the swings but cost more time and money, so agree the number of runs with the client.
  3. Log site changes with dates: theme and plugin updates, pages added or removed, URL changes. Measuring before and after a change makes causes easier to confirm.
  4. Count branded and discovery questions separately, and mentions and citations separately.
  5. State in the contract or proposal that AI answers vary and that appearance and position are not guaranteed (turning AEO into a retainer).

Checklist for a drop

  1. Measured with the same questions, runs, AI and language as last month
  2. Drop holds across several runs on different days
  3. No tool update or failed runs (and the Search Console data anomalies page checked)
  4. Drop located by AI, by question, and by mention versus citation
  5. Client falling separated from competitors rising
  6. Search crawlers not blocked in robots.txt
  7. No noindex, nosnippet or Search Console exclusion
  8. Previously cited pages still live (no 404 or redirect)
  9. Structured data and llms.txt still output
  10. Facts match across the site, Google Business Profile and directories
  11. Report separates what was observed, what was checked and what is unknown

FAQ

My client's AI visibility fell this month. What should I do first?

Before changing anything, confirm that you measured with the same questions, runs and AI as last month, and re-run a few times on different days. A single run can swing by 10 to 30 points. If the drop holds, narrow down which AI and which questions it is in.

How many runs before I can call it a drop?

There is no fixed number. More runs mean smaller swings: in one vendor study, a single run differed from the 10-run average by about 30 points on average, and 5 runs by about 11. Agree a number of runs with the client and compare like with like.

It only dropped in ChatGPT. Is that the site's fault?

When only one AI drops, first check that its search crawler (OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT) is not blocked in robots.txt or by bot protection. If it isn't, the change may be on that AI's side, but if you can't confirm it, report it as a possibility.

How do I report a drop when I don't know the cause?

Write what you observed, the measurement conditions, what you checked, what you don't know, and your next action and next measurement. Being clear about what you verified builds more trust than blaming "the algorithm" without evidence.

Did blocking GPTBot cause the drop?

GPTBot is OpenAI's training crawler; ChatGPT search uses OAI-SearchBot. Blocking the training crawler has no direct bearing on citations in AI search. Check which of the two your robots.txt blocks.
