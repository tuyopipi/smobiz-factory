Do FAQ pages and FAQ structured data help in AI search? Where things stand in 2026, and how to write FAQs AI can use

Google's FAQ rich results (questions and answers shown as expandable rows in search results) were reported to have stopped appearing on May 7, 2026. FAQPage is still a valid schema.org type, though, so there is no need to rush to remove markup you already have. At the same time, we found no official documentation saying FAQPage makes a page more likely to be chosen as a source by ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews. What helps in AI search is the FAQ itself: short, accurate answers, in the page text, to questions people actually ask. The markup comes second.

Written November 19, 2026 (JST). We covered blog posts in how to get blog articles cited by AI and business structured data in general in SEO plugins and local business schema. This guide is only about FAQs: what Google's change did and did not change, which questions to include and how to answer them so AI can use them, and how to publish and check them on WordPress.

AI answers change with the day, the wording and the service. The steps here help you build FAQs that AI can find answers in and measure them properly. They do not guarantee that you will appear in any answer.

Choosing questions AI is likely to use

The questions that belong in an FAQ are the ones customers actually ask, not the ones you would like to answer. The questions people put to AI come from the same people with the same doubts, so they overlap heavily. These are good places to collect them:

  1. Questions that came in at least twice in the last three months by phone, email, contact form or chat
  2. What people ask just before booking or buying (what the price includes, cancellation, payment, parking, how long it takes)
  3. Search terms in Search Console's performance report that show your site for "can I", "how much" or "difference between" questions
  4. Things your staff explain out loud every time (what happens on a first visit, what to bring, what is not covered)
  5. Misunderstandings in reviews and on listing sites (services you don't offer, old prices)

Leave out promotional questions nobody asks, such as "What makes us special?". FAQPage should not be used for advertising or promotion, a point Google has made (as summarised by Search Engine Land).

How to write answers: answer in the first sentence, then add conditions

AI builds its answer by pulling the parts of a page that match the question. A long preamble makes the answer harder to find. Three rules: state the answer (yes or no, a price, a time) in the first sentence. Put conditions and exceptions after it. Leave the long explanation to another page and link to it.

QuestionAnswer AI is unlikely to useAnswer AI can use
Are you open on Saturdays?We aim to be flexible so we can fit around our customers' schedules.Yes. On Saturdays we are open 9:00 to 13:00. We are closed on Sundays and public holidays.
How much does it cost?It depends on the job, so please get in touch.The basic plan is $80 including tax. Visits outside the city add $15.
Is there a cancellation fee?Please see our cancellation policy.Cancelling up to the day before is free. Same-day cancellations are charged 50% of the price.
Do you handle X?We meet a wide range of needs.No, we don't handle X. The closest thing we offer is Y.

The prices and conditions in the table are examples of the wording. Use your own real values. Saying clearly what you don't do matters too: if you don't say it, AI may fill the gap with another business's details or old information.

When you change an answer, update every other page with the same fact (price list, about page, booking page) on the same day. If pages disagree, there is no telling which value AI will use.

Rules for adding FAQPage

If you add it, there isn't much to remember. The most important rule is that the questions and answers in the markup are the same text that is shown on the page. Putting questions only in the markup goes against Google's rules. For AI features too, Google asks that structured data match the visible text (AI features and your website).

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Are you open on Saturdays?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "Yes. On Saturdays we are open 9:00 to 13:00 (last check-in 12:30). We are closed on Sundays and public holidays."
      }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Is there parking?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "There are 3 free spaces behind the building. If they are full, use the pay car park across the street."
      }
    }
  ]
}
</script>
<!-- The questions and answers must be the same text that is shown on the page -->

Two FAQPage items on one page make it unclear which one is right. Choose one thing to output the FAQ: your SEO plugin's FAQ block, your theme's feature, or hand-written code.

Should you remove the FAQ markup you already have?

No. Only the rich result has gone; the FAQPage type has not been retired, and leaving it is reported not to create Search Console errors (TechWyse). Use the moment to fix three things:

  1. Questions in the markup that aren't shown on the page (added only for the rich result) → show them on the page or take them out of the markup
  2. Two or more FAQPage items on the same page → keep one source
  3. Out-of-date answers (prices, hours or staff changed) → fix the text and the markup together

On the other hand, deleting the FAQ text itself because the rich result is gone would be a loss. Customers and AI both read the text.

How to measure: ask the same questions before and after

With FAQ rich results gone, Search Console impressions can no longer show what your FAQ does. To see the effect in AI search, ask AI the questions from your FAQ without your business name, and record whether your URL is among the sources, before and after, under the same conditions.

Checklist

  1. FAQ questions were chosen from questions customers actually asked
  2. Every answer states the answer in the first sentence (yes or no, a price, a time)
  3. You also wrote what you don't do or don't cover
  4. Prices say whether tax is included
  5. Answers to the same question are word-for-word the same on every page
  6. Questions and answers in the markup match the text on the page
  7. There is only one FAQPage per page
  8. Questions not shown on the page are out of the markup
  9. You recorded AI answers to unbranded questions before adding the FAQ
  10. When prices or hours change, the FAQ is updated the same day

FAQ

When did Google's FAQ rich results end?

They were reported to have stopped appearing on May 7, 2026. Since August 2023 they had been limited to well-known government and health websites. Google's documentation announced that the Search Console FAQ report and Rich Results Test support would end in June 2026, and Search Console API support in August 2026.

Should I remove my FAQ structured data?

There is no need. FAQPage is still a valid schema.org type, and leaving it is reported to cause no problems. Fix it only if it contains questions not shown on the page or appears twice on the same page.

Does FAQPage make ChatGPT or Perplexity more likely to cite me?

In our research for this guide we found no official documentation that says so. AI reads the page text, so improve the questions and answers on the page first, and treat markup with the same content as an extra.

Do I need FAQ markup to appear in Google's AI Overviews?

Google says no special structured data is needed for AI features. If you use structured data, Google asks that it match the visible text.

How many questions should an FAQ have?

There is no set number. Include every question customers ask repeatedly, and leave out promotional questions nobody asks. When there are many, group them under headings.

Should FAQs go on one FAQ page or on each service page?

Questions about the whole business go on the FAQ page; questions about one service go on that service's page. If a question is in both places, use the same answer. If values differ, there is no telling which one AI will use.

If I update the FAQ, will AI answers change right away?

No AI service publishes how long it takes. Note the date of the change and check again a few days to a few weeks later with the same questions and conditions. Whether you are chosen as a source is up to the AI and cannot be guaranteed.
