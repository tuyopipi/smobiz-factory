AI search for real estate agencies: how to get AI to read your area, specialties and company facts correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a real estate agency comes down to publishing, accurately and consistently, which areas you cover, which kinds of transactions you handle (sales, lettings, property management, valuations), which situations you are good at, and your company facts (address, opening hours, days closed, license number), both in your page text and in schema.org RealEstateAgent structured data. On top of that, the step that is specific to real estate is keeping listings current, so AI does not pick up information that is no longer true.

Written November 11, 2026 (JST). Information from Google, OpenAI and Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency was checked on each official page on the dates given in the text.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your agency's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your agency or rank it anywhere.

What people ask AI about real estate

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews to find a real estate agency, the AI reads what it can find on the web and builds an answer. The questions mix area, type of transaction, situation and day: "an agent near the station who is good with pet-friendly rentals", "a company in my city that can help me sell a house I inherited", "an agency that is open on Wednesdays", "someone who helps foreign residents find an apartment".

Questions about a specific property ("two bedrooms within ten minutes' walk of the station, under a set rent") are better served by listing portals, and AI often cites the big portals or agencies' listing pages for them. Where a local agency is more likely to be chosen is when someone asks who to talk to. So the first thing to fix is not the number of listings but the company information that lets AI read "this agency handles this kind of case in this area".

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special schema.org structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual basics still matter, such as making structured data match the visible text (checked November 1, 2026). Google: AI features and your website. The work is getting your agency's facts right, not finding an AI trick.

The facts an agency site should publish

In structured data, a real estate agency can be described as a schema.org RealEstateAgent. Google's local business documentation lists name and address as required, recommends openingHoursSpecification, telephone, url and others, and asks you to use the most specific type possible (checked October 29, 2026). Google: local business structured data. If your type is still Organization or LocalBusiness, your structured data does not say you are a real estate agency.

If you have several branches, give each its own page with its own address, phone, hours and service area. Listing every branch only on the head office page makes it hard to tell which branch covers which area.

What matters most is that the same fact has the same value everywhere: your site, structured data, Google Business Profile, your company profile on listing portals, and llms.txt. When your days closed change or you move, update all of them together.

Building area and specialty pages

For an AI to answer "a company in my city that can help me sell an inherited house", some page has to say clearly that you cover that city and handle that kind of sale. Pages that combine an area with a type of case help with that. But mass-producing pages that are the same text with the place name swapped does the opposite: they are empty for readers, and every one looks the same.

  1. On an area page, write which transactions you actually handle there, the questions you often get, which branch covers it, and things only a local would know (the hill from the station, where people shop, the kind of housing).
  2. On a specialty page (inheritance, moving up, vacant homes, commercial property, rentals for foreign residents), write how a consultation goes, which documents are needed, how long it usually takes, and common questions, following how you really work.
  3. When you quote prices or statistics, say which public data or survey they come from and as of when. Leave out "market prices" and "X% of people" claims with no source.
  4. Show track record (closed deals) only with the client's consent and in a way that does not identify them. If you give a count, add the period and how you counted.

Keeping listings current so AI does not repeat old information

The issue specific to real estate is how quickly listing information goes out of date. If the page for a property that has already been sold or let stays up, AI and search engines can read it and present it as still available. A client who contacts you because of an AI answer, only to hear the property is gone, trusts you less.

  1. Take sold or let properties down promptly, or mark them clearly as closed on the page and remove the enquiry button.
  2. Show on each listing page when the information was last updated and when it will next be updated.
  3. Keep figures such as price, rent, walking time and building age the same in page text, structured data and listing portals.
  4. If your listings only appear after logging in or filtering, AI crawlers cannot read them. At minimum, put your company information (service area, services) on ordinary pages anyone can read.

Real estate advertising is regulated, and the rules differ by country. In Japan, for example, the Real Estate Brokerage Act and the industry's fair competition code on real estate advertising apply. Check how to present listings and which words you may use against the current rules from your trade association or regulator; this article does not explain individual provisions. Text written with AI in mind has to follow the same advertising rules.

Reviews and ratings: what not to add for AI

Some guides recommend adding star ratings (AggregateRating) to your agency or agent pages in structured data. Marking up reviews of your own business on your own site will not get you review stars in Google. In 2019 Google said that self-serving reviews, meaning reviews about a business placed on that business's own website, aren't allowed for LocalBusiness and Organization. Google Search Central blog, September 16, 2019

Rules on undisclosed advertising differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you. In Japan, stealth marketing has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations since October 1, 2023, and the Consumer Affairs Agency treats reviews that a business asks or instructs third parties to post as that business's advertising (checked November 1, 2026). Consumer Affairs Agency: stealth marketing (Japanese). If you publish client testimonials, publish real ones, without hiding that they are promotional.

What AI needs from you is not praise but facts it can check: area, services, hours, license number. Claims you cannot back up, such as "No. 1 in the area" or "we always get the highest price", are safest left out of both your text and your markup.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Name, address, phone, opening hours and days closed match across your site, Google Business Profile and listing portals.
  2. Your structured data type is RealEstateAgent, not Organization. Each branch has its own page and details.
  3. Your service area is written in concrete place names in your page text.
  4. Your services (sales, lettings, management, valuations) and specialties list only what you actually handle.
  5. Trust facts such as your license number and associations are written as text on your company page.
  6. No sold or let property is left up as if available, and listing pages show an update date.
  7. Reviews of your own business are not marked up as ratings, and there are no unsupported superlatives.
  8. llms.txt lists your company overview, service area, services and contact page. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, ask AI the questions your clients would ask (area plus type of transaction plus situation), without your agency's name, and see whether you come up by name or URL. Also ask by name ("What are the opening hours of X Realty?") and check that the facts in the answer are correct. Answers change between runs, so ask the same questions a few times, spread over time, and record them. FAQ

Where should a real estate agency start with AI search?

Write your service area, services, specialties and company facts (address, opening hours, days closed, license number) correctly as text on your pages. Then publish the same facts as RealEstateAgent structured data and in llms.txt, and make sure they match your Google Business Profile and portal profiles.

If my listings are on portals, do I still need to fix my own site?

For property searches, AI often cites portal pages. But the material for answering "who should I talk to about this kind of case in this area" is on your own site, where you can describe your specialties and area in detail. Keeping your company details the same in both places is the safest approach.

Should I create lots of area pages?

Substance matters more than number. Pages that only swap the place name are useless to readers and work against you. Create pages for the areas you really cover, with the branch, common questions and local knowledge.

Do listing pages need structured data too?

Fix your company information first (RealEstateAgent, services, FAQ). What matters most on listing pages is that figures such as price and rent match the portals, that closed properties are not left up as available, and that each page shows an update date.

Will this guarantee my agency appears in ChatGPT?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear.
