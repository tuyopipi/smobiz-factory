How products get into ChatGPT shopping: product feeds, how it differs from Merchant Center, and what a shop outside the US can do now

ChatGPT's shopping results are not ads. They are chosen from what ChatGPT reads on your site, from product feeds merchants send, and from Shopify and Etsy catalog data. OpenAI says a feed is not required if ChatGPT can already crawl your site; a feed gives you more control. As of November 2026, shopping is live for ChatGPT users in the US, so for most shops the first job is making product pages readable and keeping prices and stock identical everywhere.

Written November 22, 2026 (JST). OpenAI and Google facts were checked on their official pages that day. This is for people who run their own online shop, for example on WooCommerce, and for the agencies that build and maintain those shops.

AI answers and product listings vary by day, wording, and the user's country and plan. This is not a way to guarantee your products appear. It is a way to make them readable to AI and to pick the programs that actually apply to you. Availability will change, so recheck the official pages before you apply.

The shop-wide basics (OnlineStore markup, return policy and so on) are in AI search for online shops. This guide is only about how individual products get into AI shopping answers.

Where ChatGPT gets product information

OpenAI describes three entry points. The first is the public web, read by ChatGPT's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says sites that block OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt won't appear in ChatGPT search answers, and that robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to take effect (OpenAI bots documentation).

The second is a product feed sent by the merchant. The merchant page FAQ answers "Do I need a product feed if ChatGPT already crawls my site?" with no, adding that feeds give you greater control. The third is Shopify and Etsy: if you sell there, OpenAI says your catalog is already integrated and no extra setup or application is needed (merchants page, OpenAI Help: shopping from Shopify merchants).

On which products are shown, OpenAI's September 2025 launch post says results are organic and unsponsored, ranked on relevance to the user. Where several merchants sell the same product, it looks at availability, price, quality, and whether the merchant is the maker or primary seller (OpenAI: Buy it in ChatGPT; the Instant Checkout and fee parts of that post have since been superseded by the merchant page).

So even a shop that cannot send a feed can be source material, as long as product pages are readable and state price, stock, brand and description plainly. Pages where the price is only in an image, stock loads later through JavaScript, or bot protection turns crawlers away are harder to use.

You apply through the form on the merchants page, giving your company, site, main category and number of SKUs. Merchants who are invited follow the developer steps: claim the business in Business Hub, verify identity, choose the countries and currencies you sell in, then send the feed by manual upload, a hosted HTTPS URL or SFTP, as a full snapshot each time (submitting feeds). The official pages we read state no approval time or criteria. As of November 2026 the standard feed targets the US. A shop that only sells in Japan may still want to apply, but shouldn't expect that alone to put it in Japanese users' answers.

How Google AI Mode and Gemini differ

Google says shopping in AI Mode and Gemini is powered by the Shopping Graph, with Merchant Center as the central hub for product data, and that adopting UCP checkout doesn't influence how offers are ranked (UCP FAQ). There is no special markup for AI features in Search: keep structured data consistent with visible text and Merchant Center up to date (Google: AI features and your website).

What to do now, in order

  1. Write product name, price including tax, stock, shipping, brand, material and size as text on the product page. Don't put price or specs only in images.
  2. Check that the product page's structured data (Product, Offer) carries price, currency and availability, and that it matches the visible text.
  3. Make sure robots.txt doesn't block OAI-SearchBot and other AI search crawlers, and that CDN or firewall bot protection doesn't turn product pages away.
  4. Put products in Google Merchant Center with the same price, stock, shipping and return terms as the site.
  5. If you also sell on marketplaces, reduce mismatches in product name, price and GTIN for the same item.
  6. If you sell, or plan to sell, to the US, apply on OpenAI's merchant page and prepare a feed (stable SKUs, price and stock updates).
  7. Log whether you appear in AI answers with a fixed set of questions (see "How to check" below).

Steps on WordPress (WooCommerce)

With Nurevo AEO (free on WordPress.org), choosing the Online shop site type (suggested automatically when WooCommerce is active) publishes the business as an OnlineStore with your product catalogue (names, prices, currency, and stock where known) and return policy. Product pages keep WooCommerce's own Product markup, with no duplicates. WooCommerce products are also listed in llms.txt, and AI search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot are allowed in robots.txt.

Nurevo does not build product feeds for OpenAI or Merchant Center. Use a WooCommerce feed plugin or a Merchant Center integration for that. Nurevo's job is to make the pages and shop facts behind the feed accurate and readable to AI.

  1. Give every WooCommerce product a SKU (it becomes item_id; never change it).
  2. Turn on stock management and keep stock status accurate. Say so on the page for pre-orders and backorders.
  3. In Nurevo settings, set the site type to Online shop and point it at your returns and shipping pages.
  4. Install a way to send products to Merchant Center and confirm price and stock match the site.
  5. When a price changes, update the product page, feeds, Merchant Center and marketplaces on the same day.

How to check: readable, and appearing

First, check that AI crawlers can read your product pages. Replace example.com and the product URL with your own.

# 1. Does robots.txt block OAI-SearchBot?
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | grep -i -B1 -A3 "oai-searchbot\|user-agent: \*"

# 2. Does bot protection turn a product page away (403 or a challenge page)?
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" \
  -A "Mozilla/5.0 AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko); compatible; OAI-SearchBot/1.4; +https://openai.com/searchbot" \
  https://example.com/product/sample/

# 3. Does the product page's structured data carry price and availability?
curl -s https://example.com/product/sample/ | grep -o '"price[^,]*\|"availability[^,]*' | head

Bot protection usually looks at more than the claimed user agent, such as IP addresses, so a 200 here doesn't prove the real crawler gets through. Check your bot protection logs as well.

Next, log appearances. Pick 5 to 10 questions that don't name your shop, such as "best [product category]" or "[category] with free shipping", and ask them on the same day each month in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode. Record whether your shop or product pages appear in the answer text or sources. The method is in How to measure AI visibility. Outside the US you may appear as a cited link in a text answer rather than in a product carousel, so note which.

Nurevo's free monthly check and the Pro measurement also use category questions without your shop's name. Pro measurement currently runs on Perplexity, with ChatGPT once configured. Plans: Free $0, Standard $20/month, Pro from $99/month.

Checklist

  1. Product pages state name, price, stock, shipping and brand in text.
  2. Price, currency and stock in the structured data match the visible text.
  3. robots.txt doesn't block OAI-SearchBot or other AI search crawlers.
  4. Bot protection logs confirm product pages aren't turned away.
  5. Every product has a SKU that never changes.
  6. Merchant Center price, stock, shipping and returns match the site.
  7. Marketplace listings for the same item match on name, price and GTIN.
  8. Decided whether to apply for OpenAI feeds based on where you sell (the US or not).
  9. Haven't promised clients or colleagues "buy it in ChatGPT" or "always shown by AI".
  10. Log appearances monthly with questions that don't name the shop.

FAQ

Are ChatGPT shopping results ads?

OpenAI says product results are organic and unsponsored, ranked on relevance to the user. ChatGPT ads are a separate, labeled placement and a separate system.

Without a product feed, can my products appear in ChatGPT?

OpenAI's merchant page says a feed isn't required if ChatGPT already crawls your site; feeds give you greater control. Make product pages readable first.

Can a shop in Japan send a product feed to OpenAI?

Japan is in the application form's country list. But as of November 2026, shopping is live for US users and the standard feed upload targets the US. A shop selling only in Japan shouldn't expect an application alone to put it in Japanese users' answers.

Can customers buy inside ChatGPT (Instant Checkout)?

OpenAI says it is moving away from a standalone Instant Checkout and prioritizing merchant-owned checkout. Plan for ChatGPT to send shoppers to your product page, where they buy.

Does this work on WooCommerce, not just Shopify?

OpenAI says Shopify and Etsy catalogs are already integrated, but other shops can be source material through crawled pages and feeds. On WooCommerce, start by aligning product page text, structured data, SKUs and stock.

What do I need to appear in Google AI Mode shopping?

Google says AI Mode shopping is powered by the Shopping Graph, with Merchant Center as the central hub. Register products in Merchant Center and keep price and stock matching your structured data. There is no AI Mode-specific markup.

Does Nurevo create product feeds?

No. Nurevo publishes the OnlineStore, product catalogue, return policy and llms.txt without duplicating WooCommerce's Product markup, and allows AI search crawlers. Use a WooCommerce feed plugin or Merchant Center integration for feeds.
