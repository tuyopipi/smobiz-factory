AI search for dermatology and cosmetic skin clinics: how to get AI to read insured vs self-pay care, fees and risks correctly

The single most effective thing a dermatology or cosmetic skin clinic can do for AI search is to state, in text on each treatment page, whether the treatment is covered by insurance, what it costs if it is not, and what the main risks and downtime are. When AI answers a question with conditions in it — "a dermatologist near the station who treats acne on insurance", "how much does laser spot removal cost and how long is the downtime" — it relies on pages where those conditions can be read. Then you follow your medical advertising rules (limits on testimonials and before/after photos, fee and risk disclosure for self-pay care) and publish the same facts in structured data, llms.txt and your Google Business Profile.

What patients ask AI about skin clinics

Dermatology questions fall into two groups. General dermatology questions carry a symptom, a patient, a time and the insurance question: "a dermatologist who sees children with eczema", "a skin clinic open on Saturday", "is athlete's foot treatment covered by insurance?". Cosmetic questions carry price, number of sessions, downtime and risk: "typical price of laser spot removal", "how many sessions does medical hair removal take?", "acne scar treatment with short downtime".

In both cases, AI can only use a page where those conditions are written down. A price list that exists only as an image, a treatment page that never says whether it is insured or self-pay, or a page that only says "we will explain fees at your consultation" gives AI nothing to cite for a question with conditions in it.

Cosmetic medicine also draws a lot of complaints. Japan's National Consumer Affairs Center recorded 6,281 consultations about cosmetic medicine in 2023, 10,744 in 2024 and 7,944 in 2025 (PIO-NET figures as of May 31, 2026, page updated July 31, 2026). National Consumer Affairs Center: cosmetic medicine (Japanese). Patients want to check prices and risks before they book, and they ask AI about them. Publishing accurate fees and risks is patient information first and AI optimisation second.

Separate insured and self-pay care

AI most often gets a skin clinic wrong when the same label — "acne treatment", "pigmentation treatment" — covers both insured and self-pay options. State near the top of each symptom or treatment page whether it is insured or self-pay. The Ministry's Q&A also says that self-pay treatments using approved products (it gives dental implants and whitening as examples) must state that they are self-pay and give the standard fee. Self-pay treatment pages: fees and main risks on the same page

Under Japan's medical advertising rules, a website that patients seek out themselves may explain in detail things that cannot normally be advertised, provided certain conditions are met (known as "lifting the limit"). The Ministry's Q&A says that for self-pay care these conditions include describing the treatment normally required and its fees, and its main risks and side effects — and that listing one or two generic drawbacks is not enough (Q6-12). Another condition is a contact point for enquiries; a booking-only number or an auto-reply line does not meet it (Q6-10). These items must be easy to see: very small text or text the same colour as the background does not count (Q6-11). MHLW: Q&A (PDF, Japanese)

This points the same way as AI search. When AI is asked about price, downtime or risk, it can use only pages where those are written in the body text. A treatment page reads well in this order:

  1. The treatment's name, and that it is self-pay.
  2. What concern it addresses (without promising results).
  3. How it is done, time per session, usual number of sessions and interval.
  4. Fees (per session and per course, tax included or not, extras such as anaesthetic, medication or follow-up).
  5. Main risks and side effects, and typical downtime.
  6. Who cannot have it or should take care.
  7. A contact point that answers questions (phone number and hours), and who to call if there is a problem afterwards.
  8. The date the page was last updated.

Treatments that use unapproved drugs or devices, or approved drugs for unapproved purposes, need additional explanation. The Q&A says off-label use is treated the same way as unapproved drugs (Q2-14). Check the items required in the guidelines themselves.

On cosmetic medicine specifically, the Ministry's expert panel report (November 22, 2024) calls for stronger online monitoring of medical advertising and for clinics to set up a contact for problems after treatment and tell patients about it. MHLW: report of the panel on the appropriate practice of cosmetic medicine (PDF, Japanese). Putting an after-care contact on each treatment page fits that direction too.

Adding medicalSpecialty

Nurevo AEO does not output medicalSpecialty on its own. To add it, use the filter that changes the business structured data (webmcp_canary_business_node), in your theme's functions.php or a small plugin. Add only the fields your pages describe.

add_filter('webmcp_canary_business_node', function ($org, $settings) {
    // Add only the specialties your pages actually describe
    $org['medicalSpecialty'] = array('Dermatology', 'PlasticSurgery');
    return $org;
}, 10, 2);

Checklist before you publish

  1. Every symptom and treatment page says whether it is insured or self-pay.
  2. Self-pay treatment pages give the fee (tax included or not, extras), usual number of sessions, main risks and side effects, and downtime.
  3. A contact point that answers questions — not booking-only — is shown clearly.
  4. The price list is text, not only an image, and has a last-updated date.
  5. If general and cosmetic dermatology differ in hours or booking, each is written out.
  6. No testimonials, before/after photos, superlatives or comparisons appear in the body, FAQ or structured data.
  7. Treatments using unapproved products or off-label use carry the explanation the guidelines require.
  8. Service prices in the structured data match the prices on the page.
  9. Name, address, phone and hours match across structured data, Google Business Profile and any official health directory.
  10. llms.txt lists your clinic information, price list, main treatments and access pages. What is llms.txt

FAQ

Where should a dermatology clinic start with AI search?

Start by stating on each symptom and treatment page whether care is insured or self-pay. Then give each self-pay treatment page its fee, usual number of sessions, main risks and side effects and downtime, and make your name, address, phone and hours match across structured data and your Google Business Profile.

Should a cosmetic skin clinic publish its prices?

Yes. When AI is asked about price, it uses pages where the price is written in the text. In Japan, the Ministry's Q&A also lists the usual treatment, its fees and its main risks and side effects among the conditions for explaining self-pay care in detail on a website.

Which structured data type should I use?

schema.org's Dermatology type represents a dermatology practice and is a kind of MedicalBusiness and LocalBusiness. If you also offer cosmetic treatments, you can add Dermatology and PlasticSurgery as medicalSpecialty values. Matching the page matters more than the type.

Do before/after photos help a clinic get chosen by AI?

There is no verified evidence that they do. Under Japan's medical advertising rules, before and after photos are treated as claims about treatment effect and are restricted. Check the guidelines, the case book and your local health centre for when they may be used.

Will low ratings on review sites keep my clinic out of AI answers?

AI providers do not publish how much weight they give such information, so no one can say for certain. Independent reviews the clinic did not request are not advertising, but the clinic may not publish reviews it paid for or requested on its own site. Focus on making your own site's facts accurate.
