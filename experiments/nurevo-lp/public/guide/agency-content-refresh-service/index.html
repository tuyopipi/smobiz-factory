Turning a review of existing articles for AI search into a monthly agency service

To sell the review of a client's existing articles as a monthly service, run a full inventory once as a one-off project, then make the monthly work this: take an agreed number of posts, decide whether each one is kept, updated, rewritten, merged or retired, do that work, and report a before-and-after comparison measured the same way both times. Nobody can promise that AI will cite a page, so the contract promises the number of posts, the kinds of work and the report, and merging or retiring a page always waits for the client's approval.

Written 18 November 2026 (JST). This guide is for web agencies, SEO firms and WordPress care companies that look after client sites. How to write a single article is covered in How to write blog posts that AI search cites, and the shape of an AI search retainer as a whole in Offering AEO as a monthly service. This page covers the part in between: what to do with the pile of posts a client already has, and how to sell that work.

AI answers change from day to day and from one ask to the next. The work described here keeps the facts in the articles AI reads correct and current; it does not guarantee that the site is cited or shown.

Five decisions: keep, update, rewrite, merge, retire

Merging and retiring remove pages from the client's site. Show the list at the decision stage and record the approval before doing the work.
DecisionPosts it fitsWhat to do
KeepFacts are current, the question is clear and people read itLeave it alone, and record that you did
UpdateThe answer is right but prices, rules, figures or screenshots are out of dateFix only the stale facts and say in the text what changed and as of when
RewriteThe answer is not at the top, or it is unclear which question the post answersSettle on one question and restructure so the first two or three sentences answer it. Keep the URL
MergeTwo or more posts answer the same questionMove the content into the post that is read most (or is best), redirect the others to it with a permanent (301) redirect, and repoint internal links
RetireTopics the business no longer offers, finished campaigns, errors that cannot be fixedWith the client's approval, redirect to the closest post or unpublish

When in doubt, "don't break what people read" comes before "make it easier for AI to use". For posts with many impressions or clicks in Search Console, start with an update rather than a rewrite, so it is easier to undo if it goes wrong. Check that WordPress revisions are kept before you start.

We could not read Google's own pages on redirect types, removed pages (404 and 410) and canonical URLs on the day this was written, so their details are not given here. Check the relevant Google Search Central pages before doing the work.

Dates: make them change only when the content does

Google says it estimates the date a page was published or significantly changed from several signals and may show that date in results. It recommends a visible date with a label such as "Published" or "Last updated", matching the datePublished and dateModified of Article or BlogPosting structured data. It also says not to give a future date, and that showing a date is not guaranteed (Google Search Central: byline dates).

The catch for agencies is WordPress's modified date. WordPress sets it to now every time a post is updated. If the theme shows "Last updated" from that date, fixing a single typo makes the post look as if it was updated today. The dateModified in the BlogPosting that Nurevo AEO publishes for posts is that same WordPress date (where an SEO plugin publishes the article's structured data, that one is used as it is).

Do not sell date changes on their own. Google's "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" encourages explaining how content was produced (Google Search Central). A short line in the post saying what was reviewed is part of that explanation.

Which posts are in llms.txt depends on the order of updates

The /llms.txt that Nurevo AEO builds lists the 30 most recently modified published posts and pages, leaving out any set to "leave out of llms.txt" in the per-page settings. It is rebuilt when posts or settings change.

So saving an old post during a review can put it into llms.txt and push another post out. Saving a finished campaign post just to fix a typo can bring it back into the list. In a review month, open /llms.txt after the work, check that nothing you do not want listed has come in, and leave out unwanted posts with the per-page setting.

Sites connected to the Nurevo service also send the titles and URLs of their 30 most recently updated posts and pages for measurement. It helps to plan the order of work knowing that the list of posts AI is pointed at is tied to the order in which posts were updated. How to write llms.txt

The monthly routine

  1. Start of the month: pick the month's posts from the inventory (for example three updates and one merge). If any are to be merged or retired, send the list to the client for approval.
  2. Before the work: record the results of the chosen questions asked to AI and the Search Console numbers for those posts (next section).
  3. The work: do what was decided. Confirm facts that need checking (prices, rules, content needing an expert's review) with the client before changing them.
  4. After the work: check that the dates in the structured data match the dates on the page, the /llms.txt list, that redirects work and that no internal links are broken.
  5. Following months: after a gap, measure again the same way and put the result in the report.

Contracts and pricing

Promise the work, not the result. The Rank Masters' service page, for one, states that it commits to the work and the report but does not guarantee recovered traffic (The Rank Masters). Sentences like these in the contract or proposal prevent disagreements later:

  1. "Each month we will work on [number] existing posts according to the agreed decision (keep, update, rewrite, merge or retire) and report records from before and after the work. We do not guarantee appearance or citation in AI answers, search rankings or increases in traffic."
  2. "Before merging, redirecting or unpublishing any post, we will send you the list and wait for your approval."
  3. "Facts that need checking, such as prices, regulations and medical or legal content, will be published only after you have confirmed them."
  4. "Where we use generative AI in the work, we will tell you how, and a person will check facts and wording before publishing."

On price, building it up from time per post is easier to explain, and less likely to lose money, than working back from market rates. Set a time per post for each kind of work (an update takes [x] hours, a rewrite [y], a merge [z]), multiply by the month's numbers, and add the time for the report. Record the real time for the first three months and check it against the estimate. We give no market prices for agencies because we found no reliable primary source for them.

Do not make rewriting posts in bulk to raise the count the goal of the service. Read Google's spam policies in the original before you start. Even under a contract that promises a number of posts, keep a reason in the inventory for every post you change.

FAQ

Will reviewing existing articles get them cited by AI?

That cannot be guaranteed. AI answers change from day to day, even for the same question. What a review can promise is the work of bringing facts up to date and making answers easier to read, plus records from before and after. Report the result as measurements taken the same way both times.

Should we write new articles or review existing ones first?

If the client's site already has many posts with old prices or rules in them, start with the review. Stale facts are a reason AI gives wrong answers about a business. If no post answers a question that matters, a new article is needed.

Does changing the updated date on its own help?

We recommend not offering date-only changes. Google says it estimates the date a page was published or significantly changed, and recommends that the visible date and the structured data date match.

Fixing a typo changes the last updated date. What can we do?

WordPress updates the modified date on every save. Saving typo and wording fixes together with the month's real updates keeps the date from moving for no reason. Check in the theme's settings where the displayed date comes from.

Will merging posts lose search traffic?

It can. Keep the post people read most as the one that stays, redirect the others to it and repoint internal links. Record impressions and clicks by page before and after, and always get the client's approval first.

How many posts should we review each month?

There is no set number. It depends on how many posts there are, how many stale facts they hold and how long the client takes to confirm changes. Set priorities in the first inventory, start with a number you can sustain, and review it after about three months of recording the real time spent.

Can we rewrite with generative AI?

You can use it for drafts, but a person should check facts and wording before publishing. Google encourages explaining how content was made, including how automation or AI was used. Tell the client at the contract stage how you will use it.
