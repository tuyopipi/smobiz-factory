AI search for veterinary clinics: getting AI to read the species you treat, your hours and your after-hours care correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a veterinary clinic or animal hospital comes down to publishing the facts pet owners ask about — which species you treat, your hours and last check-in time, how you handle nights, weekends and emergencies, whether you take appointments or walk-ins, parking and payment — accurately and consistently, in the text of your pages and in structured data (schema.org's VeterinaryCare). Two things set clinics apart in AI answers more than anything else: stating plainly which animals you see (and which you don't), and saying what happens after hours, including where you send people when you're closed.

Written November 13, 2026 (JST). Facts about schema.org, Google, OpenAI and Japanese regulation were checked on the dates given in the text. Veterinary advertising rules differ by country; check the rules of your own veterinary regulator.

AI answers vary by day and by how a question is phrased. The steps here make your clinic's facts easy for AI to read correctly. They cannot guarantee that you appear in an AI answer or rank in any position.

What pet owners ask AI

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews to find a vet, the AI reads what it can find on the web and answers with a few clinic names. The questions are rarely just "vet near me". They look like "a vet in [town] that sees rabbits", "which animal hospital is open on Sunday evening", "where can I get my cat's teeth cleaned" or "a vet with parking near the station". Species, day and time, the service and how easy the clinic is to get to are all in the question.

The big difference from a human dental or medical practice is that the species narrows the list first. Some clinics see only dogs and cats, some also see rabbits, ferrets, birds or reptiles, and some see only cats. Owners want to know that before anything else. Yet on many clinic websites the species appear only in a banner image, or not at all. AI is poor at filling in what isn't written, so a clinic that never says "we see rabbits" is easy to leave out of a "vet for rabbits" answer.

The other is after-hours care. These questions are often asked when an animal is unwell, and a wrong answer costs more here than anywhere. Google says there are no extra requirements or special structured data for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual SEO basics apply: structured data should match the visible text, and your Business Profile should be up to date (checked November 12, 2026). Google: AI features and your website. The work is not a trick; it is getting your clinic's facts right and keeping them that way.

Independent clinics have reason to care. A June 2026 news report cited findings attributed to 5W Public Relations that a few large chains received a large share of veterinary recommendations in AI answers and that most independent practices tested showed little presence. The report did not publish its method or sample, so treat the figures as indicative only. myVETcandy, June 14, 2026

Structured data type: a caveat about VeterinaryCare

schema.org has a type for vets: VeterinaryCare. What's worth knowing is where it sits. VeterinaryCare is a subtype of MedicalOrganization. Unlike Dentist or Physician, it is not under LocalBusiness, the type for businesses with a physical place (checked November 13, 2026 against the type definitions in schema-dts 2.1.0, which is generated from the schema.org vocabulary). Opening hours (openingHoursSpecification) are a property of a Place, and LocalBusiness is a kind of Place.

Google's local business structured data documentation asks you to use the most specific LocalBusiness subtype you can (checked October 29, 2026). Google: Local business structured data. There is no veterinary subtype under LocalBusiness, so each option has a trade-off.

Whichever you pick assumes the page text already states the species, hours and location. Structured data restates the page's facts for machines; it does not add facts the page lacks.

Advertising rules and text written for AI

Veterinary advertising is regulated in many countries, and AI-friendly copy is still copy. Avoid comparisons and superlatives ("the best vet in town", "safer surgery than anywhere else") and outcome claims you can't support. AI tends to reuse your wording, and the same text often ends up in ads. What AI can actually use in an answer is the checkable facts: species, hours, equipment and costs.

In Japan, for example, the Veterinary Practice Act (Article 17) and its guideline limit what a clinic may advertise. A 2024 revision, in force from April 1, 2024, widened the list, and for the newly allowed items it asks clinics to show the enquiry contact, the usual treatment involved, the main risks or side effects and the cost alongside. Aichi Prefecture (Japanese). The guideline (as revised in 2014) treats a clinic's own website as in principle not an advertisement under the Act, but search ads and banner ads can be, and it bans comparative and exaggerated advertising. Guideline PDF (Japanese). Clinics elsewhere should check their own regulator's rules before publishing.

Checklist before you publish

  1. The species you treat, including those you don't, are written as text.
  2. Hours, last check-in time and closed days are text, not an image.
  3. After-hours and emergency arrangements are stated, with a referral if you don't cover them.
  4. Clinic name, address, phone and hours match across your site, Google Business Profile and booking sites.
  5. The structured data type is VeterinaryCare rather than a leftover LocalBusiness or MedicalBusiness (or you have decided on another option for a reason).
  6. The hours in structured data match the hours shown on the page.
  7. No comparisons, superlatives or unsupported outcome claims in the text or in structured data.
  8. llms.txt lists your overview, species, hours, booking, directions and FAQ pages. What is llms.txt

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, ask AI the questions owners would ask (area + species + day or time + service), without your clinic's name, and see whether your clinic comes up by name or URL. Then ask by name — "Does [clinic] see rabbits? Is it open on Sunday?" — and check the answer against today's facts. If it's wrong, look at which page the AI cited and fix that page or the outside listing. AI answers change from one run to the next, so ask the same questions several times, spaced out, and keep a record. FAQ

Where should a vet clinic start with AI search?

Write the species you treat (and those you don't), your hours and last check-in, closed days, after-hours arrangements and how to book as text on your pages. Then publish the same facts in structured data and llms.txt, and make them match your Google Business Profile and booking sites.

Which structured data type fits a vet clinic?

schema.org has VeterinaryCare. It sits under MedicalOrganization, not LocalBusiness, so place facts such as opening hours fall outside its definition. Use VeterinaryCare to say you are a vet, or both VeterinaryCare and LocalBusiness where you can publish two types. Every option assumes the facts are on the page.

How do I show that we treat rabbits or birds?

There is no structured data property for the species a clinic treats, so write it in the description, services, FAQ and page text: "We treat rabbits and ferrets. We do not treat birds." Saying what you don't treat makes it less likely AI sends the wrong owners to you.

Should we say so if we don't offer after-hours care?

Yes. Say you don't, and where you refer people: "Outside our hours we refer to [emergency hospital]." After-hours questions are often urgent, and an AI answer that sends an owner to a closed clinic does real harm.

Can we add "best vet in town" for AI?

Avoid it. Veterinary advertising rules in many countries restrict comparative and exaggerated claims, and AI can use checkable facts far more than slogans. Check your regulator's rules before publishing such wording.

Will this guarantee my clinic appears in ChatGPT?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear.
