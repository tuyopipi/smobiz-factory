AI search for tour and activity operators: make AI read your timings, who can join, weather calls and cancellation fees correctly

AI search optimisation (AEO) for tours and activities comes down to one thing: when someone asks an AI "what can we do in X for half a day with a 6-year-old?", "does it run in the rain?" or "is there an English-speaking guide?", your site should answer each condition. Give every experience its own page and write, as text, the time breakdown, the meeting point, the all-in price and what it includes, age and fitness requirements, the minimum group size and how weather cancellations are decided, the cancellation fees and the guide's languages. Publish the same facts in structured data and llms.txt, and keep your OTA listings and Google listings in step.

Written 24 November 2026 (JST). Legal, government and Google information was checked on the official pages linked in the text. Most of the legal points describe Japan; outside Japan, check your own country's licensing and consumer rules. This article is not legal advice.

AI answers change by day and by wording. These steps make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that you will appear in AI answers or rank higher. Classes for local residents are covered in AI search for lesson studios, and how to structure pages per language in AI search for multilingual sites.

What travellers ask AI about tours and activities

Once people have picked a destination, they look for things to do there. They rarely ask for an operator by name. They ask with conditions: dates, who is coming, the weather, language and budget. AI answers by listing several experiences. If those conditions are answered on your page, AI can say "from age 6, runs in light rain" and name you. If they are not, it guesses from OTA listings and reviews, or leaves you out.

More travellers plan with AI. The travel trade site PhocusWire reports Phocuswright research that 56% of US leisure travellers use AI for travel, and 35–40% in the UK, France and Germany (secondary source). PhocusWire. We have not verified a comparable figure for other markets.

One page per experience: nine facts to write

A single "Activities" page that lists everything briefly makes it hard for AI to tell which condition belongs to which experience. Give each experience its own page and write the nine facts below as text. If your price list or itinerary is only an image or a PDF, put the same content in the page body as well.

The examples describe a fictional experience in this article. Replace amounts and conditions with your own facts.

Put what you do and how long it takes in the experience name (for example "Half-day canoe trip (about 3 hours)"). An internal name such as "River Adventure A" tells AI nothing. Rain, cancellations and refunds: turn vague words into conditions

The most common worry before booking an activity is the weather and whether it will go ahead. "Subject to weather" alone gives AI nothing to decide whether you run in the rain, so it may answer from a generic OTA policy or a line in a review. Write cancellations as conditions: what triggers them, when and who decides, how guests are told, and what happens to their money.

These rewrites are this article's recommendation. Fee percentages are examples, not market rates.
AvoidWhyWrite instead
"Tours may be cancelled due to weather"No trigger, no time"Runs in light rain. Cancelled for high water, lightning or storm warnings. We decide at 18:00 the day before and 7:00 on the day"
"We will contact you if cancelled"No channel, no time"We email and text the number on your booking by 7:00 on the day"
"Refunds available"Full or minus fees?"If we cancel, full refund including card fees, or move to another date"

If you also sell through OTAs, their cancellation terms may differ from yours. Say so on your site ("Bookings made on [OTA] follow [OTA]'s terms") so AI is less likely to mix the two.

Rules in Japan: travel agency registration and the online booking confirmation screen

Depending on what you sell, Japan's Travel Agency Act may apply. The Japan Tourism Agency explains that anyone carrying out the acts set out in Article 2 of the Act as a business for payment must register as a travel agency or travel agency subagent (with the Commissioner for Type 1, otherwise with the prefectural governor). Japan Tourism Agency: Travel Agency Act overview. Wakayama Prefecture's guidance says registration is needed when a product includes buses, trains or other transport, or lodging. Wakayama Prefecture: travel agency. A guide on selling tourism content, published on the Japan Tourism Agency's site, also says selling content with transport or lodging in principle requires registration. Tourism content sales guide (PDF).

So if you bundle transport or lodging, such as "with pick-up from the station" or "with a night's stay", check whether you need to register. These sources do not state outright how an experience sold on its own is treated, so we do not either. Ask the prefectural travel agency office before you design the product. If you are registered, write the registration number (in a format such as "[Prefecture] Governor Registered Travel Agency No. 2-XXX", per Wakayama's guidance) on your company page and in the footer. It also tells AI what you are allowed to sell.

If you take bookings online on your own site, Japan's Act on Specified Commercial Transactions also applies to mail-order sales. The Consumer Affairs Agency's guideline on display at the application stage covers services too, and asks that the final confirmation screen show the content and number or period of the service, the price, when and how to pay, when the service is provided, and the conditions and method for withdrawing or cancelling. Guideline (copy hosted by the Japan Chamber of Commerce and Industry, PDF). Writing the same cancellation table on your booking screen and on your site helps both with the law and with AI search.

Google Things to do: listings come from feeds, and Google checks your page

Google Search and Maps can list tours and activities with booking links under "Things to do". Google describes free listings and ads that you pay for only when clicked, and operators take part through a connectivity partner such as their booking system. Google strongly recommends a connectivity partner for an individual operator or for a booking system with fewer than 100 operators. Google Ads Help: Things to do on Google. Adding structured data to your site does not, by itself, get you into Things to do.

What matters is that Google checks listings against your pages. Under the official site policies, the booking page may sit on a different domain, provided your main site clearly links to it; Google verifies this through sources such as your Google Business Profile. A generalised landing page where users must navigate elsewhere to find key ticket information is not accepted. Google Ads Help: Things to do official site policies. The product optimisation guide asks for a price that covers what one adult needs to take part, accurate highlights, inclusions and exclusions without exaggeration, and a cancellation policy; locations are checked against the landing page, and regions not shown there are excluded (updated 10 September 2026). Google: Experiences product optimisation. The operator's name must also be visible on the landing page. Google: Operator booking module optimisation.

Both assume your booking system and your own pages carry accurate, specific facts. The nine facts above are the groundwork not only for AI answers but for Google's listings to work properly.

Your site, OTAs and Google Business Profile: roles and the facts to keep in step

Most operators list on several OTAs and have a Google Business Profile as well as their own site. AI reads them side by side, so an old price or a discontinued tour left in one place can end up in an answer. Give each a role, and change the facts that move most often on the same day everywhere.

The split of roles is this article's suggestion. If your meeting point and office differ, say on your site which one your Google Business Profile uses.

Structured data: LocalBusiness for you, Service for each experience

For an operator's site, publishing the business as LocalBusiness and each experience as a Service is simple and accurate. schema.org also has TouristTrip, which can carry an itinerary, offers, a provider and touristType (checked in schema-dts 2.1.0). It suits guided tours that visit several places, but Nurevo AEO does not output TouristTrip, so if you use it, write it by hand and include only facts visible on the page.

Google says no special schema.org markup is needed for AI Overviews or AI Mode. Google: AI features and your website. Whatever type you use, having the nine facts in the description matters more. With Nurevo AEO, the output looks like this (values are examples).

Setting it up on a WordPress tour or activity site

  1. Install and activate Nurevo AEO (free) from WordPress.org. Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO, SEO SIMPLE PACK and VK All in One Expansion Unit keep working (structured data is not duplicated).
  2. In the setup wizard choose "Other" and enter the address (meeting point or base), phone and office hours. It is published as LocalBusiness.
  3. In the business description, write what experiences you run, the meeting point and nearest station, guide languages, and your travel agency registration status (the number if registered, or that you do not arrange transport or lodging).
  4. Add each experience as a service (up to 20). Put what it is and how long in the name (for example "Half-day canoe trip (about 3 hours)"), the all-in adult price as a number, the currency, that experience's booking page as the URL, and the time breakdown, inclusions, who can join, minimum group, weather rule and cancellation fees in the description. Each becomes a Service with an Offer and a reserve action, and a line in llms.txt.
  5. Enter FAQs (rain, children's ages, whether you need to swim, languages, same-day booking, cancellations, parking) and place the Nurevo FAQ block (or the [nurevo_faq] shortcode) on your experience pages. See AI search for FAQ pages.
  6. If your booking system is on another domain, link from each experience page straight to its own booking page, not just to the booking home page. See AI search for booking pages.
  7. Open /llms.txt and check that each experience page, prices, cancellation policy and directions are listed. Run the diagnosis on the AEO score screen and fix what it flags.

Pre-publish checklist

  1. Each experience has its own page, and its name says what it is and how long it takes.
  2. Meeting and finish times and the time breakdown are written as text.
  3. The meeting point address, directions from the nearest station and parking are written.
  4. The all-in price including tax, what is included and what costs extra are written.
  5. Age, fitness, swimming, pregnancy and similar conditions are written.
  6. The minimum group, the weather cancellation trigger, decision time, how guests are told and the refund are written.
  7. There is a cancellation fee table, and the page says how OTA bookings are handled.
  8. Guide languages, and any day-by-day differences, are written.
  9. For products that bundle transport or lodging, registration has been checked with the authorities (in Japan, the prefectural office), and any registration number is on the company page and footer.
  10. Prices, policies and operating seasons match across your site, OTAs, Google Business Profile and the booking page.

Check whether you appear, and whether AI answers correctly

Afterwards, ask AI the questions travellers would ask. Check whether you appear for area plus condition questions such as "[town] half day activity kids", "[town] rainy day things to do" or "[town] English guided tour". Then ask by name, "does it run in the rain?", "what age?", "cancellation fee?", and record whether the answers match your site. If they do not, look for old information on an OTA listing, an old blog post or a page for a tour you no longer run.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time. FAQ

Where should a tour or activity operator start with AI search?

Give each experience its own page and write, as text, the time breakdown, meeting point, all-in price and inclusions, who can join, minimum group, weather cancellation rule, cancellation fees and guide languages. Then publish the same facts as LocalBusiness and Service structured data and in llms.txt, and keep them in step with your OTA listings and Google Business Profile.

How should I describe what happens when it rains?

State the trigger ("runs in light rain; cancelled for high water, lightning or warnings"), when you decide (for example 18:00 the day before and 7:00 on the day), how you tell guests, and whether they get a refund or a new date. "Subject to weather" alone does not let AI tell whether you run in the rain.

Do I need a travel agency licence to sell activities in Japan?

Guidance from Wakayama Prefecture and a guide published on the Japan Tourism Agency's site say products that include transport or lodging in principle require registration. How an experience sold on its own is treated depends on the product, so ask the prefectural travel agency office. This article is not legal advice.

Will structured data get me into Google Things to do?

No. Google recommends taking part through a connectivity partner such as your booking system. But Google checks listings against your pages, so you still need accurate facts on each experience page and a direct link from each one to its booking page.

Should I use TouristTrip?

It is not required. LocalBusiness for the business and a Service for each experience, with the conditions in the description, carries the facts well. If you use TouristTrip for multi-stop tours, include only the itinerary and prices shown on the page. Nurevo AEO does not output TouristTrip.

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read, and keep checking whether you appear and whether the answers are correct.
