How to measure AI visibility: choosing questions, how many runs, and reading the numbers

You measure AI visibility by choosing the questions your customers would actually ask, putting each to an AI several times, and counting the share of answers that name your business or cite your site as a source. Three things matter most: keep a fixed set of questions that do not contain your name, ask them repeatedly under the same conditions, and read the trend across runs rather than any single answer.

Written October 31, 2026. What OpenAI, Perplexity and Google say was checked in their own documentation that day. It can change, so check the current pages too.

AI answers change between days and between runs of the same question. The numbers this method produces are a sample, not a view of every answer. And nothing you do can guarantee that an AI will mention you or rank you anywhere.

What "AI visibility" means: four numbers to track

Different tools use "AI visibility" for slightly different numbers. If you measure it yourself, keeping these four apart makes your results much easier to compare later.

Runs that failed and returned no answer are left out of the denominator. One answer can cite several domains, so the share's denominator is not the number of answers.
NumberHow to count itWhat it tells you
Mention rateAnswers whose text names your business (including common alternative names) ÷ answers receivedWhether the AI suggests you as an option
Citation rateAnswers whose sources include your domain ÷ answers receivedWhether the AI uses your site as a source
Appearance rateAnswers with either a mention or a citation ÷ answers receivedWhether you show up in the answer at all
Citation share against competitorsCitations of your domain ÷ (citations of your domain + citations of the competitor domains you chose)How often you are chosen as a source compared with named competitors

An AI may name you while citing a review site or a map service instead of your own site, and the reverse happens too. Record the two separately.

How to measure it, in six steps

  1. Pick the AI platforms: start with the one or two your customers are most likely to use. Record each platform separately and never pool them. Each one uses its own search system and its own model, so a result on one does not carry over to another.
  2. Write the questions: 5 to 20 "discovery" questions that do not contain your name, such as "Can you recommend a dentist in Umeda?" Questions that name you are kept out of the rate and used to check whether the AI states your facts correctly (next section).
  3. Decide how many runs: answers change each time, so ask each question two or three times. The fewer runs, the more a lucky or unlucky answer moves the number.
  4. Keep the conditions the same: the same wording, the same platform and mode, signed out, every time. Consumer apps can change their answers with sign-in, location and chat history.
  5. Record each answer: date and time, platform, question, whether you were named, whether your domain was among the sources, who appeared instead, and an excerpt (see the sheet below).
  6. Repeat monthly with the same set: compare trends only between runs with the same questions, the same platform and the same conditions. If you change the questions, start a new series.

Named questions return your name almost every time. Mixing them into the rate only makes it look higher, so keep them separate. Write discovery questions in the words your customers use, and leave out questions about services you do not offer.

Do not mark an answer as correct automatically: a person should check it against your own facts. If you find an error, check that the fact in question (hours, address, services) is stated clearly on your site. See also how businesses appear in ChatGPT.

Five things to keep in mind when reading the numbers

  1. Do not act on a single run. Look at the trend under the same conditions. A rate based on two or three answers means very little.
  2. The order of sources is not a ranking. A source list comes back in the order the platform returns it; it is not a search ranking or a "best first" order.
  3. The numbers cannot tell you why you are missing. All they show is that the answers you observed did not cite you.
  4. Site improvements and appearance do not always move together quickly. When Nurevo studied 62 local businesses, its on-site AEO score showed no relationship with how often Perplexity named or cited them (ρ = 0.03). A study that size can only detect fairly strong relationships, so this does not prove there is none, but do not assume that fixing your site alone will raise the rate. Read the study
  5. Read each platform on its own. Appearing on Perplexity does not mean you appear on ChatGPT or in Google's AI features.

FAQ

How many questions and runs are enough?

There is no single right number, but 5 to 20 discovery questions asked two or three times each is a practical start. The fewer questions and runs, the more chance moves the result. What matters most is measuring the same questions under the same conditions every month.

Can't I just ask ChatGPT myself?

It is a useful first look. But answers can change with sign-in, location and chat history, and one answer carries a lot of chance. Record what you see in a sheet and repeat it under the same conditions, and you get numbers you can compare.

If the AI names me when asked about me by name, am I fine?

Named questions return your name almost every time, so they say little about visibility. Use them to check that facts such as your address and hours are right and that your site is cited, and measure visibility with questions that do not contain your name.

My rate is low. What should I do first?

First check that AI can read your site: structured data, llms.txt, and that robots.txt is not blocking AI search crawlers. Then look at what the businesses and sites that appear instead say about themselves; it often shows which facts you are missing. Fixing your site does not guarantee the rate will rise quickly.

Will measuring make AI show my business?

No. Measuring tells you where you stand; it does not change the answers. AI answers change daily, and no method can guarantee a mention or a position.
