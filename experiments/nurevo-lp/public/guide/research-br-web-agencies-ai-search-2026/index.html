AI search readiness of Brazilian web design agencies: 261 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 261 Brazilian web design agencies' own websites. 34.1% (89 sites) had an llms.txt — third among the 17 countries with 100+ sites in our survey, after Singapore and India (US 23.1%, Japan 13.4%). 25.3% met all six AI-search items we checked, more than in four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries combined (Mexico, Colombia, Argentina and Chile: 18.3%) or the US (18.9%), with no statistically significant difference from Spain (27.4%). 5.5% (12 sites) blocked an AI bot in robots.txt; the only site blocking a search crawler was one that disallowed every bot.

Published 25 November 2026. Data aggregated 12 November 2026. Research and writing: Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社, Japan). Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, so we have an interest in this topic. This is the eighth of our country reports and the first for Latin America (after the US, the UK, Australia, India, Canada, France and Singapore). A Brazilian Portuguese version is also available.

Key findings

  1. Of 261 Brazilian web agency websites, 34.1% (89) had an llms.txt — third of the 17 countries with 100+ reached sites, after Singapore (38.8%) and India (36.6%). That is higher than the US (23.1%) and all other overseas agencies (26.2%) (p<0.001 and p=0.004) and 2.5 times Japan (13.4%). There was no statistically significant difference from Spain (31.9%, p=0.59).
  2. 25.3% (66) met all six AI-search items and the average was 4.11 of 6 — higher than four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries combined (18.3%, 3.09; all six p=0.040). The gaps were large for JSON-LD (74.7% vs 54.8%), meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt (all p<0.001), and for the WordPress share (76.6% vs 47.7%). Among WordPress sites only, llms.txt adoption was almost identical: Brazil 38.5%, Spanish-speaking Latin America 38.3%.
  3. Of the 217 sites with a robots.txt, 5.5% (12) blocked an AI bot — no statistically significant difference from the US (4.3%, p=0.39) or other overseas agencies (5.1%), and higher than Japan (1.3%). 10 of the 11 sites blocking GPTBot still allowed OAI-SearchBot. The only site blocking any of the four bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks was one that disallowed every bot, Googlebot included.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of the 383 agency sites listed as Brazil in our collection list, 261 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of all business websites. For comparison we use the same survey's four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries (Mexico 116, Colombia 81, Argentina 76, Chile 50; 323 in total), Spain (248), Portugal (46), the US (4,472), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites except Brazil (13,091).
  2. What we fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), between early November and 12 November 2026, aggregated on 12 November.
  3. Definitions: an llms.txt counts if it returned status 200 with non-HTML text over 20 characters. JSON-LD means at least one JSON-LD block on the home page; "broken" means at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type means an @type containing Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress means wp-content, wp-includes or a WordPress generator tag in the HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: for each bot, we apply the matching robots.txt group's rules to the home page (/) using longest match. The base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" means blocking any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot. "Names an AI bot" means naming any of the 11 AI bots (all listed bots except Googlebot and Bingbot) in a User-agent line.
  5. "Six items" counts how many of these a site meets (0–6): (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. It is a simple yardstick for this survey and does not show whether a site appears in AI answers.
  6. HTTP 202: 9 Brazilian home pages (3.4% of reached) answered with status 202, fewer than in the US (15.0%) or other overseas (11.2%). A 202 page scores "no" on almost every item, which affects country comparisons, so we also report figures for status-200 sites only.
  7. Statistics: differences between Brazil and other groups were tested with the chi-square test, and AI bot blocking (small counts) with Fisher's exact test. Groups under 30 sites are not shown (AI bot blocking for Portugal and Chile, which have fewer than 30 sites with a robots.txt, is shown as "—").

Results: Brazil compared with Latin America, Spain, Portugal, the US and Japan

Brazil was higher than the US and other overseas agencies on llms.txt (34.1%), FAQPage (13.4%) and all six items (25.3%) (llms.txt: vs US p<0.001, vs overseas p=0.004; FAQPage: p<0.001, p=0.023; all six: p=0.011, p=0.17). Against Spain there was no statistically significant difference on llms.txt, all six items or JSON-LD (p=0.59, 0.59, 0.15), nor against Portugal (46 sites) on llms.txt (26.1%, p=0.29).

Compared with the four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries, Brazil was 16–20 points higher on JSON-LD (74.7% vs 54.8%), Organization-type (68.2% vs 51.4%), meta descriptions (77.4% vs 60.4%), canonical links (79.7% vs 60.4%) and robots.txt (83.1% vs 67.2%) (all p<0.001), and its WordPress share was much higher (76.6% vs 47.7%). Among WordPress sites only, llms.txt was almost the same (38.5% vs 38.3%), so much of the overall gap coincides with the difference in WordPress share.

Among status-200 sites only (Brazil 252), llms.txt was 35.3%, still higher than the US (27.2%, p=0.005) and other overseas (29.5%, p=0.046) and no different from Spain (33.8%, p=0.72). All six items was 26.2%, and the gap with the US (22.3%, p=0.15) and other overseas (24.5%, p=0.53) is no longer clear.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. 10 of the 11 sites blocking GPTBot did not block OAI-SearchBot. The remaining one disallowed every bot, including Googlebot and Bingbot, without naming any AI bot.
  2. Of the 13 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots, 7 used the same combination of seven: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended. The rest were the one site blocking every bot, one blocking only Bytespider, one blocking only CCBot, and three other combinations.
  3. 29 sites (13.4% of sites with a robots.txt) named an AI bot; 17 of them blocked none of the AI bots from the home page.
  4. 6 of the 12 sites blocking an AI bot also had an llms.txt.
  5. 88.5% of robots.txt files included a Sitemap line.

llms.txt contents and what five-item sites were missing

  1. The median llms.txt among the 89 sites that had one was 64 lines. 9 sites (3.4%) had an llms-full.txt.
  2. 99 sites met five of the six items; for 83 of them (83.8%) the missing item was the llms.txt.
  3. 37 sites (14.2%) met none of the six items: 9 answered 202, and 28 answered 200 but had no structured data, meta description, canonical link, robots.txt or llms.txt that we could find (only one had a robots.txt). Sites that render their content with JavaScript score "no" in this survey.

Structured data types

The most common types in Brazilian home-page JSON-LD were WebSite (70.9%), Organization (64.8%), WebPage (64.0%), SearchAction (62.8%) and ImageObject (62.1%) — close to Spain and the US and above Spanish-speaking Latin America. These are the types WordPress SEO plugins typically output. FAQPage questions and answers (Question/Answer) appeared on 13.4%, more than twice the US (6.2%). PostalAddress appeared on 28.4%.

WordPress and SEO plugins

76.6% (200) of Brazilian agency sites run WordPress. WordPress sites had llms.txt 38.5%, JSON-LD 89.0% and a six-item average of 4.80; the 61 non-WordPress sites (9 of which answered 202) had llms.txt 19.7% (12 sites), JSON-LD 27.9% and an average of 1.82. Comparing WordPress sites only, Brazil's llms.txt share (38.5%) was higher than US WordPress sites (30.1%, p=0.012) and not significantly different from other overseas WordPress sites (33.0%, p=0.10) or Spain (42.5%, p=0.43).

Yoast SEO was detected on 54.0% and Rank Math on 26.0%. AIOSEO (4 sites) is under 30 and not shown. llms.txt was present on 55.8% of Rank Math sites, 28.7% of Yoast SEO sites and 36.1% of sites with no plugin detected. Plugin features or settings for llms.txt may play a part, but this survey did not test causes.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not all business websites. Country comes from our collection list, which is not a representative sample of Brazilian agencies. 261 reached sites is small among the 17 countries, and we did not analyse by state or city. Some counts (e.g. by bot) are small.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt is judged for the home page (/); partial blocks and firewall or CDN blocking are not counted. We checked whether an llms.txt exists, not its quality.
  3. Each site was fetched once; unreachable sites (122) are excluded. The 9 sites answering 202 may have served something other than their real home page. Content or structured data added by JavaScript is not counted.
  4. SEO plugins are detected from HTML strings; sites that hide traces or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. These figures are from the 12 November 2026 aggregation. We re-run the survey weekly, so later figures may differ.

How to read this (opinion)

This section is our interpretation. Brazilian agency sites rank near the top of 17 countries on llms.txt and look much like Spain. The gap with Spanish-speaking Latin America looks large, but among WordPress sites llms.txt adoption was almost the same, so the CMS mix may be a big part of it; this survey did not test causes. AI bot blocking was rare and mostly limited to training crawlers. Which bots to block is each site's own decision. Having these items in place does not guarantee appearing in AI answers.

FAQ

Are Brazilian agencies ahead on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt, yes: 34.1%, third of 17 countries after Singapore and India. 25.3% met all six items, more than in the US (18.9%) (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

How does Brazil compare with the rest of Latin America?

Brazil was higher than four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries (Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Chile) on llms.txt (34.1% vs 24.5%) and JSON-LD (74.7% vs 54.8%). Its WordPress share was also much higher (76.6% vs 47.7%); among WordPress sites only, llms.txt was almost the same (38.5% vs 38.3%).

Do many Brazilian sites block AI crawlers?

Few: 5.5% of sites with a robots.txt (12 sites), not significantly different from the US (4.3%). The only site blocking OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot was one that disallowed every bot.
