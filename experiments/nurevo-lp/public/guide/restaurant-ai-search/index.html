AI search for restaurants, cafés and bars: getting AI to read your menu, hours, booking and allergen information correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a restaurant, café or bar means writing your menu and prices, opening hours and last orders, days closed, cuisine, seating and private rooms, and how to book as text on your own site, then publishing the same facts in structured data (schema.org Restaurant and its relatives) and in your Google Business Profile. That gives ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI what they need to answer "a bar near the station with a private room that's open after 10pm" without guessing. If you publish allergen information, publish only what you can keep accurate and current, together with the risk of cross-contact.

Written November 3, 2026 (JST). Information from Google, schema.org, OpenAI and Japanese public bodies was checked on each official page that day.

AI answers change with the day, the wording of a question and where the person asking is. The steps here make your restaurant's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will recommend you or list you near the top.

What diners ask AI, and the facts an answer needs

Restaurant questions pack place, time, party size, occasion, budget and dietary needs into one sentence. An AI looks for information that lets it check each condition, so the starting point is to make it readable, as text, which conditions your restaurant meets. Here is how question types map to the facts they need and where to publish them.

Where AI reads about your restaurant

When an AI answers about restaurants, its sources fall into a few groups: your own website, your Google Business Profile (your listing on Google Maps), restaurant guides and booking sites, reviews and social media, and news or list articles. Google says Business Profiles can help your business be found in both AI responses and other Google Search results. Google: optimizing for generative AI features

When ChatGPT searches the web, it uses general location information based on your IP address, and its answers include inline citations and a Sources list. OpenAI: Searching the web with ChatGPT. For "near me" questions, then, the answer depends on where the person asking is. When you test for yourself, put your area's name in the question so your results are comparable.

No AI service publishes how much weight it gives each source. What is certain is that when your website, Business Profile and guide listings disagree on hours or prices, an AI cannot tell which is right and may repeat outdated or wrong information. The core of the work is to make your own website the reference for correct information and bring every other listing into line with it.

Put your menu on the page as text, not only as images or PDFs

If your menu exists only as photos or a PDF, there is no guarantee that AI or search engines read dish names and prices correctly. Give your site a menu page with dish names, prices and short descriptions written as text, and add photos alongside. For seasonal menus, state the period ("Winter set menu, December to February") and remove items once they end.

Show prices the way local rules require. In Japan, for example, the National Tax Agency requires prices shown to consumers in advance to include consumption tax, whatever the medium. National Tax Agency: total price display (Japanese). If eat-in and takeaway prices differ, list both so neither people nor AI misread them.

Google Business Profile has a menu editor for food and drink businesses, with sections, dish names, prices and descriptions, and a separate field for a menu link. Google may also transcribe menu data from your website; you can edit or delete wrong items, or opt out of transcription. Google Business Profile Help: About the menu editor. Keeping the menu on your own site accurate pays off here too.

Structured data: what to put in Restaurant

In structured data, your business is a kind of schema.org FoodEstablishment, which is a kind of LocalBusiness. Its sub-types include Restaurant, CafeOrCoffeeShop, BarOrPub, Bakery, FastFoodRestaurant and IceCreamShop. Google's local business documentation asks you to use the most specific type possible, and its own example uses Restaurant (document last updated September 8, 2026, checked November 3). With hasMenu you can describe each dish using Menu, MenuSection and MenuItem, and schema.org's examples also show dietary suitability (suitableForDiet). Google's documentation, however, only recommends the menu URL (menu). Marking up every dish can also fall out of date quickly when your menu changes often, so make the text menu on your page right first.

Values in structured data should match what the page shows. Do not put dishes that are not on the page, or a price range that does not reflect reality, into your markup alone.

Allergen and dietary information: decide this before telling AI

Rules differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you. In Japan, food served in restaurants is outside the mandatory allergen labeling of the Food Labeling Act. An industry guide on providing allergen information when eating out asks restaurants that do publish it to check ingredients against suppliers' specifications and keep the information current, to tell customers that unintended cross-contact can happen because dishes share a kitchen, and to say in advance when they cannot provide reliable information. It also notes that information on a website is convenient because diners can check it before visiting, but becomes a cause of accidental exposure if it is not kept up to date. Guide to providing allergen information in eating-out and ready-meal businesses (Japanese; industry council, revised March 9, 2023)

AI summarizes the text on your site. If all you write is a short label such as "allergy friendly" or "gluten-free, worry-free", the conditions and exceptions can be lost in the summary. If you publish this information, put the scope ("we offer three dishes made without egg or dairy"), the cross-contact risk ("egg and dairy are handled in the same kitchen") and how to confirm ("please call us when booking") in the same paragraph. If you cannot accommodate allergies, saying so is useful information too.

In structured data, do not state what you cannot verify. schema.org has a property for dietary suitability, but it cannot express cross-contact risk, so anything that touches on allergies is safer explained in your page text, with its conditions.

Reviews and restaurant guides: what not to do for AI

Marking up reviews or star ratings that you post on your own site will not get you review stars in Google. In 2019 Google said that self-serving reviews, meaning reviews about a business placed on that business's own website, aren't allowed for LocalBusiness and Organization. Google Search Central blog, September 16, 2019

Rules on undisclosed advertising differ by country. In Japan, stealth marketing has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations since October 1, 2023, and posts or reviews a business asks or instructs third parties to make count as that business's advertising. Consumer Affairs Agency: stealth marketing (Japanese). Do not grow undisclosed sponsored reviews, or claims you cannot back up such as "No. 1 in town", in the hope that AI will speak well of you.

Restaurant guides and booking sites are sometimes used as sources by AI. Where you can edit your listing, update it to the same hours, prices and days closed as your website; where you cannot, ask the site to correct it.

When an AI answer gets your restaurant wrong

  1. Open the source links in the AI's answer and find which page the wrong information came from.
  2. If the source is your website, fix the page text and structured data. If it is your Business Profile, fix it in Google's dashboard.
  3. If the source is a restaurant guide or a list article, edit your listing or ask the publisher to correct it. Check for leftover pages about a closed branch or your address before a move.
  4. When you close temporarily, change hours or change prices, update your website, Business Profile, guide listings and llms.txt on the same day.
  5. Corrections are not always reflected in AI answers right away. Ask the same question again after a few days to a few weeks.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Your menu is written as text with dish names and prices; seasonal items show their period.
  2. Hours for each day, last orders and days closed match across your website, Business Profile and guide listings.
  3. The structured data type fits the business (Restaurant, CafeOrCoffeeShop, BarOrPub and so on) and includes address, phone and opening hours.
  4. Your page text says what cuisine you serve, your seating and private rooms, and conditions such as children and smoking.
  5. Set menus and party plans show their contents, price, duration and how to book.
  6. Allergen information, if published, states the scope, the cross-contact risk and how to confirm; if not published, the page says so.
  7. Reviews on your own site are not marked up as ratings, and no undisclosed sponsored reviews are used.
  8. llms.txt lists your overview, menu, directions and booking pages. What is llms.txt?

FAQ

Where should a restaurant start with AI search?

Write your menu (dish names and prices), opening hours, last orders, days closed, address and how to book as text on your own site. Then bring your Google Business Profile and guide listings into line with it, and publish the same facts in Restaurant structured data and llms.txt.

If I am on restaurant guide sites, do I still need my own website?

Guide listings can be used as sources by AI, but your own site is where you can publish your restaurant's information in the most detail and most accurately, and it is the reference you use when correcting other listings. Keeping them in agreement is the safest approach.

Is a photo of my menu enough?

With only photos or a PDF, there is no guarantee that dish names and prices are read correctly. A menu page with dish names, prices and descriptions written as text, with photos alongside, is the better choice.

Should I tell AI about allergen options?

Only if you can keep the information accurate and current. Where you publish it, give the scope, the cross-contact risk and how to confirm together. If you cannot be sure, saying that you do not provide allergen information is also important.

Will this guarantee ChatGPT recommends my restaurant?

No. AI answers change with the wording of a question and where the person asking is, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear and whether the answers are right.
