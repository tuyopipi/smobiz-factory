AI search for restaurants: make your menu, prices and allergen information readable to ChatGPT and Google

The most important part of AI search optimisation (AEO) for a restaurant is to put, as text on your own site, the facts diners ask AI before they choose: what they can eat, what it costs, and whether they can eat it safely. That means a menu written as page text rather than only a PDF or photo, full prices with any service or cover charges, allergen information with its limits and a date, and the real detail behind "vegan" or "halal". Then align the same facts on Google Business Profile, booking and review sites and the new Businesses in ChatGPT, and support them with Restaurant and Menu structured data.

Written 28 November 2026 (JST). FDA, UK government, schema.org, Google and OpenAI pages were checked at the links in the text on 28 November 2026. This article is not legal advice; check allergen and labelling duties with your local food authority.

AI answers change from day to day and with the wording of a question. The steps here help AI read your restaurant's facts correctly; they do not guarantee that you will appear in an AI answer or rank higher.

What diners ask AI about restaurants

People do not only ask "lunch near Example Station". They add conditions: "Is it OK with kids?", "I'm allergic to shellfish, is there anything I can eat?", "Do they have vegan dishes?", "How much is dinner per person?". AI can only answer these if the answer is written somewhere as text.

Write the menu as page text, not only a PDF or photo

If your menu exists only as a photo or PDF, AI may not read it, or may read it wrongly. Put each dish name, a short description and the price on the page as HTML text. Keep the PDF or photo as an addition to the text menu, not instead of it.

  1. Use the same dish names as your printed menu, with one line saying what it is (main ingredients, portion, spice level). "Chef's whim" alone tells AI nothing.
  2. Group by lunch, dinner, set or tasting menus, drinks and takeout. Mark dishes served only at lunch or only on weekdays.
  3. For seasonal or limited dishes, give the dates and remove them when they end. A dish left on the page is a dish AI says you still serve.
  4. Show the date the menu was last updated. When prices change, update your site, Google Business Profile and listing sites on the same day.
  5. If many guests are visitors from abroad, publish the menu as text in their languages too. See multilingual sites for AI search.

On Google Business Profile, food and drink businesses can add sections, dishes, descriptions and prices in the menu editor, and changes can take 24 to 48 hours. You can also add menu photos (PDFs too) or a menu link; Google may transcribe the menu from your website, and owners can choose which menu is shown. Google Business Profile Help: About the menu editor Getting the text menu on your site right also helps the menu Google reads from it.

Prices: what is included, and what is extra

AI may quote one sentence from your page. "Mains from $15" with the conditions at the bottom of the page can become just "$15". Put the conditions in the same sentence as the price: "Lunch mains are $15 to $24; a 3% kitchen charge is added to every bill."

In the US, chain restaurants with 20 or more locations must show calories for standard menu items; the compliance date was 7 May 2018 (FDA: Menu labeling requirements). The rule's definition of a menu includes "menus on the Internet" (21 CFR 101.11, 2019 edition), so a covered chain's website menu needs the same calories as its printed one.

  1. List every extra charge: service charge, cover, kitchen or wellness fees, private-room minimums. Say when each applies (dinner only, groups of six or more).
  2. For set or tasting menus, give the number of courses, what drinks are included, the minimum party size and the booking cut-off next to the price.
  3. If you give a "typical spend per person", say what it includes (drinks or not, tip or not) in the same sentence.
  4. If takeout and dine-in prices differ, say so in both places.

Allergen information: what the rules ask, and how to write it

In the US there are nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame, which was added from 1 January 2023 (FDA: FASTER Act). Federal labelling rules apply to packaged food. For unpackaged food, the 2022 FDA Food Code says the permit holder shall notify consumers in writing of major food allergens in the food, by physical or electronic means such as a menu; the Food Code is a model, and applies only where a state or local jurisdiction adopts it (FDA: 2022 Food Code and sesame). Check which edition your jurisdiction uses.

In the UK, information on 14 allergens must be available for non-prepacked food. It can be given orally by staff, as long as a notice, menu or label tells customers where to find it; prepacked-for-direct-sale food has needed full ingredient labelling since 1 October 2021; and for online orders the information must be available before purchase and at delivery (UK technical guidance, updated 3 November 2026). The Food Standards Agency's best-practice guidance (24 February 2025) recommends written information supported by a conversation, and online, on the main menu or no more than one click away. It is guidance, not law (FSA best practice).

Examples only. Write what your kitchen actually does, and leave out anything you cannot confirm.
What to writeExample
Scope"Our allergen chart covers the 14 allergens for every dish. For other ingredients, please ask our staff."
FormatA text table with dishes as rows and allergens as columns, not only an image
Cross-contact"We cook shellfish and wheat in the same kitchen and fryer. We cannot guarantee any dish is free from traces."
How to check"Tell us about allergies when you book. On the day, the manager will confirm ingredients."
Date"Ingredients as of [date]. We review the chart whenever a supplier changes."

The risk with AI is that "no shellfish" survives as a quote while "cooked in the same fryer as shellfish" is dropped. Put the cross-contact warning at the top of the page and on the relevant dishes, and separate "does not contain" from "can be prepared without". Avoid broad phrases such as "allergy-friendly" on their own in your Google description or FAQs.

If you run a restaurant in Japan, the rules differ: allergen labelling is mandatory only for packaged food, nine specified ingredients apply since April 2026, and rice origin must be conveyed for rice dishes. The Japanese version of this page covers those rules.

Vegetarian, vegan, halal and other diets

  1. If you say "vegan", say which dishes, and whether stocks and sauces are free of animal products too. If a dish has no meat but uses fish stock, write exactly that.
  2. For halal or kosher, name the certification if you have one. If you do not, say only what you do: "Some dishes contain no pork or alcohol (not certified)."
  3. Even if only a few dishes qualify, name them. AI can then say "you can eat this dish here" instead of guessing.
  4. Words such as "homemade", "local" or "line-caught" become your restaurant's description in AI answers. Use them only where they are true.

Structured data: Restaurant and Menu

Use Restaurant (or CafeOrCoffeeShop, Bakery, BarOrPub). Cuisine goes in servesCuisine, price level in priceRange, and bookings in acceptsReservations (true/false or the URL of your booking page). For the menu, schema.org's current property is hasMenu, which supersedes menu; Google's documentation lists menu (the menu URL), servesCuisine and priceRange as recommended properties (Google Search Central: Local business structured data). Giving both the same URL is consistent.

Dishes can be described with Menu, MenuSection and MenuItem. MenuItem has suitableForDiet, whose values are the 11 RestrictedDiet types such as VeganDiet, VegetarianDiet, HalalDiet and GlutenFreeDiet. schema.org has no property for allergens, so allergen information belongs in a text table on the page, not in structured data.

      "url": "https://example.com/",
      "telephone": "+1-000-000-0000",
      "address": {
        "@type": "PostalAddress",
        "addressRegion": "CA",
        "addressLocality": "Example City",
        "streetAddress": "123 Example St"
      },
      "openingHours": ["Tu-Su 11:30-14:00", "Tu-Sa 17:30-22:00"],
      "servesCuisine": ["French", "Seafood"],
      "priceRange": "$$",
      "menu": "https://example.com/menu/",
      "hasMenu": { "@id": "https://example.com/menu/#menu" },

    },
    {
      "@type": "Menu",
      "@id": "https://example.com/menu/#menu",
      "name": "Lunch menu",
      "hasMenuSection": {
        "@type": "MenuSection",
        "name": "Mains",
        "hasMenuItem": [
          {
            "@type": "MenuItem",

Only mark up individual dishes if you can keep them identical to the page. If your menu is long or prices change often, pointing hasMenu at the menu page is enough. A price in structured data that differs from the page leaves AI unsure which is right.

Setting it up on a WordPress site

  1. Install and activate Nurevo AEO (free) from WordPress.org. Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO, SEO SIMPLE PACK and VK All in One Expansion Unit keep working, without duplicate structured data.
  2. In the setup wizard, choose "Restaurant or café". It publishes Restaurant. For a café, bakery or bar, enter CafeOrCoffeeShop, Bakery or BarOrPub in "Business type (schema.org type)" in Settings.
  3. Enter your name, address, phone and hours exactly as on Google Business Profile. Start the description with your cuisine, signature dishes and how lunch and dinner differ.
  4. Add the ways guests use you as Services: tasting menu, private dining, events, takeout, catering (up to 20). Services appear in structured data (Service) and in llms.txt. Individual dishes belong on the menu page, not in Services.
  5. There are no fields for servesCuisine, priceRange, menu/hasMenu or acceptsReservations, so add them with the webmcp_canary_business_node filter below, using the same values as your page text. If Yoast SEO or a similar plugin is active, values it already publishes take precedence.
  6. Enter FAQs on allergens, vegan dishes, children, private rooms and walk-ins, and place the Nurevo FAQ block (or the [nurevo_faq] shortcode) on your menu or visit page. FAQ structured data is published only on a page that shows the questions. See FAQ pages for AI search.
  7. Open /llms.txt and check that the menu, visit and booking pages are listed. Run the check on the AEO score screen and fix what it flags.
// Add cuisine, price range, menu and booking URLs to the business node (same values as the page text)
add_filter('webmcp_canary_business_node', function (array $node, array $settings) {
    $node['servesCuisine'] = array('French', 'Seafood');
    $node['priceRange'] = '$$';
    $node['menu'] = 'https://example.com/menu/';
    $node['hasMenu'] = 'https://example.com/menu/';
    $node['acceptsReservations'] = 'https://example.com/reserve/';
    return $node;
}, 10, 2);

Pre-publish checklist

  1. The menu page has dish names, short descriptions and prices as text, not only as a photo or PDF.
  2. Lunch, dinner, set menus, drinks and takeout have their own headings, and the menu shows an updated date.
  3. Every price sentence says what is included, and extra charges and when they apply are listed.
  4. If you are a US chain with 20+ locations, the website menu shows the same calories as the printed one.
  5. The allergen information states its scope, has a text table, a cross-contact warning, how to check, and a date.
  6. Vegan, vegetarian, halal or gluten-free claims name the dishes and what is behind them (stocks, sauces, certification).
  7. "Homemade", "local" and similar words are used only where true.
  8. Name, address, phone, hours and menu prices match across your site, Google Business Profile and listing sites.
  9. Structured data uses Restaurant (or a café, bakery or bar type) with servesCuisine, priceRange, menu/hasMenu and acceptsReservations matching the page.
  10. llms.txt lists your menu, visit and booking pages. What is llms.txt?

Check whether AI mentions you, and whether it is right

Once the facts are in place, ask AI what diners would ask. Check area-plus-food questions such as "grilled fish lunch near Example Station", and then ask about you by name: "How much is dinner per person?", "Can I eat there with a shellfish allergy?", "Do they have vegan dishes?". Compare each answer with your site. If something is wrong, open the page the answer cites and fix the information there first.

FAQ

Where should a restaurant start with AI search?

Start with the menu page: dish names, short descriptions and prices as text, not only a PDF or photo. Then add extra charges, allergen information with its limits and a date, and the detail behind dietary claims, and make Google Business Profile and listing sites match your site.

Is a PDF menu a problem?

Having a PDF is fine, but if the menu exists only as a PDF or photo, AI may not read it, or may read it wrongly. Publish the same menu as page text and keep the PDF for printing.

Do restaurants have to publish allergen information?

It depends on where you are. In the UK, information on 14 allergens must be available for non-prepacked food, and can be given by staff if signposted. In the US, federal labelling rules cover packaged food, while the FDA Food Code, where adopted, asks for written notification for unpackaged food. Whatever the rule, if you publish it, make it accurate, with a cross-contact warning and a date.

Can allergens go in structured data?

schema.org has no allergen property. You can mark dishes suitable for diets such as vegan or halal with suitableForDiet on MenuItem, but publish allergen information as a text table on the page.

Should we claim our restaurant in ChatGPT?

If you are in the US and an existing listing for you appears in Business Hub, claiming it lets you correct your website, description and category. OpenAI says it does not guarantee more visibility. Outside the US, local businesses cannot claim yet, so keep your site and other listings accurate.

Will this guarantee ChatGPT recommends us?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your menu, prices and dietary facts easy for AI to read, and keep checking whether you are mentioned and described correctly.
