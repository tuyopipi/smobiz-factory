AI search readiness of UK web design agencies: 942 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 942 UK web design agencies' own websites. 21.5% (203) had an llms.txt — higher than Japan (13.4%) and not statistically different from the US (23.1%). 4.7% of sites with a robots.txt blocked at least one AI bot, and all 28 sites blocking GPTBot still allowed OpenAI's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot.

Published 2026-11-18. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic. This is the second of our country reports (the first covered the United States).

Key findings

  1. 21.5% (203) of 942 UK web agency websites had an llms.txt. That is higher than Japan (13.4%, p < 0.001) and not statistically different from the US (23.1%, p = 0.31). Excluding the 140 home pages that answered HTTP 202, the share is 25.3%.
  2. 17.7% (167) of UK sites met all six AI-search items we checked — about 1.7 times Japan's 10.4% and close to the US (18.9%). JSON-LD use (68.8%) was the same as in Japan (69.4%) and the US (69.0%).
  3. Of 699 UK sites with a robots.txt, 4.7% (33) blocked an AI bot (Japan 1.3%). All 28 sites blocking GPTBot allowed OAI-SearchBot, and no site blocked PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot or ChatGPT-User.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 1,555 agency sites listed as UK in our collection list, 942 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of UK businesses in general. For comparison we use the same survey's US (4,472), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites outside the UK (12,410).
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: 140 UK home pages answered with status 202 (none in Japan). None of them had JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they may have returned a bot-check or other interim page. We therefore also show figures excluding them.
  7. Statistics: differences between the UK and other groups were tested with chi-square tests. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown.

Results: the UK compared with the US and Japan

On almost every item the UK was within one or two points of the US. Compared with Japan, the UK was higher on llms.txt, FAQPage and "all six items", and lower on meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt. The lower UK values are partly due to the 140 HTTP 202 pages, which fail almost every item; excluding them, meta description is 81.4%, canonical 85.4% and robots.txt 86.8%. Against all overseas sites outside the UK (12,410), the UK was lower on llms.txt (21.5% vs 26.7%, p < 0.001) and on all six items (17.7% vs 22.1%, p = 0.002).

Blocking by bot

Share of the 699 UK sites with a robots.txt that block each bot from the home page. Crawlers generally described as training crawlers (Bytespider, CCBot, anthropic-ai, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, GPTBot, Applebot-Extended) were blocked by 3.9–4.7% — slightly above the US and three to four times Japan. Among bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks, only OAI-SearchBot was blocked, by one site; ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot were blocked by none. No site blocked Googlebot or Bingbot.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. All 28 sites blocking GPTBot (100%) did not block OAI-SearchBot. In the US the figure was 119 of 128 (93.0%).
  2. Of the 35 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots above, 27 (77.1%) used the same combination: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all seven together (US 58.5%).
  3. 30 sites (4.3% of those with a robots.txt) named an AI bot in robots.txt but blocked none from the home page.
  4. 12 of the 35 sites blocking an AI bot (34.3%) also had an llms.txt.

Structured data types

The @types found in home page JSON-LD followed a very similar pattern in the UK and the US. The largest gaps with Japan were SearchAction (UK 60.8%, Japan 28.9%), WebPage (60.4% vs 34.4%) and GeoCoordinates (13.6% vs 3.7%). PostalAddress, which states the business address, appeared on 26.6% of UK sites and 16.4% in Japan. Of UK sites with JSON-LD, 2.0% had at least one block that did not parse.

WordPress and SEO plugins

73.4% (691) of UK agency sites ran WordPress (86.2% excluding HTTP 202). WordPress sites had llms.txt 28.1%, JSON-LD 92.6% and a 6-item average of 4.81; non-WordPress sites (251, of which 140 answered 202) had llms.txt 3.6% and an average of 0.31. Among WordPress sites, Yoast SEO was detected on 58.0% and Rank Math on 20.7% — close to the US (Yoast 56.4%, Rank Math 18.0%) and unlike Japan, where AIOSEO leads (35.2%) ahead of Yoast (14.4%). AIOSEO (25 sites) and SEOPress (4) are below our 30-site threshold and not shown.

llms.txt adoption: Rank Math 34.3%, Yoast SEO 27.7%, no plugin detected 20.3%. We did not check whether the llms.txt was generated by a plugin or added by hand.

Distribution of the six items

Like the US, the UK split into 0 items (23.6%) and 5–6 items (55.1% combined). Most of the zeros are HTTP 202 pages; excluding them, 0 items falls to 10.5%. Japan has a thicker middle (3–4 items, 30.7% combined) where the UK has 15.2%.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the sample is not representative of all UK agencies. With 942 UK sites, counts such as per-bot blocking are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites (613) are excluded. The 140 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. UK agency sites look much like US ones; the gap with Japan is in llms.txt and in having everything in place. AI bot blocking is a minority practice, and where it happens it is limited to training crawlers — search crawlers are almost always let in. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision, but sites that want to appear in AI search may want to confirm they are not blocking search crawlers by accident. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are UK agencies ahead of Japan on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt (UK 21.5%, Japan 13.4%) and on meeting all six items (17.7% vs 10.4%), yes. JSON-LD is about the same (68.8% vs 69.4%), and Japan is higher on meta descriptions and canonical links (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

How does the UK compare with the US?

We found no statistically significant difference on llms.txt (21.5% vs 23.1%), all six items (17.7% vs 18.9%) or AI bot blocking (4.7% vs 4.3%). Yoast SEO is the most common plugin in both.

Do many UK sites block AI crawlers?

4.7% of sites with a robots.txt, more than Japan (1.3%) but a small minority. Blocking is limited to training crawlers: no site blocked PerplexityBot or Claude-SearchBot, and one blocked OAI-SearchBot.
