AI search for house cleaning and housekeeping services: getting AI to read your prices, extra charges, damage cover and key policy correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a house cleaning or housekeeping business comes down to writing, as text on your site, what people worry about before they let someone into their home: the tax-inclusive price per job or per hour and the exact conditions for extra charges, what happens if something gets damaged and whether you are insured, whether you hold keys and how, who your staff are, and your cancellation terms. Then name the towns you cover, and publish the same facts in your Google Business Profile and in structured data (HomeAndConstructionBusiness, Service and areaServed).

Written November 25, 2026 (JST). The consumer-agency, ministry and certification examples in this article are from Japan, because that is where we checked them on November 25, 2026; insurance, licensing and consumer rules differ by country, so check the ones that apply where you work. This article is not legal advice.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your terms readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention you or rank you anywhere. For plumbing, locks and other urgent call-outs, see AI search for emergency home repairs.

House cleaning and housekeeping get different questions

House cleaning here means one-off jobs on specific areas, such as an oven, a bathroom or an air conditioner, done by staff who bring their own tools and products. Housekeeping means recurring help with cleaning, laundry or cooking, billed by the hour. Many businesses offer both, but customers ask AI different questions about each, so give each its own page.

Write the price per item, and exactly when extras apply

In February 2025 Japan's National Consumer Affairs Center warned about house cleaning disputes. In one case a customer was quoted 5,000 yen by phone to clean a kitchen extractor fan, then had the bathroom, washroom and toilet added one after another, ending with a contract of about 600,000 yen; in another, a part broke during air-conditioner cleaning and the company could no longer be reached. Its advice to consumers: get quotes from several companies and compare the work and price, and check what compensation is offered for damage before signing. National Consumer Affairs Center, February 28, 2025 (Japanese)

For customers to compare quotes, and for AI to answer "how much?", your site needs the base price and, in words, what makes the price go up. Show tax-inclusive prices where consumers expect or the law requires them.

Damage cover: insurance, scope and reporting deadline

As the consumer agency's advice shows, customers want to know what happens if something breaks before they book. To let AI answer "Which cleaners are insured?", write the following. Conditions, not phrases like "fully covered", are what AI can use.

Wording is an example. Match the scope and conditions to your actual policy.
ItemExample wording
InsuranceWe carry liability insurance for accidents during work (naming the insurer is optional)
What is coveredDamage to or staining of your belongings caused by our staff during work
What is notFaults present before the job, wear and tear, cash and jewellery
Reporting deadlineTell us within 7 days of the job; photos speed things up
Pre-job checkWe test appliances before cleaning and tell you about any existing problem first

On a related note, the same agency reported in June 2026 that consultations about air-conditioner repair rose from 451 in fiscal 2021 to 1,251 in fiscal 2025, and advised checking the work and price before it starts and testing the unit together before the technician leaves. National Consumer Affairs Center, June 3, 2026 (Japanese). That notice is about repairs, but writing "we tell you the price before work" and "we check with you before we leave" as your own procedure answers the same worry for cleaning.

Keys and empty-home visits: whether you do it, and how

"Can they come while I'm out?" is a common housekeeping question. A Japanese industry guideline for home-support services (published March 30, 2025 by an association of care-related service providers and hosted on the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry's site) covers cleaning and laundry help and says keys should be held only when unavoidable, with a key receipt, a written procedure and a log of each time a key is taken out. Guideline (PDF, Japanese). It was written mainly with older customers in mind, but its items make a useful checklist for any key policy.

  1. Say clearly whether you clean an empty home (for example: "We ask you to be home for the first visit. From the second visit we can hold a key and clean while you are out.").
  2. If you hold keys, say that you issue a receipt, how keys are stored, that each use is logged, and how they are returned when the contract ends.
  3. If you use smart locks or key boxes, explain how, and how access codes are handled.
  4. Say whether staff take photos during work and how you report when the job is done (chat or email).

Staff, qualifications and certification: only what you hold, by its exact name

In Japan, house cleaning is one of the national trade skills tests run under the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare; people who pass may call themselves certified house cleaning technicians. The ministry describes the trade as cleaning occupied homes and real-estate properties, including bathrooms, kitchens and fixed equipment, and points to the designated testing body, the All Japan House Cleaning Association. Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (Japanese)

For housekeeping, Japan has a third-party certification created in 2016, run by the national housekeeping services association and reviewed by the Japanese Standards Association, that assesses safety, service quality and customer manners. Cabinet Secretariat paper (PDF, Japanese). A ministry-commissioned report says 19 companies held it at the end of January 2025. METI report (PDF, Japanese). Other countries have their own schemes; use the ones that exist where you work.

  1. List only qualifications and certifications you actually hold, by their official names. Add your own facts, such as the level, how many staff hold it, or when you were certified, so AI can use them as they are.
  2. Say whether staff are employees or contractors, what you check when hiring (background checks, if you do them and the law allows), and how long and what kind of training they get.
  3. Say whether the same cleaner comes each time and whether a customer can ask for a change. Housekeeping customers ask this often.
  4. Publish staff names and photos only with their consent.

Cancellation and rescheduling: dates and percentages

To let AI answer "Is there a fee if I cancel the day before?", write cancellation fees by date and percentage. The guideline above also asks providers to show expected cancellation fees before the contract, and notes that in Japan such fees may not exceed the provider's actual loss (Consumer Contract Act, Article 9).

Dates and percentages are examples. Write your real terms.

The Japanese consumer agency notes that a cooling-off right may apply when certain conditions are met. Which consumer rules apply to your contracts, for example ones signed in the customer's home, depends on the country; ask a professional. Use the same wording on your pricing and booking pages. Service area: named towns, matched to Google Business Profile

You go to the customer, so name the towns you cover so AI can answer "Do they come to [town]?", and say how travel is charged outside that area. If you do not receive customers at your address, Google Business Profile treats you as a service-area business: hide the address, list up to 20 areas, keep within about 2 hours' drive of your base, and do not add keywords or places to the business name. Google: Guidelines · Manage service areas. More detail is in AI search for on-location photographers.

  1. Make the area on your site, your Google Business Profile service areas and your listings on booking marketplaces the same.
  2. Keep prices, damage cover and cancellation terms the same on marketplaces and on your site. If they differ, AI may use the oldest version.
  3. Franchisees should check that the head office's location page and their own site do not disagree on area or price.

Structured data: there is no cleaning type, so use HomeAndConstructionBusiness with Service

schema.org has no dedicated type for house cleaning or housekeeping. The subtypes of HomeAndConstructionBusiness are for electricians, general contractors, painters, HVAC, locksmiths, movers, plumbers and roofers (checked in schema-dts 2.1.0 on November 25, 2026). DryCleaningOrLaundry is for laundries, not home cleaning. Publish the business as HomeAndConstructionBusiness (or LocalBusiness), describe each cleaning job or housekeeping plan as a Service with an Offer, and put your area in areaServed. If you do not publish an address, leave address out.

Treat structured data as a copy of the prices, area and cover in your page text, and keep them identical. A fact that exists only in structured data reaches neither customers nor AI well.

Pre-publish checklist

  1. House cleaning and housekeeping have separate pages, each with tax-inclusive prices.
  2. The conditions for extra charges, and that you price extras before starting, are written.
  3. What is included (travel, parking, products) is written.
  4. Liability insurance, what is and is not covered, and the reporting deadline are written.
  5. Whether you clean empty homes, and how keys are held, logged and returned, is written.
  6. Staff employment, training and any qualifications or certifications are written, by official name, only if held.
  7. Cancellation and rescheduling terms use the same wording on the pricing and booking pages.
  8. Your area is written as named towns and matches your Google Business Profile and marketplace listings.
  9. The structured data type is HomeAndConstructionBusiness or LocalBusiness, with prices and area matching the page text.
  10. llms.txt lists your pricing, area, cover, booking and FAQ pages. What is llms.txt

Check whether AI mentions you, and whether it is right

Once your pages are in order, ask AI the questions customers would ask. Check whether your name comes up for area-plus-service questions such as "oven cleaning in [town]" or "weekly housekeeper in [town]", and also ask with your name, such as "Are they insured?", "What is their cancellation fee?" or "Can they clean while I'm out?", to see whether the answers match your site. Look out for old prices or services you no longer offer.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time, and keep a record. FAQ

Where should a cleaning business start with AI search?

First, write tax-inclusive prices per item and the conditions for extra charges on your pricing page as text. Then add your damage cover, insurance and cancellation terms. After that, name the towns you cover, match your Google Business Profile, and publish the same facts in structured data and llms.txt.

Should house cleaning and housekeeping share one page?

Separate pages work better. House cleaning is priced per one-off job and housekeeping per hour on a recurring plan, and customers ask AI different questions about each. Keep one set of business details and give each service its own page.

Will higher prices make AI less likely to recommend me?

There is no official information that price level decides whether AI mentions a business. What matters is that prices and extra-charge conditions are accurate and written so AI can answer with the conditions. A gap between a cheap advertised price and the final bill is one of the problems consumer agencies warn about.

Which structured data type should a cleaning business use?

schema.org has no cleaning or housekeeping type, so use HomeAndConstructionBusiness or LocalBusiness, and describe each job or plan as a Service. DryCleaningOrLaundry is for laundries.

Do I need a certification to be recommended by AI?

There is no official information that a certification is required. List the qualifications and certifications you hold by their official names; if you have none, write facts that can be checked, such as your training and the terms of your damage cover. Never claim one you do not hold.

Will this guarantee ChatGPT recommends me?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your prices, cover, key policy and cancellation terms easy for AI to read, and keep checking whether you appear and whether the answers are correct.
