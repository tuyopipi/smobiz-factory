AI search for OB-GYN clinics: how to publish delivery booking deadlines, costs and epidural information so AI reads it correctly

The single most useful thing an obstetrics and gynecology clinic can do for AI search is to state, in dated text on its own site, by what week and how a delivery must be booked, what it costs and how that relates to public benefits, and how epidural (labor analgesia) care is organized. People who have just found out they are pregnant ask AI before they choose where to give birth. If the clinic's site does not state these conditions, AI answers from old reviews, comparison sites or another hospital's details. Then make the same facts match on public directories, insurance-scheme listings, structured data and Google Business Profile.

Written November 20, 2026 (JST). The examples come from Japan, where Nurevo is based. The childbirth lump-sum benefit (Japan Health Insurance Association), the 2026 amendment to the Health Insurance Act (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare overview), the 出産なび directory (MHLW leaflet and committee document), prenatal checkup counts (MHLW notice and survey), epidural disclosure items (MHLW self-inspection checklist, May 2025 edition), the obstetric compensation scheme (Government of Japan public relations site) and the schema.org types (V30.1) were checked that day. The MHLW medical advertising Q&A items are as checked on November 16, 2026. Outside Japan, benefits, insurance and advertising rules differ: check your own country's rules.

AI answers change by day and by how a question is asked. This guide makes your clinic's facts readable to AI. It does not guarantee that you will appear in AI answers or rank higher. It is not legal or medical advice.

What pregnant people and their families ask AI

Obstetric questions are less about symptoms and more about timing, cost and how care is organized. People planning to give birth near their parents' home, partners and parents also do the research. In gynecology, checkups, contraception and menopause questions are common. Six question types come up most.

Delivery booking: give the deadline in weeks, and the method

Booking deadlines vary widely. For example, Aiiku Hospital's FAQ says delivery booking is "usually at 8–9 weeks", and the Japanese Red Cross Medical Center asks for booking "by week 20". Aiiku Hospital (PDF, Japanese), Japanese Red Cross Medical Center (PDF, Japanese). AI is asked "by what week do I need to book?" directly, so give a week number, not "please book early".

What to writeExampleAvoid
The deadline"Please book your delivery by week 20 of pregnancy"Only "please book early"
How to book"Book at your first outpatient visit. We do not take delivery bookings by phone or online"No method given
DepositWhether there is one and how it is treated, such as "deposit of X, deducted from the delivery fee"An amount with no word on refunds or deduction
Availability"Bookings for due dates in May 2027 are closed (as of November 20, 2026)"Only "we are very busy", with no date
Giving birth away from homeWhether accepted, when to contact you, by what week to be seenOnly "please ask us"

Availability changes month by month. Put it in a dated notice and rewrite it when it changes. If an ended notice stays online, AI may still answer "bookings closed" months later.

Costs: the lump sum, and the system that is due to change

In Japan, for births on or after April 1, 2023, the childbirth lump-sum benefit is JPY 500,000 per child when the birth is at 22 weeks or later at a facility enrolled in the obstetric compensation scheme (JPY 488,000 under 22 weeks). With the direct payment system, the facility receives it straight from the health insurer, and the patient pays only the amount above it. Japan Health Insurance Association (Japanese).

The system is due to change. The amendment to the Health Insurance Act promulgated on June 5, 2026 sets a national base unit price for delivery, paid by health insurance directly to the facility (benefit in kind), together with a flat cash benefit for every pregnant woman. It takes effect on a date set by cabinet order within two years of promulgation, and for the time being each facility may stay on the current system. MHLW overview (PDF, Japanese), Committee document, October 1, 2026 (PDF, Japanese). As of the date of this guide, the start date and amounts have not been set.

So write the cost page with conditions and dates, as below. Do not write things that are not decided yet, such as "giving birth is now free".

  1. A cost guide and its conditions (vaginal or caesarean, private or shared room, weekday daytime or night and holiday, extra cost for an epidural).
  2. Whether you accept the direct payment system.
  3. Whether you are enrolled in the obstetric compensation scheme (it affects the lump-sum amount).
  4. A date such as "amounts as of month/year".
  5. If you mention the new system, say "the start date is not yet set; we will update this page when it is", and update it when it is.

Epidurals: publish the same items as the self-inspection checklist

The MHLW epidural self-inspection checklist (May 2025 edition) lists the items facilities offering epidural labor analgesia should publish on their own website and on the site of the Japanese council of labor analgesia societies (JALA), updated at least once a year. Self-inspection checklist (PDF, Japanese). These items are also the concrete facts AI can use when asked "is the epidural at this clinic safe?".

  1. Clinical record (such as the number of cases).
  2. The standard explanatory document and the standard method (planned or available 24 hours).
  3. The arrangements for emergencies, and the record of crisis-response simulations.
  4. Training and course records of the anesthesia manager and doctors, and the expiry dates of their resuscitation courses.
  5. Participation in the Japan Association of Obstetricians and Gynecologists' adverse event and maternal death reporting programme.
  6. The date and time the website was last updated.

Write these facts rather than words that promise an effect or safety, such as "painless" or "worry-free epidural". Keep your own site and the JALA site saying the same thing.

Match your site to the maternity directory and the compensation scheme

出産なび is a site the MHLW launched on May 30, 2024 that shows the services and costs of delivery facilities nationwide. It lists facilities that use direct payment and handle 21 or more deliveries a year (smaller facilities can opt in), and listed 2,097 facilities as of February 20, 2026. Cost information is updated quarterly from billing data. 出産なび leaflet (PDF, Japanese), Committee document (PDF, Japanese). AI reads both public sites and your site, so a mismatch can make one of them look wrong.

Prenatal checkups and gynecology: who can get what, and when

Following the intervals in an MHLW notice, prenatal checkups come to about 14 visits, and as of April 1, 2022 every municipality subsidized 14 or more (most through vouchers). MHLW notice (Japanese), MHLW survey (PDF, Japanese). Subsidy amounts and rules vary by municipality, so state which municipalities' vouchers you accept, and do not quote amounts.

  1. Whether you accept checkup-only patients, and which hospital you work with for delivery ("from week X at our partner hospital" and so on).
  2. Which municipalities' vouchers you accept (and how you handle vouchers from another municipality for women coming home to give birth).
  3. For gynecology, hours by day, the types of screening such as cervical cancer screening, and whether booking is needed.
  4. If you list a female doctor's days, give the day and name, and update them the same day they change.
  5. For self-pay care (such as contraceptive pill prescriptions), give the fee, main risks and side effects, and a contact point on the same page (medical advertising rules).

Where common AI advice collides with medical advertising rules

AI search guides often say "get more reviews" and "show results". On a medical website in Japan, follow the MHLW medical advertising guidelines and their Q&A. Birth stories are tempting content, so take particular care.

Common adviceHow it applies to clinics (MHLW Q&A)What to do instead
Publish stories from mothersTestimonials about treatment content or effect cannot be published (Q1-18, Q2-9)Explain the facts in detail: the stay, number of days, what to bring, cost conditions

"The most chosen maternity clinic in the area"False, exaggerated or comparative claims are treated as prohibited examplesState only what you can as fact, such as the number of deliveries
Describe self-pay care in detailFor self-pay care, the content and cost, main risks and side effects, and a contact point that answers questions must be given (Q6-10 to 6-12)Put the fee and main risks on the same page, meeting those conditions

Sources: MHLW medical advertising guidelines Q&A (PDF, Japanese), MHLW case book (PDF, Japanese). Keep FAQs to factual questions such as "By what week should I book my delivery?" and "Do you accept direct payment?".

Google says no special structured data is needed for AI Overviews and similar features, and that structured data should match the visible content of the page. Google: AI features and your website. Delivery costs depend on conditions, so do not enter them as a single fixed price in structured data.

Adding medicalSpecialty

Nurevo AIO does not output medicalSpecialty on its own. To add it, use the filter that changes the business structured data (webmcp_canary_business_node) in your theme's functions.php or a small plugin.

add_filter('webmcp_canary_business_node', function ($org, $settings) {
    // Add only specialties your pages describe (drop Obstetric if you do not deliver babies)
    $org['medicalSpecialty'] = array('Obstetric', 'Gynecologic');
    return $org;

Pre-publish checklist

  1. The delivery booking deadline is given in weeks, with the booking method and how any deposit is treated.
  2. Availability (such as months that are closed) is dated and rewritten when it changes.
  3. The cost page gives conditions, a date, whether you accept direct payment and whether you are enrolled in the compensation scheme.
  4. Nothing about the new system is written as decided when it is not.
  5. If you offer epidurals, the checklist disclosure items are on your site and match the JALA site.
  6. Your 出産なび listing and your site agree on services and costs.
  7. Checkup-only acceptance, accepted vouchers, and gynecology days and hours are stated.
  8. No testimonials, promised results, superlatives or comparisons in the page text, FAQs or structured data.
  9. The structured data type is Obstetric or Gynecologic and prices match the page.
  10. Name, address, phone and hours match across structured data, Google Business Profile and the official medical directory, and llms.txt lists the delivery, cost, epidural and access pages. Clinic basics, What is llms.txt

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the pages are in order, write five to ten questions pregnant people and their families might ask, combining area × giving birth × booking deadline, area × epidural, area × giving birth away from home and area × gynecology × Saturday × screening. FAQ

Where should an OB-GYN clinic start with AI search?

State the delivery booking deadline in weeks, together with how to book. Then add conditions and a date to your cost page, and publish the disclosure items if you offer epidurals. Finally, make your name, address, phone and hours match across structured data, Google Business Profile and the national maternity directory.

How should we write delivery costs on our site?

Give a cost guide with conditions (delivery method, room, time of day) and an "as of month/year" date. Also say whether you accept direct payment and whether you are enrolled in the obstetric compensation scheme. For the new system in the 2026 law, say that the start date is not yet set, and update the page when it is.

Can we say "giving birth is now free"?

Avoid writing undecided things as decided. The amendment promulgated on June 5, 2026 has health insurance pay a base delivery price to the facility, but it takes effect on a date set by cabinet order within two years, and for the time being facilities may choose to stay on the current system.

What should we publish about epidurals?

The disclosure items in the MHLW self-inspection checklist: clinical record, the standard explanatory document and method, emergency arrangements, the anesthesia doctors' training and so on. Keep them the same as on the JALA site and update them at least once a year. Do not use words that promise an effect, such as "painless".

Which structured data type should we use?

schema.org's Obstetric if you deliver babies, or Gynecologic for a gynecology clinic that does not. Both are a MedicalBusiness and a LocalBusiness. If you do both, add the other as a medicalSpecialty.
