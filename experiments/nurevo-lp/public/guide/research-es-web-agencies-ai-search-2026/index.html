AI search readiness of Spanish web design agencies: 248 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 248 Spanish web design agencies' own websites. 31.9% (79 sites) had an llms.txt — fourth among the 17 countries with 100+ sites in our survey, after Singapore, India and Brazil (US 23.1%, UK 21.5%, Japan 13.4%). 27.4% met all six AI-search items we checked, third of 17, but 25.8% met none, so the six-item average (3.67) ranked only tenth. The split follows the CMS: none of the 167 WordPress sites met zero items and 80.2% met five or more, while 79.0% of the 81 non-WordPress sites met none.

Published 27 November 2026. Data aggregated 12 November 2026. Research and writing: Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社, Japan). Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, so we have an interest in this topic. This is the ninth of our country reports (after the US, the UK, Australia, India, Canada, France, Singapore and Brazil). A Spanish version is also available.

Key findings

  1. Of 248 Spanish web agency websites, 31.9% (79) had an llms.txt — fourth of the 17 countries with 100+ reached sites. That is higher than the US (23.1%, p=0.002), the UK (21.5%, p<0.001) and Italy (20.0%, p=0.022), with no statistically significant difference from France (26.4%, p=0.14) or Brazil (34.1%), and 2.4 times Japan (13.4%).
  2. 27.4% (68) met all six AI-search items, third of 17 after Singapore and India. At the same time 25.8% (64) met none, so the six-item average was 3.67 (tenth) and JSON-LD 69.0% (twelfth).
  3. The dividing line was WordPress. The 167 WordPress sites had llms.txt 42.5%, JSON-LD 97.0% and a six-item average of 5.11, and none scored zero. The 81 non-WordPress sites had JSON-LD 11.1% and an average of 0.72, and 64 of them (79.0%) met no items. Among WordPress sites only, Spain's llms.txt share (42.5%) was higher than in the US (30.1%), France (27.5%) and all other overseas agencies (33.0%) (p<0.001, p<0.001, p=0.009).

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of the 485 agency sites listed as Spain in our collection list, 248 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of all business websites. For comparison we use the same survey's four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries (Mexico 116, Colombia 81, Argentina 76, Chile 50; 323 in total), France (405), Italy (110), Portugal (46), the UK (942), the US (4,472), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites except Spain (13,104).
  2. What we fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), between early November and 12 November 2026, aggregated on 12 November.
  3. Definitions: an llms.txt counts if it returned status 200 with non-HTML text over 20 characters. JSON-LD means at least one JSON-LD block on the home page; "broken" means at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type means an @type containing Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress means wp-content, wp-includes or a WordPress generator tag in the HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: for each bot, we apply the matching robots.txt group's rules to the home page (/) using longest match. The base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" means blocking any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot. "Names an AI bot" means naming any of the 11 AI bots (all listed bots except Googlebot and Bingbot) in a User-agent line.
  5. "Six items" counts how many of these a site meets (0–6): (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. It is a simple yardstick for this survey and does not show whether a site appears in AI answers.
  6. HTTP 202: 14 Spanish home pages (5.6% of reached) answered with status 202, all of them non-WordPress — fewer than in the US (15.0%) or the UK (14.9%). A 202 page scores "no" on almost every item, so we also report figures for status-200 sites only.
  7. Statistics: differences between Spain and other groups were tested with the chi-square test, and AI bot blocking (small counts) with Fisher's exact test. Groups under 30 sites are not shown (AI bot blocking for Portugal, with 27 sites with a robots.txt, is shown as "—"). 198 of the 248 sites have no city in our list, so we did not split by region.

Results: Spain compared with Latin America, France, Italy, Portugal, the UK, the US and Japan

Spain was higher than the US and the UK on llms.txt (31.9%), FAQPage (12.5%) and all six items (27.4%) (llms.txt: vs US p=0.002, vs UK p<0.001; FAQPage: p<0.001, p=0.026; all six: p=0.001, p<0.001). JSON-LD (69.0%) was at the same level as the US (69.0%) and the UK (68.8%). Against all other overseas agencies, Spain was higher on llms.txt (p=0.048) and all six items (p=0.031) and slightly lower on meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt (p=0.044, 0.029, 0.010).

Neighbouring France looks different. France was around 20 points higher on JSON-LD (90.1%), meta descriptions (90.4%), canonical links (93.6%) and robots.txt (93.3%) (all p<0.001), and only 3.7% of French sites met no items. Yet there was no statistically significant difference on llms.txt (26.4%, p=0.14) or all six items (24.0%, p=0.32). Against Italy, Spain was higher on llms.txt (p=0.022), all six items (p=0.039) and JSON-LD (p<0.001).

Compared with the four Spanish-speaking Latin American countries, Spain was higher on JSON-LD (69.0% vs 54.8%, p<0.001) and all six items (27.4% vs 18.3%, p=0.009); llms.txt was 31.9% vs 24.5% (p=0.050). Among status-200 sites only (Spain 234), llms.txt was 33.8%, higher than the US (27.2%, p=0.028) and the UK (25.3%, p=0.011), with no clear difference from other overseas agencies (29.6%, p=0.16). All six items was 29.1%, higher than the US (22.3%, p=0.016).

Two groups: WordPress and the rest

67.3% (167) of Spanish agency sites run WordPress. WordPress sites had JSON-LD 97.0% and a six-item average of 5.11; none scored zero, 96.4% met four or more items and 80.2% met five or more. The 81 non-WordPress sites had JSON-LD 11.1% and an average of 0.72, and 64 (79.0%) met no items. Those 64 are the 14 that answered 202 and 50 that answered 200 but had no structured data, meta description, canonical link, robots.txt or llms.txt that we could find. Sites that render content with JavaScript, or whose host serves no robots.txt, score "no" in this survey.

Among WordPress sites only, Spain had llms.txt 42.5% and all six items 37.7%, higher than the US (30.1%, 25.2%), France (27.5%, 25.9%) and other overseas agencies (33.0%, 27.9%) (llms.txt p<0.001, p<0.001, p=0.009). There was no significant difference from Spanish-speaking Latin America (38.3%, p=0.44) or Italy (36.8%, p=0.45). Non-WordPress sites scoring low is a pattern we also see in the US, Italy and Spanish-speaking Latin America.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. 7 of the 9 sites blocking GPTBot did not block OAI-SearchBot. The other 2 blocked all 11 AI bots except Googlebot and Bingbot.
  2. Of the 11 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots, 5 used the same combination of seven: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended. 2 blocked all 11, and the other 4 each used a different combination (for example Bytespider only, or GPTBot and CCBot).
  3. No site blocked Googlebot or Bingbot.
  4. 25 sites (14.0% of sites with a robots.txt) named an AI bot; 16 of them blocked none of the AI bots from the home page.
  5. 6 of the 10 sites blocking an AI bot also had an llms.txt.
  6. 87.6% of robots.txt files included a Sitemap line.

llms.txt contents and what five-item sites were missing

  1. The median llms.txt among the 79 sites that had one was 60 lines. 9 sites (3.6%) had an llms-full.txt.
  2. 73 sites met five of the six items; for 66 of them (90.4%) the missing item was the llms.txt.

These are the types WordPress SEO plugins typically output. Location types GeoCoordinates (17.3%) and Place (15.3%) were the highest of the six groups in the table.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not all business websites. Country comes from our collection list, which is not a representative sample of Spanish agencies. 248 reached sites is small among the 17 countries, and we did not analyse by region. Some counts (e.g. by bot) are small.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt is judged for the home page (/); partial blocks and firewall or CDN blocking are not counted. We checked whether an llms.txt exists, not its quality.
  3. Each site was fetched once; unreachable sites (237) are excluded. The 14 sites answering 202 may have served something other than their real home page. Content or structured data added by JavaScript is not counted, so non-WordPress figures may be understated.
  4. SEO plugins are detected from HTML strings; sites that hide traces or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. These figures are from the 12 November 2026 aggregation. We re-run the survey weekly, so later figures may differ.

How to read this (opinion)

This section is our interpretation. Spanish agency sites rank near the top of 17 countries on llms.txt and on meeting all six items, and Spanish WordPress sites have a higher llms.txt share than those in the US or France. Many non-WordPress sites, however, showed none of the items with our method, which pulls the average down. This survey cannot tell whether those items are truly missing or simply invisible to our method (for example, content rendered with JavaScript). AI bot blocking was rare and mostly limited to training crawlers. Which bots to block is each site's own decision. Having these items in place does not guarantee appearing in AI answers.

FAQ

Are Spanish agencies ahead on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt, yes: 31.9%, fourth of 17 countries. 27.4% met all six items, third of 17. But 25.8% met none, so the average (3.67) ranked tenth (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

Why are Spain's ranks a mix of high and middling?

Because WordPress sites (67.3%) and the rest are far apart: a six-item average of 5.11 versus 0.72, with 79.0% of non-WordPress sites meeting no items. Sites that render content with JavaScript score "no" in this survey, so they may be understated.

Do many Spanish sites block AI crawlers?

Few: 5.6% of sites with a robots.txt (10 sites), not significantly different from the US (4.3%) or the UK (4.7%), and higher than France (1.9%). Two sites blocked search crawlers.
