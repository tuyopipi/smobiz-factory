Japan vs overseas: AI search readiness of 15,838 web design agency websites (November 2026)

We checked the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 2,486 Japanese web design agencies' own websites and 13,352 agency websites in 25 other countries and regions, using the same method. 13.4% of Japanese sites had an llms.txt, against 29.6% overseas (excluding sites whose home page returned HTTP 202) — the lowest of the 25 countries and regions compared. Japan was ahead on meta descriptions (90.7% vs 80.3%) and robots.txt (91.8% vs 87.6%), and for 94.9% of the 1,039 Japanese sites meeting five of our six items, the only missing item was llms.txt.

Published 26 November 2026. Data aggregated 12 November 2026. Research and writing: Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社, Japan). Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, so we have an interest in this topic.

Key findings

  1. 13.4% of Japanese web agency sites (333 of 2,486) had an llms.txt, against 26.4% overseas (3,522 of 13,352), or 29.6% excluding 1,477 HTTP 202 responses (p<0.001). Among 25 countries and regions with 30+ sites, Japan ranked last; Singapore ranked first (41.9%).
  2. 10.4% of Japanese sites met all six AI-search readiness items, against 24.5% overseas (excl. 202). But 986 of the 1,039 Japanese sites meeting five items (94.9%) were missing only llms.txt. The 6-item average was 4.05 in Japan and 4.30 overseas (excl. 202).
  3. Japan was higher on meta descriptions (90.7% vs 80.3%) and robots.txt (91.8% vs 87.6%); og:title (81.3% vs 81.4%) and canonical links (84.6% vs 85.9%) showed no significant difference. 1.3% of Japanese and 4.9% of overseas sites blocked an AI bot in robots.txt; blocking of search crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot) was 0.3% or less in both.

Methodology

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. From our collection list of 3,352 Japanese and 20,335 overseas agency sites, 2,486 Japanese and 13,352 overseas home pages were reachable (15,838 in total). This is not a sample of businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list.
  2. What we fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), from early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated on 12 November.
  3. Definitions: llms.txt = status 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type containing Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = wp-content/, wp-includes/ or a WordPress generator tag in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt group that applies to each bot disallows the home page (/), by longest match. The base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) llms.txt, (2) JSON-LD, (3) an Organization or LocalBusiness type, (4) a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot, (5) a meta description, (6) a canonical link. It is a simple count for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: 1,477 overseas home pages (US 671, Australia 250, UK 140 and others) returned status 202; Japan had none. None of them showed JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they may have served a bot-check or interim page. We show overseas figures both in full and excluding 202 (11,875 sites); the Japan comparison and the country table use the figures excluding 202.
  7. English-speaking 6 = US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and Ireland (7,461 sites excl. 202); other countries = the remaining 19 countries and regions (4,414). p values are 2×2 chi-square tests of Japan vs overseas (excl. 202).

Results: Japan vs overseas

Japan was lower on llms.txt, JSON-LD, Organization-type, LocalBusiness, FAQPage and microdata, and higher on WordPress share, meta descriptions, robots.txt and Sitemap lines in robots.txt. og:title, canonical links and the share of broken JSON-LD (1.7% vs 1.8%) showed no significant difference. Outside the English-speaking six, llms.txt (34.0% vs 27.0%), FAQPage (13.5% vs 8.7%) and all six items (28.0% vs 22.5%) were higher.

Blocking by bot

Share of sites with a robots.txt that block the home page, per bot. In both Japan and overseas, blocking mostly targeted crawlers described as training crawlers (CCBot, Bytespider, GPTBot, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot and others); crawlers used for answering user requests or search (ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot) were blocked by 0.3% or less.

Of sites blocking GPTBot, 28 of 28 in Japan and 429 of 448 overseas (95.8%, excl. 202) did not block OAI-SearchBot.

SEO plugins

On WordPress sites, the most common SEO plugin detected in Japan was AIOSEO (35.2%), and 48.6% had no plugin we could detect. Overseas, Yoast SEO (53.1%) and Rank Math (22.2%) led, and 17.8% had none detected. Even with the same plugin, llms.txt was less common in Japan — for AIOSEO, 19.3% vs 51.1%. We did not check whether llms.txt was made by a plugin or placed by hand.

Distribution of the six items

Japan clustered at 3–5 items (72.5% combined), with 1.6% at zero and 10.4% at six. Overseas (excl. 202), 65.4% met five or six items, but 10.0% met none. Of the 1,039 Japanese sites meeting five items, the missing item was llms.txt for 986 (94.9%), Organization-type for 38, and fewer than 10 each for the rest.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the samples do not represent all agencies in each country. Sample sizes vary widely; some countries have fewer than 100 sites.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites are excluded. The 1,477 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page, and bot-check pages may exist among other responses too.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. Japanese agency sites are ahead on classic SEO basics such as meta descriptions and robots.txt, and behind on llms.txt and some structured data (LocalBusiness, FAQPage). Since most Japanese sites meeting five items lack only llms.txt, we read the gap less as missing basics and more as newer items not yet adopted. Having llms.txt or structured data does not guarantee appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance), but publishing company facts in machine-readable form is a low-effort step worth considering.

FAQ

Is Japan behind other countries on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt (Japan 13.4%, overseas 29.6%), all six items (10.4% vs 24.5%) and JSON-LD (69.4% vs 81.0%), yes. Japan is ahead on meta descriptions (90.7% vs 80.3%) and robots.txt (91.8% vs 87.6%) (overseas excl. HTTP 202, November 2026, agencies' own websites).

Where did Japan rank?

Among 25 countries and regions with 30+ sites: last on llms.txt, 24th on all six items, 17th on JSON-LD and 14th on the 6-item average.

Do many overseas sites block AI crawlers?

4.9% of sites with a robots.txt, more than Japan (1.3%) but a small minority. Most blocking targets training crawlers; only 0.2% block OAI-SearchBot.

What is the HTTP 202 issue?

1,477 overseas home pages answered with status 202 and showed no JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they may have returned an interim page. We show figures with and without them.
