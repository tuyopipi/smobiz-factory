AI search readiness of Australian web design agencies: 1,719 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 1,719 Australian web design agencies' own websites. 24.3% (418) had an llms.txt — higher than Japan (13.4%) and not statistically different from the US (23.1%), the UK (21.5%) or New Zealand (25.4%). 20.8% met all six AI-search items we checked, exactly twice Japan's share. 4.7% of sites with a robots.txt blocked an AI bot, and 55 of the 56 sites blocking GPTBot still allowed OpenAI's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot.

Published 2026-11-19. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic. This is the third of our country reports (after the United States and the United Kingdom).

Key findings

  1. 24.3% (418) of 1,719 Australian web agency websites had an llms.txt. That is higher than Japan (13.4%, p < 0.001) and not statistically different from the US (23.1%, p = 0.32), the UK (21.5%, p = 0.12) or New Zealand (25.4%, p = 0.68). Excluding the 250 home pages that answered HTTP 202, the share is 28.4%.
  2. 20.8% (357) of Australian sites met all six AI-search items we checked — 2.0 times Japan's 10.4%. FAQPage structured data appeared on 10.3% of sites, above the US (6.2%) and Japan (3.9%) (both p < 0.001).
  3. Of 1,336 Australian sites with a robots.txt, 4.7% (63) blocked an AI bot (Japan 1.3%). 55 of the 56 sites blocking GPTBot allowed OAI-SearchBot. Only 5 sites (0.4%) blocked any of the four bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 2,289 agency sites listed as Australian in our collection list, 1,719 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of Australian businesses in general. For comparison we use the same survey's US (4,472), UK (942), New Zealand (417), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites outside Australia (11,633).
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: 250 Australian home pages answered with status 202 (none in Japan). None of them had JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they may have returned a bot-check or other interim page. We therefore also show figures excluding them.
  7. Statistics: differences between Australia and other groups were tested with chi-square tests. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown.

Results: Australia compared with the US, the UK and Japan

On most items Australia was roughly one to three points above the US and the UK; the clearest gap was FAQPage (10.3% vs 6.2% in the US). On llms.txt, all six items and AI bot blocking we found no statistically significant difference from the US, the UK or New Zealand. Compared with Japan, Australia was higher on llms.txt, FAQPage and all six items, and lower on meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt. Those lower values are partly due to the 250 HTTP 202 pages, which fail almost every item; excluding them, meta description is 82.2%, canonical 88.8% and robots.txt 90.2%. Against all overseas sites outside Australia (11,633), Australia was slightly lower on llms.txt (24.3% vs 26.7%, p = 0.04).

Blocking by bot

Share of the 1,336 Australian sites with a robots.txt that block each bot from the home page. Crawlers generally described as training crawlers (CCBot, GPTBot, Bytespider, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, anthropic-ai) were blocked by 3.4–4.3% — about the same as the US and the UK, and three to four times Japan. Among bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks, ChatGPT-User was blocked by 4 sites, PerplexityBot by 2, and OAI-SearchBot and Claude-SearchBot by 1 each. One site blocked Googlebot; none blocked Bingbot.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. 55 of the 56 sites blocking GPTBot (98.2%) did not block OAI-SearchBot. The figure was 119 of 128 (93.0%) in the US and 28 of 28 in the UK.
  2. Of the 65 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots above, 42 (64.6%) used the same combination: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all seven together (US 58.5%, UK 77.1%). The next most common was CCBot and Bytespider only (6 sites).
  3. 5 sites (0.4% of those with a robots.txt) blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
  4. 79 sites (5.9% of those with a robots.txt) named an AI bot in robots.txt but blocked none from the home page.
  5. 14 of the 65 sites blocking an AI bot (21.5%) also had an llms.txt.

Structured data types

The @types found in home page JSON-LD followed a very similar pattern in Australia, the US and the UK. Australia had the highest share of the three for types that state an address, contact details or common questions: PostalAddress (28.7%), ContactPoint (14.7%), LocalBusiness (14.2%) and FAQPage (10.3%). The largest gaps with Japan were WebPage (61.9% vs 34.4%), SearchAction (59.7% vs 28.9%) and GeoCoordinates (14.4% vs 3.7%). Of Australian sites with JSON-LD, 2.1% had at least one block that did not parse.

WordPress and SEO plugins

76.0% (1,306) of Australian agency sites ran WordPress (88.9% excluding HTTP 202). WordPress sites had llms.txt 30.7%, JSON-LD 92.3% and a 6-item average of 4.84; non-WordPress sites (413, of which 250 answered 202) had llms.txt 4.1% and an average of 0.37. Among WordPress sites, Yoast SEO was detected on 53.5%, Rank Math on 22.8% and AIOSEO on 5.6% — close to the US and the UK, and unlike Japan, where AIOSEO leads (35.2%) ahead of Yoast (14.4%). SEOPress (12 sites) is below our 30-site threshold and not shown.

llms.txt adoption: AIOSEO 49.3%, Rank Math 37.6%, Yoast SEO 28.3%, no plugin detected 22.8%. We did not check whether the llms.txt was generated by a plugin or added by hand.

Distribution of the six items

Like the US and the UK, Australia split into 0 items (20.7%) and 5–6 items (56.7% combined). Most of the zeros are HTTP 202 pages; excluding them, 0 items falls to 7.6%. Japan has a thicker middle (3–4 items, 30.7% combined) where Australia has 16.4%.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the sample is not representative of all Australian agencies. Counts such as per-bot blocking are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites (570) are excluded. The 250 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. Australian agency sites look much like US and UK ones, with slightly more FAQPage, address and contact markup; the gap with Japan is in llms.txt and in having everything in place. AI bot blocking is a minority practice, and where it happens it is almost always limited to training crawlers. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision, but sites that want to appear in AI search may want to confirm they are not blocking search crawlers by accident. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are Australian agencies ahead of Japan on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt (Australia 24.3%, Japan 13.4%) and on meeting all six items (20.8% vs 10.4%), yes. JSON-LD is about the same (71.3% vs 69.4%), and Japan is higher on meta descriptions and canonical links (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

How does Australia compare with the US and the UK?

We found no statistically significant difference on llms.txt, all six items or AI bot blocking. FAQPage markup was more common in Australia (10.3%) than in the US (6.2%). Yoast SEO is the most common plugin in all three countries.

Do many Australian sites block AI crawlers?

4.7% of sites with a robots.txt, more than Japan (1.3%) but a small minority. Most blocking targets training crawlers: only 5 sites (0.4%) blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
