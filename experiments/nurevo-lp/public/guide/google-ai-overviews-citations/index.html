How your site gets cited in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and how to check

According to Google, there are no special optimizations or dedicated files for appearing as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. The conditions are that the page is indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet, and that your site is not excluded in Search Console's Search generative AI setting. Google has not published how it chooses which pages to show, so what you can do is get the normal Search basics right, state the facts that answer questions in your page text, and check with Search Console and a manual log.

Written 18 November 2026 (JST). For reading Search Console's generative AI performance report see How to see AI Overviews and AI Mode in Search Console; for Perplexity see How Perplexity cites websites. This guide sets out the conditions for appearing as a source in Google's AI features, as far as Google's own documentation confirms them.

AI answers change with the day and the wording, and many searches show no AI Overview at all. These steps make your pages usable as sources and help you check correctly; they do not guarantee an appearance or a position.

What Google has published, and what it hasn't

Google explains how AI features relate to websites on two pages: AI features and your website (updated 10 December 2025) and Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search (updated July 2026). This guide treats only what those pages say as fact.

Query fan-out: one question becomes several searches

Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may run several related searches at once from a user's question and build the answer from more pages. A question such as "I need a dentist in my town who sees children on Saturdays" might be split into searches like "pediatric dentist [town]", "Saturday dentist [town]" and "what to bring to a child's first dental visit".

For a site, this means one page doesn't have to answer the whole question. A page that clearly answers one of the smaller questions can still be a candidate. Google doesn't publish how questions are split, so the practical step is to list the questions customers really ask and check that each has a page or heading that answers it.

  1. Give hours, prices, service area and who it is for (ages, industries) their own headings and text.
  2. Answer common questions such as "Can you ...?" or "How much does ... cost?" in one or two sentences.
  3. Split services and locations into their own pages, and keep facts consistent between them.

Check the basics in Search Console

Google's conditions are the same as for normal Search. Start by checking that the pages you want cited can appear in normal Search at all.

Google says it can process content rendered with JavaScript as long as it isn't blocked, and that perfectly semantic HTML is not required. It also says recrawling can take anywhere from several days to several months. Note the date of each fix and don't judge the result straight away.

Controls that limit display, and their effect on AI features

Google says that to limit what is shown from your pages in Search you can use nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet or noindex (robots meta tag). Its 2025 blog post adds that more restrictive settings limit how content is featured in AI experiences (Search Central blog). Check that none of these are on pages you want cited by mistake.

<!-- No snippet for the whole page (it also becomes less usable as a source in AI Overviews and AI Mode) -->
<meta name="robots" content="nosnippet">

<!-- Limit snippets to 160 characters -->
<meta name="robots" content="max-snippet:160">

<!-- Keep just this part out of snippets (for example, member prices) -->
<div data-nosnippet>Member price: ...</div>

Use data-nosnippet for member-only prices or passages you don't want quoted, and noindex or the Search generative AI setting to keep a whole page or section out. If you only want to keep your content out of AI training, look at Google-Extended rather than a Search setting.

What you don't need to do, according to Google

Google's optimization guide has a section on what you don't need to do. It lists several tasks often recommended "for AI" as unnecessary for Google Search.

  1. Creating llms.txt or other AI files, new markup or Markdown versions of pages (Google Search doesn't use them; they neither help nor harm).
  2. Breaking content into small chunks so AI can understand it (Google says there is no requirement).
  3. Adding special schema.org structured data just for AI features (not needed; using it for rich results is a separate matter).
  4. Treating AEO or GEO as a separate discipline (Google says optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, so it is still SEO).

This applies to Google Search only. An llms.txt file can still be a useful entry point for other AIs such as ChatGPT and Perplexity (llms.txt guide). Google also says that producing many pages mainly to manipulate rankings or AI answers violates its scaled content abuse spam policy.

What you can do to be a usable source

What Google lists are the same basics as for normal Search. Put in terms of being a source, they look like this.

  1. Write facts and experience only you have, not a rewrite of other sites (price breakdowns, real procedures, the conditions of cases you handled).
  2. Put important content in text, not only in images or PDFs.
  3. Link pages internally (from the service list to each service, from articles to related services).
  4. Keep structured data consistent with the visible text. Don't put facts only in structured data.
  5. Add useful images and video alongside the text that explains them.
  6. Make pages easy to read on phones and not too slow.

For reference, Ahrefs's 2025 study found that 76.10% of AI Overview citations were pages ranking in the top 10 for that search (Ahrefs, in Japanese). It is one company's study and not recent, so read it as a tendency rather than a fixed number: pages that do poorly in classic Search are unlikely sources for AI features too.

Local businesses and online shops: Business Profile and Merchant Center

Google says AI responses can include product information and information about local businesses where appropriate, and that keeping Merchant Center (product data) and Google Business Profile up to date helps products and services be visible in both AI responses and other search results. Shops and local businesses should check these two as well as the site.

TypeWhat to check
Shops and local servicesBusiness Profile hours (including holidays and closures), categories, services, address and phone match your about page and structured data
Online shopsPrices, stock and shipping in the Merchant Center feed match the product pages, with no expired sale prices left
BothWhen you change the site, update the Business Profile or feed the same day. If they disagree, neither AI nor people can tell which is right

Steps in WordPress

  1. In Settings > Reading, check that "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is not ticked (it outputs noindex).
  2. In your SEO plugin (Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO, SEO SIMPLE PACK and so on), check that the pages or post types you want cited are not set to noindex, and that no snippet limits (nosnippet, max-snippet) are set.
  3. Open https://your-domain/robots.txt and check that Googlebot or User-agent: * does not disallow the whole site.
  4. Check that a security plugin, hosting WAF or CDN does not block Googlebot (use Search Console's live URL test to confirm it can fetch the page).
  5. Nurevo AEO (free) publishes the business, company and service facts you enter as structured data and in llms.txt. Structured data must match the visible text, so put the same prices and hours in your page text too. AI training crawlers such as Google-Extended are left to your robots.txt by default.
  6. In Search Console, inspect the pages you want cited and request indexing after fixes. Check Settings > Search generative AI as well.

How to check whether you appear as a source

Google's AI features appear on some searches and not others. Don't judge from one search; combine these methods.

MethodWhat it showsWhat it misses, and cautions
Search Console generative AI performance reportHow often pages were shown in AI features (available to all sites as of 31 August 2026, Help)Not which queries, what position or how many clicks came from AI answers
Search Console Performance report (Web)Totals that include impressions and clicks from AI featuresThe AI feature share can't be separated
Search by hand and recordFor fixed questions, whether an AI Overview appeared and whether your URL was among the supporting linksVaries with login state, location and device. Record under the same conditions on several days

For a manual log, pick five to ten unbranded questions ("pediatric dentist [town] Saturday"), and record the date, device, login state, whether an AI Overview appeared, whether your URL was linked, and which sites appeared instead. Use branded questions only to check that your facts are read correctly. For reading the report in detail, see the Search Console guide.

Checklist

  1. URL Inspection shows the pages you want cited as "URL is on Google"
  2. No unintended noindex, nosnippet or very short max-snippet
  3. robots.txt, the WAF and the CDN do not block Googlebot
  4. The site is not excluded in Search Console's Search generative AI setting
  5. The facts that answer questions (prices, hours, area, who it is for) are in the page text
  6. Structured data matches the visible text
  7. Business Profile and Merchant Center match the site
  8. No separate AI-only pages or pages split up just for AI
  9. The generative AI performance report is checked monthly over the same period
  10. A fixed set of unbranded questions is recorded several times under the same conditions

FAQ

Do I need special settings or tags to appear in AI Overviews?

Google says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations: the page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet. Its optimization guide also requires that your site is not excluded in Search Console's Search generative AI setting.

Does an llms.txt file help with AI Overviews or AI Mode?

Google states that Google Search ignores llms.txt and that having one neither helps nor harms. Whether to publish one for other AIs such as ChatGPT or Perplexity is a separate decision.

I don't want my content used for AI training. Will that keep me out of AI Overviews?

Google separates the two: robots.txt rules for Googlebot control access for Google Search, while Google-Extended limits AI training and grounding in Google's other systems. To keep pages out of AI features, use the Search generative AI setting in Search Console or snippet controls.

Can a page with nosnippet be a source in AI Overviews?

Snippet eligibility is a condition, so it becomes much less usable as a source. To hide only part of a page, put data-nosnippet on that part.

If my page ranks first, will it always appear in the AI Overview?

No. Many searches show no AI Overview, and the selection criteria are not public. Third-party studies show top-ranking pages are cited more often, but that is not a guarantee.

Can I see how much traffic AI Overviews sent me?

Search Console counts clicks from AI features inside the Performance report's Web search type and does not separate them. The generative AI performance report shows how often you were shown in AI features.

An AI Overview shows outdated information about us. What can we do?

Follow the supporting links to the source page. If it is yours, fix it and request recrawling; also check your Business Profile and Merchant Center. If it is someone else's site, ask them to update it. There is no set time for changes to show.
