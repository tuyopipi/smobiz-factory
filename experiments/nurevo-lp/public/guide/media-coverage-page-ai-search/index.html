How to Build a Press Coverage Page That Helps AI Search and Readers Find Independent Coverage

Studies of AI answers find that most citations come from unpaid third-party pages: news, trade press, comparison articles and the like. A press coverage ("as seen in") page on your own site is not independent praise in itself, but listing each publication, date, headline and a link to the original in one place gives AI and readers a way in to that independent coverage. Link to articles rather than pasting images of them, and keep paid placements clearly labelled as ads.

Written 2026-11-25 (JST). The figures come from the public reports of Muck Rack (December 2025 and May 2026) and BuzzStream (March 2026); the copyright and stealth-marketing points come from Japanese sources (the Japan Newspaper Association, the Consumer Affairs Agency and a trade publication), all read on the same day. If you are outside Japan, check the equivalent rules where you operate.

A press page does not guarantee that AI will mention your business or rank it higher. AI answers change day to day, and no AI company publishes how it picks sources. This guide is about showing coverage you already have, accurately and within the rules.

What the three studies support is a tendency: (1) AI cites third-party pages far more than ads, (2) news is only part of that, alongside comparison articles, reviews and industry sites, and (3) news is used more for questions that compare and choose.

What your own press page is for

Writing "featured in the Daily Example" on your own site is still your own statement, not an independent assessment. The page earns its place in three ways.

  1. A way in: with publication, date, headline and URL in place, AI search, readers, journalists and Wikipedia editors can reach the original article.
  2. Keeping facts straight: if an article shows an old address or product name, listing what was true then next to what is true now prevents mix-ups.
  3. Making claims checkable: "as seen on TV" can't be verified. A programme name and air date can.

In BuzzStream's study, companies' own newsroom content was cited more often by ChatGPT than by the other AI tools. Your own page can be read, then, but only if what it contains is a real list of coverage.

Eight things to write for each item

The Japan Newspaper Association's Q&A says that search services presenting articles should show the publication name, date, headline, a very short snippet and the URL, and that linking to the original is the key point (it was written for search services, but it is a useful guide to what a listing contains; Japan Newspaper Association). On your own page, write these eight items as text.

ItemExampleWhy
PublicationThe Daily Example (print, regional section)Who wrote it is how independence is judged
Date published or aired14 September 2026AI tends to cite recent coverage
Headline or programmeThe headline exactly as publishedSo the original can be found by search

NoteThe address in the article is our former officePrevents confusion with outdated facts

List items newest first with a heading for each year; that is easier for both AI and readers. When a link dies, keep the item and note "no longer available at the publisher".

What not to do: copyright and hidden advertising

  1. Don't paste images, scans or screenshots of articles. In Japan, copying, scanning, photographing, copy-and-pasting web articles and printing screens all count as reproduction and in principle need the rights holder's permission. Even for posting on an internal intranet, a 2023 ruling ordered payments of about ¥6.96 million to Nikkei and about ¥1.33 million to Chunichi Shimbun (AdverTimes, 2026-07-03). If you want to show an article, ask the publication for reprint permission.
  2. Don't copy long passages. Summarise the point in one or two sentences of your own and link to the original for the rest.
  3. Check each publication's linking policy. Some refuse commercial use such as product promotion in their link policies (Japan Newspaper Association Q&A). Get permission before showing a publication's logo, too.
  4. Label paid articles as ads. In Japan, since 1 October 2023, advertising that ordinary consumers cannot recognise as advertising violates the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. It covers newspapers, magazines, TV, radio and social media, and the advertiser is the party held responsible (Consumer Affairs Agency). If you list sponsored or advertorial pieces, mark them "Advertisement (sponsored)" and keep them separate from editorial coverage.
  5. For links from a sponsored article to your site, Google asks for rel="sponsored" or similar on the publisher's side (Google: qualify outbound links). Confirm this when you book the placement.
  6. Don't exaggerate. Avoid unverifiable lines such as "featured everywhere" or "seen on TV many times" and state each item as a fact.

Structured data: mark up only the list people can see

In schema.org, articles about your organization can be given with subjectOf and awards with award (text). Neither is a Google rich result, and no AI company has said it uses these properties. If you add them, include only what is visible in the list on the page: Google's structured data guidelines ask you not to mark up content that is hidden from readers (Google: general structured data guidelines).

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "@id": "https://example.co.jp/#organization",
  "name": "Sample Co., Ltd.",
  "url": "https://example.co.jp/",
  "award": "Regional Manufacturing Award, Merit Prize (2025)",
  "subjectOf": [
    {
      "@type": "NewsArticle",
      "headline": "(the article's headline, exactly as published)",
      "datePublished": "2026-09-14",
      "url": "https://news.example.jp/articles/12345",
      "publisher": { "@type": "Organization", "name": "(publication name)" }
    }
  ]
}
</script>

Write headlines and dates exactly as published, and leave out paid placements that are not the publication's own editorial. For the company's structured data as a whole, see the about page and AI search.

Steps in WordPress

  1. Create a page called "Press" (or "Press and awards") with a short URL such as /press/. Write the eight items as a table or list in text, not as an image gallery.
  2. Keep press releases as posts (news) and third-party coverage on this page. Link to the page from your about page and the footer.
  3. Ordinary links to outside articles are fine. Check for broken links with a plugin or by hand on a schedule, and add a note to any that have died.
  4. In Nurevo AEO (a free WordPress plugin), llms.txt lists up to 30 of the most recently modified published pages and posts. A press page that goes unedited for a long time can drop off that list, so to keep it there, add a "Press coverage" line with the webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines filter (example below). You can also leave any page out of llms.txt in its per-page settings.
  5. To add subjectOf to the company's structured data, use the webmcp_canary_business_node filter. When Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO or Slim SEO is active, the values are merged into that plugin's company data and the values it already publishes win. Since 0.9.1, using it alongside SEO SIMPLE PACK or VK All in One Expansion Unit does not duplicate structured data.
  6. Then check /llms.txt and the page source (application/ld+json) to confirm they match the list on the page. Get it on WordPress.org
// Keep the press coverage page in llms.txt whatever its modified date
add_filter('webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines', function (array $lines) {
    $lines[] = '## Press coverage';
    $lines[] = '';
    $lines[] = '- [Press coverage and awards](https://example.com/press/): publication, date, headline and a link to each original article';
    $lines[] = '';
    return $lines;
});

How to check

  1. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode questions such as "What kind of company is (name)?", "Which (business type) should I choose in (area)?" and "What do people say about (name)?". Record the answers and source URLs with the date.
  2. See whether the articles cited are on your press page, and whether articles on your page never appear. If the cited sources are mostly old, add current facts in the note column.
  3. In Google Analytics 4, look at visits to the press page from AI services and from the publications' sites. In Search Console, watch searches for your company name.
  4. AI answers vary from day to day, so ask the same questions on the same day each month. How to track a fixed question set is in measuring AI visibility.
  5. Nurevo AEO's free AI check asks Perplexity three questions built from your business type and area, once a month, for each connected site. Pro (from USD 99/month), which measures continuously, also uses Perplexity today (ChatGPT once configured).

Checklist

  1. The press page gives publication, date, headline, format, type, what was covered, link and a note, as text
  2. No images, scans or screenshots of articles without permission
  3. No long copied passages; the point is summarised in our own words
  4. Paid articles are marked "Advertisement (sponsored)"
  5. Press releases and third-party coverage are kept apart
  6. No unverifiable wording such as "featured everywhere"
  7. Dead links carry a note
  8. The about page and footer link to the press page
  9. Any structured data matches the visible list only
  10. AI questions, answers and sources are recorded with dates

FAQ

Will a press page make AI mention us more?

Not necessarily. What AI tends to cite is the third-party articles themselves; your press page is the way in to them. With publication, date, headline and link in place, AI and readers can find the originals more easily.

Can we post photos of newspaper articles on the page?

In principle you need the rights holder's permission; scans, photos and screenshots all count as copies. Without permission, write the publication, date and headline as text and link to the original.

Can we list advertorials and sponsored articles?

You can, but label them "Advertisement (sponsored)" and keep them apart from editorial coverage. In Japan, since October 2023, ads that consumers can't recognise as ads violate the law, and the advertiser is responsible.

Does distributing a press release count as coverage?

No. A distribution service's page is your own announcement. It becomes coverage when a journalist reports and publishes a story. Keep press releases separate on your page too.

How should we list TV or radio appearances?

Give the station, programme, air date, segment and one sentence on what was covered. Link to on-demand or programme pages if they exist; otherwise write "broadcast only". Don't use screenshots of the broadcast without permission.

Should we remove old articles?

No need. List newest first, and if an article's address or products differ from today's, add the current facts in the note. When a link dies, keep the item with "no longer available at the publisher".

Is structured data required?

No. Writing the list as text on the page comes first. You can add schema.org subjectOf and the like, but it is not a Google rich result and no AI company has said it uses it.
