WordPress vs non-WordPress: AI search readiness of 14,361 web agency websites (November 2026)

We split 14,361 web design agencies' own websites whose home page answered normally (HTTP 200) into WordPress (12,376) and non-WordPress (1,985). JSON-LD structured data appeared on the home page of 88.8% of WordPress sites and 17.6% of non-WordPress sites; llms.txt on 29.6% and 9.8%. Non-WordPress sites blocked AI bots in robots.txt more often (13.3% vs 3.7%), almost always training crawlers only. In Japan alone, non-WordPress sites (191) had JSON-LD on 28.3% and a robots.txt on 45.0%, against 72.9% and 95.6% for WordPress.

Published 2026-11-20. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic, and in WordPress in particular. Differences between SEO plugins within WordPress are covered in a separate report.

Key findings

  1. 88.8% of web agency WordPress sites (of 12,376) had JSON-LD structured data on the home page, against 17.6% of non-WordPress sites (of 1,985). llms.txt: 29.6% vs 9.8%. All six AI-search items: 24.9% vs 4.7%.
  2. In Japan, non-WordPress agency sites (191) had JSON-LD on 28.3% (WordPress 72.9%), a robots.txt on 45.0% (95.6%), a canonical link on 42.9% (88.1%) and an llms.txt on 6.3% (14.0%). Only FAQPage showed no difference (3.1% vs 3.9%, p = 0.73).
  3. Non-WordPress sites blocked AI bots more often: 9.3% in Japan (8 of 86 with a robots.txt; WordPress 1.0%) and 13.8% overseas (89 of 643; WordPress 4.3%). Almost all of it targeted training crawlers: 5 overseas non-WordPress sites (0.8%) and no Japanese ones blocked any of the four bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 23,687 agency sites in our collection list, 15,838 home pages could be reached (Japan 2,486; overseas 13,352). This is not a sample of businesses in general.
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. 12,376 of the 15,838 reachable sites (78.1%) were WordPress: 92.3% in Japan (2,295) and 75.5% overseas (10,081). We did not identify what the non-WordPress sites run on (custom builds, other CMSs, no-code tools and so on).
  4. HTTP 202: 1,477 overseas non-WordPress home pages answered with status 202 (none in Japan, none among WordPress sites). None of them had a viewport tag, JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they most likely returned a bot-check or other interim page. They are excluded from the non-WordPress columns of the tables; figures including them are given in the text.
  5. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService.
  6. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  7. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  8. Statistics: chi-square tests; Fisher's exact test for the small Japanese non-WordPress group. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown.

Results: item by item

WordPress sites were higher on almost every item; the largest gaps were JSON-LD, Organization-type markup, canonical links and robots.txt. Non-WordPress sites were higher on only two: blocking an AI bot and naming an AI bot in robots.txt. The share of sites with broken JSON-LD (1.4–1.9%) was about the same in every group.

Japanese non-WordPress sites came close to WordPress on meta descriptions (76.4% vs 91.9%) but were low on JSON-LD, canonical links and robots.txt, leaving many of them with one or two of the six items (see the distribution below). Overseas non-WordPress sites were also low on meta descriptions (20.1%); this may partly reflect a limitation of our method (see Limitations).

Counting the 1,477 HTTP 202 pages as well (3,271 overseas non-WordPress sites), the figures are llms.txt 5.6%, JSON-LD 9.0%, all six items 2.6% and a 6-item average of 0.61.

The gap between Japan and overseas remains among WordPress sites: llms.txt 14.0% in Japan vs 33.1% overseas, all six items 10.8% vs 28.0% (both p < 0.001). Among non-WordPress sites we found no difference on llms.txt (6.3% vs 10.1%, p = 0.11), all six items (4.7% each) or AI bot blocking (9.3% vs 13.8%, p = 0.31).

Sites without a robots.txt

The most visible gap for non-WordPress sites was a missing robots.txt: 105 of 191 in Japan (55.0%) and 1,151 of 1,794 overseas (64.2%, excluding 202) returned no plain-text robots.txt at /robots.txt (an HTML page counts as none). Among WordPress sites the figures were 4.4% in Japan and 3.1% overseas.

Having no robots.txt does not mean refusing AI bots. In our six items, however, item (4) is not met without one, which is one reason non-WordPress sites score lower.

Blocking by bot

Share and number of sites with a robots.txt that block each bot from the home page. Crawlers generally described as training crawlers (Bytespider, CCBot, GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, anthropic-ai, Applebot-Extended) were blocked by 12.3–14.2% of overseas non-WordPress sites and 3.2–4.1% of overseas WordPress sites. Japanese non-WordPress sites show 7.0–8.1%, but that is 6–7 sites. Bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks (ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot) were blocked by under 1% in every group.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. Of the 93 overseas non-WordPress sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots, 73 (78.5%) used the same combination: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all seven together. Among overseas WordPress sites it was 268 of 465 (57.6%).
  2. Of the sites blocking GPTBot, those that did not block OpenAI's search crawler OAI-SearchBot were 83 of 85 overseas non-WordPress sites, 346 of 363 overseas WordPress sites (95.3%), and all of them in Japan (21 WordPress, 7 non-WordPress).
  3. Sites that named an AI bot in robots.txt but blocked none from the home page: 75 overseas non-WordPress sites (11.7% of those with a robots.txt) and 585 overseas WordPress sites (6.0%).
  4. Among sites blocking an AI bot, 162 of 423 overseas WordPress sites (38.3%) and 9 of 89 overseas non-WordPress sites also had an llms.txt.

Structured data types

WordPress sites often carried types that SEO plugins typically output site-wide, such as WebSite, WebPage, SearchAction and BreadcrumbList. Non-WordPress sites had far fewer types; the most common were PostalAddress (13.0%) and WebSite (12.5%). In Japan, PostalAddress (19.9% non-WordPress vs 16.1% WordPress) and ProfessionalService (7.3% vs 5.4%) were slightly more common on non-WordPress sites.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general, and our collection list is not a representative sample. The Japanese non-WordPress group is small (191 sites, 86 with a robots.txt), and per-bot counts are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page HTML was examined, without running JavaScript. Sites that add structured data or meta tags with JavaScript after loading count as "none"; some non-WordPress sites may be affected.
  3. Meta descriptions were counted only when the name attribute comes before the content attribute. Sites built with tools that write them the other way round count as "none", so the non-WordPress meta description figures may be understated.
  4. WordPress detection relies on markers in the HTML. WordPress sites that remove them, and set-ups that use WordPress only as a back-end for content, fall into "non-WordPress".
  5. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists. One fetch per site; unreachable sites are excluded. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.
  6. The relationship between WordPress and each item is an association, not evidence that WordPress causes it.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. We think WordPress sites have structured data and canonical links in place so often because SEO plugins and themes output them automatically. WordPress also answers /robots.txt even when no file has been uploaded, which we think explains much of the robots.txt gap. On other platforms the same things have to be added by hand or through the tool's settings, which may be why they are missed more often.

This survey cannot tell why non-WordPress sites block AI bots more often. The frequent identical seven-bot combination suggests hosting or CDN features or template robots.txt files may play a part. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision, but sites that want to appear in AI search may want to confirm they are not blocking search crawlers too. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are WordPress sites better for AI search?

In this survey WordPress sites more often had structured data, llms.txt and canonical links in place (JSON-LD 88.8% vs 17.6%). That describes the state of the sites; it does not measure how likely they are to appear in AI answers.

What do non-WordPress sites most often miss?

A robots.txt (missing on 55.0% of Japanese non-WordPress agency sites) and home page structured data (missing on 71.7%). Meta descriptions were present on 76.4% in Japan.

Do non-WordPress sites block AI crawlers?

9.3% (8 sites) in Japan and 13.8% overseas of those with a robots.txt, more than WordPress (1.0% and 4.3%). Most of it targets training crawlers; under 1% also blocked search crawlers.
