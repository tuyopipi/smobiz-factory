When a client asks for AI search tricks: a judgement table, a refusal script and an audit for agencies

When a client asks you to add text only AI can see, or to publish a "best providers" list that ranks them first, the safe answer for an agency is to decline and offer a legitimate way to reach the same goal. On 15 May 2026 Google updated its documentation to make clear that its spam policies also apply to AI responses in Google Search, such as AI Overviews and AI Mode, and disguised advertising is regulated in many markets. This guide gives a table of commonly requested tactics, where the grey zone ends, a refusal email, a contract clause and commands to audit a site you inherit.

Written 21 November 2026 (JST). For agencies, SEO firms, WordPress care companies and freelancers who build or maintain client sites. Where a legal judgement is needed, ask a lawyer or the regulator. This guide is not legal advice.

AI answers change by day and by wording. The steps here keep a client's site safe in both search and AI; they do not guarantee an appearance in any AI answer.

What the top-ranking guides cover, and what they leave out

On 21 November 2026 we read the pages ranking for "black hat GEO", "AI search what not to do" and similar queries: four in English (Similarweb, 26 February 2026, updated 20 May; Lily Ray, 17 June 2026; Citable, 25 June 2026; and a Search Engine Land piece on hidden prompt injection) and six in Japanese, three of which list tactics to avoid (Uravation, Jinrai, Sungrove).

They agree on the basics: no hidden text, no mass-produced thin content, no fake reviews. What an agency needs when a client actually asks for one of these was mostly missing.

Some of the top pages quote figures such as "60-80% traffic loss within days" or "3-6 months to recover" with no source, so we do not use them here.

How to say no: a sample email

Write it in three parts: the decision, the reason (the rule's name and date), and an alternative that serves the same goal. "It's risky, let's not" on its own usually ends with the client hiring someone else to do it.

Subject: Your AI search request - a change to our proposal

Hi <name>,

We looked at the two ideas you raised: hidden text written for AI, and a
"best providers" article that ranks your company first.

We won't do either, for two reasons:
1. On 15 May 2026 Google updated its documentation to make clear that its
   spam policies also apply to AI responses in Google Search, such as AI
   Overviews. Sites found in breach can rank lower or drop out of results.
2. Advertising made to look like a neutral review can break consumer-protection
   or advertising-disclosure rules, and the advertiser is usually the one held
   responsible.

Here is what we suggest instead, for the same goal (AI describing your
strengths accurately):
- State prices, service areas and results on the page, with sources and dates
- Publish a comparison page that says clearly it is written by you
- Set up a way to collect customer reviews without conditions attached

We'll send a revised quote on this basis. Happy to talk it through.

Give outside rules as the reason, not your agency's taste; the client can forward them to a manager as they are. Pick the alternative to fit what the client actually wants, such as being recommended by AI or looking stronger than a competitor.

What to put in the contract and scope

To avoid "the last agency did it" or "that was in the contract" later, write the excluded methods into the agreement or the statement of work. A sample:

(AI search methods)
1. In performing the services, the Agency will not:
   (a) place text or instructions on a page that visitors cannot see;
   (b) return different content to search engine or AI crawlers than to visitors;
   (c) present the Client's advertising as an independent, neutral review,
       unless it is clearly labelled as advertising;
   (d) create reviews or endorsements from people who do not exist or are not
       real customers;
   (e) put facts in structured data that the page does not show.
2. If the Client asks for any of the above, the Agency may decline, and
   declining is not a breach of this agreement.
3. AI answers vary by day and by question; the Agency does not guarantee
   appearance or position in any AI answer.
Note: this is a sample. Have a lawyer review the wording for your contract.

A retainer that promises a number of AI mentions as its outcome gives everyone a reason to reach for the tactics above. Report on what facts were added and on a fixed set of recorded questions instead (selling AI search as a monthly service).

Auditing an inherited site

Sites you take over for care or a rebuild can carry tactics added by a previous vendor or plugin. These commands look for hidden styling, crawler-specific content, signs of mass production and the structured data types in use. Treat matches as leads only: display:none is normal in menus and accordions, so check each one by eye.

# 1. Hidden text or instructions aimed at AI (home page and key pages)
curl -s https://www.example.com/ | grep -inE 'display: ?none|visibility: ?hidden|font-size: ?0|ChatGPT|Perplexity|AI assistant|ignore (all|previous)'

# 2. Do AI crawlers and browsers get the same content? (size and diff)
curl -s -A 'Mozilla/5.0' https://www.example.com/ > browser.html
curl -s -A 'OAI-SearchBot' https://www.example.com/ > bot.html
wc -c browser.html bot.html; diff <(sed 's/nonce="[^"]*"//g' browser.html) <(sed 's/nonce="[^"]*"//g' bot.html) | head

# 3. Hidden styling inside WordPress posts
wp post list --post_type=any --post_status=publish --s='display:none' --fields=ID,post_title,post_type
wp db query "SELECT ID, post_title FROM $(wp db prefix)posts WHERE post_status='publish' AND post_content REGEXP 'visibility:[ ]*hidden|font-size:[ ]*0'"

# 4. Pages mass-produced in a short time (published per month)
wp db query "SELECT DATE_FORMAT(post_date,'%Y-%m') m, COUNT(*) FROM $(wp db prefix)posts WHERE post_status='publish' AND post_type IN ('post','page') GROUP BY m ORDER BY m DESC LIMIT 12"

# 5. Which structured data types and ratings (AggregateRating, Review) appear
curl -s https://www.example.com/ | grep -o '"@type": *"[A-Za-z]*"' | sort | uniq -c

If step 2 shows a large difference in size or content, look for user-agent switching in plugins or server settings; caching and ads cause small differences. In Search Console, the Manual actions report under Security & Manual Actions shows violations a Google reviewer has found (help). Fix the cause, then request a review.

If you find mass-produced pages, either merge them into a page with substance or remove the ones not worth keeping. The decision process is in selling content refresh as a monthly service.

Steps in WordPress (with Nurevo AEO)

Here is how to confirm, with the free plugin Nurevo AEO, that the AI-facing output matches what visitors see. With another plugin or by hand, check the same things.

  1. Nurevo AEO does not read the visitor's user agent. Structured data is rendered on the server into the page HTML, so browsers and AI crawlers receive the same thing. Per-crawler groups in robots.txt are ordinary configuration, not an exception to this.
  2. FAQ structured data (FAQPage) is output only on a page where the Nurevo FAQ block or [nurevo_faq] is placed and the questions are shown. Nothing is output for an FAQ that is not visible.
  3. Product ratings (AggregateRating) are output only when the WooCommerce product has reviews. There is no field to type a rating in.
  4. Business details are filled from your SEO plugin's settings and public content into empty fields only; values you typed are never overwritten. Check against the diagnosis that the admin values and the page copy agree.
  5. Mass-produced or advertising pages can be left out of llms.txt and have Nurevo's structured data switched off in the per-post Nurevo AEO panel. Whether to keep the page itself is a separate decision.
  6. The free AI check asks Perplexity three questions a month, built from business type and area and never the business name. A business appears when asked by name anyway, so this shows whether facts, not manipulation, changed anything. Read Pro measurement (beta) numbers as indicative too.
  7. After the audit, open key pages in the Rich Results Test and confirm structured data values match the visible text.

Checklist after declining

  1. The client received the decision, the rule's name and date, and an alternative by email.
  2. The quote and scope were redone around the alternative.
  3. The contract lists excluded methods and what is not guaranteed (reviewed by a lawyer).
  4. The home page and key pages carry no invisible text or instructions for AI.
  5. Browsers and AI crawlers receive the same HTML.
  6. Monthly publishing counts show no burst of mass-produced pages.
  7. FAQ and rating structured data match visible content.
  8. Comparison pages and paid placements are clearly labelled as the client's own or as advertising.
  9. Review requests carry no conditions such as five stars.
  10. Results are reported from a fixed set of recorded questions without the business name.

FAQ

Even if Google catches hidden text, doesn't it still work on ChatGPT or Perplexity?

We found no reliable evidence that it does. AI companies publish defences against hidden instructions, and informal tests found no effect. Even if it worked briefly, Google Search treats it as hidden text, so it is not worth the risk.

Is any comparison article that includes our own company off-limits?

No. Make clear it is your own page, state the criteria and date, and say where competitors fit better. The line is presenting it as a neutral third-party review.

Under Japan's stealth marketing rule, is the agency the one penalised?

The Consumer Affairs Agency Q&A says the rule covers the business that decided the content (the advertiser), not third parties asked to post. That is why the client needs the explanation, so they can decide for themselves.

Did Google's May 2026 change ban any new tactic?

According to reports, it did not add new rules; it made clear that the existing spam policies apply to answers in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Check Google's changelog for the original wording.

Does Anthropic's Usage Policy matter to agencies that do not use Claude?

It directly governs use of Claude. It is still useful when explaining a refusal, because it shows what an AI company treats as deceptive, whichever AI tool you use.

We found hidden text left by a previous vendor. What now?

Show it to the client and remove it with their agreement. Check the Manual actions report in Search Console; if a violation is listed, request a review after fixing it. Record the date you removed it so you can compare later.

Will legitimate work alone get the client recommended by AI?

That cannot be guaranteed. AI answers change by day and wording. What you can do is put facts such as prices, areas and results on the pages with sources, make sure AI can read them, and track changes with the same questions.
