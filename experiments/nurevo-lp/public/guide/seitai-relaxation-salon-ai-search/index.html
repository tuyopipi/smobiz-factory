AI search for bodywork and relaxation salons: drawing the "not medical treatment" line and getting AI to read your menu, prices and practitioners correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a bodywork or relaxation salon comes down to making your own site able to answer what people ask AI, such as "My back hurts: should I see a bodywork shop or a doctor?" or "60-minute body care near the station, how much?" Write what you do and what you do not do (not medical treatment, no health insurance, who should see a doctor first), the length and price of each session, and your practitioners' real training as text on your pages, then publish the same facts as HealthAndBeautyBusiness structured data and in llms.txt. Bold claims that you "fix" or "cure" things do not make AI more likely to pick you.

Written November 22, 2026 (JST). The legal and government information comes from Japanese sources (the National Consumer Affairs Center, the Consumer Affairs Agency and the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications), as cited in the text. In Japan, seitai, chiropractic and relaxation bodywork have no national licence. Rules differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you. This article is not legal advice.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your shop's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your shop or rank it anywhere. Licensed clinics (in Japan, judo therapists' seikotsuin) are not covered here.

What people ask AI about bodywork and relaxation salons

Questions about bodywork mix place and time ("near the station, open until 9 pm") with worries about the body ("Can I go with a pulled back?", "What is the difference between bodywork and a licensed clinic?"). The second kind is close to medicine, so AI answers it carefully. If your site does not say what you do and what you do not do, AI either finds your shop hard to use in an answer or guesses from general explanations and reviews.

In this trade, facts with a clear line are more useful to AI than clever marketing copy.

What the top-ranking guides leave out

On November 22, 2026, we read 13 top-ranking articles in Japanese and English for searches such as "seitai AI search" and "massage chiropractic AI search". Few Japanese articles dealt with bodywork shops directly; most lumped them in with licensed clinics or were about esthetic and hair salons. One did state clearly that bodywork has no national licence and contrasted wording to avoid with wording to use, but none cited the public sources behind that line (harm reports from consumer agencies, the health ministry's notices), gave WordPress steps, said which structured data type to use, or explained how to check what AI says.

The English articles focused on Google Business Profile and reviews, and said almost nothing about how to describe results. This article fills those gaps.

The line drawn by Japan's public sources

In Japan there is no statutory licence for seitai, chiropractic or relaxation bodywork. A National Consumer Affairs Center release of August 2, 2012 explains that Article 1 of the act on massage, acupuncture and moxibustion practitioners requires anyone other than a doctor to hold a licence to practise anma, massage or shiatsu as a business, and that Article 12 in principle bans other medical-like practices as a business. It adds that, following a Supreme Court judgment of January 27, 1960, the ban is read as covering only practices that may harm health. National Consumer Affairs Center: harm from hands-on medical-like practices, August 2, 2012 (Japanese)

The same release cites a health ministry notice of June 28, 1991 on chiropractic: do not treat people with conditions such as herniated discs, spinal stenosis or osteoporosis; ban dangerous techniques such as sudden twisting thrusts to the neck; stop and send the person for medical care if symptoms persist or worsen; and regulate exaggerated advertising such as claims to cure cancer. It also reports that at a ministry meeting on February 29, 2012, calling a place that does not offer anma, massage or shiatsu a "massage" shop was described as possibly misleading, and prefectures were asked to correct such advertising.

The harm figures are public too. On May 26, 2017, the Consumer Affairs Agency reported 1,483 harm cases from hands-on practices with no statutory licence between September 2009 and March 2017, of which 240 (about 16%) needed treatment for a month or more. Most reports involved seitai, relaxation massage and chiropractic, with 290 cases of nerve or spinal cord injury and 122 fractures. Consumer Affairs Agency, May 26, 2017 (Japanese, copy on Osaka City's site). The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications also issued recommendations on preventing these accidents to the Consumer Affairs Agency and the health ministry on November 17, 2020. MIC: accidents from medical-like practices, November 17, 2020 (Japanese)

For a shop, the meaning is plain. AI writes its answers having read sources like these. If your pages contain wording that reads as medical or that promises safety, you cannot predict how AI will present you. A shop that says "we are not medical" and "these people should see a doctor first" is easier for AI to use as a source when it answers questions about the body.

Eight facts to put on your menu and shop pages

  1. What the session involves: hands-on or with tools, clothed or not, with or without oil.
  2. Length and price for each menu, including tax: pair them, as in "60 minutes, $45". Add extension, nomination and first-visit terms.
  3. That you are not a medical provider: one sentence such as "We do not diagnose or treat illness."
  4. Health insurance: if it does not apply, write "Health insurance does not apply."
  5. Who should see a doctor first: the conditions your shop has set, such as severe pain or numbness, illness under treatment, pregnancy or recent surgery.
  6. Practitioners' background: years of practice, training, the exact names of qualifications (for a private certificate, the issuing body and the words "private certificate"). If anyone holds a national licence, say which.
  7. Booking and cancellation: how to book, same-day slots, cancellation fees, payment methods.
  8. Basic shop details: address (building and floor), opening hours, closing days, directions from the nearest station.

These eight are the minimum AI needs to answer "Should I go?", "How much?" and "Who will do it?". Many shops post their price list only as an image. Text inside an image may not be read by AI, so put it in the page text as well. Structured data: do not use a medical type

schema.org has no type just for bodywork or relaxation. Checked in schema-dts 2.1.0, the subtypes of HealthAndBeautyBusiness are BeautySalon, DaySpa, HairSalon, HealthClub, NailSalon and TattooParlor (checked November 22, 2026). A bodywork or body-care shop fits HealthAndBeautyBusiness; a spa or bathing facility fits DaySpa.

Be careful with Physiotherapy. The name sounds close to hands-on work, but it sits under MedicalBusiness and is described as the treatment of disease, injury or deformity by physical methods such as massage. A shop without a licence that uses it is calling itself a medical provider in its structured data. Chiropractic, too, exists in schema.org only as a value of MedicineSystem, not as a business type.

Publish each menu as its own Service, with a description of the session and a note on who should see a doctor first. Registered in Nurevo AEO, it looks like this (the values are examples):

Setting it up on a WordPress bodywork or relaxation site

  1. Install and activate Nurevo AEO (free) from WordPress.org. Your SEO plugin, such as Yoast SEO, Rank Math or All in One SEO, keeps working.
  2. In the setup wizard, choose "Beauty salon", then change "Business type (schema.org type)" in the settings to HealthAndBeautyBusiness (DaySpa for a spa facility). Do not choose "Clinic or dental": that sets a medical type (MedicalBusiness).
  3. Add "We are not a medical provider and do not diagnose or treat illness" and "Health insurance does not apply" to the business description, and check the address (with building and floor), phone and hours.
  4. Register each menu as a service (up to 20): the length in the name, the price including tax (number only), currency, booking URL, and in the description what the session involves and who should see a doctor first. They are published as Service, Offer and a booking action, and each gets a line in llms.txt.
  5. Add FAQs (the difference from a licensed clinic, insurance, who should not come, adjusting pressure, same-day booking) and place the Nurevo FAQ block (or the [nurevo_faq] shortcode) on your menu page.
  6. Open /llms.txt and check that your menu, prices, access and practitioner pages are listed. Rewrite old blog posts such as "How we cured X", or exclude them from llms.txt in the Nurevo AEO panel. Run the check on the AEO score screen and fix what it finds.

On sites that are not WordPress, the points to check are the same. Enter your URL in the free check, no sign-up needed, to see the state of your structured data, llms.txt and AI crawler access. For booking pages, see AI search for booking pages.

Keep booking sites and Google Business Profile in step

Relaxation shops are often listed on booking and coupon sites. AI reads those pages too, so if menu lengths or prices differ between your site and a booking site, or a booking site still says something like "fixes stiff shoulders", the old wording can end up in answers even after you fix your own site.

Keep menu names, lengths, prices including tax and opening hours the same on your site, booking sites and Google Business Profile. When a price changes, update all of them on the same day; give campaign prices an end date and remove them when they end. Apply the rewording from the table above to booking-site descriptions and your Business Profile as well.

Checklist before you publish

  1. "We do not diagnose or treat illness" and "Health insurance does not apply" are written as text.
  2. The conditions for seeing a doctor first are written.
  3. No medical-sounding words such as "cure", "treatment" or "patients".
  4. In Japan, no "massage" unless a licensed practitioner works there.
  5. No "safe", "zero pain" or "healing reaction".
  6. Each menu has its length and price including tax, plus extension, nomination and cancellation fees.
  7. Practitioners' qualifications are named exactly (private certificates with the issuing body and the words "private certificate").
  8. The structured data type is HealthAndBeautyBusiness (or DaySpa), not Physiotherapy or MedicalBusiness.
  9. Menu, prices, hours and wording match across your site, booking sites and Google Business Profile.
  10. llms.txt lists your menu, prices, access and practitioner pages. What is llms.txt

Check whether AI shows you, and whether it is right

Once the pages are in order, ask AI what your clients would ask. Check whether you are named for local, condition-based questions such as "body care near [station], open late" or "bodywork in [city] with female staff", and also ask with your shop's name, such as "Does insurance cover it?" or "Is it OK to go with back pain?", and record whether the answers match your site. If AI describes your shop as if it were a medical provider, look for medical-sounding wording left on your site or a booking site.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time. FAQ

Where should a bodywork shop start with AI search?

Write "We are not a medical provider and do not diagnose or treat illness", "Health insurance does not apply" and the conditions for seeing a doctor first on your pages, along with the length and price of each menu as text. Then publish the same facts as HealthAndBeautyBusiness structured data and in llms.txt, and make them match your booking sites and Google Business Profile.

Won't saying "not medical" put clients off?

Not necessarily. AI answers questions about the body carefully, so a shop with a clear line is easier for it to use as a source. If you leave it out, AI describes your shop by guessing from general explanations and reviews.

Can a bodywork shop in Japan call its service "massage"?

Avoid it unless a licensed anma-massage-shiatsu practitioner works there. At a 2012 health ministry meeting, calling a place that does not offer anma, massage or shiatsu a "massage" shop was described as possibly misleading. Use words such as "body care" or "relaxation".

Which structured data type should a bodywork shop use?

HealthAndBeautyBusiness is the closest. Use DaySpa for a spa facility. Physiotherapy is a medical type under MedicalBusiness, so an unlicensed shop should not use it.

Can I post client reviews saying "my back pain is gone"?

Posted on your own pages as testimonials about results, they read as if the shop claims to cure. It is safer to keep testimonials to the atmosphere and service and leave out claims about effects. Check specific cases with your local health authority.
