About page AI search optimization: get your company name, address, founding date and business described correctly

When someone asks an AI about your company by name, the page the answer leans on is your About or company profile page. Put your legal name and the names people actually use, your address, founding date, leadership, what you do and how to reach you on one page in plain text; publish the same values as Organization structured data; and make the spelling match across your other pages and your profiles elsewhere. Those three steps are the core of the work.

Written November 15, 2026 (JST). This guide is for any company, shop or organization that has an About or company profile page, and for the agencies that build those sites. Address and opening-hours markup for shops is covered in local business schema and SEO plugins. This guide is about the page that says who you are and where you are.

AI answers change by day and by wording. These steps make the information AI reads accurate; they do not guarantee that you will appear in an answer.

What AI uses your About page for

ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews read web pages when they answer and show them as sources. For a question that names a company, that company's own site, and above all the page that collects its basic facts, is an obvious candidate. When the About page is a single image, or the name and address are written differently from page to page, the AI may build its answer from someone else's old listing, or from a different company with the same name.

Questions that name a company tend to fall into a few patterns. Each one can be answered from an About page that states the facts in text.

  1. "What does Example Ltd do?" (business description)
  2. "Where is Example's head office? Do they have an office in Manchester?" (locations)
  3. "When was Example founded, and who runs it?" (founding date, leadership)
  4. "Which company is behind the Example app?" (brand versus company name)
  5. "Is Example the same company as Sample?" (former names, group companies, namesakes)
  6. "How do I contact Example?" (phone, form, hours)

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special structured data for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that normal SEO best practices apply (Google Search Central: AI features and your website). The About page work is not a special way of writing for AI. It is stating the facts accurately, in a readable form, with the same values everywhere.

On registration and tax numbers: Google describes taxID as the tax number for the country in address, and vatID as the VAT number where one applies. If your number is a tax or VAT number for your country, those properties fit. If it is a different kind of identifier, such as Japan's corporate number, Google does not say whether it belongs in taxID, so state it on the page and decide for yourself whether to mark it up.

Do not inflate figures. If you round headcount or client numbers up, AI will quote the larger number and it is hard to take back. Give the exact figure and the date it applies to.

How to write the page: not just a table, never an image

Many About pages are nothing but a two-column table. A table is easy for people and machines to read, but on its own it lacks a sentence that says what the company is. Put two or three sentences of introduction above the table and the AI has something it can use as is when it describes you.

  1. First sentence: the legal name, the town or city, and what you do. For example: "Sample Ltd, based in Leeds, helps small businesses set up accounting software."
  2. Second sentence: facts that set you apart from namesakes, such as how the brand relates to the company and the year you were founded.
  3. Then the table, as an HTML table or a list with labels, never as an image or a PDF.
  4. History as one line per year. For a change of name, a move or a merger, write both the old and the new: "In March 20XX the company changed its name from Old Name Ltd."
  5. Keep the founder's message and the mission under separate headings from the facts.
  6. End the page with the date it was last updated.

schema.org has an AboutPage type for the page itself, and some SEO plugins let you choose it as the page type. It is optional, and fine to use as long as it does not contradict your other structured data.

Remove variants, on your site and elsewhere

AI builds an answer from several pages. If your name or address is written differently from page to page, it may not treat them as one company, or it may pick the old spelling. Start by deciding one form for each of the usual variants.

Where to check on your site: About, footer, contact page, privacy policy, any legally required trading disclosures, careers pages, press releases, author boxes on the blog, and the brochure PDF.

Where to check elsewhere: your Google Business Profile, the social profiles the company controls, job boards, trade association and local directories, and descriptions of you on partners' sites. Some of these you cannot edit. Get your own site right first, then ask the publisher to correct theirs. When an AI answer is already wrong, trace its source and fix each kind of mistake where it comes from.

Help AI tell you apart from a company with the same name

If your name is a common word, or another company elsewhere has the same name, AI may blend the two. What separates you is facts that only you have.

  1. Put the town or city and what you do in the first sentence of the introduction.
  2. State facts a namesake will not share: founding year, registration number, the name of your chief executive.
  3. In sameAs, list only official accounts and profiles the company controls. Leave out namesakes and pages you do not manage.
  4. If the brand and the company name differ, say "Example is run by Sample Ltd" on the pages for both.
  5. Google says properties such as iso6523Code and naics help distinguish an organization. If you have the relevant codes, consider adding them.

Organization structured data: what Google's documentation says

The key points of Google's Organization structured data documentation (last updated September 15, 2026; read November 15):

  1. There are no required properties; Google recommends adding as many of the properties that apply to your organization as you can.
  2. Put the markup on the home page or on one page that describes the organization, such as the About page. It does not need to be on every page.
  3. For a local business such as a restaurant or shop, use the most specific LocalBusiness subtype and follow the LocalBusiness guidance as well.
  4. foundingDate uses ISO 8601 (e.g. 2015-04-01).
  5. The logo must be at least 112×112 pixels and crawlable and indexable.

Google lists making structured data match the visible text as one of the basics (AI features and your website). Structured data does not change answers by itself. Treat it as the machine-readable answer key for what the page already says, and you will keep the priorities right. Do not put a fact in structured data that the page text does not state.

About page checklist

  1. Two or three sentences above the table give the name, location and what you do.
  2. The relationship between the legal name, the brand and any other-language name is written as a sentence.
  3. Address and phone are written the same way on every page of the site.
  4. Founding date, leadership and headcount are correct and dated.
  5. Name changes and moves appear in the history with both old and new.
  6. The About page is not only an image or a PDF.
  7. Organization (or, for a shop, the most specific LocalBusiness subtype) is on the home page or the About page, and its values match the text.
  8. sameAs lists only accounts the company controls.
  9. Your Google Business Profile and other profiles use the same spelling.
  10. The page ends with a last-updated date.
  11. You checked with questions that include your name, on different days, and recorded the results.

FAQ

Do I need an About page separate from the home page?

Not if the home page already collects the company's basic facts. Google recommends putting Organization markup on the home page or on one page that describes the organization. If your home page is mostly about your services, a separate About page keeps the facts in one place.

Is a table-only About page a problem?

No. A table is easy to read. But it lacks a sentence that says what the company is, so add two or three sentences of introduction above it.

Should Organization structured data be on every page?

According to Google, it does not need to be: the home page or one page such as the About page is enough. If your SEO plugin prints it on every page, that is fine as long as the values are correct.

Should our company registration number go in taxID?

Google describes taxID as the tax number for the country in your address. If your number is that, it fits; if it is another kind of identifier, Google does not say. State it on the page first and decide whether to mark it up.

How do we make sure people asking about our former name find the current company?

Write both names in the history ("In March 20XX the company changed its name from Old Name Ltd") and mention it in the introduction. If profiles elsewhere still show the old name, ask their publishers to update them.

An AI answer gets our company facts wrong. What should we do?

You cannot edit the AI's answer directly. Trace the sources: fix the About page and other pages on your own site, and ask other publishers to correct theirs. There is no fixed time for corrections to show up.

If we follow these steps, will AI always describe us correctly?

That cannot be guaranteed; AI answers change by day and wording. These steps make the information AI reads accurate and consistent, so mistakes are less likely.
