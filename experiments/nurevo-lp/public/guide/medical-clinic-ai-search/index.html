AI search for primary care and paediatric clinics: how to get AI to read your hours, same-day visits and vaccinations correctly

AI search optimization for a primary care or paediatric clinic comes down to answering, on the page and down to the day and the minute, the questions patients and parents now ask AI: are you open now and until when can I check in, can I be seen today and do I need an appointment, can I come in with a fever and what should I do first, and when can my child get vaccinated or have a check-up. Then you publish the same facts consistently in structured data, llms.txt, your Google Business Profile and any official health directory you are listed in. Healthcare advertising rules apply to text written for AI just as they do to the rest of your site.

Written November 14, 2026 (JST). The Japanese Ministry of Health page on its national medical directory, schema.org's types (as defined in schema-dts 2.1.0) and the studies cited were checked that day. Examples of rules come from Japan; check the rules where you practise.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your clinic's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your clinic or rank it anywhere.

What patients and parents ask AI

Primary care and paediatrics are what people look for when someone has just become unwell. So the questions carry time and conditions: "a GP near [station] that can see me now", "a paediatrician open on Saturday afternoon", "I have a fever, can I walk in without booking?", "where can my two-month-old get vaccinated?", "a clinic that offers online consultations".

For an AI to name your clinic in an answer like that, it has to be able to read, as text, that you meet those conditions. Hours shown only as an image, no check-in cut-off, or a line saying only "patients with a fever, please call" with no times are hard to use when the question has conditions.

A study of 5,460 ChatGPT answers by Mayui, a Japanese company (July 2026, 19 specialties, six cities), found that official clinic websites were cited in 96% of answers to questions about prices, but in only 31% of answers about reputation, where review and comparison sites appeared in 76%. Press release (Japanese). When AI is asked for facts, your own site is a likely source, so facts such as hours and how to visit are worth writing precisely there. These figures come from a single vendor, and AI answers change.

Write your hours as text, not as an image

Clinic sites often show their hours as an image of a grid with ticks and crosses. People can read it, but AI crawlers cannot be relied on to read text inside an image. Put the same information in an HTML table or a sentence.

For example: "Mon, Tue, Wed, Fri 9:00-12:00 (check-in until 11:30) and 15:00-18:00 (check-in until 17:30). Sat 9:00-12:00. Closed Thursday afternoon, Sunday and public holidays. If you have a fever, please call during opening hours and come at the time we give you." Days, times, check-in cut-offs, closures and exceptions all in one place.

When AI is asked whether you are open now, this is what it answers from. If public holidays or the check-in cut-off are missing, it may tell someone you are open when you are not.

Match the official medical directory

Many countries run an official directory of doctors and clinics. In Japan, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare has operated the national directory 医療情報ネット (nicknamed ナビイ) since April 2024. It covers hospitals, clinics and pharmacies, and can be searched by consultation days and specialties as well as by the conditions and treatments a clinic handles and the services it offers. MHLW: medical function information system (Japanese) / 医療情報ネット

Directory entries are built from what clinics report to the authorities. If an AI or a patient compares them with your website and finds different hours, neither can tell which is right. When you change your hours or specialties, check what you have reported as well as your site and Business Profile.

Healthcare advertising rules: what not to write for AI

In Japan, clinic websites are covered by medical advertising rules. The Ministry's case book lists false, exaggerated and comparative claims and patient testimonials among the prohibited examples. MHLW website case book (PDF, Japanese). The same applies to text, FAQ and structured data written for AI. Other countries have their own rules; check the ones that apply to you.

  1. No superlatives, comparisons or promised outcomes: "best in the area", "guaranteed cure", "seen faster than anywhere else".
  2. No patient testimonials or comments in your FAQ, or as reviews in structured data.
  3. On symptom pages, do not map a symptom to a diagnosis. Say when to see a doctor and what your clinic can do (tests and treatment you offer).
  4. For self-paid vaccines and tests, give the content and the price together.

If you are unsure, check the case book or your local health authority. For dental examples, see AI search for dental clinics.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Your hours are written as text, not only as an image, with check-in cut-offs, half days and public holidays.
  2. You say whether visits are by appointment, by queue, or open to walk-ins.
  3. How to visit with a fever or suspected infection is written with the number to call and the hours.
  4. For children, the age you see them from and the days, hours and booking method for vaccinations and check-ups are written.
  5. Whether you offer online consultations, and for whom, is written.
  6. Temporary closures and holiday dates are next to your hours.
  7. Name, address, phone and hours in structured data, your Business Profile and the official directory match your page text.
  8. No superlatives, comparisons or testimonials in your FAQ or structured data.
  9. llms.txt lists your services, hours, access and booking pages. What is llms.txt

FAQ

Where should a primary care clinic start with AI search?

Write your hours as text, with the check-in cut-off, half days and public holidays. Then put booking rules, how to visit with a fever and vaccination and check-up times in your page text, and make your name, address, phone and hours match across structured data, your Google Business Profile and any official directory.

Should a clinic use MedicalClinic or Physician structured data?

Both are kinds of LocalBusiness and carry an address and opening hours. As a rule of thumb, use MedicalClinic for a practice with several doctors and Physician for a single-doctor practice (PhysiciansOffice also exists for a doctor's office). That the values match your page text matters more than which type you pick.

Should we remove the image of our opening hours?

You do not need to remove it, but write the same information as text too, in an HTML table or a sentence. AI crawlers cannot be relied on to read text inside an image.

How much should we say about fever visits?

Say whether patients should call first, the hours for these visits, the number to call, whether you have a separate entrance or waiting area, and which tests you can do. With only "please call", an AI cannot tell whether someone can be seen.

Can we add patient reviews to our FAQ to help AI pick us?

Check your local rules first. In Japan, clinic websites are covered by medical advertising rules and patient testimonials are treated as prohibited. Do not put them in FAQ or structured data written for AI either.
