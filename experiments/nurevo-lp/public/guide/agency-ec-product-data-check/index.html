Adding a product data consistency check to an ecommerce care plan: steps and a report template for agencies

Add one recurring task to every online shop care plan: each month, for a fixed set of products, check that four places agree - the price and stock visible on the product page, the page's structured data (Product and Offer), the Merchant Center or other product feed, and llms.txt and AI answers. Google says a feed price that does not match the landing page or its structured data can get products disapproved and may lead to account suspension. AI answers also quote outdated prices. This guide covers why mismatches happen, how to run the check (with a script), a report template, and how to write it into the contract.

Written 23 November 2026 (JST). For agencies that build or maintain online shops, WordPress (WooCommerce) care companies, and freelancers who run a shop's day-to-day operations. Merchant Center details follow Google's help pages as of writing; screens and rules change, so check the linked pages before you start.

AI answers vary by day and by wording. Consistent product data is a precondition for being read correctly by AI and search; it does not guarantee that a shop will appear in AI answers or rank anywhere.

Why make it a maintenance task: Google's rules and AI answers

Product data drifts apart again with every sale, price change and stock-out, even after it has been fixed once. A fixed monthly check lets you find mismatches before the client does. Google's help pages say:

What Google saysSource
Googlebot regularly crawls landing pages and compares the feed price with the price on the page and in structured data, if present. A mismatch may lead to disapproval and may lead to account suspension.Merchant Center Help: mismatched product price
Prices in the HTML must match the Merchant Center price exactly; prices inserted with JavaScript after load trigger an error. If the visible page matches the feed but the structured data does not, disapprovals persist until the structured data is fixed.Merchant Center Help: mismatched product price
When policy reviewers find price or availability mismatches, the account can get a warning or pre-emptive item disapprovals. A review takes seven working days, and a failed review starts a seven-working-day cooldown.Merchant Center Help: feed and landing page inconsistency

Structured data must be in the HTML returned by the server and match the values shown to the user. Automatic item updates need price, priceCurrency, availability and condition.Merchant Center Help: structured data

AI answers get prices wrong too. A Product.ai study reported by Business Insider Japan (September 2026) asked ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity 220 shopping questions five times each; of 913 answers whose price could be checked, 85% matched the current price (Business Insider Japan; Google said the study used the developer API rather than the consumer app). What a shop controls is the source data: make it say the same thing wherever it is read.

An article on Productrise data reports that the same product's price differed between Google AI Mode and traditional search in 38.1% of matched cases (TechWyse; about three weeks of August, more than 2 million listings, with no cause established). For ChatGPT Shopping, feed-connected sources reportedly made up 61.54% of product recommendations in Profound's tracked prompts in July (DesignRush; not a figure for all of ChatGPT). When the feed and the page disagree, the shop does not get to choose which value an AI uses.

These are the causes from Google's help pages that agencies most often find during maintenance, with the fix for each.

The monthly routine

  1. Choose the products to check (for example 30): some best sellers, products on sale now, products whose price or stock changed this month, products with variants, and new products. Reliably checking the products most likely to drift is more useful than skimming everything.
  2. Look at Merchant Center diagnostics first for price or availability mismatch warnings and disapprovals. A warning with a deadline comes before everything else.
  3. Export id, link, price and availability for those products from the feed.
  4. Run the script below to check whether the feed's price and stock appear in each page's JSON-LD.
  5. Open about five products in a browser, including some that passed, and compare the visible price, stock and shipping before add to cart (the script does not read visible text).
  6. If the shop publishes llms.txt, confirm the price and stock listed there for the checked products.
  7. Ask AI assistants a few questions about the checked products' price, stock and shipping, and keep the questions and answers with the date.
  8. Fix each mismatch using the causes table, and check the same products again next month.
# Usage: python3 check_offers.py feed.csv > result.tsv
# feed.csv needs the columns id, link, price, availability
# (an export of the Merchant Center product list, or those columns of your feed)
# Result: OK = the feed's price and stock are in the page's JSON-LD / MISMATCH
#         NO_JSONLD = no Product/Offer on the page / FETCH_ERROR = page not fetched
import csv, json, re, sys, urllib.request

def offers(node):
    if isinstance(node, list):
        for n in node:
            yield from offers(n)
    elif isinstance(node, dict):
        if "@graph" in node:
            yield from offers(node["@graph"])
        types = node.get("@type", [])
        types = types if isinstance(types, list) else [types]
        if "Product" in types or "ProductGroup" in types:
            yield from offers(node.get("offers", []))
            yield from offers(node.get("hasVariant", []))
        if "Offer" in types or "AggregateOffer" in types:
            yield node

def num(text):
    m = re.search(r"\d[\d,]*(?:\.\d+)?", str(text or ""))
    return float(m.group(0).replace(",", "")) if m else None

def page_offers(url):
    req = urllib.request.Request(url, headers={"User-Agent": "Mozilla/5.0 (offer-check)"})
    html = urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=20).read().decode("utf-8", "replace")
    found = []
    for block in re.findall(r'<script[^>]+application/ld\+json[^>]*>(.*?)</script>', html, re.S | re.I):
        try:
            found += list(offers(json.loads(block)))
        except ValueError:
            pass
    return found

print("id\tfeed_price\tjsonld_price\tfeed_availability\tjsonld_availability\tresult")
for row in csv.DictReader(open(sys.argv[1], encoding="utf-8-sig")):
    try:
        found = page_offers(row["link"])
    except Exception as e:
        print(f"{row['id']}\t{row['price']}\t\t{row['availability']}\t\tFETCH_ERROR {e}")
        continue
    prices = sorted({num(o.get("price", o.get("lowPrice"))) for o in found} - {None})
    avail = sorted({str(o.get("availability", "")).rsplit("/", 1)[-1] for o in found})
    feed_avail = row["availability"].replace("_", "").lower()
    ok_price = num(row["price"]) in prices
    ok_avail = any(a.lower() == feed_avail for a in avail)
    result = "OK" if ok_price and ok_avail else ("NO_JSONLD" if not found else "MISMATCH")
    print(f"{row['id']}\t{row['price']}\t{','.join(f'{p:g}' for p in prices)}\t{row['availability']}\t{','.join(avail)}\t{result}")

An example script that compares a feed export with each page's JSON-LD (Python 3, standard library only). Use it with the client's agreement and on a few dozen URLs so as not to load the server.

Checking AI answers

Fixing the shop's data does not change AI answers right away. Ask the same questions every month and record the change.

  1. Phrase questions the way customers would: "How much is <product> at <shop>?", "Is <product> in stock?", "What does <shop> charge for shipping?", "Can I return items to <shop>?"
  2. Record the assistant (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode...), date, answer and cited URLs. If there are sources, note whether the answer came from the shop's own product page, a marketplace or a comparison site.
  3. If the answer is outdated and cites the shop's own page, check that page's cache and structured data. If it cites a marketplace or comparison site, the client needs to update that listing.
  4. Answers vary even for the same question, so judge by the trend over several months, not one wrong answer.

FAQ

Is this worth doing for a shop that does not use Merchant Center?

Yes. Without a feed, comparing the visible page, structured data, llms.txt and AI answers is still useful, because AI and search read the page's structured data.

If automatic item updates are on, can we skip the check?

Google's help says mismatches are still treated as critical errors that can lead to suspension, even when automatic updates correct the price. Treat automatic updates as a backstop and keep fixing the cause.

Do we need to check every product every month?

For most shops, a few dozen products weighted towards best sellers, items on sale, items changed this month and items with variants catch most mismatches. When you find a recurring cause, check every product it could affect.

The script says MISMATCH. What do we look at first?

Open the product page in a browser and see whether the visible price matches the feed or the JSON-LD. If the visible price and JSON-LD differ, suspect structured data or caching; if the visible price and feed differ, suspect feed timing or sale settings.

An AI assistant quotes an old price. What can we do?

Check the cited source. If it is the shop's own page, fix that page's display, structured data and cache. If it is a marketplace or comparison site, that listing needs updating. Answers may not change right away, so look at several months of records.

Clients also ask us to enter prices and set up sales. Should that be in the care plan?

Keep it separate in the contract. Put the check and fixes for settings or caching causes in the care plan, and product entry and pricing decisions with the client or in a separately priced operations service.

If product data is consistent, will products appear in ChatGPT or Google's AI?

That cannot be guaranteed. AI answers change by day and wording and draw on other shops and reviews too. Consistent data means that when a product does appear, it is shown with the right price and stock.
