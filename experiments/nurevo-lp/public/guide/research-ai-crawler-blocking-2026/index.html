How web agencies block AI crawlers: 12,581 robots.txt files by bot and country (November 2026)

We read the robots.txt files of web design agencies' own websites. 1.4% in Japan (30 of 2,158) and 5.6% in other countries (579 of 10,423) blocked at least one of 11 AI bots. 93.3% of the blocking sites (568 of 609) blocked only training crawlers and still allowed the search and user-initiated bots (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, ChatGPT-User).

Published 2026-11-11. Data aggregated 2026-11-05. Research and writing: Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社). Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and has an interest in this field.

Our overview report covers all items for Japan. This report looks only at how robots.txt treats AI bots, bot by bot and country by country.

Key findings

  1. 1.4% of Japanese agency websites (30 of 2,158) and 5.6% of agency websites in other countries (579 of 10,423) blocked at least one AI bot. Japan had the lowest share of the 23 countries and regions compared.
  2. Blocking targets training crawlers. Across all sites, 495 (3.9%) blocked GPTBot, but only 21 (0.2%) blocked OpenAI's search crawler OAI-SearchBot. 93.3% of blocking sites (568 of 609) blocked none of the four search or user-initiated bots.
  3. The most common pattern was blocking exactly the seven training crawlers: 62.4% of blocking sites (380 of 609). And 53.6% of sites that name an AI bot in robots.txt (686 of 1,280) name it without blocking it.

About the survey

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. In Japan, sites we confirmed as agencies; elsewhere, our own list of agencies. Not business websites in general.
  2. Counts: 20,720 sites attempted; 15,657 home pages reached (2,357 in Japan, 13,300 elsewhere). The base for this report is the 12,581 of those with a robots.txt (2,158 in Japan, 10,423 elsewhere).
  3. What we fetched: each site's home page and /robots.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), in early November 2026, aggregated on 5 November.
  4. Method: a robots.txt counts if it returns status 200, is not HTML and has more than 20 characters. For each bot we took the User-agent group that names it (or User-agent: * if none does) and checked, by longest match, whether the home page (/) is disallowed.
  5. AI bots: seven training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider, Applebot-Extended) and four search or user-initiated bots (OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot), 11 in total. The split follows the operators' public descriptions and is our own classification. Googlebot and Bingbot were checked for reference.
  6. The overview report's "blocks at least one AI bot: 1.3% (28 sites)" counted six bots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot, OAI-SearchBot). This report counts 11, which gives 1.4% (30 sites) for Japan.

Results: block rate by bot

Every bot was blocked by 1.3% or fewer of Japanese sites, below the 4–5% seen elsewhere. The seven training crawlers have similar rates because the same sites tend to block them together. The four search and user-initiated bots were blocked by 0.3% or fewer in both groups.

Stop training, let search through

We looked at how the 609 sites that block at least one AI bot (30 in Japan, 579 elsewhere) do it.

  1. Block only training crawlers, none of the four search or user-initiated bots: 568 (93.3%). Japan 28 of 30, elsewhere 540 of 579.
  2. Block exactly the seven training crawlers and nothing else: 380 (62.4%). Japan 19 of 30, elsewhere 361 of 579.
  3. Of the 495 sites blocking GPTBot, 476 allow OAI-SearchBot (Japan 27 of 27, elsewhere 449 of 468).
  4. Of the 459 sites blocking ClaudeBot, 441 allow Claude-SearchBot (Japan 21 of 21, elsewhere 420 of 438).
  5. Sites that also block Googlebot (the whole site disallowed): 9 outside Japan (0.09%), none in Japan.

Naming a bot is not the same as blocking it

2.5% of Japanese sites (54) and 11.8% of sites elsewhere (1,226) name at least one AI bot as a User-agent. Of these, 30 in Japan (55.6%) and 564 elsewhere (46.0%) block one; the other 24 and 662 name AI bots but allow them to reach the home page. A fair number of sites explicitly allow AI bots.

Blocking sites were not less likely to have an llms.txt. Outside Japan, 200 of the 579 blocking sites (34.5%) had one, similar to 33.1% of the 9,844 non-blocking sites. In Japan it was 5 of 30 blocking sites (16.7%).

By country and region

Among the 23 countries and regions with at least 30 sites that have a robots.txt, Japan had the lowest share blocking an AI bot (1.4%), followed by France (1.8%). In the larger groups: United States 4.7%, Australia 4.9%, United Kingdom 5.0%, India 6.5%. In small groups such as South Korea or Malaysia, a single site moves the share noticeably.

Limitations

  1. The robots.txt check is for the home page (/) only. A site that blocks only certain directories counts as not blocking.
  2. Blocking by other means (firewalls, CDN bot protection, IP rules) is not measured, so the result may differ from whether a bot can actually reach the site.
  3. robots.txt is a request; whether a bot follows it depends on its operator. Operators may also rename bots or change their roles. The classification reflects this survey as of November 2026.
  4. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. The lists are ours and do not cover every agency, and the number of sites differs a lot by country.
  5. Each site was fetched once. Sites whose home page could not be reached, or with no robots.txt, are not in the base.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. In every country, few agency websites block AI bots, and most of those that do stop training crawlers while letting search bots through. Many sites seem to draw the line at "don't train on us, but do show us in AI search". Blocking every AI bot without knowing that training and search use different names could make a site harder to find in AI search without meaning to. A tidy robots.txt does not guarantee appearing in AI answers, but we think it is worth checking that the settings match the site's intent.

FAQ

How many websites block AI crawlers in robots.txt?

In this survey of web design agencies' own websites, 30 of 2,158 in Japan (1.4%) and 579 of 10,423 elsewhere (5.6%) blocked at least one AI bot (November 2026).

Do sites treat GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot differently?

Yes. 495 sites (3.9%) blocked GPTBot but only 21 (0.2%) blocked OAI-SearchBot. 96.2% of the sites blocking GPTBot (476 of 495) allowed OAI-SearchBot.

Do these figures apply to business websites in general?

Not necessarily. The survey covers web design agencies' own websites and checks robots.txt for the home page (/) only.
