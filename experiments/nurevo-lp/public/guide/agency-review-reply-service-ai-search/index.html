Running review replies for clients in the AI search era: the agreement, reply patterns and industry cautions

In the public tests we could find, ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode did not quote business owners' review replies directly. Replies still matter: they sit next to the review on Google Maps, where people and AI systems read them as a correction of facts and a record of how the business responds. If your agency replies on a client's behalf, put the rules on one page first (whose name, which reviews need the client's approval, what never goes in a reply), use a pattern that never confirms someone was a patient or client in healthcare and legal work, have a person check every AI draft, and answer recurring questions on the client's website, not only in replies.

Written 27 November 2026 (JST). We read Google Business Profile Help (managing reviews, reply tips, managing a profile with Gemini), the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) announcements and the Japanese Penal Code on the same day.

This guide is for web and local SEO agencies that manage clients' Google Business Profiles. Asking for reviews and building testimonial pages are covered in Reviews, testimonials and AI search; this page is only about replies. Replying consistently does not guarantee a client will appear in AI answers. Answers change by day and by wording, and no AI company has published how it treats owner replies.

What is actually known about replies and AI answers

Many articles claim that replying makes AI more likely to recommend you. Almost none cite evidence. This is what we could verify.

The practical reading: a reply's job is less "text an AI will quote" and more "show readers, human or machine, the current facts and how the business handles problems" — and never to become the story. A hostile reply is more likely to reach an AI answer through the blog post or thread that criticises it than through the reply itself.

Agree the rules with the client before you reply

Replies are published under the client's business name. The client is responsible for what they say, and an apology or refund your team writes on its own becomes a promise the client never made. Agree one page like this at the start and urgent replies stop being a judgement call.

Ask the client to add your agency's account as a user on the Business Profile rather than sharing their password (check Google's help for the role types), and write down that access is removed when the contract ends.

[Review reply agreement: one page signed off with the client]
Signed as: business name + role (e.g. "Store manager, Main St.") — never a person's full name
We reply without asking: 4–5 star reviews that mention nothing personal
Client approves first: 1–3 stars; refunds, compensation, accidents, injury,
  health or legal matters; staff named; factual errors; anything in the press or going viral
Deadline: within X business days of the review; 1 business day after approval
Never write: whether the person is a customer or patient, dates, room or order numbers,
  symptoms or case details, discounts or offers, blame, promises the client has not made
Log: review date, stars, topic, reply date, who approved, whether the site was updated

Reply patterns

Patterns are there to make sure the right elements go in, not to make every reply start the same way. Identical replies read as boilerplate to people and machines alike. Every reply should mention at least one specific thing from the review.

[Positive review]
Thank you for the kind words about (what they mentioned).
(One fact the next customer can use, e.g. "After 6 pm on weekdays we can usually seat you without a booking.")
We hope to see you again. (Business name, store manager)

[Complaint where the business was at fault]
We're sorry for the trouble. About the (wait time) you mentioned: (since November we have changed
how many bookings we take per hour). We'd like to hear more — please contact us at (phone / email).
(Business name, store manager)

[Review with a factual error]
Thanks for your feedback. (Parking): we currently offer (two hours free at the partner car park),
and it's listed on the Directions page of our website. We'll make that clearer. (Business name)

[Medical, legal and other confidential services]
Thank you for taking the time to write. We don't discuss individual visits or cases here.
Please call (front desk number) and a member of our team will be glad to talk with you.
(Practice / firm name)

On a positive review, add one fact the next customer can use (booking, parking, payment). As Google's tips say, leave out offers and promotions.

On a complaint, separate what you apologise for from what is factually wrong. Say what changed if you can; if not, invite the reviewer to contact you directly. Reply once and don't argue.

When a review gets a fact wrong (closing days, prices, parking), state the current fact briefly and name the page on the website where it is published. If the site doesn't say it yet, add it the same day.

Star-only reviews and short thank-yous don't need a reply; Google's tips say there is no need to thank every reviewer publicly.

Industry cautions: what never goes in a reply

Even when reviewers reveal that they used a service, a reply that says "when you came in for X" means the business has published that person's information. In healthcare and legal work, use a pattern that doesn't even confirm the person was a patient or client.

IndustryKeep out of repliesWhy / example
Healthcare (clinics, dentists, pharmacies)Whether the person is a patient, visit dates, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, insuranceHHS settled with a New Jersey psychiatric practice for $30,000 (2023-06-05) after a reply to a negative review included diagnosis and treatment details, and three other patients' information appeared in replies. A California dental practice paid $23,000 (2022-12-14) over patient information in replies to online reviews. HHS: "This is a clear NO."
Legal and other professional servicesWhether someone consulted you, case details, the other partyLawyers and other professionals have confidentiality duties under their own rules; in Japan, the Penal Code (Article 134) covers lawyers as well as doctors and pharmacists
Beauty, wellness, physical therapyPromised results, the person's body or condition"Guaranteed results" is still an advertising claim when it appears in a reply. Discuss conditions in person
RestaurantsWhat the person ordered or who they were with; conclusions about foreign objects or illnessNeither admit nor deny before you have investigated. Ask them to contact you
HotelsStay dates, room numbers, companionsIt reveals when and with whom someone stayed
Online shopsOrder numbers, addresses, delivery detailsConfirm through direct contact
Real estateProperty addresses, deal terms, the other partyIt identifies the property or the parties

Sources: HHS, psychiatric practice (2023-06-05), HHS, dental practice (2022-12-14), Japanese Penal Code (e-Gov). Rules differ by country and profession; when a legal judgement is needed, ask the client's own counsel.

When AI writes the first draft

Google itself offers AI drafting through the Gemini web app, but only for a personal account managing a single verified profile, which doesn't fit an agency handling many clients. Whatever tool you use, a person should check these before anything is published:

  1. Facts: hours, prices, parking and booking match the client's current website and Business Profile. AI tools can repeat outdated details or invent plausible ones.
  2. Personal information: no confirmation of a visit, dates, order numbers or symptoms (see the table above).
  3. Promises: no refunds, free services or discounts the client hasn't agreed.
  4. Repetition: openings and sign-offs vary, and the reply mentions something specific from the review.
  5. Approval: 1–3 star reviews and anything about health, accidents or legal matters were approved by the client as agreed.

Before pasting reviews into any AI service, check how that service handles what you enter. Reviews of medical and legal clients often contain diagnoses or case details.

Don't stop at the reply: fix the site

If the same issue keeps coming up (no parking, unclear total price, hard to book), answering it in replies leaves the next customer just as confused. AI systems read both reviews and the website; when the site answers the question, there is less room for guesses based on reviews alone.

  1. Once a month, pull every topic that appeared twice or more in the log.
  2. Write the answer in the body of the relevant page (directions, pricing, booking) — the same page your replies point to.
  3. Add common questions to the FAQ. On WordPress with Nurevo AEO, enter the question and answer in Settings and place the Nurevo FAQ block (or the [nurevo_faq] shortcode) on the page: the questions are displayed there and published as FAQPage structured data on that page only. Page-specific questions go in the Nurevo AEO panel in the editor.
  4. Update the Business Profile (hours, attributes) the same day so it doesn't contradict the site.
  5. Mark the change in the log and include it in the monthly report.

FAQ

Does replying to reviews make AI more likely to recommend a business?

We found no published study showing that. In the public testing we could find, ChatGPT and Google AI Mode did not display owner replies directly. Treat replies as a way to show readers, people and AI systems, the current facts and how the business responds.

Do we need to reply to every review?

Google's reply tips say to reply as needed and that there is no need to thank every reviewer publicly. Prioritise complaints, reviews with factual errors and reviews that ask a question.

Can we sign replies with the agency's name?

Replies appear as a response from the owner under the business name. Sign them with the business name and a role; keep the agency's and staff members' names out. Record who wrote each reply in the log.

Is "Thanks for visiting our clinic" a safe reply?

No. It confirms the reviewer is a patient. U.S. providers have paid settlements over patient information in review replies. Use a pattern such as "We don't discuss individual visits here; please call our front desk."

Can we let AI post replies automatically?

Limit it to what the agreement allows, such as short thank-yous on 4–5 star reviews. Complaints, health, accident or legal matters, and all healthcare and legal clients need a person to check the draft and the client to approve it.

The client wants to argue with a bad review.

For factual errors, a short statement of the current fact and a link to the relevant page is enough. Arguing invites third parties to write about the exchange, and that coverage can end up in AI answers. Reply once and give a direct contact.

Why hasn't our reply appeared yet?

Google reviews replies against its content policy before publishing them. It usually takes under 10 minutes but can take up to about 30 days. A reply that is not approved can be edited and resubmitted.
