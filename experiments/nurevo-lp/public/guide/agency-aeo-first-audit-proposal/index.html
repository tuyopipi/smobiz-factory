The first AI search audit and proposal: what agencies should check before the meeting and what they can promise

Build the first AI search optimization proposal on three things: audit the site before the meeting and show the specific facts AI cannot read; commit to the work, the schedule, how often you report and how you measure; and do not promise mentions in AI answers or positions. In June 2026 Google revised its guide to hiring an SEO and now suggests that site owners check whether AEO and GEO advice follows Google's official guidance. Write the proposal expecting it to be read that way.

Written November 15, 2026 (JST). This is for web agencies, SEO firms, local SEO firms and WordPress care companies pitching AI search optimization to a client (a shop, a clinic, a small company) for the first time. How to run it as a monthly service is covered in offering AEO as a monthly service; this guide stays with the step before that: the first audit and the first proposal.

AI answers change from day to day and with the wording of the question. The steps here are for telling clients accurately where they stand and what the work covers. They do not guarantee that a business will appear in AI answers.

The 2026 baseline: clients can check your pitch against Google's guide

Google's "Do you need an SEO?" page (last updated June 5, 2026) now lists "Optimizing for generative AI" among the services SEOs offer, and has a new section on evaluating an SEO's recommendations and tools. It suggests checking whether advice about AI experiences (AEO or GEO) matches Google's guidance on optimizing for generative AI. The same page says: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google" (Google Search Central: Do you need an SEO?).

A companion page, "Google Search's guidance on using third-party SEO tools, services, and advice", names AEO and GEO tools among third-party services and says Google doesn't evaluate third-party services, that any predictions such tools make may not happen, and that using a tool doesn't guarantee success. Good advice, it says, either labels its claims as opinion based on data or experience, or cites official Google guidance (Google: third-party SEO tools, services, and advice).

In a proposal, that comes down to three rules.

  1. Keep what Google and other vendors document apart from your own experience and opinion, and link the official sources.
  2. Do not write "Google-approved", "guaranteed to appear in AI answers" or "will rank higher".
  3. Say what results you expect, by when, and how you will measure them. Google's guide suggests owners ask about results and timeframe, how success is measured, and whether all site changes will be shared (same page); answer those in the proposal before you are asked.

The pre-meeting audit: what to check and how to write it up

Using public information only, a first audit takes about 30 to 60 minutes. The goal is not a score. It is to show the facts AI cannot read from the site, in a way the client can verify with their own eyes.

Always add the date and the method, so the client can repeat the check later.

Google says a page needs to be indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet to appear in its AI features, and that SEO best practices apply to them as usual (Google: AI features and your website). With access to the client's Search Console you can also read impressions in Google's AI features in the generative AI performance report, available to all sites as of August 31, 2026 (Search Console Help).

Three cautions when you use AI answers in an audit

A single run tells you little. In a study by AI検索ドック (Stellagent) in October 2026 (30 questions, 3 AIs, 10 runs each), one run differed from the 10-run rate by 29.9 points on average, three runs by 16.5 and five runs by 10.7 (AI検索ドック, a study by a tool vendor). In the first meeting, avoid saying "you don't appear"; present it as "the result under these conditions today".

  1. Separate questions that include the business name from discovery questions with only the type and area. A named question will usually return the name; what matters there is whether the facts are right.
  2. Record the date, the AI, the number of runs and whether you were signed in, so you can repeat it under the same conditions next month.
  3. If a competitor shows up, do not use it against them. State the fact: "for this question, this company was cited".

There is research that AI summaries reduce clicks on regular results. Pew Research Center looked at the March 2025 browsing of 900 U.S. adults: when an AI summary appeared, a traditional result was clicked in 8% of visits, against 15% when none appeared, and a link inside the summary in 1% (Pew Research Center). It is a U.S. study; say that it may not carry over to another country or a particular site.

A first proposal does not need to be long. Fit it into seven parts so the client can pass it around internally and decide.

Checklist before you send the proposal

  1. The audit date, method and screenshots are kept.
  2. Indexing, robots.txt, structured data, llms.txt, conflicting facts and image-only information were checked.
  3. AI questions are split into named and discovery, with date, AI and number of runs recorded.
  4. Official facts have sources; your opinions are labeled as opinions.
  5. The scope is written with page names and URLs.
  6. The monthly measurement conditions (questions, runs, AIs) and the reporting date are written down.
  7. No "guaranteed", "top position" or "Google-approved" wording is left.
  8. It says you will tell the client before installing anything, and that paid plans are taken out with the client's consent.

FAQ

Should the first audit be free?

There is no rule. One split is to make the public-information audit (the table in this guide) a free sales document, and charge for a deeper audit with Search Console access or competitor research. Either way, writing down what you checked makes your audit easier to compare.

Can we install a plugin on the client's site to audit it?

Before you win the work, auditing from public information only is the safe choice. Install anything only after the client agrees. Nurevo's free check (/check) needs only the URL.

Can the proposal mention that a competitor appears in AI answers?

Yes, as a fact with the date, question and AI. Answers change, so instead of "you are losing to them", write "under these conditions, this company was cited". Avoid running the competitor down.

AEO, GEO, LLMO or AIO: which term should the proposal use?

Pick the one the client has heard of and pair it once at the start, for example "AI search optimization (also called AEO or GEO)". Google's guide refers to "AI experiences (sometimes called AEO or GEO)".

Can we offer a pay-for-results contract?

Because AI answers vary for the same question and no one can guarantee a mention or position, we advise against making AI appearances the condition for payment. Contract for completed work and for measuring and reporting under agreed conditions.

Does this work for sites not built on WordPress?

Yes. The audit items (indexing, robots.txt, structured data, llms.txt, conflicting facts) are the same on any site. Nurevo covers non-WordPress sites with a snippet and businesses without a website with a hosted page.

If we pitch this way, will the client appear in AI answers?

That cannot be guaranteed; AI answers change by day and wording. These steps are for telling the client accurately where they stand and what the work covers, so they can compare and decide.
