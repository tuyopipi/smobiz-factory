AI search for nurseries, daycares and preschools: getting AI to read your ages, hours, tours and enrolment route correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a nursery, daycare or preschool comes down to publishing the facts parents ask AI about — the ages you take, opening hours and extended care, occasional care, meals and allergy handling, how to book a visit, and where to apply — accurately, in the text of your pages and in structured data (schema.org's ChildCare or Preschool). The one fact that matters most for childcare is the enrolment route: where a place is allocated by the local authority rather than by you, say so plainly, so AI does not tell parents to "just apply to the nursery".

Written November 13, 2026 (JST). Facts about schema.org, Google, OpenAI and the Japanese examples were checked on the dates given in the text. How places are allocated and funded differs by country and by local authority; check the current rules where your setting is.

ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews answer questions like "a nursery near the station that takes babies under one", "daycare open until 7pm" or "a preschool I can visit this week" by combining setting websites, local authority pages and map listings. AI answers change from one question to the next and no tool or tactic can guarantee you appear. But settings whose facts are written out in text, and match their other listings, are more likely to be described correctly.

What parents ask AI, and what AI tends to get wrong

Parents' questions usually combine a place, a child's age and a time constraint, so the facts an answer needs are predictable. The table pairs common questions with what to write on your site and the mistake AI tends to make when it isn't there.

Common questionWhat to write on your siteTypical mistake when it's missing
Do you take babies?The age range you accept (e.g. from 8 weeks to age 5) and places per roomParents are sent to you for an age you don't take
How late can I collect?Opening hours, core sessions, extended-care hours and how to book themExtended-care closing time is quoted as normal opening hours
Saturdays and holidays?Whether you open on Saturdays and when; closures on Sundays, public holidays, year-endAI says you are open on a public holiday
How do I apply?Your type of setting and where applications go (local authority or you), and whenParents are told to apply to you when places are allocated by the council
Can I visit?Visit days and times, how to book, how long a visit takes"Visit any time" during a period when you don't take visits
Meals and allergies?Whether meals are cooked on site, which allergies you can cater for, how to discuss themA diet you can't provide is described as available
Occasional care?Whether you offer it, ages, booking, how fees are setOccasional care you don't offer is recommended

Write each of these in the text of the page, not only in an image or a PDF prospectus. If you publish a handbook as a PDF, copy these points into the page as well.

Spell out where to apply, by type of setting

In Japan, the route depends on the type of setting. Kumamoto City is a typical example: for licensed nurseries, the childcare part of certified centres and small-scale childcare, families apply through the ward office (the first-choice nursery also accepts forms) or online, and the city scores each application and the documents proving the need for childcare to allocate places. Kindergartens, the kindergarten part of certified centres and unlicensed childcare facilities take applications directly (Kumamoto City: nursery admissions for FY2026, in Japanese, last updated October 1, 2026, read November 13, 2026). Many other countries have similar splits between council-allocated and directly-enrolled places, so check your own authority's rules.

Asked "how do I get into X nursery?", AI often goes by the only clue on the nursery's site — a contact form — and tells parents to apply to the nursery. One plain sentence on your site prevents most of that.

Vacancies change daily. If you publish them, date them ("as of November 1, 2026"); if you can't keep them current, link to the authority's vacancy page instead. Stale vacancy information that AI keeps repeating is the worst outcome.

What an unlicensed facility should state

In Japan, an unlicensed childcare facility (ninkagai hoiku shisetsu) is one without a licence from the prefectural governor or the municipality, and must notify the prefecture under Article 59-2 of the Child Welfare Act. Kumamoto Prefecture explains that from October 2024, unlicensed facilities that do not meet the national supervision standards are no longer covered by the free early-childhood education and care scheme (Kumamoto Prefecture: unlicensed childcare facilities, in Japanese, updated July 8, 2026, read November 13, 2026).

When a parent asks AI "is this nursery covered by free childcare?" and your site doesn't say, AI can only give a general answer. Confirm your status with your local authority and state it in the text. Equally important: don't write "covered" if you haven't confirmed it.

Kumamoto Prefecture points parents to the national search site "Kokode Search" (childcare information disclosure system, in Japanese). Whatever your type, check that the name, address, phone number and hours listed on public search sites and your authority's list match your own site. Outside Japan, the equivalent is your regulator's or council's provider directory.

Structured data types: ChildCare and Preschool

schema.org has ChildCare for childcare settings and Preschool for preschools and kindergartens. Checking the type definitions generated from the schema.org vocabulary (schema-dts 2.1.0) on November 13, 2026: ChildCare is a subtype of LocalBusiness, while Preschool is a subtype of EducationalOrganization. EducationalOrganization is both an organization and a place (CivicStructure), so either type can carry an address, phone number and opening hours. Google recommends using the most specific LocalBusiness subtype you can (Google: LocalBusiness).

OptionSuitsCaveat
ChildCareNurseries, daycares, unlicensed and small-scale childcare, centres that are mainly childcareA LocalBusiness subtype, so it is read as a local business listing
PreschoolKindergartens and centres that are mainly educationNot under LocalBusiness, so it may be read less readily as a map-style business listing
Both ChildCare and PreschoolCertified centres offering both childcare and education placesNeeds a setup that can output more than one type; either way the facts must be in the page text

Never put children's names, faces or anything that identifies a child into structured data. What you want AI to read are facts about the setting, not about the children. If you use images, choose the building, garden or meals, with no identifiable children.

Facts worth publishing

  1. Your type of setting (licensed nursery, certified centre, kindergarten, small-scale, unlicensed, etc.) and the operator's name.
  2. The ages you accept and places per room — including ages you don't take.
  3. Opening hours, core and short sessions, extended-care hours and how to book, whether you open on Saturdays.
  4. Closures (Sundays, public holidays, year-end and so on).
  5. Where and when to apply, by type of place, and how to book a visit.
  6. Meals (cooked on site or not), which allergies you cater for, and how to discuss them.
  7. Occasional or extra care: whether you offer it, ages, booking.
  8. What to bring and drop-off arrangements (parking, bicycle space).
  9. Address, directions from the nearest station and phone number, written the same way on your site, your Google Business Profile and your authority's list.

Pre-publish checklist

  1. Your type of setting and where to apply are written in the page text (with a link to the authority's admissions page if places are allocated there).
  2. The ages you take — and don't take — are stated.
  3. Opening hours, extended care, Saturday care and closures are in text, not only in images.
  4. Any vacancy information is dated (or replaced with a link to the authority's page).
  5. Name, address, phone and hours match across your site, Google Business Profile, your authority's list and public search sites.
  6. Your structured data type is ChildCare or Preschool, not plain LocalBusiness.
  7. No child's personal information is in structured data or image descriptions.
  8. Anything about free or subsidised places is limited to what your authority has confirmed.
  9. llms.txt lists your overview, admissions, daily routine, visits, access and FAQ pages. What is llms.txt

Checking whether you show up in AI answers

Once it's in place, ask AI the questions parents would ask (place + age + time constraint, e.g. "a nursery near X Station that takes under-ones and has extended care until 7pm") without your setting's name, and see whether you're named or linked. Then ask by name — "Where do I apply for X Nursery? How late is extended care?" — and check the answer matches today's facts. If it's wrong, look at the page AI cited and correct that page or the outside listing. AI answers change each time, so record the same questions several times, spread over time. FAQ

Where should a nursery start with AI search optimization?

Write your type of setting and where to apply, the ages you take, opening hours and extended care, closures and how to book a visit in the text of your pages. Then publish the same facts in structured data and llms.txt, and make them match your Google Business Profile and your local authority's list.

Should a nursery and a kindergarten use different structured data types?

Yes. Nurseries, daycares and unlicensed childcare fit ChildCare (a LocalBusiness subtype); kindergartens fit Preschool (an EducationalOrganization subtype). A centre offering both can pick the one it mainly wants to be known as, or output both if its setup allows more than one type.

Should a licensed nursery publish vacancies on its site?

Only with a date, and only if you'll keep them current. Otherwise, a link to your authority's vacancy page plus one sentence saying where applications go is safer — AI won't keep repeating out-of-date vacancies.

Should an unlicensed facility say whether it's covered by free childcare?

Parents ask this often, so it's worth stating once you've confirmed it with your local authority. In Japan, Kumamoto Prefecture notes that since October 2024 unlicensed facilities that don't meet the national supervision standards are not covered. Don't state anything you haven't confirmed.

Can I use photos of the children in structured data?

Avoid it. What AI should read are facts about the setting. Use photos of the building, garden or meals with no identifiable children, and keep children's names and faces out of structured data and image descriptions.
