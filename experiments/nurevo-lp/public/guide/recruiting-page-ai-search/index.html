AI search for careers pages: how to get AI to read your pay, location, working pattern and hiring process correctly

AI search optimization for a careers page comes down to answering, on one page per job and with the conditions spelled out, the questions candidates now ask AI: what does this company pay, can I work remotely and will I be relocated, can I apply without experience, and how many interviews are there and how long does it take. Then you publish the same facts consistently in JobPosting structured data, on your company page and on the job boards you use, and you close filled roles promptly. In Japan, keeping job information accurate and current is a legal duty, and text written for AI is no exception.

Written November 14, 2026 (JST). Google's JobPosting documentation, the Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare pages on the Employment Security Act and the survey cited were checked that day. The legal examples come from Japan; check the rules where you hire.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your job facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your openings or that applications will rise.

What candidates ask AI

People who research jobs with AI ask longer questions, with more conditions, than they type into a job board. For example: "web designer jobs in [city] with at least three remote days a week", "what does [company] pay its sales staff, and how much overtime is there?", "construction site manager roles open to people with no experience", "how many interview rounds does [company] have, and how long does it take?"

For an AI to name your company in an answer like that, or to answer correctly about you, the conditions have to be written somewhere it can read them as text. If your careers page is a few lines about a friendly team plus photos, and pay and location appear only on job boards, AI will answer from old board listings or review sites instead.

A survey by TaTap, a Japanese company (October 2026, 334 workers and students aged 20 to 49), found that of the 247 who use generative AI, 18.6% would drop an employer or move it down their list when the information AI gave them about it was missing or wrong, and 40.5% would check the official site or social accounts again. Survey article (Japanese). It is one vendor's small survey, but it suggests many people check what AI tells them against your own site, which is a reason to keep exact facts there.

The facts to publish, and how to write them

Most top-ranking articles stop at "add more hiring content" and "write an FAQ". What decides whether AI can use your page is how concretely you write the conditions. Put these on each job's own page, as text:

The list is based on the conditions candidates ask AI about, and the items the top-ranking articles left out.

Job titleThe common title candidates search for (for example, "front-end engineer"), not an internal nameDo not rely on a made-up title such as "digital experience creator" alone
What the work isMain tasks, tools and technologies, a typical day, the teamAlso state how far the duties may change after hiring (see the legal section)
PayMonthly or annual range, bonuses and allowances, whether fixed overtime pay is included and how muchDo not make pay look higher than it is; say what the range depends on

Keep company-wide facts (legal name, address, what you do, headcount, year founded) on your company page and link to it from each job. Use the same name and address on the company page, careers pages, job boards and your Google Business Profile. When they differ, AI can confuse you with another company or repeat an old value.

The legal side: job information must be accurate and current

In Japan, the amended Employment Security Act, in force since October 1, 2022, made accurate display of job information mandatory. MHLW: the 2022 amendment (Japanese). A leaflet from the ministry's Hiroshima Labour Bureau tells employers not to show false or misleading information, and to keep job information accurate and up to date, for example by updating their recruiting website promptly when hiring ends or the terms change. Its examples of false information include listing a job under a company other than the one hiring, calling a part-time job a permanent full-time one, and showing higher wages than the job pays. Leaflet (PDF, Japanese)

Since April 1, 2024, employers in Japan must also state, when recruiting, the scope of possible changes to the duties, the scope of possible changes to the place of work, and, for fixed-term contracts, the criteria for renewal (including any cap on total length or number of renewals). MHLW: items added to what must be shown when recruiting (Japanese)

  1. Do not put pay or conditions in an FAQ or in structured data that differ from the job page.
  2. Do not headline an exceptional figure such as "up to ¥10 million" on its own; give the range and the conditions together.
  3. Remove closed roles from the page, the structured data and llms.txt.
  4. State the scope of changes to duties and location in the job description itself.

When you are unsure about a specific case, check the ministry's materials or ask your local labour bureau or public employment office (or the equivalent authority where you hire).

Structured data: required and recommended JobPosting properties

JobPosting is the schema.org type for a job opening. Google's documentation (updated September 8, 2026) lists these properties for the structured data its job features use. Google: Job posting (JobPosting) structured data

PropertyStatusHow to fill it in
titleRequiredThe job title only (no location, pay or symbols mixed in)
descriptionRequiredThe job description you show on the page: duties, conditions and so on
datePostedRequiredThe real date the job was posted
hiringOrganizationRequiredThe company that is hiring (the same name as on your company page)
jobLocationRequiredThe workplace address. For fully remote jobs, applicantLocationRequirements can take its place
validThroughRecommendedThe closing date. After it passes, the job is treated as closed
baseSalaryRecommendedThe actual base pay as provided by the employer, not an estimate. Use minValue and maxValue for a range and a unit such as MONTH or YEAR
employmentTypeRecommendedFULL_TIME, PART_TIME, CONTRACTOR and so on
jobLocationType and applicantLocationRequirementsRecommendedFor work-from-home jobs, use TELECOMMUTE and name at least one country where applicants can be
directApplyRecommendedWhether candidates can apply on that page without unnecessary extra steps
identifierRecommendedYour internal requisition number, for example

Google asks you not to add JobPosting to pages that list jobs, only to each job's own page that candidates can read. To close a filled role, set validThrough to a past date, delete the page so it returns 404 or 410, or remove the JobPosting markup. Google states that not acting on expired jobs in time may result in a manual action. For job URLs it recommends notifying it through the Indexing API rather than relying on sitemaps alone. In 2021 Google added directApply and introduced an editorial content policy for job pages (covering things such as obstructive content and excessive ads). Google Search Central blog, July 2021

JobPosting is markup for Google Search's job features. We found no public documentation on how ChatGPT or Perplexity use it. What works for every AI is having the same facts written as text on the page. Keep your structured data identical to what the page says.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Every job has its own page with title, duties, pay, location, working pattern, contract type, requirements, hiring process and how to apply, written as text.
  2. Pay is a range with conditions, and any fixed overtime pay is broken out.
  3. The scope of changes to duties and location, and renewal criteria for fixed-term roles, are stated.
  4. Remote terms (days a week, which roles) are spelled out.
  5. JobPosting is on job pages only, has every required property and matches the page.
  6. Closed roles are gone from the page, the JobPosting markup and llms.txt.
  7. Company name and address match across the company page, careers pages, job boards and Google Business Profile.
  8. Careers pages can be read without logging in, and robots.txt does not block AI search crawlers.
  9. llms.txt lists the careers overview (and the open roles). What llms.txt is

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the pages are in shape, write 5 to 10 questions a candidate might ask and put them to ChatGPT and Perplexity. Use both kinds: your name plus a condition ("what does [company] pay sales staff?") and, without your name, area plus role plus condition ("remote-friendly accounting jobs in [city]"). For the first kind, record whether the facts are right; for the second, whether your company or page appears.

AI answers change from one run to the next, so ask the same questions several times over a few weeks rather than judging one result. If an old salary or a closed role shows up, find where the old information lives, your pages or a job board, and fix it there. See how to measure AI visibility for the method in detail.

FAQ

Where should a careers page start with AI search?

Give each job its own page and write pay, location, working pattern, contract type and hiring process as text. Then match your company name and address with your company page and job boards, and decide how you will close filled roles. JobPosting markup can come after that.

If I add JobPosting, will my jobs show up in ChatGPT?

Not necessarily. JobPosting is markup for Google Search's job features, and we found no public documentation on how ChatGPT or Perplexity use it. What helps with every AI is having the same facts written on the page.

Can I add JobPosting to my job listing page?

Google asks you not to. Add it to each job's own page and link to those pages from the list.

What should I do with the page of a role that has closed?

Set validThrough to a past date, delete the page so it returns 404 or 410, or remove the JobPosting markup. In Japan, employers are also expected to update their recruiting site promptly when hiring ends. Take the page out of llms.txt too.

Can I leave pay out and say "to be discussed"?

That is your call, but without pay on your page, AI may answer from job boards or review sites. If you publish it, avoid making it look higher than it is and give the range with its conditions. In JobPosting, baseSalary must be the actual figure from the employer, not an estimate.

No. AI answers vary, and no tool or method can guarantee that you appear or rank. What you can do is make your job facts readable to AI and keep checking whether you appear and whether the facts are right.
