How ready are websites for AI search? A 6-item score across 15,838 web agency sites (November 2026)

We scored the home pages of 15,838 web design agencies' own websites in Japan and other countries on six items related to AI search (llms.txt, JSON-LD, an organization type, robots.txt, meta description, canonical). The 2,486 Japanese sites averaged 4.05 of 6 and 52.2% scored 5 or more, but only 10.4% had all six. For 94.9% of the sites scoring 5, the missing item was llms.txt.

Published 2026-11-15. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社). Nurevo sells AI search optimization (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic.

Key findings

  1. Across 2,486 Japanese web agency sites, the average was 4.05 of 6 items (median 5). 52.2% (1,297) scored 5 or more and 10.4% (258) had all six. Across 13,352 agency sites outside Japan: average 3.83, 58.2% at 5+, 21.8% at 6/6.
  2. Of the 1,039 Japanese sites scoring exactly 5, 986 (94.9%) were missing llms.txt. Half of all Japanese sites (50.0%, 1,244) had every item except possibly llms.txt.
  3. WordPress made a large difference: Japanese WordPress sites averaged 4.21, non-WordPress sites (191) 2.14. Outside Japan WordPress sites averaged 4.87 and non-WordPress sites 0.61 (1.09 after excluding the many HTTP 202 responses in that group).

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites — 3,352 in Japan (confirmed as agencies) and 20,335 in other countries, 23,687 in total. Not businesses in general.
  2. Reached: 15,838 home pages could be fetched (Japan 2,486, outside Japan 13,352). These are the base of this report.
  3. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  4. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt (HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters); (2) the home page has JSON-LD; (3) the parseable JSON-LD has a type containing Organization or LocalBusiness; (4) a robots.txt exists and none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot or OAI-SearchBot is disallowed from the home page (/) (longest match); (5) a meta description of 10+ characters; (6) a canonical link. The score is the number of items met, 0–6. It is a simple count made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  5. Item (4) scores 0 when there is no robots.txt. Without one, crawling is generally not restricted, but this count gives the point only when a robots.txt is present and can be confirmed not to block those bots.
  6. HTTP 202: 1,477 home pages outside Japan (11.1% of those reached) answered with status 202, and 1,435 of them scored 0. These may be bot-check pages rather than the real page, so we also show figures excluding them. No Japanese site returned 202.
  7. Country is the country in our own collection list. Groups under 30 sites are not shown (country rows only for countries with 100+ reached sites).

Results: score distribution

In both Japan and elsewhere 5 is the most common score. In Japan more than four in ten sites (41.4%) sit at 2–4, while only 1.6% score 0. Outside Japan 6/6 is about twice as common as in Japan (21.8% vs 10.4%), but 19.6% score 0, giving two peaks; most of those zeros were HTTP 202 responses (see Method).

Limitations

  1. The six items are a simple, equally weighted count made for this survey. They do not measure how much any item affects AI answers.
  2. Only the home page was examined; structured data and canonical links on inner pages were not. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted.
  3. Item (4) scores 0 without a robots.txt. A different rule would raise scores, especially for non-WordPress sites, which more often have no robots.txt.
  4. One fetch per site. Responses that may not be the real page, such as HTTP 202, are counted as reached (figures excluding 202 are shown alongside).
  5. For llms.txt we checked only existence, not quality. Types come from JSON-LD only; microdata is not counted.
  6. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Countries come from our collection list, not a random sample of each country's agencies.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. Japanese agency sites mostly have the basics — meta description, canonical, robots.txt — and the gaps are in llms.txt and an organization type (Organization or LocalBusiness). Both can be added with some extra work, so they are where we would look first. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

What are the six items?

Has an llms.txt; has JSON-LD; has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; has a robots.txt that does not block GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot or OAI-SearchBot; has a meta description; has a canonical link. The score is how many are met, 0–6. It is not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.

What is the average for Japanese agency sites?

4.05 of 6 across 2,486 sites (median 5). 52.2% scored 5 or more and 10.4% scored 6 (November 2026).

Which item is missing most often?

llms.txt. Only 13.4% of Japanese sites had one, and for 94.9% of the 1,039 Japanese sites scoring 5 it was the missing item.

Does a score of 6 mean a site will appear in AI answers?

No. The six items count basic preparation for AI systems to read a site; whether it appears in an answer also depends on the question and other sources.
