How to see AI traffic in GA4: telling ChatGPT and Perplexity visits apart, and what stays hidden

In GA4, start with the AI Assistant channel under Reports > Acquisition > Traffic acquisition. Google describes it as visits from sources like ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Copilot and Grok. For AI platforms that do not land there, such as Perplexity in some properties, check the source column, and if you need one clean row that also covers past dates, build a custom channel group that lists AI domains. Even then, GA4 cannot count everything: Google puts visits from AI Overviews and AI Mode in Organic Search, and visits that arrive without a referrer end up in Direct.

Written November 10, 2026. Checked that day against the Google Analytics help center and OpenAI's and Perplexity's own documentation. Screen names and rules can change, so check the current help pages too.

This guide is about visits: people who clicked a link in an AI answer and came to your site. Whether an AI named you or cited your site (your appearance rate) never reaches GA4 unless someone clicks. That is covered in how to measure AI visibility. AI answers vary from day to day, and nothing you do can guarantee more visits from AI.

Where AI visits land in GA4

Two people who both "came from AI" can end up in different places in GA4, depending on how they arrived. Knowing the difference first saves you from misreading the numbers.

Google's help page lists the services in the AI Assistant channel as examples ("sources like"). Check your own report's source column to see where Perplexity and Claude visits land for you.

For ChatGPT, OpenAI says it automatically adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to links from ChatGPT search results (OpenAI: publishers and developers FAQ). So even when the browser sends no referrer, the source is recorded as chatgpt.com as long as that parameter survives. If someone copies the URL and the parameter is lost, the visit becomes Direct.

Step 1: make sure GA4 is installed correctly (on WordPress)

  1. On WordPress, Google's own plugin, Site Kit by Google, is a simple way to connect GA4 (WordPress.org: Site Kit by Google). If your theme settings or another plugin already add the tag, keep only one. With two tags, every visit is counted twice.
  2. Open Reports > Realtime in GA4, visit your site on your phone, and check that the visit appears.
  3. If you use a caching plugin or a CDN, clear the cache after any change before you check.

Step 2: look at the built-in AI Assistant channel

  1. In GA4, open Reports > Acquisition > Traffic acquisition.
  2. Check that the first column is Session default channel group, and look for the AI Assistant row. If there is no row, no visits landed in that channel during the period.
  3. Use the + above the table to add Session source as a secondary dimension. That shows what is inside AI Assistant (chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com and so on).
  4. Then switch the first column to Session source and type perplexity or claude in the search box to see whether any AI traffic sits outside the AI Assistant row.

Step 3: build a custom channel group to catch what the default misses

If some AI traffic lands outside the built-in channel, or you want to compare with earlier periods, build your own channel group. According to Google, custom channel groups can be applied to your reports retroactively (Google Analytics Help: custom channel groups).

  1. Go to Admin > Data display > Channel groups.
  2. On the Default channel group, choose Copy to create new. Starting from a copy keeps all the existing channels, which is safer than starting from scratch.
  3. Name the group something like "Channels with AI split out", click Add new channel and call the channel "AI (all)".
  4. Set the condition to Source, matches regex, and paste the pattern from the next section.
  5. Use Reorder to move this channel above Organic Search and Referral. As Google's help explains, traffic goes into the first channel whose definition it matches, top to bottom, so a channel placed lower loses visits to Referral and others.
  6. After saving, pick your new group from the first-column selector in the Traffic acquisition report.

A standard GA4 property can have 2 custom channel groups (5 on GA4 360), each with up to 50 channels. Custom channel groups are not included in the BigQuery export (all from the same help page).

A ready-to-use regex, and what to watch when writing your own

You can use this pattern in the source condition. It lists the main AI service domains as of November 2026.

chatgpt\.com|chat\.openai\.com|(www\.)?perplexity\.ai|claude\.ai|gemini\.google\.com|copilot\.microsoft\.com|copilot\.com|chat\.deepseek\.com|grok\.com

GA4 regular expressions are full match by default (Google Analytics Help: regular expressions). Written as above, the pattern matches chatgpt.com itself but not some other domain that merely contains chatgpt.com.

  1. Before you paste it, look at the source values that actually appear in your report and add any missing domains, separated by |. AI service domains do change.
  2. openai.com is deliberately left out. Visits from its developer documentation and other pages that are not AI answers would mix in.
  3. Broad patterns such as "contains ai" or "contains gpt" can catch sites that have nothing to do with AI. Listing domains is safer.
  4. Write a dot as \. because a bare . means "any single character".

Step 4: see which pages AI visitors land on, and what they do

With AI traffic, where people land and whether they go on to contact or book you tells you more than the visit count. It shows which parts of your site AI is using.

  1. Open Explore > Free form. Add Session source and Landing page as dimensions, and Sessions and Key events as metrics.
  2. Add a filter: Session source, matches regex, with the pattern above. Now only AI visits remain.
  3. The pages with the most landings are a clue to what AI takes from your site. Check first that the facts on them, such as prices, opening hours and service area, are current.
  4. If enquiries, bookings or purchases are not set up as key events yet, set them up now. Counts for the period before they were set up cannot be produced later.

AI answers are often read to the end without anyone clicking a link. A small AI row in GA4 does not mean you are absent from AI answers, and a growing AI row alone does not prove that AI rates you more highly. Tracking clicks (GA4) and appearances (a record of answers) separately is the reliable approach.

If you see zero AI visits, check these first

  1. Is robots.txt blocking AI search crawlers? If it is, AI cannot read your pages or cite them. Security plugins and CDN bot protection sometimes block them without you meaning to.
  2. Is the period too short? Small sites often get only a handful of AI visits a month. Look at around three months.
  3. Is AI traffic sitting in Referral or another channel? Check the source column.
  4. Is the GA4 tag on every page (Step 1)?

FAQ

There is no AI Assistant row in my GA4. Do I need to set something up?

No. It is a built-in channel. If the row is missing, no visits landed in it during the period. Also check the source column for AI domains sitting in other channels.

Where do Perplexity visits go?

Perplexity is not among the examples Google gives for the AI Assistant channel, and its visits can land in Referral. Look for perplexity.ai under Session source, or use a custom channel group to collect it in your AI row.

Can I count visits from Google's AI Overviews on their own?

Not in GA4. Google includes visits from AI Overviews and AI Mode in Organic Search. Appearances in Google's AI features are counted together with ordinary search in the Search Console performance report.

Can I count people who came from the ChatGPT app?

OpenAI says links from ChatGPT search results carry utm_source=chatgpt.com, so if that survives, the visit is counted as chatgpt.com. If neither a referrer nor the parameter survives, GA4 records the visit as Direct and it cannot be told apart.

Will a custom channel group change my existing reports?

No. The default channel group stays as it is; your group is only used when you select it in a report. It can also be applied to past periods.

Is there a way to get more visits from AI?

No method can guarantee it. What you can do is let AI search crawlers read your pages, state facts such as prices, hours and service area clearly, and make them easy to pick up with structured data and llms.txt. Then record GA4's AI rows and your appearance rate separately, the same way every month.
