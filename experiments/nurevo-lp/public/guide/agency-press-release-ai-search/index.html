Writing Press Releases AI Search Can Read: Structure, the Original on the Client's Site, and an Agency Service Menu

AI does cite press releases. A study of about 82,000 AI answers found that 15.05% cited at least one release, and six in ten of those citations pointed at the distribution service's page, not the company's site. To make a release usable, put who, what, when and the key number in the first three lines, give every figure its date and how it was counted, and publish the same text as an original on the client's own site. Do not engineer the links inside a release for rankings: Google's spam policies name that as link spam.

Written 2026-11-25 (JST). The figures come from Optyino.ai's July 2026 release (a Japanese study) and the public reports of Muck Rack and Meltwater; the link rules come from Google's spam policies (last updated 2026-11-24). All were read on the same day. Some examples refer to Japanese services such as PR TIMES; elsewhere the same role is played by newswires such as Business Wire, PR Newswire or GlobeNewswire.

This guide is for web agencies and consultancies that write or distribute press releases for clients. A better release does not guarantee that AI will mention the client. AI answers change day to day, and no AI company publishes how it picks sources. What follows is how to get accurate facts to AI and journalists, and how to explain that work as a monthly service.

Together they support only this much: (1) AI does cite releases, (2) how often depends heavily on the AI and the subject, and (3) releases remain a small share of all cited URLs. "Publish a release and you'll appear in AI" is not a conclusion they support.

Which URL gets cited

In the Optyino.ai study, 60.5% of cited release URLs were PR TIMES pages. What AI lists as the source is usually the distribution service's page, not the client's site. Links placed inside the answer text appeared in only 0.63% of answers; almost all citations sat in the source list under the answer.

That makes two pieces of work matter for an agency.

  1. Make the distributed page stand on its own: AI reads that one page. Put the legal company name, location, date and figures inside the release itself.
  2. Publish an original on the client's site: distributed copies can be hard to correct later. An original that you can update with corrections or price changes, linked from the foot of the release, gives AI and journalists somewhere to check what is true now.

A structure both AI and journalists can use

The advice in the top-ranking guides comes down to the inverted pyramid (most important first) and subheadings that answer questions. Here it is as a single release outline. It also works as the intake form when you collect material from the client.

[Headline] (Company) launches (what) in (where), from (date) — add a number if there is one
[Summary, three lines]
- What is new (one sentence)
- Who it helps (one sentence)
- Numbers, dates, place (one sentence)
[Lead] (CITY, date) — (Legal company name), based in (city), ...
[Body] One fact per subheading. Each number with its date and how it was counted, e.g. "(as of 31 October 2026, internal count)"
[FAQ] Three to five questions a journalist or reader would ask; two or three sentences each
[Quote] Named person and title, saying only what they can stand behind
[About] Legal name, address, founded, what the company does, URL (same wording as the About page)
[Original] URL of this release on the company's own site / media contact

Match the company details character for character with the client's About page and Google Business Profile. Inconsistent names or addresses can make AI treat them as different companies. See company profile pages for AI search.

Survey releases: put the method in the text

Survey figures are the kind of material AI answers quote, but a figure without its method spreads in a distorted form once quoted. Put the period, method, population, sample size, questions and how answers were tallied in the body.

The Optyino.ai release is itself a good example: alongside the period, number and type of prompts and number of answers, it states its limits — automatic classification, API answers that may differ from what users see, figures that change with model updates. Stating limits does not weaken a client's survey; it is what lets it be quoted correctly.

What not to do: engineered links and mass production

  1. Don't engineer release links for rankings: Google's spam policies list, as an example of link spam, links with optimized anchor text in articles, guest posts or press releases distributed on other sites (Google: spam policies). The press release example was added in 2013 (Search Engine Land). Write links plainly: "our website", "the original of this release".
  2. Google says paid links are not a policy violation when they carry rel="nofollow" or rel="sponsored". Check how the distribution service handles links.
  3. Don't publish the same release reworded many times: Google treats generating many pages mainly to manipulate rankings as scaled content abuse. One release when a fact changes protects the client's credibility better than a release every month with nothing new in it.
  4. Don't use superlatives you can't support: "No. 1" and "industry first" only when the basis is in the text. Advertising rules apply too.
  5. Don't add text written only for AI: hidden text or unrelated keyword lists harm readers and journalists.

Publishing the original on the client's WordPress site

  1. Create a "Press releases" category for posts with a short index URL such as /news/press/. Publish the same text on the same day it is distributed.
  2. Put the three-line summary into the excerpt field. Nurevo AEO (a free WordPress plugin), on sites with no SEO plugin, uses the excerpt as the description in the post's structured data (BlogPosting), or the opening words when there is no excerpt. The headline, published and modified dates, author and featured image are included, and the publisher points at the business node.
  3. Where Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO or Slim SEO is active, the article structured data stays exactly as that plugin publishes it. From 0.9.1, Nurevo AEO also works alongside SEO SIMPLE PACK and VK All in One Expansion Unit without duplicating structured data.
  4. When something changes, edit the text and add a dated note at the top: "Corrected 3 December 2026: the price is now ...". Ask the distribution service to correct its copy too, so both match.
  5. Nurevo AEO's llms.txt lists up to the 30 most recently modified published pages and posts. On sites with many releases, older ones drop off; to keep the index page listed, add a line with the webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines filter (example below). Any post can be left out of llms.txt from its own settings.
  6. Then check /llms.txt and the post's source (application/ld+json) to confirm the excerpt and dates match the text. Get it on WordPress.org

How to check results

  1. Pick three to five questions that fit the release (for example "Which companies started (service) in (area)?" or "How much is (company)'s (service)?"). Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode the day before distribution and again two to four weeks later.
  2. Record, with the date, whether the company appeared, whether the facts were right, and whether the source was the distributed copy, the original or a third-party article.
  3. If the facts are old or wrong, correct the original and ask the service to correct its copy first. Keep the records in the way described in how to measure AI visibility.
  4. In Google Analytics 4, watch referrals from AI services to the original post; in Search Console, watch searches for the company and service names.
  5. Nurevo AEO's free AI check asks Perplexity three questions built from the business type and area once a month for each connected site. Pro (from USD 99 a month) measures continuously, currently on Perplexity, with ChatGPT once configured.

Checklist

  1. The first three lines say who, what, when and the key number
  2. Every number has a date and how it was counted
  3. Survey releases state period, method, population, sample size and limits
  4. Company details match the client's About page
  5. "Industry first" and "No. 1" appear only with the basis in the text
  6. Links are plain: the company site and the original, nothing engineered
  7. No near-identical releases published with different wording
  8. The original is on the client's site with the summary in the excerpt
  9. Corrections are dated on the original and requested from the service
  10. Answers and sources for fixed questions recorded before and two to four weeks after

FAQ

If we distribute a press release, will AI mention the company?

Not necessarily. In a study of about 82,000 answers, 15.05% cited a release, and the share varied widely by AI and subject. Getting accurate facts into a usable form is the part you can control.

Does AI cite the newswire page or the company's own site?

In that study 60.5% of release URLs were on PR TIMES, a distribution service. Make the distributed page complete on its own, keep a correctable original on the company site, and keep the two consistent.

Should a release include lots of links?

No. Google lists links with optimized anchor text in press releases distributed on other sites as an example of link spam. Plain links to the company site and the original are enough.

Is it worth adding a Q&A section to a release?

Short answers to the questions journalists and readers would ask give AI something it can use as part of an answer. No AI company has said it favours this, and the content still has to be factual.

Will publishing the same text on our own site count as duplicate content?

Hosting the original of your own announcement on your own site is not mass production aimed at rankings. What to avoid is publishing many reworded copies of the same release.

Do we need structured data?

It is not required. Published as a WordPress post, the release gets article structured data from Nurevo AEO or your SEO plugin. No AI company has said it uses release structured data.

How soon can we see results?

No AI company publishes how long it takes. Ask the same questions the day before and two to four weeks after, and record answers and sources. AI answers vary from day to day, so keep checking at a fixed interval, such as monthly.
