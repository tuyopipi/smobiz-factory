How to run a first AI search (AEO) audit and write the proposal: a guide for agencies

Run the first AI search audit in two parts: can AI read the site (a technical check), and how does the business show up in AI answers today (a sample from a fixed set of questions). Then write a proposal that covers four things: the problems found, the work to fix them, how you will check progress, and what you will not promise. AI answers change between days and runs, so promise the work and the reporting, not mentions or positions. That is what keeps the relationship clean later.

Written November 2, 2026. Google's guidance was checked that day in its own documentation (Google Search Central and Search Console Help). It can change, so check the current pages before you pitch.

This guide covers the step before the sale. For the scope, monthly work, report and pricing of the service itself, see how agencies can offer AI search optimization monthly.

Split the audit in two: can AI read it, and how does it show up

Conversations about AI search often start with "I asked ChatGPT and we weren't there". That is a single run, and AI answers vary for the same question. More importantly, the fix depends on why the business is missing: AI cannot read the site, or it can read it but does not pick it. The first audit separates the two.

In the proposal, treat A as "work to fix" and B as "how we check and report". What you can and cannot promise then separates on its own.
PartWhat you checkHow fast it can change
A. Can AI read the site? (technical)AI crawler access, structured data, consistency with visible text, llms.txt, indexing statusMostly fixable on the site itself
B. How does it show up? (answers)Mentions and citations for a fixed set of questions, and whether AI gets the business's facts rightCannot be changed on demand. Keep checking

The first audit in seven steps

  1. 1. Interview: ask for the business type, service area, main services or products, and three to five questions the client thinks prospects ask AI. Questions in the client's own words make the proposal about their business, not about AI in general.
  2. 2. URL check: use a no-sign-up tool such as the free check to look at structured data, key business facts, body text, llms.txt and consistency. Note what is red or yellow rather than the headline score.
  3. 3. AI crawler access: open /robots.txt and make sure the crawlers AI search uses are not blocked. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI: overview of crawlers), and Perplexity recommends allowing PerplexityBot (Perplexity: crawlers). Whether to allow training crawlers such as GPTBot is a separate decision, and it is the client's.
  4. 4. Structured data: use the Rich Results Test or the Schema Markup Validator to see whether the business name, address, phone, opening hours and type are published, and whether the SEO plugin and theme publish them twice. Adding local business schema to your SEO plugin
  5. 5. Consistency: compare the structured data, the home page, the about page and the Google Business Profile for opening hours, prices and address. This is where most outdated AI answers start.
  6. 6. Ask the fixed questions: put the questions from step 1 to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Mode and so on, and record whether the business is mentioned or cited, a summary of the answer, and the conditions: date and time, signed in or not, location. Write down from the start that one run is not a verdict. How to measure AI visibility
  7. 7. Search Console (if you have access): the Generative AI performance report shows impressions of links to the site in AI Overviews and AI Mode. It shows no clicks or positions, and it may not appear at all for sites with too few such impressions (Search Console Help).

Which questions to use: mix three kinds

For a first audit, a mix of question types gives you more to work with than a long list of similar ones. We suggest three kinds.

  1. Category and area: "Which physio clinics in Leeds are open on Saturdays?" No business name. This is close to how prospects ask, and a missing name here does not by itself mean AI cannot read the site.
  2. Compare and choose: "How do I choose a web design agency for a small business?" Look at which sites get cited. That points to the pages worth building: comparison tables, pricing pages, FAQs.
  3. Fact check by name: "What are [business name]'s Saturday opening hours?" If AI gets the hours or prices wrong here, the fix is concrete, which makes it the finding clients understand most readily.

Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Search with a snippet to appear in its generative AI features, with no further special requirements (Google: optimizing for generative AI features). The same page says Google Search ignores files such as llms.txt and that there is no special schema.org markup for AI. Ordering your priorities to match this official guidance makes them easier to explain to the client.

Proposal outline (template)

  1. 1. Summary (one page): in three lines, the number of technical problems from part A and what the questions in part B showed. Do not lead with a big score.
  2. 2. Method and conditions: the tools, the questions, the dates and conditions. State that AI answers are a sample and vary between days and runs.
  3. 3. Problems and the work to fix them: one row per problem, as in the table above: problem, meaning, work, priority.
  4. 4. Scope and fees: separate the one-off setup from the monthly upkeep and checks. Show tool subscriptions the client holds in their own name apart from your own fees.
  5. 5. How you will check and report: what you check every month (or quarter) and what goes in the report. Example monthly report items
  6. 6. What we do not promise: a mention in AI, a position in AI, more enquiries. Give the reason in one sentence.
  7. 7. What we need from you: current prices, opening hours, common questions and their answers, photos. AI search work stalls without the client's facts.
  8. 8. Timeline: when setup will be finished and when the first report arrives.

Promises to avoid, and what to say instead

In its page on hiring an SEO, Google states that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google and advises finding someone else if an SEO guarantees first place (Google: do you need an SEO?). On third-party tools and advice, it says they cannot guarantee performance and lists services promising improvements for AI experiences among the claims to evaluate carefully (Google: third-party SEO tools and advice). Make sure your proposal still holds up if the client reads those pages later.

Tempting claimSay instead
You will appear in ChatGPTWe will make sure ChatGPT search can read the site, check a fixed set of questions every month and report what we find
We will get you to number one in AIAI answers have no positions and change for the same question. We record mentions and citations under the same conditions and show you the trend
Add llms.txt and AI will find youllms.txt helps some services. Google says Search does not use it, so we do not promise an effect

Google also suggests asking an SEO, "Will you share with me all the changes you make to my site?" Answer it in the proposal: commit to a monthly log of changes under "How you will check and report".

FAQ

Should the first audit be free?

There is no rule. A common split is to keep the quick part free (a URL check and a handful of questions) and charge for a deeper audit with more questions and competitor comparison. Saying up front where the free part ends makes the proposal easier.

The audit shows the business is not in AI answers. How do I explain it?

Explain that one run is not a verdict, and that the fix depends on whether AI cannot read the site or reads it but does not pick it. Then propose the two separately: the work that makes the site readable (fixable now) and ongoing checks of how it shows up (which cannot be changed on demand).

The client asks, "Isn't this just SEO?"

Google itself says optimizing for generative AI features is still SEO. Accept that most of the work overlaps, then explain what you add: keeping the facts AI reads (hours, prices, services) accurate, and checking regularly how the business shows up in AI answers. AEO vs SEO vs GEO

Can I put a forecast of AI traffic in the proposal?

We advise against forecasts without a basis. The Search Console Generative AI performance report shows impressions; clicks from AI features cannot be separated out. If you include numbers, use values you actually measured and state the conditions.

Does the same audit work for sites not built on WordPress?

Yes. robots.txt, structured data, consistency checks and question checks are the same on any site. Nurevo covers other sites with a snippet, and businesses without a website with a hosted page.
