AI search for acupuncture and massage clinics in Japan: following the 2025 advertising guideline to get AI to read your fees, treatments, risks and insurance conditions correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for an acupuncture, moxibustion or massage clinic in Japan (run by licensed hari-shi, kyu-shi or anma-massage-shiatsu practitioners) means making your own site able to answer what people ask AI, such as "Does insurance cover acupuncture?" or "How much is acupuncture near the station, and how many visits will I need?" The health ministry's advertising guideline of February 18, 2025 treats a clinic's own website as, in principle, not advertising, but asks clinics that offer self-pay treatment to put the treatment and its usual cost, the main risks and side effects, and a contact point on the site. Those are exactly the facts AI uses in its answers. Adding claims about results does not make AI more likely to pick you.

Written November 22, 2026 (JST). The legal and government information comes from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's advertising guideline (February 18, 2025) and from ministry and Tohoku Regional Bureau material on insurance (ryoyohi), as cited in the text. It describes Japanese rules; other countries regulate acupuncture and massage differently, so check the ones that apply to you. This article is not legal advice, and some prefectures give stricter guidance than the national guideline, so check wording with your local public health centre.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your clinic's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your clinic or rank it anywhere. For unlicensed bodywork shops see AI search for bodywork and relaxation salons, and for doctors' clinics see AI search for medical clinics.

What people ask AI about acupuncture and massage clinics

Questions about acupuncture and massage cluster around worries before the first visit: will it hurt, does insurance cover it, how many visits and how much? The difference from unlicensed bodywork is that these are licensed clinics, so many questions involve the insurance system (ryoyohi) and home-visit treatment. Those rules come with detailed conditions. If your site does not state them, AI answers from general explanations or leaves the conditions out.

What the February 18, 2025 advertising guideline changed

On February 18, 2025, the health ministry issued a guideline for prefectures and public health centres on advertising by anma-massage-shiatsu, acupuncture, moxibustion and judo-therapy clinics (notice 医政発0218第1号, known as the ahaki-jusei advertising guideline). It followed 11 meetings of an expert panel between May 2018 and July 2024 and a public consultation from August 30 to September 28, 2024 (72 comments). It is not a law but technical advice to local governments; public health centres guide clinics along its lines. Guideline text (copy on Arakawa City's site) · MHLW: guideline overview

The guideline treats something as advertising when three conditions are met: it aims to draw people in, it identifies a specific clinic, and the general public can come across it. A clinic's website is visited by people who type the URL or search for it, so it does not meet the third condition and is still, in principle, not treated as advertising. The following, however, can be.

From an AI-search point of view, this is the key point. AI reads your site, your SNS and listing sites. SNS and listing sites can only carry the limited advertising items, so detailed fees and treatment descriptions are hard to put there. The only place AI can find your detailed facts is, in practice, your own site. Writing them there is the most direct way to get AI to answer correctly.

What your site should carry (guideline VI-3), and the AI questions each item answers

The guideline lists three things that clinics offering self-pay treatment should put on their website. It also asks that they not be hidden in small print or behind hard-to-find links.

  1. A contact point that people can reach easily. Answers AI questions such as "How do I book?" and "What is the phone number?"
  2. The self-pay treatments and their usual cost: if there is no set fee, a range from the minimum to the maximum; for multi-visit tickets and prepaid cards, what they include and the contract terms. Answers "How much per visit?" and "Do you sell multi-visit tickets?"
  3. The main risks and side effects, together with the standard cost, treatment period and number of visits. Answers "Does it hurt? Any side effects?" and "How many visits will I need?"

Write these three as the guideline asks and you have the material for the questions AI is asked most. Many clinics post their prices only as an image of a price list. Text inside an image may not be read by AI, so put it in the page text too. For pricing pages in general, see AI search for pricing pages.

This rewording does not hurt you with AI either. A page that says what you do, what it costs, what the risks are and who should see a doctor first is easier for AI to use as a source than a page that asserts how well treatment works.

How to describe insurance (ryoyohi) for acupuncture, moxibustion and massage

"Does insurance cover acupuncture?" is a common AI question, and the conditions are many, so the safest course is to state them on your site. According to ministry material (April 30, 2026) and Tohoku Regional Bureau slides (2026), acupuncture and moxibustion ryoyohi covers six conditions (neuralgia, rheumatism, cervico-brachial syndrome, frozen shoulder, lumbago and after-effects of cervical sprain) plus other diseases whose main symptom is chronic pain, judged case by case by the insurer. MHLW: payment criteria and consent forms (April 30, 2026) · Tohoku Regional Bureau: briefing slides

The same material says ryoyohi needs the consent of the doctor actually treating the patient for that condition, given after an examination; consent cannot be issued through an online consultation. Consent lasts up to six months from the first treatment or re-consent (it changed from three months to six on October 1, 2018). While the patient receives insurance-covered medical care for the same condition (other than examination, tests and issuing the consent form), ryoyohi cannot be used. Massage ryoyohi covers cases where massage is medically needed for muscle paralysis, muscle atrophy, joint contracture and similar; massage for fatigue, relaxation or prevention is not covered.

At clinics using the proxy-receipt system (juryo-inin, from January 2019), patients pay only their share at the clinic and the clinic claims the rest from the insurer. MHLW: proxy-receipt handling. Clinics may not reduce or waive the patient's share, and must issue itemised statements and receipts free of charge. The home-visit fee applies only when there is an unavoidable reason the patient cannot attend, such as suddenly being unable to walk.

On your site, state how your clinic handles this, along the lines below. The guideline asks that any advertising mention of ryoyohi show that a doctor's consent is needed, and gives a plain-language example along the lines of "With health insurance, you pay only part of the treatment cost." Wording such as "all insurance accepted", "traffic accidents handled", "workers' compensation handled" or "compulsory car insurance handled" is not allowed in advertising.

Example: "We handle acupuncture and moxibustion ryoyohi under the proxy-receipt system. It covers conditions such as neuralgia, rheumatism, cervico-brachial syndrome, frozen shoulder, lumbago and after-effects of cervical sprain, and needs a consent form from your treating doctor (valid for up to six months). It cannot be used while you receive insurance-covered hospital care for the same condition. Treatment for stiff shoulders or fatigue is self-pay."

Keep your clinic name and your SNS and listing-site wording consistent

The guideline also sets out which clinic names can be used in advertising. Names that include the type of practice, such as "[Name] Acupuncture Treatment Clinic" (○○鍼灸治療院) or "[Name] Hari-Kyu-in", are allowed; words such as "clinic" (クリニック), "medical", "medical office" (診療所), "rehab", "seitai" and "salon" are not, nor is a bare "treatment clinic" (治療院) without the type of practice. The items allowed in advertising are set by Article 7 of the act and a ministry notice: the practitioner's name and address, the type of practice, the clinic's name, phone number and location, treatment days and hours, and items such as treatment by appointment, weekend and evening treatment, home visits, parking, and that ryoyohi can be claimed (when the need for a doctor's consent is shown). Practitioners' skill, methods and career cannot be advertised (holding a national licence can be).

What matters for AI is that your name and basic details are the same on your site, Google Business Profile, listing sites and SNS. If the site says "[Name] Acupuncture", a listing site "[Name] Treatment Clinic" and SNS "[Name] Acupuncture Clinic", AI may not recognise them as one clinic. Pick one name that is allowed in advertising and use it everywhere. Keep detailed fees and explanations on your site, and use SNS and listing sites only for the permitted items and a link to the site.

Structured data: choosing a type, with an example

schema.org has no type just for acupuncture clinics. In schema-dts 2.1.0, MedicalBusiness is described as a business for medical purposes, with businesses run by health professionals as an example. This article considers it the closest fit for clinics run by nationally licensed practitioners (checked November 22, 2026). Do not use MedicalClinic or Physician, which suggest a doctor's practice. Physiotherapy is described as the treatment of disease, injury or deformity by physical methods such as massage, which reads as a treatment claim, so this article does not recommend it. Clinics centred on cosmetic acupuncture or relaxation that do not handle ryoyohi can use HealthAndBeautyBusiness.

The type is not a name shown on screen, but in the description, write that you are a clinic of nationally licensed practitioners and how you handle ryoyohi, in a way that does not suggest a medical institution. Publish each treatment as its own Service, with what it involves and what can happen afterwards. Registered in Nurevo AEO, it looks like this (the values are examples):

Checklist before you publish

  1. Each self-pay treatment has its content and price including tax (or a minimum-to-maximum range) in text.
  2. The main risks and side effects, and the usual number of visits and period, are stated.
  3. What multi-visit tickets and prepaid cards include, and their terms, are stated.
  4. Whether you handle ryoyohi, the qualifying conditions, the need for a doctor's consent and how it differs from self-pay are stated.
  5. No "cure", "root cause", "completely safe", "X% satisfaction" or "No. 1".
  6. No page of hand-picked praise and no retouched before-and-after photos.
  7. Each practitioner's licence is stated accurately (no private certificate presented as a national licence).
  8. The clinic name is the same on your site, Google Business Profile, listing sites and SNS, and does not use words such as "clinic" (クリニック).
  9. The structured data type is MedicalBusiness (or HealthAndBeautyBusiness), not MedicalClinic, Physician or Physiotherapy.
  10. llms.txt lists your treatment, fees, insurance, access and practitioner pages. What is llms.txt

Check whether AI shows you, and whether it is right

Once the pages are in order, ask AI what your patients would ask. Check whether you are named for local, condition-based questions such as "acupuncture near [station], Saturday" or "home-visit massage in [city]", and also ask with your clinic's name, such as "Does insurance apply?", "How much per visit?" and "How many visits?", noting whether the answers match your site. If AI says simply "insurance applies" without the conditions, look for wording without conditions still on your site or listing sites.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time. FAQ

Does advertising regulation apply to an acupuncture clinic's website in Japan?

Under the health ministry's advertising guideline of February 18, 2025, a clinic's website is, in principle, not treated as advertising. The guideline does list things the site should carry (self-pay treatments, their cost and risks) and things it should not (result claims, comparisons). SNS posts, search ads and pages on listing sites can be treated as advertising.

Where should an acupuncture clinic start with AI search?

Write the three things the guideline asks websites to carry (a contact point, self-pay treatments and their cost, main risks and side effects with the usual number of visits and period) and the conditions for insurance (ryoyohi) as text. Then publish the same facts as structured data and in llms.txt, and use the same clinic name as on your Google Business Profile and listing sites.

Can I put prices on my site?

The guideline asks clinics that offer self-pay treatment to show the usual cost on their website (a minimum-to-maximum range if there is no set fee). Avoid over-emphasising low prices. Where something counts as advertising, such as SNS or listing sites, the permitted items are limited by law and a ministry notice, so check with your public health centre.

Can I make pages for symptoms such as back pain?

Yes, if they state facts rather than claim results: for example, that lumbago is one of the conditions that can qualify for ryoyohi, that a doctor's consent is needed, what treatment involves and a guide to the number of visits, and when to see a doctor first. Leave out "cure" and "X% improvement".

Which structured data type should an acupuncture clinic use?

For clinics run by nationally licensed practitioners, this article recommends MedicalBusiness. MedicalClinic and Physician suggest a doctor's practice, and Physiotherapy is defined as treating disease, so avoid them. Clinics centred on cosmetic acupuncture or relaxation that do not handle ryoyohi can use HealthAndBeautyBusiness.
