AI search for nursing and care homes: how to get AI to read your fees, admission criteria and vacancies correctly

AI search optimization for a nursing or care home comes down to answering, in plain text with numbers and conditions, the questions families and care managers now ask AI: what it costs each month (what is included and what is extra), which care needs and medical care you accept, how many staff are on at night, and whether there is a vacancy and how to visit. Then you publish the same facts consistently in structured data, llms.txt and any official care directory you are listed in. Advertising rules for care homes still apply, so vague claims like "fully staffed" have no place in content written for AI either.

Written November 14, 2026 (JST). The Japanese prefectural, municipal and Consumer Affairs Agency documents, and schema.org's list of types, were checked that day. Examples of rules come from Japan; check the rules where you operate.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your home or rank it anywhere.

What families and care managers ask AI

The search for a care home is often done by a son or daughter before the future resident is involved. The questions they put to AI stack several conditions at once: "a care home in [city] that takes a mother with high care needs for under a set monthly budget", "a nursing home that accepts tube feeding", "a home that provides end-of-life care", "memory care with a vacancy now".

For an AI to name your home in an answer like that, it has to be able to read, as text, that you meet those conditions. A page that says only "from X per month" with no breakdown, admission criteria that say only "please ask us", or a vacancy notice with no date is hard to use when the question has conditions.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that normal SEO basics still apply. Google: AI features and your website. For a care home, the work is not a trick: it is writing down, accurately, the facts a family uses to decide.

The facts to publish, and how to write them

Most top-ranking articles stop at "publish your pricing, vacancies and tour booking". What decides whether AI can use them is how far you go. Details vary by type of home (nursing home, assisted living, residential care, memory care, day service), but this is a good baseline:

The list is based on the items Japan requires care providers to post online and the items its care-home advertising rules cover (both below).

In Japan, posting key information online has been mandatory since April 2025

Following Japan's 2024 revision of long-term care fees, every long-term care service provider has had to post its key information (重要事項) on a website since April 1, 2025, either on its own or its operator's website or in the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's care service information system. Tochigi Prefecture notice (PDF, Japanese)

The items include an outline of the operating rules, the staffing arrangement, partner medical institutions, fees and other information that helps people choose. A municipal training deck adds that staffing can be shown as numbers by job and by full-time or part-time status (names are not required), and lists the response to accidents, complaint handling and whether a third-party evaluation was carried out. The existing requirement to display the information at the office still applies. Toyoake City training deck (PDF, Japanese). The exact items depend on the standards for each type of service, so check with the authority that designated you.

From an AI search point of view, this key information is exactly what families want to know. Rather than only uploading a PDF, put the essentials (fees, staffing, partner medical institutions) on an HTML page as well and link to the PDF from it. That is easier for both AI and people to read.

Advertising rules for care homes: what not to write for AI

In Japan, fee-charging homes for the elderly (有料老人ホーム) are covered by a notice under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, "Misleading representations concerning fee-charging homes for the elderly", and its operating guidelines (listed on the Consumer Affairs Agency's page of notices and guidelines, Japanese). The guidelines include points such as these. Operating guidelines, copy posted by Wakayama Prefecture (PDF, Japanese)

  1. Describing care staff as "many" or "ample" without specific figures is a problem. Show numbers such as the full-time-equivalent count.
  2. For nights, show the night-time hours the home has set and the minimum number of care and nursing staff on duty in that period.
  3. Fees such as a "management fee" or "service fee" must say what they are used for, and the breakdown must match how they are actually used.
  4. Services not covered by long-term care insurance must each show their content, price and how they are charged.
  5. Required information must be placed near the claim it qualifies, in a form older readers can easily read.

Structured data, FAQ and llms.txt are your page content rewritten for machines. If you cannot say it on the page (an unsupported "best in the area", "excellent nursing care" with no numbers), do not say it in the places written for AI. Other countries have their own licensing and advertising rules, and in Japan the rules differ by type of home, so check with the authority responsible for you.

Structured data: there is no care-home type

schema.org has no type for nursing homes, care homes or assisted living (checked against schema.org's type list on November 14, 2026). There are types called Nursing and Geriatric under MedicalBusiness, but they describe a health profession and a branch of medicine, not a kind of home. Using them for your home risks being read as a hospital or clinic, so it is safer not to.

The safer choice is to describe the home as a LocalBusiness with its name, address, phone, visiting or enquiry hours and URL, link the type of home and services such as day care as services, and explain the details in the page text. In Google's local business structured data, name and address are required. Google: Local business structured data

Vacancy counts and detailed fee breakdowns change often and have no settled place in structured data, so the practical place for them is a table in the page text with an update date. Keep structured data to facts that rarely change: name, location, contact details and types of service.

Checklist before you publish

  1. The type of home is written as its formal category, not just "care home".
  2. The monthly fee is broken down, and costs outside it are shown with prices or "per time" amounts.
  3. Admission criteria are written for care level, dementia and each kind of medical care, as yes, yes with conditions, or no.
  4. Staffing is shown as full-time-equivalent numbers and the night-time minimum, not just words like "ample".
  5. Vacancy information has an update date.
  6. Required key information is posted on your site or the official directory, and its figures match your site.
  7. Name, address and phone in structured data match the page and your Google Business Profile, and the type is not a medical one such as Nursing or Hospital.
  8. llms.txt lists your overview, fees, admission criteria and visit pages. What is llms.txt?

FAQ

Where should a care home start with AI search?

Write your monthly fee breakdown and the costs outside it, the care needs and medical care you accept, your staffing and your vacancies (with an update date) as text on your pages. Then make your name, address and phone match across structured data and your Google Business Profile.

Which structured data type should a care home use?

schema.org has no care-home type, so use LocalBusiness and link the type of home and services such as day care as services. Nursing and Geriatric describe a profession and a branch of medicine, so they are not a good fit for the home itself.

We cannot update the vacancy count every day. Should we leave it out?

Showing a status such as "vacancy" or "waiting list" with the date it was last updated is already useful. Vacancy information with no date tends to be treated as stale, so choose a format you can keep current.

Can we write "fully staffed" to help AI pick us?

In Japan, the care-home advertising guidelines treat claims of many or ample staff without figures as a problem. Use numbers such as the full-time-equivalent count and the night-time minimum instead. Check the rules that apply where you operate.

If we are in an official care directory, do we still need our own site?

In Japan the official system is an accepted place to post the required key information. But your own site can explain fees, admission criteria and visits in far more detail, which is what families use to decide. Keep the values in both identical.
