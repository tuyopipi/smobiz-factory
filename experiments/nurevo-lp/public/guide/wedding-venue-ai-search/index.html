AI search for wedding venues: how to get AI to read your total price, capacity and vendor rules correctly

AI search optimization for a wedding venue comes down to answering, in plain text with numbers and conditions, the questions couples now ask AI: what a wedding for 60 guests really costs (and what pushes the first quote up), how many people each room holds, whether they can bring their own photographer or dress and what that costs, and when cancellation fees start after they sign. Then you publish the same facts consistently in structured data, llms.txt, your Google Business Profile and the wedding directories you list on. A headline "from" price on its own is as unhelpful to AI as it is to a couple with a budget and a guest list.

Written November 15, 2026 (JST). The Zexy trend survey, the Shiga Prefecture consumer page, The Knot's study release and schema.org's list of types were checked that day. Examples of consumer rules come from Japan; check the rules where you operate.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your venue or rank it anywhere.

What couples ask AI

More couples now start their venue search by giving AI a list of conditions. The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study of 10,474 US couples married in 2025 found that AI adoption nearly doubled in a year, to 36% of the engaged couples surveyed, used mainly for inspiration, early questions and drafting messages. The Knot Worldwide: 2026 Real Weddings Study. We found no comparable official figure for Japan, but the shape of the questions is the same.

Real questions stack location, guest count, budget, facilities and rules: "a venue near [station] with a chapel for 60 guests, about $30,000 all in", "somewhere we can have a family-only dinner for 20", "venues that let us bring our own caterer", "a garden venue with a rain plan". For an AI to name your venue in an answer like that, it has to be able to read, as text, that you meet those conditions.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that normal SEO basics still apply. Google: AI features and your website. For a wedding venue, the work is not a trick: it is writing down, accurately, the facts couples use to compare.

The facts to publish, and how to write them

Most top-ranking articles stop at "publish your pricing, capacity and amenities". What decides whether AI can use them is how far you go. Details vary by kind of venue (exclusive-use house, hotel, dedicated wedding venue, restaurant, venue partnered with a church or shrine), but this is a good baseline:

The list combines what top-ranking articles recommend with the points that most often cause consumer complaints: rising quotes, deposits and cancellation fees.

Average costs: cite the source, and keep them apart from your own prices

"How much does a wedding cost on average?" is a question AI gets constantly. In Japan, Recruit Bridal Souken's Zexy Wedding Trend Survey 2024 (a postal survey of 3,656 people who held a ceremony or reception between April 2023 and March 2024, weighted to a national estimate excluding Okinawa) found an average of 52.0 guests and an average total of 3,439,000 yen for ceremony and reception. Zexy Wedding Trend Survey 2024 (PDF, Japanese). In the US, The Knot's 2026 study put the average wedding at $34,000 with 117 guests. The Knot Worldwide

If you quote averages like these on your site, name the survey and year, and keep them clearly separate from your own figures ("an example total for 60 guests at our venue"). When a national average and your own price look mixed together, an AI may repeat the average as your price. Write your own figures for conditions close to a real quote, with what they include.

Quotes, deposits and cancellation fees: where consumer complaints start

Shiga Prefecture's consumer page on wedding venue cancellation fees describes a man who was told at a showcase event that signing that day meant a discount, paid a 100,000-yen deposit and was refused a refund, and a woman whose in-budget quote grew by more than 1,000,000 yen with add-ons and who was then charged a cancellation fee on top of the deposit. Shiga Prefecture: wedding venue cancellation fee trouble (Japanese)

The page advises taking the quote home to think it over, checking before paying whether the money is refundable whatever it is called (application fee, booking fee, deposit), and checking in the contract when it is formed and when cancellation fees start and how much they are. It also explains that, under Article 9(1)(i) of Japan's Consumer Contract Act, the part of a cancellation fee that exceeds the business's "average damage" may be void, decided case by case.

From an AI search point of view, these are exactly the questions couples put to AI. Avoid writing your site like this:

  1. Leading with the lowest price without saying for how many guests, what it includes and what usually gets added.
  2. Putting the conditions of a showcase-day offer (sign today only, minimum spend) on another page or in small print.
  3. Answering "is the deposit refundable?" and "when do cancellation fees start?" only with "contact us for details".
  4. Saying outside vendors "may incur a fee" without naming the items and amounts.

Structured data, FAQ and llms.txt are your page content rewritten for machines. If you cannot say it on the page (an unsupported "number one in the area", "no outside-vendor fees" without the conditions), do not say it in the places written for AI. For contract and advertising rules, check with a professional or your local consumer authority.

Structured data: there is no wedding-venue type

schema.org has no type for wedding venues (checked against schema.org's type list on November 15, 2026). The nearest is EventVenue, but it sits under Place alongside civic structures, not under LocalBusiness. Google's local business structured data expects a LocalBusiness type, with name and address required. Google: Local business structured data

The safer choice is to describe the venue as a LocalBusiness with its name, address, phone, tour or enquiry hours and URL, link ceremonies, receptions and small dinners as services, and explain the details in the page text. If you also want structured data for the capacity of each room, you can describe each room as an EventVenue with maximumAttendeeCapacity (the total number of people who may attend). Which type you choose matters less than the values matching your page text.

Price examples and open dates change often, so the practical place for them is a table in the page text with an update date. Keep structured data to facts that rarely change: name, location, contact details, rooms and the kinds of package you offer.

Checklist before you publish

  1. The kind of venue and the ceremony styles you host are stated specifically.
  2. Capacity is given for each room, seated and standing, with your minimum and any small-wedding package.
  3. Price examples by guest count say whether tax and service are included and what is covered.
  4. Common add-ons and their rough amounts are listed.
  5. Outside vendors are covered item by item, with whether they are allowed and the fee and its unit.
  6. Deposit refunds, when the contract is formed, and when and how much cancellation fees are, are shown in a table.
  7. Any national average has its source and year and is kept apart from your own prices.
  8. Name, address and phone in structured data match the page, your Google Business Profile and your directory listings.
  9. llms.txt lists your overview, packages, pricing, outside-vendor and tour booking pages. What is llms.txt?

FAQ

Where should a wedding venue start with AI search?

Write capacity per room, price examples by guest count (with what is included and how tax and service are handled), common add-ons, outside-vendor rules and fees, and your deposit and cancellation terms as text and tables on your pages. Then make your name, address and phone match across structured data and your Google Business Profile.

Which structured data type should a wedding venue use?

schema.org has no wedding-venue type, so publish the venue as a LocalBusiness and link your packages as services. To show the capacity of each room, you can describe rooms as EventVenue with maximumAttendeeCapacity. EventVenue is a type of place, not a LocalBusiness.

Does showing a low "from" price help AI pick us?

A starting price alone does not answer questions with a guest count or budget, and it can be repeated as if it were your typical total. Show example totals by guest count along with what they include and what usually gets added.

Won't publishing our cancellation fees put couples off?

Whether the deposit is refundable and when cancellation fees start are questions couples put to AI, and among the most common causes of consumer complaints. A clear table of when and how much makes you easier to explain when you are compared.

If we are on wedding directories, do we still need our own site?

Directory listings can be one of the sources AI draws on. But your own site can state outside-vendor rules, cancellation tables and per-room details more precisely. Keep the values in both identical.
