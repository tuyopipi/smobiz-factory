AI search for private investigators: getting your registration, fee estimates, the cases you refuse and your contract steps read correctly

For a private investigation office, AI search optimization mostly means answering, in the text of your own pages, what a prospective client asks an AI assistant: "Is this investigator properly registered?", "How much does a surveillance case cost?", "What paperwork will I get?" and "Can I stop the job halfway?". In Japan, since April 2024 an investigator must also display a self-made registration sign (標識) on its website unless it has five or fewer regular employees, and how you present that sign and your fees is much of what AI and people use to judge whether you can be trusted. Then keep the same facts consistent across structured data, llms.txt and your Google Business Profile.

This guide is written for investigation offices in Japan, where Nurevo is based; the legal sections describe Japanese rules. If you work elsewhere, the same pattern applies to whatever licence or registration your jurisdiction requires. AI answers change with the day and the wording, and nothing can guarantee you will appear. The aim is that AI does not get your facts wrong and has what it needs to recommend you. This is not legal advice; check details with your local police or a lawyer.

What prospective clients ask AI

People looking for an investigator are often dealing with something they cannot easily talk about, so some ask an AI assistant before they search. Their questions combine the type of case, the area, money and trust: "An investigator in [city] for an infidelity case. What does it usually cost, and can the evidence be used in court?", "My relative has gone missing. Can I hire an investigator after reporting it to the police?", "How do I spot a dodgy investigator?"

Google says there are no extra requirements or special structured data for AI Overviews and AI Mode: the usual SEO basics, structured data that matches the page and an up-to-date Business Profile are what matter. Show your registration sign on the website

The amended Detective Business Act came into force in Japan on 1 April 2024. The notification certificate the Public Safety Commission used to issue was abolished. Instead, investigators make their own sign (標識) and display it in a visible place at the office and on any website they manage. It is printed black on white A4 and carries the receipt number of the business's start notification (the old eight-digit certificate number, renamed). Kanagawa Prefectural Police

On the website, the examples given are a reduced image on the top page, or a link such as "View our sign" that opens it as a PDF. Businesses with five or fewer regular employees, and businesses with no website, are exempt from the website duty. Company officers and sole proprietors themselves do not count as employees, but sales and office staff do, not just investigators. Having someone else run the site does not exempt you. Nagano Prefectural Police, Ibaraki Prefectural Police

Even if you are exempt, publishing the sign gives AI something concrete when it is asked whether you are registered. Present it like this:

What to publishExampleNotes
The signSmall image at the foot of the top page, or a link to a PDFAI reads images and PDFs poorly. Repeat the same details as text on your About page
Authority and receipt numberNotified to [Prefecture] Public Safety Commission, receipt no. ********Drop the old "certificate number" wording
OfficesMain office address (building and floor), other officesEach office is notified separately. Keep addresses identical on site and Business Profile
Legal name and representative[Company] (trading as [Name] Investigations), representative [Name]The trading name used in ads is part of the notification. Explain how the names relate

When notified details change you must file a change notification, and if the sign's details change you must remake the sign. Updating the sign and the About page text on the same day keeps old numbers and addresses out of AI answers.

Publish fees as an estimate for a whole case

Japan's Act requires an investigator to explain in writing, before the contract, an approximate amount of the fee and other money the client must pay, and when it is due. Detective Business Act, Article 8 (enacted text). Apply the same idea on your website: rather than an hourly rate alone, show what a typical case comes to under stated conditions, so AI can answer with conditions instead of just "from X".

Offices differ in whether they charge by the hour, sell packages of hours or include a result-based fee. Whatever your model, put these items in a table on your fees page:

ItemExampleNotes
How fees are setTwo investigators, per hour: X (tax incl.) / X-hour package: X (tax incl.)Minimum hours, how many investigators
ExpensesTravel, vehicle and lodging at cost / includedIncluded or extra, any cap or prior approval
ReportPhoto report included / XCost of video or data hand-over
Result-based feeIf you have one, define what counts as a resultAn undefined "success fee" invites disputes
Worked estimatesWeekday evenings, 4 hours × 3 days, two investigators, one car: total X (tax incl.)Two or three common cases, expenses included
Payment timingX% on signing, balance after the reportWhat is paid up front and how an early stop is settled

In Japan, prices shown to consumers in advance must include consumption tax, whatever the medium. National Tax Agency: total price display (Japanese)

Avoid claims such as "cheapest in the industry" or "evidence guaranteed" unless you can back them up. AI may quote such phrases verbatim, and if they turn out to be untrue they become a source of disputes with clients.

State what you investigate, and what you won't take on

Under Japan's Act, investigation work means collecting, at a client's request, information on a specific person's whereabouts or conduct through field work such as interviews, tailing and stake-outs, and reporting the results. Detective Business Act, Article 2. On your site, give each case type you handle (infidelity, locating people, background on conduct, corporate requests and so on) its own page, with what you can find out, what you cannot, what information you need and a typical duration.

The Act requires you to obtain a written statement from the client that the results will not be used for crime, unlawful discrimination or other illegal acts (Article 7), and not to carry out work you know will be used for such purposes (Article 9). Clients and AI both want to know how far you will go, so state your intake policy on the page: the kinds of request you refuse, and that you ask clients to sign this statement.

  1. A page per case type (what you can and cannot find out, typical duration).
  2. Your policy on refusing requests (for example, any request you learn is for an illegal or discriminatory purpose).
  3. That clients sign a statement, when contracting, not to use the results for illegal purposes.
  4. Whether you ever subcontract work (under the Act, only to other registered investigators).

Explain the steps from enquiry to report, and the documents

Japan's Act sets out what must be explained in writing before a contract (Article 8(1)) and what the contract document handed over afterwards must contain (Article 8(2)). Using those items as the headings of your "How it works" page tells clients and AI what they will receive, and when.

StepWhat the law requires (summary)What to write on your site
Enquiry and estimate—How consultations work, length, whether free, and that a consultation alone is fine

Client's statementObtain a written statement that results won't be used illegallyThat clients sign this when contracting
Contract documentPerson who handled the contract and date, scope, period and methods, how and when results are reported, subcontracting, amount and payment terms, cancellation, disposal of materialsThat clients get a copy, and whether it can be electronic
Investigation and report—How you give progress updates, report format (photos, video, data)
Disposal of materialsTake measures to prevent misuse of materialsWhen and how you dispose of materials after reporting

Item 2 of Article 8(1) became "the name of the Public Safety Commission notified" in the 2024 amendment. Kanagawa Prefectural Police. We summarised the enacted text from the House of Representatives site and filled in the amendment from police guidance; check your local police guidance for how to prepare the documents today.

Write your cancellation terms on the site exactly as they are in your contract: until when cancelling costs nothing, and how fees are settled once work has started, with an example.

Confidentiality, and names that could mislead

Japan's Act forbids disclosing secrets learned in the work without good reason, even after leaving the job, and requires measures against misuse of documents, photos and other materials produced in an investigation. Detective Business Act, Article 10. On your site, say how you handle what clients tell you and what you do so that letters or calls do not reveal the matter to family (for example, not using the office name, agreeing on contact times), describing only what you actually do.

For case studies, change or blur the area, timing and personal details so neither the client nor the person investigated can be identified, and get the client's consent. If you publish figures, state what was counted, the period and the number of cases in the same paragraph.

In 2016 Japan's National Consumer Affairs Center warned about a rise in complaints involving investigators advertising help with adult-website billing disputes. Examples included firms using names similar to local consumer centres, falsely claiming to work with the police, and charging high cancellation fees to a client who tried to cancel an hour after ordering. National Consumer Affairs Center, 15 December 2016 (Japanese). With that history, avoid any name or wording that could be mistaken for a public office, and make clear that you are a private investigation business.

Structured data: there is no investigator type

schema.org has no type for private investigators or investigation firms. ProfessionalService "was deprecated due to confusion with Service". schema.org: ProfessionalService. Publish the office as a LocalBusiness and link your free consultation and case plans as Service with Offer.

Keep structured data identical to the page text. An Offer carries a single price, so for a case plan use a representative fee and put expenses and report terms in the service description and your fee table. Put your registration number in the page text (the About page), not in structured data.

Pre-publication checklist

  1. The registration sign is visible on the top page or through a link, and the same details (authority, number, offices) appear as text on the About page.
  2. No old "certificate number" wording or outdated addresses remain.
  3. Each case type has what you can find out, what you cannot and a typical duration.
  4. How fees are set, expenses, report costs and payment timing are in a table including tax, with two or three worked estimates.
  5. No unsupported claims such as "guaranteed" or "cheapest in the industry".
  6. Your intake policy and the client's written statement are described.
  7. The steps from pre-contract explanation to contract document, report and disposal of materials are described.
  8. Confidentiality and contact arrangements are explained; case studies are anonymised with consent; no name could be mistaken for a public office.
  9. Name, address, phone and hours match across your site, structured data, Google Business Profile and directory listings.
  10. llms.txt lists your fees, how-it-works, About (registration) and free consultation pages. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, write five to ten questions a prospective client would ask, combining case type, area, budget and concerns (registration, confidentiality, reports). Put them to ChatGPT and Perplexity and record whether your office appears by name or URL, whether fees and registration details are right, and whether the cited source is your site or a directory. FAQ

Where should a private investigator start with AI search?

Publish your registration (authority, number, offices) on the site and as text on your About page, put how fees are set and worked estimates including expenses and tax in a table, and describe the cases you handle, the ones you refuse and your contract steps. Then make your name, address and phone identical across your site, structured data and Google Business Profile.

Do we have to show the registration sign on our website?

In Japan, since 1 April 2024 investigators must display a self-made sign at the office and on their website. Businesses with five or fewer regular employees, or no website, are exempt from the website part. Even if exempt, publishing it helps AI and clients confirm your registration. Check the details with your local police.

Is "from X per hour" enough on the fees page?

Usually not. Japanese law requires an approximate amount and payment timing to be explained in writing before a contract. On your site, two or three worked estimates covering investigators, hours, days and expenses let AI answer with conditions.

Can we publish case studies?

Yes, if neither the client nor the person investigated can be identified: change or blur the area, timing and details, and get the client's consent. The Act forbids disclosing secrets learned in the work. Give what was counted, the period and the number of cases with any figures.

Which structured data type should an investigator use?

schema.org has no investigator type, and ProfessionalService is deprecated. Publish the office as a LocalBusiness and link your free consultation and case plans as Service with Offer. Put your registration number in the page text.
