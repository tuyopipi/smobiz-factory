AI search for funeral homes: making AI read your total prices, transfers and venues correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a funeral home comes down to publishing, accurately and consistently in your page text and structured data, what each package includes and leaves out (with an example total), whether families can call at night for a transfer and where the deceased will rest, which venues hold how many people, and which faiths and non-religious services you handle. What sets a funeral home apart here is not a low starting price but a clear typical total and the conditions that change it.

Written 13 November 2026 (JST). Facts from schema.org, Google, OpenAI and the Japanese authorities were checked on the dates given in the text. Funeral pricing rules differ by country; check your own regulator's guidance or ask a professional.

AI answers change by day and by how a question is asked. This guide is about making your facts easy for AI to read correctly; it does not guarantee that you will appear in AI answers or rank higher.

What families ask AI: urgent questions and planning questions

Questions about funeral homes fall into two groups. The first come right after a death: "a funeral home that can collect from the hospital tonight", "where can the body rest if we can't take them home", "a small family funeral we can arrange today in [town]". Time and place matter. The second are planning questions: "how much does a small funeral cost in total", "what's the difference between direct cremation and a one-day service", "a funeral home that handles Buddhist rites", "can we have a non-religious service". Families use these to compare.

ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews read the web and answer by naming a few funeral homes. For urgent questions, if AI can't find that you take calls around the clock, which areas you collect from and whether you have a place for the deceased to rest, you are less likely to be a candidate. For planning questions, the way you write prices decides how accurate the answer is.

Google says there are no special structured data or extra requirements for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that ordinary SEO basics matter, such as matching structured data to the visible text and keeping your Business Profile current (checked 12 November 2026). Google: AI features and your website. The work is not a trick; it is publishing your facts correctly and in a form people can compare.

How you write prices decides how accurate AI answers are

Funeral prices are a common source of complaints. In Japan, the National Consumer Affairs Center's page on graves and funeral services shows consultations about funeral services registered in its database rising from 886 in fiscal 2023 to 979 in fiscal 2024 and 1,005 in fiscal 2025 (counts as of 31 May 2026), and lists recent cases where the final bill was higher than the amount shown on the funeral home's website (checked 13 November 2026). National Consumer Affairs Center of Japan (Japanese)

Regulators act on this too. On 18 September 2026, Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency issued an order against a family-funeral hall operator whose TV commercials showed "family funeral from ¥104,500 (tax incl.)" when, except in a few cases, ¥308,000 or more was actually needed, finding the ads misleading about price under Japan's Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations (checked 13 November 2026). Consumer Affairs Agency order (PDF, Japanese). In the United States, price disclosure by funeral providers is covered by the FTC's Funeral Rule; see the FTC's compliance guide for what applies to you.

AI runs into the same problem. If your site only says "funerals from $X", AI may quote that as the cost of a funeral and line it up against other homes' totals. Unless the conditions for the starting price (no venue, up to a certain number of guests, one day of holding) are written next to it, AI has no way to know them. Splitting your price page into what's included, what's not, what triggers extra charges and an example total is the clearest form for families and for AI alike.

Write your real prices, including tax where it applies. When prices change, update the page, structured data, Google Business Profile and any comparison listings on the same day.

Structured data type: there is no funeral home type

schema.org has no type for funeral homes. It has Cemetery and Crematorium, but both are kinds of CivicStructure (public places), not kinds of LocalBusiness (checked 13 November 2026 against the type definitions in schema-dts 2.1.0, generated from the schema.org vocabulary). Using them for your company tells machines you are a place rather than a business.

So the simple choice is LocalBusiness, with "funeral home", "family funerals" and "24-hour calls" conveyed through your name, description, services and page text. Google's local business documentation asks you to use the most specific type you can (checked 29 October 2026), Google: local business structured data, but when no type fits there is no need to force a near one such as ProfessionalService; looking like a different trade is more likely to mislead AI.

If you have several venues, keep company facts (head office address, 24-hour phone) separate from venue facts (venue address, capacity). Nurevo AEO publishes one business per site, so write each venue's address, capacity and parking as text on its own page, and list the venue pages in llms.txt.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Each package says, in text, what's included, what isn't, and what triggers extra charges.
  2. An example total for a common case is shown with what it includes and whether tax is included.
  3. Any "from $X" price has the conditions for that price right next to it (in ads, banners and structured data too).
  4. Your 24-hour number, the areas you collect from and whether you have a holding room are written down.
  5. Each venue's address, capacity and parking are written as text and not mixed with the company address.
  6. The faiths you serve and whether you hold non-religious services are stated.
  7. Your name, address and phone match across your site, Google Business Profile and comparison listings.
  8. Your structured data type is LocalBusiness, not Cemetery, Crematorium or an unrelated trade.
  9. No claim you can't prove, such as "cheapest" or "number one in the area", appears in text or structured data.

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once things are in order, ask AI the questions families would ask (area plus "family funeral" or "direct cremation" plus conditions such as cost, 24 hours or faith) without your name, and see whether your name or URL appears. Also ask by name, for example "How much does a family funeral at [your name] cost in total, and what's included?", and check that the answer matches your current prices and inclusions. If a starting price is being quoted as a total, look at the page AI cited and fix it or the listing it came from. AI answers vary from one run to the next, so ask the same questions several times, spaced out, and keep a record. FAQ

Where should a funeral home start with AI search?

Rewrite your price page into what's included, what isn't, conditions for extra charges and an example total. Then write your 24-hour number and collection area, holding, each venue and the faiths you serve as text, and publish the same facts in structured data and llms.txt.

Which structured data type fits a funeral home?

schema.org has no funeral home type, so use LocalBusiness and convey that you are a funeral home, and that you take calls 24 hours, through your name, description, services and text. Cemetery and Crematorium are types for public places, not businesses.

Is it wrong to write "funerals from $X"?

A starting price isn't banned in itself, but unless the conditions for it sit right beside it, families and AI will read it as a total. In September 2026 Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency issued an order over TV ads showing a family funeral from ¥104,500 when, except in a few cases, ¥308,000 or more was needed. Show an example total and the conditions together.

Should we enter 00:00-24:00 as our opening hours?

If your phone line is 24 hours but planning meetings and viewings are not, put the meeting and viewing hours in opening hours and write the 24-hour phone line in the description and text. If everything really is 24 hours, say so in the text and enter it as your hours.

Should we publish what to pay clergy?

That amount isn't yours to set, so don't state a figure. Say it isn't included, who to discuss it with, and whether you can make an introduction.

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear.
