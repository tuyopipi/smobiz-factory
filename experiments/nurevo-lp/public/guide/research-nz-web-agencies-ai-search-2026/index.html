AI search readiness of New Zealand web design agencies: 417 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 417 New Zealand web design agencies' own websites. 25.4% (106) had an llms.txt — higher than Japan (13.4%) and not statistically different from Australia (24.3%), the US (23.1%) or the UK (21.5%). 20.9% met all six AI-search items we checked, almost exactly Australia's 20.8% and twice Japan's share. Sites on a .co.nz domain trailed .com sites on both llms.txt (18.2% vs 34.4%) and meeting all six items (13.6% vs 32.8%).

Published 2026-11-28. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic. This is the tenth of our country reports (earlier ones include the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia). For Japan against all 25 overseas countries and regions, see our comparison.

Key findings

  1. 25.4% (106) of 417 New Zealand web agency websites had an llms.txt. That is higher than Japan (13.4%, p < 0.001) and not statistically different from Australia (24.3%, p = 0.68), the US (23.1%, p = 0.31) or the UK (21.5%, p = 0.13). Excluding the 37 home pages that answered HTTP 202, the share is 27.9%.
  2. 20.9% (87) of New Zealand sites met all six AI-search items we checked — 2.0 times Japan's 10.4% and almost identical to Australia (20.8%). The 6-item average was 3.80 (Australia 3.77, Japan 4.05).
  3. By domain, 18.2% of 132 .co.nz sites had an llms.txt, against 34.4% of 122 .com sites (p = 0.005). Comparing WordPress sites only, the gap remained: 20.9% vs 41.2% (p = 0.004).

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 544 agency sites listed as New Zealand in our collection list, 417 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of New Zealand businesses in general. For comparison we use the same survey's Australia (1,719), US (4,472), UK (942), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites outside New Zealand (12,935).
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: 37 New Zealand home pages (8.9% of the 417 reached) answered with status 202 (Australia 14.5%, US 15.0%, UK 14.9%, Japan none). None of them had JSON-LD, WordPress markers, a robots.txt or an llms.txt, so they may have returned a bot-check or other interim page. We therefore also show figures excluding them.
  7. Statistics: differences between New Zealand and other groups were tested with chi-square tests. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown. Region or city was blank for 470 of the 544 listed sites, so we did not split by region.

Results: New Zealand compared with Australia, the US, the UK and Japan

On most items New Zealand was within about a point of Australia. On llms.txt, all six items and AI bot blocking we found no statistically significant difference from Australia, the US, the UK or all overseas sites outside New Zealand. FAQPage was 7.4%, below Australia's 10.3%, but the difference was not clear-cut (p = 0.09). Broken JSON-LD affected 1.0% of sites with JSON-LD, the lowest of the groups compared. Against Japan, New Zealand was higher on llms.txt, FAQPage and all six items, and lower on meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt. Those lower values partly reflect the 37 HTTP 202 pages; excluding them, meta description is 76.3%, canonical 85.3% and robots.txt 86.1%.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. 13 of the 15 sites blocking GPTBot (86.7%) did not block OAI-SearchBot (Australia 55 of 56, US 119 of 128).
  2. Of the 21 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots in the table (all except Googlebot and Bingbot), 13 (61.9%) used the same combination: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all seven together (Australia 64.6%, US 58.5%). CCBot alone and Bytespider alone accounted for 2 sites each.
  3. 2 sites (0.6% of those with a robots.txt) blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
  4. 13 sites (4.0% of those with a robots.txt) named an AI bot in robots.txt but blocked none from the home page.
  5. 4 of the 21 sites blocking an AI bot (19.0%) also had an llms.txt.

By domain: .co.nz and .com

Of the 417 reachable sites, 132 used .co.nz, 122 used .com and 163 used other domains (other .nz domains numbered 27, below our 30-site threshold, and are not shown). .co.nz sites were lower than .com sites on both llms.txt (18.2% vs 34.4%, p = 0.005) and meeting all six items (13.6% vs 32.8%, p < 0.001). Because WordPress was also less common on .co.nz (68.9% vs 83.6%), we compared WordPress sites only: llms.txt was 20.9% (of 91) vs 41.2% (of 102), so the gap remained (p = 0.004). Excluding HTTP 202, the figures were 20.0% and 36.2% (p = 0.009).

We did not examine how domain choice relates to where an agency's clients are (mainly domestic or mainly overseas).

WordPress and SEO plugins

75.3% (314) of New Zealand agency sites ran WordPress (82.6% excluding HTTP 202). WordPress sites had llms.txt 31.8%, JSON-LD 89.8% and a 6-item average of 4.79, and none scored zero. Non-WordPress sites (103, of which 37 answered 202) had llms.txt 5.8%, JSON-LD 10.7% and an average of 0.78; 78 of them scored zero. Among WordPress sites, New Zealand's llms.txt share was in line with the US (30.1%, p = 0.56) and Australia (30.7%, p = 0.74) and above Japan (14.0%, p < 0.001).

Yoast SEO was detected on 52.2% of WordPress sites, Rank Math on 20.7%, and no plugin on 21.3%. AIOSEO (16 sites) and SEOPress (2) are below our 30-site threshold and not shown. llms.txt adoption: Rank Math 41.5%, Yoast SEO 29.3%, no plugin detected 23.9%. We did not check whether the llms.txt was generated by a plugin or added by hand.

Distribution of the six items

Like Australia, the US and the UK, New Zealand split into 0 items (18.7%) and 5–6 items (57.8% combined). Part of the zeros are HTTP 202 pages; excluding them, 0 items falls to 10.8%. Of the 154 sites with five items, 140 (90.9%) were missing only the llms.txt. The 106 llms.txt files had a median length of 75 lines.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the sample is not representative of all New Zealand agencies. At 417 sites it is smaller than the US or Australian samples, and per-bot and per-domain counts are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists and its line count, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites (127) are excluded. The 37 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. New Zealand agency sites look very much like Australian ones on every item. The notable pattern is the .co.nz vs .com gap; one possible explanation is that agencies on .com are more often aiming at overseas clients, but this survey did not test that. Nine in ten sites with five items lacked only an llms.txt, which is a relatively easy item to add. AI bot blocking is a minority practice, and where it happens it is almost always limited to training crawlers. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision, but sites that want to appear in AI search may want to confirm they are not blocking search crawlers by accident. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are New Zealand agencies ahead of Japan on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt (New Zealand 25.4%, Japan 13.4%) and on meeting all six items (20.9% vs 10.4%), yes. JSON-LD is about the same (70.3% vs 69.4%), and Japan is higher on meta descriptions and canonical links (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

How does New Zealand compare with Australia?

We found no statistically significant difference on llms.txt (25.4% vs 24.3%), all six items (20.9% vs 20.8%) or AI bot blocking (5.8% vs 4.7%). FAQPage markup was somewhat more common in Australia (10.3% vs 7.4%), but the difference was not clear-cut.

Is there a difference between .co.nz and .com agency sites?

Yes. 18.2% of 132 .co.nz sites had an llms.txt, against 34.4% of 122 .com sites (p = 0.005), and the gap remained among WordPress sites only (20.9% vs 41.2%). We did not investigate why.
