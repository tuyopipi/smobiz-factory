AI search for gyms and yoga or pilates studios: how to get AI to read your prices, hours, trials and cancellation terms correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a gym, a personal training studio or a yoga or pilates studio comes down to publishing, accurately and consistently, your membership fees and joining fees (with tax), your opening hours and staffed hours, what your trial class includes and costs, how freezing and cancelling work, and your facilities and classes, both in your page text and in schema.org structured data such as ExerciseGym. The biggest difference in this trade comes from writing each membership plan so that it is clear what it costs, when it can be used, what it includes and how to leave.

Written November 12, 2026 (JST). Information from Google, OpenAI and Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency was checked on each official page on the dates given in the text. Contract and advertising law is not explained in detail here; check current official sources or a professional.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your gym's or studio's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention you or rank you anywhere.

What people ask AI when choosing a gym or studio

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews to find a gym or studio, the AI reads what it can find on the web and answers with a few names. The questions combine place, price, time, facilities and conditions: "a gym near the station that's open after 11 pm and costs under $70 a month", "a beginner-friendly pilates studio for women in my town", "a personal training gym with a free trial", "a gym with showers and parking", "a yoga studio that lets you pause your membership".

Almost every question in this trade is a comparison. What an AI can use in its answer are facts it can compare: price, hours, facilities, trials and how to leave. Phrases like "transform your body" or "unbeatable results" give it nothing to compare, and if your price page is an image or says "contact us for pricing", you are easy to leave out of the shortlist.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special schema.org structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual basics still matter, such as making structured data match the visible text (checked November 12, 2026). Google: AI features and your website. Google's AI optimization guide also says structured data isn't required for generative AI search and that Google Search doesn't use llms.txt. Google: AI optimization guide. Structured data and llms.txt help AI systems, including ChatGPT and Perplexity, read your facts more easily; the facts in your page text are the real work.

Decide first: your business type in structured data

In structured data, the kind of business is expressed as a schema.org type. Exercise venues sit under SportsActivityLocation, which has more specific types. A gym can use ExerciseGym, and a health-club style venue can use HealthClub, which belongs to both SportsActivityLocation and HealthAndBeautyBusiness. There is no type for yoga or pilates studios (all checked against the schema.org type definitions on November 12, 2026). Google's local business documentation asks you to use the most specific type possible (checked October 29, 2026). Google: local business structured data.

This is a guide. Match the type to the service at the center of what you actually offer. None of these types guarantees any display in Google.

Businesses built around hands-on treatment, such as massage or bodywork, should look for a type that fits that treatment instead.

Turning membership plans into records AI can compare

The page you most want AI to read on a gym or studio site is the price page. Yet many price pages show only plan names and a big monthly figure, with the joining fee, admin fee, usable hours, minimum term and cancellation deadline tucked into small print or an image. That leaves nothing to answer "a gym under $70 a month that's open late" with. Write each membership plan as a record in a text table, with these items.

The items are the facts that people comparing venues, and AI, need. The values are placeholders; write your actual prices and terms exactly as in your membership agreement.

For high-value packages such as a personal training course of a set number of sessions, write the total, the price per session, the expiry date, what happens to unused sessions and how refunds work if someone stops partway, exactly as in the contract. Depending on the type, length and value of a contract, consumer protection rules may apply (in Japan, for example, the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions). This article does not explain when they apply, so check with your regulator or a professional.

Some venues worry that publishing prices invites comparison with cheaper rivals. But a venue whose price is unknown gives AI nothing to put in a comparison answer. If you charge more, add the facts that explain it: the coaching, the time, the facilities, the support.

Results, rankings and reviews: what not to add for AI

Claims such as "lose 10 kg in two months" or "guaranteed results" and before-and-after photos are common in fitness advertising. Effect claims you cannot support can be a problem under advertising rules. If you show results, say on the same page who achieved them, under what conditions and over what period, and that results vary. AI leans on facts it can check rather than strong words, so leave unsupported claims out of both your text and your markup.

Take care with claims such as "No. 1 for satisfaction in the area" as well. In a report of September 26, 2024, Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency said that a No. 1 claim needs an appropriate choice of what is compared, of who answers, of survey method, and a match between the claim and the results, and that claims based on surveys asking people who had never used a service for their impressions may be a problem (checked November 12, 2026). Consumer Affairs Agency: report on No. 1 claims (Japanese)

Some guides recommend adding star ratings (AggregateRating) to your venue pages in structured data. Marking up reviews of your own business on your own site will not get you review stars in Google. In 2019 Google said that self-serving reviews, meaning reviews about a business placed on that business's own website, aren't allowed for LocalBusiness and Organization. Google Search Central blog, September 16, 2019

Rules on undisclosed advertising differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you. In Japan, stealth marketing has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations since October 1, 2023, and the Consumer Affairs Agency treats reviews that a business asks or instructs third parties to post as that business's advertising (checked November 1, 2026). Consumer Affairs Agency: stealth marketing (Japanese). Publish real member stories, with consent, without hiding that they are promotional.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Name, address, phone, opening hours and closed days match across your site, Google Business Profile and booking sites.
  2. Your structured data type is not LocalBusiness but one that fits, such as ExerciseGym.
  3. Opening hours and staffed hours are written separately.
  4. Each membership plan shows the monthly price (with tax), up-front costs, usable hours, minimum term, and freezing and cancellation terms in a text table.
  5. The price page shows a revision date, and ended promotions and old prices are gone.
  6. The trial's price, length, what to bring and how to book are written down and added as a service.
  7. Class types, timetable and who they're for are text, not images.
  8. There are no unsupported effect claims or superlatives, and reviews of your own business are not marked up as ratings.
  9. llms.txt lists your overview, prices, trial, classes, access and FAQ pages. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, ask AI the questions a prospective member would ask (place plus price plus time plus conditions), without your venue's name, and see whether you come up by name or URL. Also ask by name ("What is X Gym's monthly fee and how do I cancel?") and check that the prices and terms in the answer are current. If they are wrong, look at which page the AI cited and fix that page or the outside listing. Answers change between runs, so ask the same questions a few times, spread over time, and record them. FAQ

Where should a gym or studio start with AI search?

Write your prices (with tax), opening and staffed hours, trial, freezing and cancellation terms, facilities and classes correctly as text on your pages. Then set your structured data type to one that fits, such as ExerciseGym, add your trial and classes as services, and make sure everything matches your Google Business Profile and booking site profiles.

Is there a structured data type for yoga or pilates studios?

schema.org has no type specifically for yoga or pilates. Use a type such as ExerciseGym to say you are an exercise venue, and describe what you teach, such as yoga or reformer pilates, in services and page text.

Should I publish my prices?

If you want to be included in comparison answers, yes. A venue with unknown prices gives AI nothing to work with. Include joining fees, admin fees and any minimum term, with tax, and add the revision date.

Can I show before-and-after photos?

If you do, use real cases with the member's consent, and state the period, conditions and that results vary on the same page. Effect claims you cannot support can be a problem under advertising rules, so check before you publish.
