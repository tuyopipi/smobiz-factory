AI search for B2B and SaaS: make your pricing, case study and comparison pages readable to AI

B2B and SaaS buyers shortlist vendors by asking AI things like "What is the best tool for X?", "How does A compare with B?" and "What does this usually cost?". The core of the work is to have pages that answer those questions in text (pricing, case studies, comparisons and alternatives, integrations and security), with conditions and dates attached, and to keep review sites and outside profiles consistent with them. Structured data and llms.txt then restate the facts on those pages in a form machines can read.

Written November 17, 2026 (JST). schema.org types were checked that day in schema-dts 2.1.0. Statements from Google, OpenAI and Perplexity come from their pages as read between October 31 and November 10, 2026 for earlier Nurevo guides. Examples of consumer rules come from Japan; check the rules where you operate.

This guide is for companies that sell software (SaaS), outsourced work or consulting to other businesses, and for the agencies that build their sites. How to write the About page is covered in AI search for your About page, and how to write blog posts in how to write articles AI can cite. This guide covers the pages that answer "which vendor should we choose?". AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention you or rank you anywhere.

What B2B buyers ask AI

A local business is found with "business type + area". A B2B vendor is found from what the buyer wants to get done and under which conditions. Many of these questions come before the buyer knows your name, so if your name is missing from the answer you never make the shortlist. In a survey run by the Japanese agency TaTap in November 2026, 62.2% of 135 executives who had looked for a vendor themselves said they asked a generative AI for recommended companies, and 27.4% said they dropped or deprioritised companies whose names did not appear (TaTap, a small survey by a firm that sells this work).

The questions fall into roughly six kinds, and each kind is answered by a different page.

Kind of questionExamplePage that answers itTypical mistake in AI answers

The questions Nurevo's measurement asks for B2B / SaaS sites follow these kinds too ("What is the best tool for ...?", "Compare the main ...", "Which ... do you recommend for a small business?", "What is the typical price range for ...?", "Which ... have the most customer case studies?", "What is a good alternative if I want to switch ...?"), all without your name. Questions with your name are checked by hand, as described below.

Pricing page: give the inputs for a decision, even without every number

For "How much does it cost?" or "How much is X?", AI answers from your pricing page or, without one, from numbers on review sites and comparison articles. Current terms on your own page are safer than old numbers written by someone else. Even with quote-based pricing you can say what the price depends on.

FieldExampleWhat happens without it
Billing unit"Per user per month", "per location", "usage-based, per 1,000 records"Totals misread by a large factor
Plan differencesPlan names, included features and limits (users, retention) in a tableTop-plan features described as included in every plan
Setup fees and minimumsSetup fee, minimum seats, minimum term, annual vs monthly billingOnly the monthly price spreads, and the contract surprises people
TaxOne clear line above the table, such as "All prices exclude tax"Prices with and without tax compared side by side
Free trial or free planLength, what is included, whether billing starts automaticallyReported simply as "free"
Add-onsOnboarding, extra storage, API access, premium supportNecessary costs missing from the answer
Quote-based pricing"Pricing depends on X and Y. Plans start from about Z per month."Reported only as "pricing not public", and you drop off the list
Last updated"Prices revised November 1, 2026" on the pageNo way to tell old from new prices

When you change prices, update the comparison page, the FAQ, old blog posts and your review-site listings on the same day as the pricing page. When your own pages disagree, AI picks one of them.

Do not publish prices only as an image or a PDF. Use an HTML table or a labelled list.

Case study pages: match the conditions buyers search with

B2B questions carry conditions: "Which time tracking tools do manufacturers use?", "Which ... have the most case studies?". What matters in a case study is that these conditions are easy to find, more than that it reads well. Put the following fields in a table at the top of each story, then the narrative.

FieldExample
Industry and size"Food manufacturer, about 250 employees, 2 plants"
Who uses it"HR and the managers at each plant"
Problem before"Paper time cards tallied by hand at month end"
Why they chose it, and the criteria"Shift support and an integration with their existing payroll software" (no need to name competitors)
Features and integrations used"Clock-in, shift planning, payroll export"
Time to go live"About 2 months from contract to production"
Result, and how it was measured"Monthly tally went from 3 days to half a day (HR work log, 6 months after launch)": what, when, how measured
Date and point in time"Interviewed October 2026. Titles and figures as of the interview"

Publish only results the customer can stand behind, with the method and the date. Inflated figures get quoted by AI as they are, and are hard to correct later. Get written permission to show the customer's name, logo and quotes.

While you have few stories, add an index page by industry and company size that links to each story. A page called "Customer stories" or "Case studies", linked from the home page, is easy for people and AI to find.

Comparison and switching pages: say when you are not the right fit

Asked "A vs B?", an AI with nothing to go on from your site answers from competitors' pages or comparison sites. Build a comparison page as material for deciding which product suits which company, not as a scoreboard.

  1. Decide the criteria first (company size, billing unit, integrations, support, data location and so on) and compare every product on the same criteria.
  2. For competitors' features and prices, give the date you checked their official page and a link. Leave out anything you cannot check.
  3. Say plainly when you are not the right fit ("Under 100 people, A's free plan may suit you better"). A one-sided comparison is not trusted by readers either.
  4. On a switching page, state what data can be imported, the steps and time involved, how long to run both in parallel, and whether migration help is paid or free.
  5. Date the comparison page and review it whenever you or a competitor change prices.

When you name competitors, stick to statements of fact, and do not use their logos or screenshots without permission.

Integrations, requirements, security and support: answer the condition questions

Conditions such as "integrates with X", "holds ISO/IEC 27001" or "support on weekends" are facts AI tends to use when narrowing a list. A single line in a feature list is weak material, so give each condition its own page or heading.

  1. Integrations: the services you connect to, how (built-in, API, CSV) and whether it costs extra.
  2. Requirements: browsers, whether there is a mobile app and for which OS, how it behaves offline.
  3. Security: for each certification you hold, its name, scope and registration number or date. Do not list ones you do not hold. Where data is stored (country or region).
  4. Support: hours, channels (phone, email, chat), differences by plan, what onboarding includes.
  5. Contract and cancellation: how to cancel and what happens to the data afterwards.

Outside sources: keep review sites and profiles in line with your site

For questions with your name ("What do people say about X?"), review sites such as G2, Capterra, ITreview and BOXIL, press releases and comparison articles are often used as sources. You cannot edit those pages, so focus on two things.

  1. Keep the listing current: product description, prices, features and company facts written the same way and with the same numbers as your site. Ask each site's operator for updates under its own listing rules.
  2. Collect reviews honestly: in Japan, stealth marketing has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations since October 1, 2023, and reviews a business asks or instructs third parties to post count as that business's advertising (Consumer Affairs Agency). If you offer an incentive for reviews, check each review site's rules and how the incentive must be disclosed, and the rules where you operate.

When you show a review score on your site, say which site, as of when and from how many reviews. Do not publish ratings you collected yourself as an aggregateRating in structured data without saying where they come from.

Can AI read the page? Crawlers and JavaScript

SaaS sites sometimes render pricing tables or feature comparisons with JavaScript after the page loads. What you see in a browser may be missing from the HTML a crawler receives. It is safer to have the pricing and case study text in the HTML the server returns first. Fetch the page's HTML (with curl, for example) and check that the prices and the story text are there.

Also check that robots.txt does not block the search crawlers of AI services. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI crawlers); this is a different bot from GPTBot, which is for training. Perplexity also documents PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User separately (Perplexity crawlers). A WAF or bot-protection service can block them without anyone noticing.

Structured data: Organization for the company, SoftwareApplication or Service for what you sell

Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special structured data or files, and that structured data should match what is visible on the page (AI features and your website). With that in mind, the usual combination for B2B and SaaS is:

  1. The company: Organization (or a subtype such as Corporation), with name, URL, logo, description and contact. See AI search for your About page.
  2. Software: SoftwareApplication. In schema.org it is a kind of CreativeWork, with applicationCategory, operatingSystem, featureList, softwareVersion and offers among its properties. Browser-based software also has the subtype WebApplication (schema.org SoftwareApplication).
  3. Services such as outsourced work or consulting: Service, linked to the company through provider.
  4. FAQ: FAQPage only on a page that shows those questions and answers.
{
  "@type": "SoftwareApplication",
  "@id": "https://example.com/#nurevo-service-1",
  "name": "Example Timesheets",
  "provider": { "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" },
  "description": "Cloud time tracking for companies with 50 to 300 employees: clock-in, shifts and leave in one place.",
  "url": "https://example.com/product/",
  "applicationCategory": "Time tracking software",
  "offers": { "@type": "Offer", "price": "4", "priceCurrency": "USD" }
}

The example is the shape Nurevo AEO publishes when a service is marked "This is software (SaaS or an app)" (values are made up). The provider points at the company node, the category becomes applicationCategory, and price and currency become an Offer. State the billing unit, such as "per user per month", in the text of the pricing page.

Google's software app rich result in search results has its own requirements, such as ratings or reviews. If you want that rich result, check the current requirements in Google's software app structured data documentation. Nurevo AEO never publishes a rating that has no real reviews behind it.

llms.txt: a way in to pricing, case study and comparison pages

llms.txt is a single text file with a summary of the site and links to its main pages. No AI company has officially described an effect, and Google says it is not needed (see how to write llms.txt). If you add one, for B2B and SaaS the point is to make sure it lists your pricing, case study, comparison and integration pages. With the site type set to "B2B / SaaS", Nurevo AEO builds it like this (values are made up):

# Example Timesheets

> Cloud time tracking for companies with 50 to 300 employees, made by Example Inc.

## Key facts

- URL: https://example.com/
- Email: info@example.com
- Type: Corporation
- Pricing: https://example.com/pricing/
- Case studies: https://example.com/customers/

## Pages

- [Pricing](https://example.com/pricing/)
- [Customer stories](https://example.com/customers/)
- [How we compare](https://example.com/compare/)
- [Integrations](https://example.com/integrations/)

## Services

- [Example Timesheets](https://example.com/product/) — Clock-in, shifts and leave in one place · USD 4 · Time tracking software

Pricing and Case studies under Key facts are the URLs entered in Settings as "Pricing page (B2B)" and "Case studies page (B2B)". B2B / SaaS sites do not publish opening hours. Pages lists the 30 most recently updated pages and posts, so on a site with a busy blog the comparison page can be pushed out. In that case, mark posts that do not need to be there with "leave this page out of llms.txt", or add lines with the developer filter webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines.

How to check: separate unbranded and branded questions

For B2B, record two kinds of question separately. Unbranded questions tell you whether you make the shortlist of people who do not know you. Branded questions tell you whether people who do know you get the right facts. Ask the same question of the same AI on different days, and never judge from a single answer.

KindExampleWhat to record
Unbranded (category)"Which time tracking software do you recommend for a small business?"Whether you were named, whether your site was cited, which other products appeared
Unbranded (condition)"Time tracking that integrates with our payroll software?"Whether the condition was applied correctly
Branded (price)"How much is Example Timesheets?"Whether price, billing unit and tax match your pricing page today, and what the source was
Branded (reputation)"What do people say about Example Timesheets?"Which review site or article was the source, and whether old information crept in

B2B and SaaS site checklist

  1. The first sentence of the product page says what it is, for whom (industry and size) and how it is delivered.
  2. The pricing page states the billing unit, plan differences, setup fee, minimums, tax and a last-updated date (or, for quotes, what the price depends on and a starting point).
  3. Pricing and case study text is in the HTML without running JavaScript.
  4. Case studies give industry, size, team, problem, features used, time to go live, and how and when results were measured.
  5. Comparison pages use the same criteria for every product, date and source competitor facts, and say when you are not the right fit.
  6. Integrations, requirements, security and support each have their own heading or page.
  7. On the day prices change, there is a routine to update comparison pages, the FAQ, old posts and review-site listings too.
  8. robots.txt and the WAF do not block search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot.
  9. Organization and SoftwareApplication (or Service) values match the page text, and no made-up rating is published.
  10. llms.txt lists the pricing, case study and comparison pages.
  11. Unbranded and branded questions are recorded separately, on different days.

FAQ

If we do not publish prices, will AI leave us out?

You can still be mentioned. But without material for pricing questions, AI answers from old numbers on other sites or just says pricing is not public. Even if you cannot show amounts, say what the price depends on and give a starting point.

Can we name competitors on a comparison page?

Many companies do, as long as the facts are accurate. Give the date you checked each competitor's official page and a link, leave out what you cannot check, and do not use their logos or screenshots without permission. Check the laws and industry rules on comparative advertising that apply to you.

Should SaaS use SoftwareApplication or Product markup?

schema.org provides SoftwareApplication for software (and WebApplication for browser-based software). Nurevo AEO publishes services marked as software as SoftwareApplication. If you want Google's rich result, check the requirements in Google's documentation.

Will a low score on review sites keep AI from recommending us?

No AI company has officially explained how review scores are used in answers. What you can do is keep listings current and collect reviews honestly. If you offer incentives, check the stealth marketing rules where you operate and each site's rules.

Does this apply to development agencies and consultancies too?

The basics are the same. Instead of a product page, write a page per service (who it is for, how it works, how long it takes, how the price is set, case studies) and use Service markup. In Nurevo AEO, add the services without ticking the software box.

AI keeps quoting our old prices. What can we do?

Trace the sources in the answer. On your own site, fix old numbers outside the pricing page (comparison pages, FAQ, old posts); on other sites, ask the publisher to update. You cannot edit AI answers directly, and there is no set time for changes to show.

Will these steps get us into AI recommendations?

There is no guarantee. AI answers change with the day and the wording, and the AI decides which products to name. These steps make the facts AI reads accurate and give it material on your own site to choose you from.
