AI search for B2B and SaaS companies: how to get AI to read your services, pricing, case studies and company facts correctly

AI search optimization (AEO, sometimes called GEO or LLMO) for a B2B or SaaS company comes down to publishing what you offer, to which companies, at what price and on what terms, where it has actually been used, and who you are, accurately in your page text and in Organization, Service and SoftwareApplication structured data, and keeping review sites and other outside profiles in agreement. That gives ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI what they need to answer "what's the best tool for X?" or "how much does X usually cost?" without guessing. You do not need special AI tags, and you should not mass-produce a page for every question.

Written November 2, 2026 (JST). Information from Google, OpenAI, Perplexity and Japan's National Tax Agency was checked on each official page on that date.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your company's and services' facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention or recommend you.

What B2B buyers ask AI

Questions about business services and software differ from questions about a shop or a restaurant: they are mostly about comparing options under conditions. The B2B questions Nurevo AEO uses for its AI visibility measurement follow the patterns below, and none of them contains the company's name.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special schema.org markup needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that structured data should match the visible text. Google: AI features and your website. Its 2026 guide to generative AI features adds that Google Search does not use llms.txt or other AI text files, that content does not need to be broken into tiny pieces, and that creating pages for every variation of a query to manipulate rankings or AI responses is scaled content abuse; it recommends unique content based on first-hand experience or expertise. Google: Optimizing for generative AI features

So what works for B2B is not "writing for AI". It is having the facts buyers look for while comparing and evaluating written clearly on your own site.

The facts a B2B or SaaS site should publish

Your company is described with Organization structured data. Google lists no required properties for it, recommends properties such as name, url, logo, description, sameAs (links to your official profiles on other sites) and contactPoint, and says it is enough to put it on the home page or one page that describes the organization, such as an about page. Before you add SoftwareApplication markup

SoftwareApplication looks like the obvious markup for SaaS, but Google's software app rich result requires name, offers.price (0 for a free app) and either aggregateRating or review; applicationCategory (such as BusinessApplication) and operatingSystem are recommended. Google: Software app structured data. Without ratings or reviews, SoftwareApplication is still readable structured data, but it is not eligible for that rich result.

Do not create reviews to get stars, or publish reviews written in exchange for a reward without saying so. Google's guidelines say not to include, on the page or in markup, reviews that are not based on a genuine experience, or reviews given in exchange for money, discounts or free products without clear and prominent disclosure. Reviews a company controls about itself, placed on its own Organization pages, are not eligible for star ratings either. Google: Review snippet

As for FAQ, Google no longer shows the FAQ rich result in Search and removed its documentation on June 15, 2026. Google: Search documentation updates. A visible FAQ is still worth keeping as plain answers to what buyers ask about contracts, cancellation and data handling. If you mark it up, mark up only the questions shown on the page.

Writing pricing, comparison and case study pages

If you don't publish pricing, an AI either cannot say anything about your price or answers from an outdated third-party page. Even if you can't show every number, you can state the following.

  1. The pricing unit: per user, per location, per transaction, or a flat fee.
  2. What changes between plans: what is included and what only higher plans have. State limits (users, volume, storage) separately from features.
  3. Costs outside the subscription: setup, onboarding, add-ons, data migration.
  4. Contract terms: minimum term, monthly or annual billing, free trial length and what happens after it, how to cancel.
  5. "Contact sales" plans: which company sizes or conditions they are for, and what the quote depends on.
  6. The date: when pricing changes, date it, and update the pricing page, sales materials and review site profiles on the same day.

A comparison page is more useful to buyers and to AI when it is not a ranking with you on top, but a set of selection criteria (company size, required features, integrations, support, pricing unit) that also says which companies would be better served by another option. When you state another vendor's features or prices, add the date you checked and a link to their official page, and fix it when it goes out of date.

A case study should give the customer's industry, employee count, the problem before, the features used and what changed. When you quote a number, say what was measured, over what period and how. Always get the customer's permission before naming them.

Making outside profiles agree

In B2B, AI answers may draw on sources beyond your own site. Google notes that its AI features can surface what is said about brands on blogs, videos and forums, while seeking inauthentic "mentions" across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem. Google: Optimizing for generative AI features

The work here is making your official outside information correct and consistent: profiles on review and comparison sites such as G2, Capterra, ITreview and BOXIL, your LinkedIn company page, press releases and integration marketplaces. Check that your name, URL, service description, category and pricing match your own site. When you ask customers for reviews, follow each site's rules and disclose any incentive.

Making sure AI crawlers can read your pages

OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers; GPTBot is a separate crawler for training, and blocking it is a different decision. OpenAI: overview of crawlers. Perplexity describes PerplexityBot as the crawler that surfaces and links websites in Perplexity search results, and asks site owners to allow it in robots.txt. Perplexity: crawlers

Two problems are common on SaaS sites: pricing tables or feature lists drawn only by JavaScript, and help centers or documents behind a login or a form. Put the facts you want AI to read on pages that open without a login, as text in the HTML.

Checklist before you publish

  1. The home or about page states your legal name, address, contact details and services as text.
  2. Each service page says, in its first few sentences, which job it does and for which industries and company sizes.
  3. The pricing page gives the pricing unit, what is included, setup fees, contract terms and the date it was last changed (terms are stated even if prices are not).
  4. Case studies give industry, size, problem and result, with how any number was measured, and customers have agreed to be named.
  5. Rating and review markup comes only from real reviews, and incentivized reviews are disclosed.
  6. robots.txt does not block OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot, and pricing, features and FAQ can be read as HTML without logging in.
  7. Your name, URL, description and pricing match across review and comparison sites, LinkedIn, press releases and your own site.

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, write about ten questions in the patterns above, without your company's name, put them to ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI, and record whether your name or URL appears and whether the description is correct. FAQ

Where should a B2B or SaaS company start with AI search?

Write the facts buyers compare on (who it is for, features, pricing unit, contract terms, proof) as text on five pages: about, services, pricing, case studies and FAQ. Then add Organization and Service or SoftwareApplication structured data, and make review sites and other outside profiles agree.

Does hiding our pricing hurt us in AI answers?

It leaves AI with nothing to say about your price, so you may be left out of answers to pricing questions, or described from an outdated third-party page. Even without prices, you can state the pricing unit, whether there are setup fees, the contract terms and which company sizes each plan is for.

Will SoftwareApplication markup get my SaaS a rich result?

Google's software app rich result requires a name and price plus either aggregate ratings or reviews. Without real reviews it is not eligible. Never create reviews or publish incentivized ones without disclosure.

Should we create an llms.txt file?

Google says Google Search does not use llms.txt and it neither helps nor harms your visibility there. Having one for other AI services and tools that may read it is fine, but fix your page text and structured data first. What is llms.txt?

Is Nurevo AEO free?

Will this guarantee that AI recommends us?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your company's and services' facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear.
