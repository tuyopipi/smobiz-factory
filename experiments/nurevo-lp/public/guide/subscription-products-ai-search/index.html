Subscription products and AI search: make your cancellation terms, commitment and totals readable

People who ask AI about a subscription want two things: what each delivery costs and what it adds up to, and how and by when they can cancel. The material for that answer is one terms table, written as text on the product page: first and later prices, the minimum commitment, a total, skip and cancellation deadlines and the cancellation method. Keep the same values on the checkout's final summary, in your FAQ and in llms.txt, and AI is less likely to quote only the introductory price or an unqualified "cancel any time".

Written 28 November 2026 (JST). Japanese government pages, schema.org, and Google's and WooCommerce's official documentation were read and checked on the same day.

This guide is for online shops that sell food, cosmetics or household goods on subscription, and for the people who build and run those shops. Shop-wide facts (shipping, returns, stock) are in AI search for online shops and sizes and colours in making product sizes, colours and specs readable by AI. This page covers only the terms of a recurring delivery.

This is not legal advice; check your disclosures against your regulator's guidance or with a lawyer. AI answers change from one query to the next, and nothing here guarantees that AI will mention you.

What AI is asked about subscriptions, and the facts each answer needs

More shoppers consult AI before buying. In a 2025 survey of 800 people by Gaprise, 44.0% had used generative AI before a purchase (MarTechLab). A subscription is a contract that continues after the purchase, so the questions are about terms more than the product.

QuestionFacts the answer needsWhat tends to happen if they are missing
How much per delivery?First price and later price, tax and shipping included or notOnly the low introductory price is quoted
Is there a minimum term?Whether there is a commitment, and how many deliveriesAI says "cancel any time" with no conditions
What will it cost in total?The total for the commitment, or an example totalThe shopper has to work it out
Can I skip or change the interval?How to skip, pause or change, and the deadlineAI says it cannot tell
How do I cancel?Method (account page, phone, email), hours, deadline, feeReviews on other sites become the source
How does it compare?All of the above in a comparable formShops with fewer facts are harder to recommend

Any of these can be answered when the facts are written as text on a page. Terms that exist only inside a banner image or a pop-up may not be read, depending on how each AI search system fetches pages.

Put the terms in one table

Keep the terms in one place, one item per line. Short labelled lines are easy for AI to extract and for people to compare. Here is an example for a fictional coffee subscription.

[Subscription terms] House blend coffee beans subscription (200 g)
- Each delivery: 200 g of beans, 1 bag
- Interval: every 4 weeks (choose 2, 4 or 6 weeks)
- Price: first delivery $12.00, then $16.00 per delivery (tax and shipping included)
- Minimum commitment: none (cancel any time after the first delivery)
- Example total: $44.00 if you receive 3 deliveries
- Billing: card charged on each shipping day
- Skip, pause or change interval: in your account, up to 5 days before the next shipping day
- How to cancel: the "Cancel subscription" button in your account, or call +1-000-000-0000 (weekdays 10am–5pm)
- Cancellation deadline: 5 days before the next shipping day (after that, one more delivery ships)
- Cancellation fee: none
- Returns: unopened, within 8 days of arrival (we pay return shipping on the first delivery)
Last updated: 28 November 2026

Four habits matter. Put the first and later prices on the same line. If there is no minimum commitment, write "none"; AI cannot infer absence. Express deadlines relative to the next shipping day. Add a last-updated date and change it whenever a price or deadline changes.

Reuse the same table on the product page, the subscription help page, the checkout's final summary and your FAQ answers. Wording can vary; numbers and deadlines must not.

Match the checkout's final summary

Many jurisdictions require the key terms of a subscription to be shown before the order is placed, so the checkout already contains most of the facts AI needs.

In the United States, the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA) requires online sellers to clearly disclose all material terms before taking billing information, obtain express informed consent and provide a simple way to stop recurring charges. The FTC's Click-to-Cancel rule, adopted in October 2024, was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025, and the FTC announced new rulemaking on 11 March 2026 (Gibson Dunn). About 30 states have their own auto-renewal laws (Jones Day).

In the United Kingdom, the subscription contracts regime of the DMCC Act is expected to come into force in January 2027, covering pre-contract information, renewal reminders, a 14-day cooling-off period and easy exit; secondary legislation and guidance were still awaited as of 22 October 2026 (DWF).

In Japan, the amended Act on Specified Commercial Transactions (in force 1 June 2022) requires six items on the final confirmation screen of an online order (Consumer Affairs Agency, Japanese; item list as explained by Saitama Prefecture):

Final-screen item (Japan)For a subscriptionLine in the terms table
QuantityContents per delivery, number of deliveries, periodEach delivery, minimum commitment
PricePrice of each delivery and the total to be paidPrice, example total
Payment timing and methodWhen and how the card is chargedBilling
Delivery timingWhen it arrives, intervalInterval
Application deadlineIf there is one(add if applicable)
Withdrawal and cancellationMethod, deadline, feeHow to cancel, deadline, fee

The Saitama page gives a small subscription notice next to a large "free trial" and a "cancel any time" with hidden conditions as examples of misleading displays; a consumer misled this way may cancel the application, and violations can lead to administrative action and penalties (Saitama Prefecture).

The same values help AI. When the product page carries exactly what the final summary shows, the page AI reads and the screen the customer agrees to say the same thing. If the product page shows only the introductory price and the terms appear only at checkout, AI is likely to see only the introductory price: the checkout sits behind a cart or a login that AI search crawlers normally do not enter.

What structured data can and cannot say

Google's merchant listing structured data documentation has no property for a subscription's interval or commitment. It documents UnitPriceSpecification for unit pricing, strikethrough (original) prices and member prices (Google).

The schema.org vocabulary defines referenceQuantity (the quantity a price refers to) and billingDuration, which "can be used, for example, to model the contractual duration of a subscription or payment plan" (UnitPriceSpecification, billingDuration). If you add markup, it could look like this, but Google does not say it uses it, so it ranks below the text table in priority.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Offer",
  "url": "https://shop.example.com/product/coffee-subscription/",
  "price": "16.00",
  "priceCurrency": "USD",
  "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock",
  "priceSpecification": {
    "@type": "UnitPriceSpecification",
    "price": "16.00",
    "priceCurrency": "USD",
    "valueAddedTaxIncluded": true,
    "referenceQuantity": { "@type": "QuantitativeValue", "value": 4, "unitCode": "WEE" }
  }
}
</script>
* Price and URL are placeholders. Change the offers of the Product that
  WooCommerce already outputs (through a plugin or filter); do not add a
  second Product. Google does not document this markup for subscriptions.

WooCommerce already outputs a Product and Offer for each product, so adding a separate Product would describe the same item twice. Change WooCommerce's Offer if you can; otherwise leave the markup alone and invest in the text table and FAQ.

Steps in WordPress (WooCommerce Subscriptions)

According to WooCommerce's documentation (updated 25 November 2026), a subscription product has a subscription price, a payment schedule (every 1 to 6 days, weeks, months or years), when to stop renewing, an optional sign-up fee and an optional free trial. The sign-up fee is added to the first payment: $75 per month with a $200 sign-up fee means $275 at checkout (WooCommerce).

  1. 1. Set the price, schedule, length, sign-up fee and trial on the product, save, and preview what customers see
  2. 2. Paste the terms table into the product description as text and check that its numbers match the preview
  3. 3. Create one help page for skipping and cancelling and link to it from the description
  4. 4. Run a test order up to the final summary and check that every term in the table appears there
  5. 5. Enter FAQ questions (minimum term, cancellation deadline, pausing) in Nurevo AEO settings and place the Nurevo FAQ block on the help page; FAQPage markup is output on that page only
  6. 6. Set the Nurevo AEO site type to Online shop and add your return and shipping policy URLs. llms.txt lists one price per product, so make clear in the product name or help page which delivery that price applies to
  7. 7. If you add another structured data plugin, check that it does not create a second Product

Each product line in llms.txt carries name, URL, price, currency, stock, SKU and categories, but not the interval or commitment. To surface the terms there too, link the help page as a main page or add lines through the developer filter webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines.

Prompts for checking AI answers

After publishing the table, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews questions like these. Changes can take time to show up, so repeat the same prompts every few weeks and log the answer and date.

  1. "Is there a minimum number of deliveries for (product) from (shop)?"
  2. "How much will (product) cost in total if I receive three deliveries?"
  3. "How and by when do I cancel the (shop) subscription?"
  4. "Which (category) subscriptions have no minimum commitment?"

When an answer differs from your table, look at the page AI cited. Old campaign pages, comparison sites and reviews are frequent sources. Update or redirect your own old pages. Nurevo AEO's Pro plan measures appearances for prompts like these on Perplexity (ChatGPT when configured).

Checklist

  1. The terms table is text on the product page, not an image
  2. First and later prices are on the same line
  3. The commitment says "none" or a number of deliveries
  4. A total, or totals by number of deliveries, is given
  5. Skip, pause and cancellation deadlines count from the next shipping day
  6. The cancellation method, hours and fee are given
  7. Numbers match the checkout's final summary
  8. Numbers match your FAQ answers
  9. No old campaign page still shows different terms
  10. The table has a last-updated date that changes with the terms

FAQ

Should the terms go on the product page or a separate page?

Put the terms table on the product page and the skip and cancel steps on a linked help page. Keep the numbers and deadlines identical on both.

Can I headline "first box free"?

You can, but put the later price and any commitment right next to it at the same size. Small subscription notices beside large trial offers are a commonly cited example of a misleading display.

Does a minimum commitment hurt my chances in AI answers?

There is no evidence either way. Leaving it out, though, makes it more likely that AI says "cancel any time" or relies on complaints in reviews. Stating the commitment and total is safer.

Can structured data alone carry the terms?

No. Google's product structured data has no property for a subscription's interval or commitment. The text table matters most; treat markup as a supplement.

What should I update when I change the WooCommerce Subscriptions settings?

The terms table, the help page, the FAQ and the final summary, all on the same day, plus the table's last-updated date. llms.txt takes the price from WooCommerce.

Does Nurevo AEO output subscription terms automatically?

No. It publishes the shop (OnlineStore), the return policy and an llms.txt product list with one price per product, and allows AI search crawlers. Write the interval and commitment in the text table and FAQ.
