AI search for lesson studios: how to get AI to read your yearly fees, make-up rules, trial lessons and home-studio location correctly

AI search optimization for a music, cooking, calligraphy or craft studio comes down to answering, in plain text with amounts and rules, the questions students and parents now ask AI: what a year really costs once you add the registration fee and materials, whether a missed lesson can be made up, and where a home studio actually is. A "lessons from $X a month" on its own leaves AI unable to answer either the cost or the logistics. Then you publish the same facts consistently in structured data, llms.txt, your Google Business Profile and any lesson directories you are listed in.

Written November 17, 2026 (JST). schema.org types were checked that day in schema-dts 2.1.0. The Kyoto Prefecture consumer page was read the same day (page updated April 30, 2025). The Google pages and Japan's National Tax Agency page were checked between October 29 and November 1, 2026. Examples of tax and consumer rules come from Japan; check the rules where you operate.

This guide is for hobby and arts studios that adults and children attend: music, cooking, calligraphy, flower arranging, tea ceremony, crafts, painting and similar. Tutoring schools and gyms or yoga studios raise different questions and rules, so they are not covered here. AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your studio or rank it anywhere.

What students and parents ask AI

Questions about lessons stack a subject, an area, who the lessons are for, how they run and what they cost. For example: "a piano teacher near [station] who takes adult beginners twice a month", "a cooking class I can drop into on a weekday evening", "calligraphy for a five-year-old where parents can watch" or "a studio with make-up lessons for about $120 a month". For an AI to name your studio, the text on your site has to show that you meet those conditions.

Google says there are no additional requirements or special structured data for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that ordinary SEO basics, structured data that matches the visible text and an up-to-date Business Profile are what matter. Google: AI features and your website. AI search for a lesson studio is not a trick; it is writing down, accurately, the numbers and rules people use to compare.

Price the year, not just the monthly fee

Lesson costs are split into the monthly fee, a registration fee, books or ingredients, facility fees, recital or exam fees, and buying or renting an instrument or tools. If only the monthly fee is prominent, AI can present it as the whole cost. For each course, show these items and an example yearly total in a table.

ItemHow to write itWatch out for
Monthly fee3 x 30-minute lessons a month, $XNumber and length of lessons on the same line; what happens in months with fewer weeks
Registration$X (waived if you join on the trial day, for example)If it can be waived, say when and until when
Books and materialsMethod book $X / ingredients $Y per classWhether they are in the monthly fee or extra
Recitals and examsOne recital a year, $X to take part (optional)Whether taking part is required
Instrument and toolsLoaned for the trial; bring your own after joiningWhether you must buy, whether you can rent, rough cost
Example yearly totalChildren's piano, 3 a month: registration + 12 months + books + recital = $XTwo or three common ways to attend

In Japan, prices shown to consumers in advance must include consumption tax, on any medium (for example "11,000円（税込）"). National Tax Agency: total price display (Japanese). Wherever you operate, say whether tax is included.

Cooking classes and workshops that mix drop-in, multi-class passes and monthly plans should list each price separately, with any expiry (how many months a pass is valid).

Put make-up, pause and withdrawal rules in a table

What happens when you miss a lesson is one of the first things people weigh, yet many studio sites only state it in the sign-up paperwork. What is not on the page, AI cannot answer, or it guesses from other studios. At minimum, put the following in a table on the page:

  1. Make-ups: how much notice is needed, how many per month, and by when they must be used (for example, by the end of the next month).
  2. Lessons cancelled by the teacher, holidays or bad weather: made up or refunded.
  3. Pausing: for how many months, and any fee while paused.
  4. Withdrawal: how many days' notice, by what method, and what happens to prepaid fees or passes.
  5. Switching to online: whether a lesson can be taken online if a student is unwell.

Contract rules can depend on the type of lessons. In Japan, contracts for foreign-language conversation schools, tutoring schools, home tutors and computer schools that run over two months and cost over 50,000 yen are "specified continuous services" under the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, with an eight-day cooling-off period from receipt of the contract document and caps on early-termination charges. Cooking and music classes are not among the seven covered services. Kyoto Prefecture: early termination of continuous services (Japanese). If you also run language or computer courses, put the legally required terms on those course pages, and check the equivalent rules where you operate. This article is not legal advice.

Trial lessons and teachers

On your trial-lesson page, give the fee (free or paid), length, who it is for, what to bring and wear, whether parents can watch, and how joining is handled afterwards (for example, that nobody has to decide on the day). "30-minute trial, $10, credited against the first month if you join" lets AI pass on the condition with the price; "free trial" alone does not.

For teachers, give the name, background and the courses they teach, and when you list a credential, name the body that issued it. Many lesson credentials are issued by private associations, and a credential name on its own tells neither AI nor people much. Home studios: the address and structured data

Many teachers run lessons from a room in their home. Even if you do not want to publish your street address, AI needs to know roughly where you are, or you will rarely come up for questions that name an area. The basic rule is to decide how much to publish and keep exactly that much consistent across your site, structured data and directory listings.

SetupWhat the site saysStructured data
Street address publicAddress, directions from the station, parkingInclude the address
Neighborhood only (details after sign-up)"[Neighborhood], [city], 5 minutes' walk from [station]. We send the exact address after you book."Leave the street address out. Never put an address in structured data that the page does not show
Community hall or rented roomThe venue name and address, and which class meets whereState the venue on each class page in the text instead of a studio address
Online onlyWhich tools you use, time slots, time zones you can serveNo address

Structured data has to match the visible page. If the page shows only the neighborhood but the structured data carries the street address, the address you meant to keep private is still read by AI and search engines. Google's local business rich result requires a name and an address. Google: LocalBusiness. Without an address you are not eligible for that display, but Google says no special structured data is needed to appear in its AI features.

Whether you can hide your address on Google Business Profile depends on things such as whether students come to you. Check the current conditions in the Google Business Profile guidelines before you set it up.

Structured data: there is no lesson-studio type

schema.org has no type for music schools, cooking schools or similar lesson studios. There is EducationalOrganization, but its subtypes are colleges and universities, elementary, middle and high schools, preschools and schools, and it is not one of the LocalBusiness types. schema.org: EducationalOrganization. For a studio run by one teacher or a small company, the sensible shape is a LocalBusiness with each course linked as a Service, with its price as an Offer. schema.org: LocalBusiness

schema.org also has Course and CourseInstance, an individual run of a course whose courseMode can say "online" or "onsite". schema.org: CourseInstance. Nurevo AEO does not output these types. Even if you add them, what helps AI answer how lessons run is the days, times and in-person or online format written in your text. Do not let structured data stand in for a timetable.

Pre-publish checklist

  1. Each course states its age range, level, class size, days and times, and whether it is in person or online.
  2. The monthly fee shows the number and length of lessons; registration, materials, recital fees and instruments are shown with whether each is included.
  3. Two or three example yearly totals for common ways of attending are shown, with tax treatment.
  4. Make-up deadlines and limits, teacher cancellations, and pause and withdrawal notice and fees are in a table.
  5. The trial lesson's fee, length, what to bring, whether parents can watch and how joining is handled are stated.
  6. Teachers have a background and the courses they teach, and credentials name the issuing body.
  7. You have decided how much of your location to publish and kept it the same across your site, structured data, Google Business Profile and directories. No street address appears in structured data that the page does not show.
  8. Any course that counts as a regulated continuous service (language or computer courses in Japan) has its cooling-off and early-termination terms on its page.
  9. llms.txt lists your course, fee, make-up rule and trial pages. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, write five to ten questions a student or parent would ask, combining subject, area (station or neighborhood), who the lessons are for, days, budget and make-up rules. Put them to ChatGPT and Perplexity and record whether your studio appears by name or URL, and whether fees and make-up rules are stated correctly. Answers vary between runs, so repeat the same questions over time. Recording separately around the busy sign-up season and before summer short courses helps you see trends. FAQ

Where should a lesson studio start with AI search?

Write, as text and tables on your pages, each course's audience, days and times and monthly fee (number and length of lessons), an example yearly total including registration, materials and recital fees, your make-up, pause and withdrawal rules, and your trial-lesson terms. Then make your name, phone and location match across structured data and your Google Business Profile.

I teach from home and don't want to publish my street address. Will that hurt?

Without a street address, the neighborhood, nearest station and walking time in your text still give AI something to match area questions. Do not put the street address in structured data either. In Nurevo AEO, leave the address field empty: as long as a phone number or hours are set, a LocalBusiness is published without an address.

Which structured data type should a lesson studio use?

schema.org has no lesson-studio type, and the subtypes of EducationalOrganization are for schools. Publish a small studio as a LocalBusiness and link your courses as Service with Offer.

Do we really need to publish our make-up rules?

Yes. What happens after a missed lesson is a common question before joining, but it often lives only in sign-up paperwork, where AI cannot read it. State make-up deadlines, limits and expiry, and the notice needed to pause or withdraw, on the page.

Do cooling-off rules apply to cooking or music classes?

In Japan, the specified continuous services are beauty treatments, cosmetic medical treatments, foreign-language conversation schools, home tutors, tutoring schools, computer schools and matchmaking services; cooking and music classes are not among them. Other rules can apply depending on how a contract is sold, and rules differ by country, so check with a consumer advice service. This article is not legal advice.

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear.
