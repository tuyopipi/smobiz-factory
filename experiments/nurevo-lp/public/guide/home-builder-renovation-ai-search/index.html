AI search for home builders and remodelers: how to get AI to read your service area, types of work and projects correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a home builder or remodeling company comes down to publishing, accurately and consistently, which towns you work in, which kinds of work you take on (new homes, kitchens and bathrooms, siding and roofing, seismic retrofits, insulation) and which licenses and qualifications you hold, both in your page text and in schema.org structured data such as GeneralContractor or HomeAndConstructionBusiness. Beyond that, the biggest difference in this trade comes from writing each project as a record with its location, building, scope, schedule and cost.

Written November 12, 2026 (JST). Information from Google, OpenAI and Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency was checked on each official page on the dates given in the text. Contractor licensing and door-to-door sales rules are only named here, without thresholds or deadlines; check them against current official sources.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your company's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your company or rank it anywhere.

What people ask AI about building and remodeling

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews to find a builder or remodeler, the AI reads what it can find on the web and answers with a few company names. The questions combine area, building, type of work and conditions: "a builder in my city who does custom homes with natural materials", "a company that can seismically retrofit a 40-year-old wooden house", "a remodeler who can replace a condo bathroom in one day", "a local company for exterior painting in my town".

To answer questions like these, an AI needs material that says "this company does this kind of work, on this kind of building, in this area". A company page that only says "from new builds to remodels, we do it all" tells the AI nothing about what you are good at. What it needs is not marketing language but facts it can check: service area, types of work and projects.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special schema.org structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual basics still matter, such as making structured data match the visible text (checked November 1, 2026). Google: AI features and your website. The work is getting your company's facts right, not finding an AI trick.

Decide first: your business type in structured data

In structured data, the kind of business is expressed as a schema.org type. Types that fit construction and home improvement companies include HomeAndConstructionBusiness (home improvement and construction in general) and, within it, more specific types such as GeneralContractor (a company that takes on whole projects), Plumber and Electrician. Google's local business documentation asks you to use the most specific type possible (checked October 29, 2026). Google: local business structured data.

This is a guide. Match the type to the work at the center of what you actually do. These are schema.org types; none of them guarantees any display in Google.

If your type is still Organization or LocalBusiness, your structured data does not say you are a construction or home improvement company. Equally, avoid a type for work you hardly ever do. A company that does both new builds and remodels picks one main type and covers the other through services (below) and page text.

What matters most is that the same fact has the same value everywhere: your site, structured data, Google Business Profile, your profiles on listing and referral sites, and llms.txt. When you move, change your days closed or renew a license, update all of them together.

Turning project pages into records AI can use

The most important pages on a builder's or remodeler's site are the project pages. Yet many of them are photos and a single line ("a bright new kitchen"), so neither AI nor homeowners can tell where it was, what kind of house, what was done, what it cost or how long it took. To answer "seismic retrofit of a 40-year-old wooden house", a project like that has to exist in text. Write each project as a record with these items.

The items are the facts homeowners and AI need when comparing companies. If a project can't fill every item, write the ones you can, accurately.

Being able to read combinations of area and type of work matters more than the number of projects. A project list that can be filtered by town and by type of work also helps homeowners. Mass-producing area pages that only swap the place name works against you. On an area page, show the projects you actually did there and what only a local company would know, such as the climate and the kind of housing.

Writing about costs and subsidies so AI doesn't repeat old numbers

"How much does a bathroom remodel cost?" is a question AI hears often. Giving a cost guide helps, but an unsupported "from X" sets expectations that your estimate won't meet and leads to disputes. Price claims in advertising are also regulated (in Japan, by the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, among others).

  1. Base cost guides on your own completed projects, and say whether tax is included, what work is covered (demolition, disposal, design) and as of when.
  2. Leave claims you cannot back up, such as "cheapest" or "No. 1 in the area", out of both your text and your markup.
  3. Subsidies and grants change from year to year. Give the program name and year, a link to the official government page and the date you checked, and rewrite the page when the program closes.
  4. Put a last-updated date on price pages too. If an old price page stays up, AI may present it as today's price.

Remodeling contracts made through door-to-door sales are regulated in many countries. In Japan, the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions sets rules such as written contracts and cooling-off. If you sell door to door, check the current rules (in Japan, the Consumer Affairs Agency's guide, in Japanese) and describe your contract process accurately on your site. This article does not explain deadlines or other conditions.

Reviews and ratings: what not to add for AI

Some guides recommend adding star ratings (AggregateRating) to your company pages in structured data. Marking up reviews of your own business on your own site will not get you review stars in Google. In 2019 Google said that self-serving reviews, meaning reviews about a business placed on that business's own website, aren't allowed for LocalBusiness and Organization. Google Search Central blog, September 16, 2019

Rules on undisclosed advertising differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you. In Japan, stealth marketing has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations since October 1, 2023, and the Consumer Affairs Agency treats reviews that a business asks or instructs third parties to post as that business's advertising (checked November 1, 2026). Consumer Affairs Agency: stealth marketing (Japanese). Publish real client comments, without hiding that they are promotional. The natural place for them is inside the project record, with the client's consent.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Name, address, phone, opening hours and days closed match across your site, Google Business Profile and listing sites.
  2. Your structured data type is not Organization but one that fits your work, such as GeneralContractor.
  3. Your service area is written in concrete town names in your page text.
  4. Your types of work list only what you actually take on, and each is added as a service.
  5. Your license number, registrations and qualified staff are written as text on your company page.
  6. Each project shows location (town), building, scope, schedule, cost (tax, scope, date) and publication date.
  7. Cost and subsidy pages show an update date and sources, and closed programs are removed.
  8. Reviews of your own business are not marked up as ratings, and there are no unsupported superlatives.
  9. llms.txt lists your company overview, service area, services, projects and contact page. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, ask AI the questions homeowners would ask (area plus building plus type of work plus conditions), without your company's name, and see whether you come up by name or URL. Also ask by name ("What are X Builders' days closed and service area?") and check that the facts in the answer are correct. Answers change between runs, so ask the same questions a few times, spread over time, and record them. FAQ

Where should a builder or remodeler start with AI search?

Write your service area, types of work, licenses and qualifications, and company facts (address, opening hours, days closed) correctly as text on your pages. Then set your structured data type to one that fits your work, such as GeneralContractor, add each type of work as a service, and make sure everything matches your Google Business Profile and listing site profiles.

Should I post lots of projects?

Substance matters more than number. Projects with location, building, scope, schedule and cost are more useful to AI and homeowners than many photo-only entries. Aim for at least one representative project for each combination of area and type of work you want to be known for.

Can I write prices as "from X"?

If you do, base them on real projects and say whether tax is included, what work is covered and as of when. A bare minimum price with no basis tends to cause disputes when the estimate comes in, and advertising rules apply.

If I'm listed on referral and listing sites, do I still need to fix my own site?

AI sometimes cites listing and referral sites, but your own site is where you can describe your specialties, service area and projects in detail. Keep your company details the same in both places and put the facts that only you can provide on your own site.

Will this guarantee my company appears in ChatGPT?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear.
