Images and video in AI search: how to get alt text, captions and video transcripts read by AI

If you want ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI features to use what your photos and videos show, the basic rule is to say it in text on the page as well. Google says it understands images through alt text, the surrounding page and computer vision together, but other AI companies do not publish how far they read the images on a page. Don't publish price lists or menus only as images, give meaningful photos alt text that says what they show, and put a summary and transcript next to each important video. In OtterlyAI's study, YouTube videos were cited most often by Perplexity and Google's AI features.

Written 26 November 2026 (JST). It is for people who run sites heavy on photos and video, and for the agencies that build them. It includes WordPress steps and a command-line check.

AI companies change their systems without notice, and AI answers vary by day and wording. These steps prepare your images and video to be read; they do not guarantee that you appear in AI answers.

Images on your site: what goes in the alt text and what goes in the body

Not every image needs long alt text. Leave decorative images empty (alt="") and give meaningful ones a short description of what they show. Facts you want AI to answer with, such as prices and hours, belong in the body text rather than the alt text.

Alt text is also what screen reader users hear. Write a sentence that tells someone who cannot see the photo what it shows, not a list of keywords. Google also lists keyword-stuffed alt text as a bad example (Google: Image SEO).

Other things to tidy up for images

  1. Captions: a sentence under a photo is read as part of the page. For a project photo, include facts such as "5 days on site, about $6,000 including tax".
  2. File names: use names that say what the image is, such as bathroom-unit-after.jpg rather than IMG_0023.jpg (Google recommends descriptive file names too).
  3. Put explanatory text near the image: Google says it also uses captions and the text around an image.
  4. The page's main image: Google says it picks a representative image from og:image or structured data. Set a photo that shows the content, not a logo or a text-heavy graphic.
  5. Formats: Google supports BMP, GIF, JPEG, PNG, WebP, SVG and AVIF. When you use srcset or picture, keep a normal src as a fallback.

Video on your site: put a summary and transcript on the page

Embedding a YouTube video does not put its captions or description into your page's HTML. The embed is an iframe, and the content lives on YouTube's page. If you want the facts in a video to be read on your own page, write them below the video.

  1. Give important videos (what to expect on a first visit, how a treatment or job works, how to use a product) a page of their own. Google calls a page whose main purpose is showing one video a watch page and recommends creating them; a blog post that discusses an embedded video is not a watch page (Google: Video SEO).
  2. Write a three-to-five-line summary below the video: the prices, timings, who it is for and any cautions the video mentions.
  3. Add a transcript below that. You can start from YouTube's automatic captions, but always fix misheard names, product names and numbers. If it is long, you can put it in a Details block; text inside it is still in the HTML (AI crawlers and JavaScript).
  4. If the video shows prices or phone numbers on screen, check those numbers are in the body too. Assume on-screen text is not read.
  5. Add VideoObject structured data. Google requires name, thumbnailUrl and uploadDate, and recommends description, duration, embedUrl and others (Google: Video structured data). It still recommends structured data when you use a third-party player such as YouTube.
  6. To point to key moments, use Clip structured data or timestamps in the YouTube description. Google supports Clip in all Search languages, and YouTube description timestamps work in all languages.

Videos on YouTube: descriptions and chapters

OtterlyAI analysed more than 100 million citations collected over 30 days across ChatGPT, AI Overviews, AI Mode, Perplexity, Copilot and Gemini, and summarised patterns in how YouTube videos are cited (OtterlyAI: The YouTube Citation Study 2026, 2 March 2026).

FindingFigure
Share of cited YouTube items that were regular videos vs Shorts94% vs 5.7%

The authors note that correlation is not causation, and that because they studied only videos already cited, the results describe repeat citation more than first selection. Even so, two working assumptions follow: small businesses' low-view videos can be cited, and description text can be a useful signal.

In the description, write the question the video answers and the key points of the answer, facts such as your name, area and prices, and the URL of the matching page on your site. For chapters, follow YouTube's rules: the first timestamp is 00:00, at least three in ascending order, and each chapter at least 10 seconds long (YouTube Help: Video chapters). Don't leave automatic captions as they are; correct names and numbers.

WordPress steps

  1. Alt text: open the image in the Media Library and fill in "Alternative Text". The same field appears in the Image block's sidebar settings. Leave decorative images empty.
  2. Captions: write a one-sentence fact under the image with the Image block's "Add caption".
  3. Price lists and menus: if you publish a table as an image, write the same content in a Table block as well. You can keep the image.
  4. Video: place YouTube with the Embed block (YouTube), then add the summary in a Paragraph block and the transcript in a Details block or paragraphs below it.
  5. Featured image: set a featured image that shows the content on posts and pages. The free Nurevo AEO plugin adds the featured image as image in that page's structured data (WebPage or BlogPosting), and the main product image for WooCommerce products.
  6. VideoObject: Nurevo AEO does not output VideoObject on its own. To add one to a video page, use the developer filter webmcp_canary_schema_graph as below. It works alongside Yoast SEO, Rank Math, AIOSEO, SEO SIMPLE PACK and VK All in One Expansion Unit without duplicating structured data. If a video or SEO plugin already outputs VideoObject, don't add a second one.
<?php
// Add a VideoObject to the page (ID 123) that carries the video.
// Put this in a child theme's functions.php or a small plugin.
add_filter('webmcp_canary_schema_graph', function (array $bundle) {
    if (!is_page(123)) {
        return $bundle;
    }
    $bundle['extra'][] = array(
        '@type'        => 'VideoObject',
        'name'         => 'Your first visit: from consultation to finish (6 min)',
        'description'  => 'How booking, timing and payment work, and what the price includes.',
        'thumbnailUrl' => 'https://example.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/flow-thumb.jpg',
        'uploadDate'   => '2026-11-20T10:00:00+09:00',
        'duration'     => 'PT6M30S',
        'embedUrl'     => 'https://www.youtube.com/embed/XXXXXXXXXXX',
        'publisher'    => array('@id' => $bundle['org_id']),
    );
    return $bundle;
});

Change the title, description, thumbnail URL, upload date and duration (PT6M30S means 6 minutes 30 seconds) to match the real video. Then confirm VideoObject appears in the page source and check it in Google's Rich Results Test.

How to check

You can check a page's images and text from a terminal. Try it on image-heavy pages such as price lists and menus.

# 1) Count images on the page with no alt or an empty alt
URL="https://example.com/menu/"
curl -sS -L --compressed "$URL" -o page.html
echo "images:     $(grep -o '<img[^>]*>' page.html | wc -l)"
echo "no alt:     $(grep -o '<img[^>]*>' page.html | grep -vc 'alt=')"
echo "empty alt:  $(grep -o '<img[^>]*>' page.html | grep -c 'alt=""')"

# 2) List the alt text in use (look for "image1", "IMG_0023" and the like)
grep -o '<img[^>]*>' page.html | grep -o 'alt="[^"]*"' | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn | head -30

# 3) Are the prices and times that sit in images also in the body text?
for WORD in 'Haircut $45' 'Last booking 7pm' '3 parking spaces'; do
  if grep -qF "$WORD" page.html; then echo "found    $WORD"; else echo "MISSING  $WORD"; fi
done

# 4) Does the video page carry a summary, a transcript and VideoObject?
grep -c 'VideoObject' page.html
grep -o '<iframe[^>]*youtube[^>]*>' page.html | head -5

If many images have no alt, add alt text in the Media Library. An empty alt is fine for decorative images. Facts that step 3 reports as MISSING probably exist only inside images or video, so add them to the body.

Without the command line, open the page, right-click "View page source" and search for your prices or hours with Ctrl+F (Command+F on Mac). Text that exists only inside an image will not be found there.

After fixing, ask AI tools the same questions and watch for changes. Don't judge from one answer; repeat the same questions on different days (how to measure AI visibility). Nurevo Pro measures whether you appear in AI answers (currently on Perplexity, in beta).

Checklist

  1. Everything in price list, menu and opening-hours images is also in the body as text (a table)
  2. Meaningful images have short alt text that says what they show
  3. Decorative images have an empty alt
  4. Alt text is not a list of keywords
  5. Project and portfolio photos have captions with facts
  6. Important photos are not only in CSS backgrounds or late-loading galleries
  7. Important videos have their own page, with a summary and transcript in the body
  8. Prices and phone numbers shown on screen in videos are also in the body
  9. Video pages carry VideoObject (name, thumbnailUrl, uploadDate)
  10. YouTube descriptions carry the key points and your page URL, and chapters follow the rules

FAQ

Do ChatGPT and Perplexity read text inside the images on a page?

Their documentation does not say, so it is not known. ChatGPT can read an image you send it, but whether it reads images on pages when searching is a separate question. Assume it does not, and put prices, hours and other facts in the body.

Should every image have alt text?

Give it to meaningful images and leave decorative ones empty. Filling every image gets in the way of screen reader users and makes the important descriptions harder to find.

How long should alt text be?

There is no fixed length. Make it long enough to tell someone who cannot see the photo what it shows in a phrase. Put detailed facts such as prices in the body, not the alt text.

If my videos are on YouTube, do I need them on my site?

YouTube videos can be cited, but the source shown is then YouTube's page. If you want your own page to be the source, create a video page on your site with a summary and transcript.

Can videos with few views be cited?

In OtterlyAI's study, 40.83% of cited videos had fewer than 1,000 views. Making clear in text which question a video answers appears to matter more than views, though nothing guarantees a citation.

Should I stop making Shorts?

In OtterlyAI's study 94% of citations went to regular-length videos, but that does not make Shorts pointless. If AI citations are the goal, prioritise videos of several minutes that answer a question, and use Shorts for other purposes.

Will VideoObject get my video into AI answers?

That cannot be guaranteed. VideoObject helps Google understand a video; how other AI tools use it is not published. A summary and transcript in the body reach every AI tool more reliably.
