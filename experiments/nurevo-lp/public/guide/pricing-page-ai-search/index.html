How to write a pricing page AI reads correctly: tax, conditions and price changes

To get AI to quote your prices correctly, write them as HTML text rather than images or PDFs, and put six things on the same line as each amount: whether tax is included, what the price is for (per visit, per month, per person), what is included, any conditions, and when the price applies. If your work is quote-based, don't stop at "contact us": give a starting price, what drives the price, and worked examples. When prices change, update every page and every piece of structured data that carries them on the same day, and don't leave old prices standing.

Written November 20, 2026 (JST). The fields a B2B or SaaS pricing page needs are in AI search for B2B and SaaS, and how to write FAQs is in FAQ pages and AI search. This guide is about writing prices themselves, for any kind of business: shops, studios, professional services, online stores and B2B.

AI answers change by day, by wording and by service. The steps here make a page whose prices AI is less likely to misread, and show how to check; they do not guarantee you will appear in an answer or that a price will be quoted correctly.

For a sense of how often AI search actually uses a company's own pricing page: the AI search measurement company Profound looked at about 7,600 answers from six AI engines to pricing questions about 100 cloud companies. The company's pricing page was cited somewhere in 46% of answers, and was the first source in 12%; the rest leaned on comparison sites, forums and the like (Profound, 2026-09-03; the vendor's own research, covering SaaS companies). An accurate pricing page is the starting point, and the prices listed elsewhere need to match it.

Six things on every price line

Asked "how much is X?", an AI pulls the amount out of the page. If the conditions aren't near the amount, it drops them, or attaches a condition from another line. Write each price with these six things on the same line (or the same table row).

ItemExampleWhat goes wrong without it
Amount$55—
Tax(including tax)A pre-tax price is passed on as the total
Unitper visit / per month / per person / per hourA monthly fee is passed on as a one-off
What's includedwash and blow-dry includedQuestions about extra charges go unanswered
Conditions$5 off for students with IDA conditional price is passed on as everyone's price
When it appliesfrom December 1, 2026No way to tell old from new

Weak: "Haircut from 50". Strong: "Haircut (wash and blow-dry included), $55 per visit including tax. $5 off for students with ID. Prices from December 1, 2026."

Rules on showing tax differ by country. In Japan, prices shown to consumers in advance must include consumption tax, on websites and ads too (National Tax Agency: Tax Answer No. 6902, Japanese). Wherever you are, writing the tax status next to each amount works better than a single "all prices include tax" line at the top of the table, because AI often lifts a single row on its own.

Quote-based work: give a starting price, the drivers and examples

Building work, professional fees, design projects and call-out services all vary by job. Even so, "contact us for a quote" leaves an AI with nothing better than "prices aren't published", and competitors that do publish prices tend to be named first. You may not have one exact price, but you can always give three things.

  1. A starting price: "from $300 including tax", with the cheapest conditions ("one room up to 10 m², weekday")
  2. What drives the price: size, number of items, distance, days, urgency, and the unit rate if you have one
  3. Worked examples: two or three typical jobs with every condition, the amount and the month the example was written

Example: "Exterior painting, two-storey house of about 100 m² including scaffolding: $12,000 including tax (example quote, November 2026). The price depends on area, paint and the state of the walls. Site visits and quotes are free." Written this way, an AI can say "under these conditions, about this much", and people can judge their budget before they get in touch.

Say "free" plainly where something is free. Quotes, first consultations, call-out fees and cancellations are exactly the things customers wonder about, so a written "no charge" is worth having.

Discounts, promotions and "regular price"

A time-limited price without dates can keep being quoted by AI as the current price after it ends. Give promotions a start and end ("bookings made December 1 to December 31, 2026"), and on the end date remove them or mark them as ended.

Showing a comparison price ("was $80, now $60") is regulated in many places. In Japan, the Consumer Affairs Agency says that if the comparison is a past price that was not "the price at which it was sold over a reasonable recent period", the display may mislead unless it states accurately when and for how long that price applied; prices never actually charged and "manufacturer's suggested prices" that don't exist are also given as problem cases (Consumer Affairs Agency: double price display, Japanese). Check the rules where you sell. AI picks up comparison prices as readily as real ones, so an unfounded "regular price" misleads people and AI alike.

When prices change: leave no old price behind

The most common reason AI quotes an old price is that the old amount still sits somewhere other than the pricing page. Before a change, list every place a price appears.

  1. Search the site for the old amount (in WordPress: posts and pages in the admin, blog posts, news, FAQs, PDF brochures)
  2. On the pricing page, write "Prices changed on December 1, 2026" and say which date the prices apply from
  3. Update structured data amounts (plugin and theme settings, hand-written JSON-LD) the same day
  4. Update your Google Business Profile, booking sites, directories and social profiles the same day
  5. For posts that only show the old price, delete them or add a first line: "Prices in this post are from before the change. See the pricing page for current prices" with a link
  6. Keep a record of the change date and every place you updated, for checking AI answers later

No AI service publishes how long it takes to pick up a new price. Check with the same questions a few days to a few weeks after the change (see the checking section below).

Make prices readable: check images, PDFs, tabs and JavaScript

A price table saved as an image for design reasons can be readable to people and unreadable to AI crawlers. Prices only in a PDF, hidden behind a tab, or drawn in later by JavaScript may or may not be read; it differs by AI service and isn't published. HTML text and table elements are the safe choice. Profound's study also found pricing pages whose content was partly unreadable: of 77 public pricing pages, 57 were in an AI-readable state.

This command shows whether your prices are in the page's HTML as text.

curl -s https://example.com/pricing/ | grep -iE '\$[0-9]|per month|including tax'
# If nothing prints, the prices live in an image, a PDF or JavaScript - not in the HTML

Without a terminal, open the pricing page, view the page source and search for an amount; it tells you the same thing.

Structured data: writing Offer and PriceSpecification

Structured data restates the prices on your page in a form machines read easily. It doesn't replace the page text. Google says AI features need no special structured data, and that structured data must match the visible text (AI features and your website, checked 2026-11-18). If you add it, follow these rules.

  1. price takes digits only: no thousands separators, currency symbols or "from", with "." for decimals; the currency goes in priceCurrency as "USD", "JPY" and so on (schema.org/price usage notes)
  2. Whether tax is included can be stated with valueAddedTaxIncluded (true / false) on a PriceSpecification (PriceSpecification)
  3. Ranges use minPrice and maxPrice; dated prices use validFrom and validThrough (priceValidUntil on an Offer)
  4. Units such as "per month" use billingDuration and unitText on a UnitPriceSpecification, and list vs sale price use priceType (UnitPriceSpecification)
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Service",
  "name": "Haircut (wash and blow-dry included)",
  "provider": { "@id": "https://example.com/#business" },
  "offers": {
    "@type": "Offer",
    "price": 55.00,
    "priceCurrency": "USD",
    "priceSpecification": {
      "@type": "UnitPriceSpecification",
      "price": 55.00,
      "priceCurrency": "USD",
      "valueAddedTaxIncluded": true,
      "unitText": "per visit",
      "validFrom": "2026-12-01"
    }
  }
}
</script>
<!-- Digits only (no commas, currency symbols or "from"). Use the same value as the "$55 including tax" shown on the page -->

Check the syntax with the Schema Markup Validator. Google's requirements for special search displays (rich results) differ by type, so check Google's documentation for the type you are aiming at (we could not open Google's product documentation directly while writing this). We found no official statement that ChatGPT or Perplexity use price markup.

How to check: fix your price questions and keep a record

To see whether your prices come across correctly, ask the same questions to the same AI on different days and record the answers. Don't judge from a single answer.

QuestionWhat to look at
"How much is a haircut at (business)?"Is the amount, the tax status and what's included right?
"Where in (area) can I get (service) for under $100?"Are you on the list for an unbranded question? Price-qualified questions tend to drop pages without prices
"What is (business)'s cancellation fee?"Is the amount or percentage right?
"Did (business) raise its prices?"Is the old price being quoted? Which URL is the source?

Checklist before you publish

  1. Prices are HTML text (not only in an image, PDF or tab)
  2. Each amount says whether tax is included; consumers can see the total
  3. Each amount has a unit (per visit, per month, per person ...)
  4. What's included, extra charges and free items are written down
  5. Quote-based work has a starting price, the price drivers and worked examples
  6. Promotions have start and end dates; comparison prices have a basis
  7. The pricing page says which date the prices apply from
  8. Every old price on the site has been updated
  9. Structured data amounts are digits only and match the page
  10. Google Business Profile, booking sites and directories show the same prices

FAQ

Will publishing prices make AI mention us more?

That can't be guaranteed. But for price-qualified questions such as "under $100", a page without prices gives AI nothing to work with. A starting price and what drives the price at least give AI something it can use.

Can we show pre-tax prices only?

It depends on where you sell. In Japan, prices shown to consumers must include consumption tax (National Tax Agency, Tax Answer No. 6902), though the pre-tax amount may be shown alongside. Wherever you are, stating the tax status next to each amount helps prevent misreading.

Our price table is an image. Is that a problem?

Whether an image can be read differs by AI service and isn't published. You can keep the image for design, but put the same content in HTML text or a table too.

AI keeps quoting our old price. What should we do?

Check the source URL the AI shows, then fix the price on that page or add a note at the top that it shows old prices. Review old posts on your site, your Google Business Profile and directories too. No service publishes how long updates take, so check with the same questions a few days to a few weeks later.

Can structured data alone carry our prices?

No. Structured data supports the prices on the page and must match them. Don't put prices in structured data that aren't on the page.

We quote every job, so we can't give a price.

Even without an exact amount you can give a starting price with its conditions, what changes the price, and two or three worked examples with every condition stated. If quotes are free, say so.

Can I type "from $55" in the WordPress price field?

Nurevo's Price field is published as the structured data price exactly as typed. schema.org asks for digits only, so enter 55.00 and put "from" and the tax status in the description and on the page.
