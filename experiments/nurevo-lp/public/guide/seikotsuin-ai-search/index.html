AI search for seikotsuin and sekkotsuin clinics: getting AI to read your hours, insurance coverage and fees correctly, within Japan's advertising rules

AI search optimization (AEO) for a seikotsuin or sekkotsuin (a clinic run by licensed judo therapists, often called bone-setting clinics) comes down to publishing your reception hours, days closed, location, booking method, which injuries public health insurance covers, and your self-paid treatments and fees, accurately and consistently in both page text and structured data. Under the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare guideline of February 2025, a clinic's own website is in principle not treated as advertising, but claims like "guaranteed to cure" and edited before-and-after photos should stay off it too. What AI needs from you is checkable facts, not sales copy.

Written November 11, 2026 (JST). Information from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), the Japan Health Insurance Association and local governments was checked on each official page on the dates given in the text. Most linked sources are in Japanese.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your clinic's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your clinic or rank it anywhere. This article is not legal advice: if you are unsure about a phrase, ask the public health center that oversees your clinic. If you run a chiropractic or physiotherapy clinic outside Japan, the approach carries over, but your advertising rules and insurance terms will differ.

What patients ask AI

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews to find a seikotsuin, the AI reads what it can find on the web and builds an answer. The questions mix place, day, time, insurance and treatment: "a sekkotsuin near the station open on Saturday evening", "can I use health insurance for a sudden back strain?", "what is the difference between a seikotsuin and a seitai clinic?", "a clinic that treats children's sports injuries".

To answer them, an AI has to be able to read, as text, where your clinic is, until what time it takes patients on which days, whether booking is needed, which injuries insurance covers, and what self-paid treatments cost. If your hours are only in an image, days closed differ between pages, or there is no explanation of insurance, your clinic is harder to use in an answer.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special schema.org structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual basics still matter, such as making structured data match the visible text and keeping your Business Profile up to date (checked November 1, 2026). First: your website and your ads follow different rules

The Judo Therapists Act limits what a judo therapy clinic may advertise. On February 18, 2025, the MHLW issued an advertising guideline for massage, acupuncture, moxibustion and judo therapy (notice 医政発0218第1号) that sets out what counts as an ad: something that meets all three conditions of inducement, specificity and recognizability. A clinic's own website, which people reach by searching or typing its address, is in principle not treated as an ad (checked November 11, 2026). MHLW: summary of the guideline (PDF, Japanese), Bunkyo City: advertising rules for treatment clinics (Japanese)

That does not mean anything goes on your website. The same guideline asks websites to explain self-paid treatments, their usual fees and their risks or side effects, and says they should not carry claims that cannot be verified ("guaranteed to cure"), edited before-and-after photos, comparisons such as "No. 1 on a review site", excessive emphasis on low prices, or wording that plays on fear. Banner ads and paid listings shown as sponsored search results are treated as ads when they meet the three conditions, and so are social media posts.

What an ad may say includes: that the practitioner is a judo therapist, the clinic's name, phone number and location, treatment days and hours, "bone-setting" (ほねつぎ or 接骨), that patients can claim health insurance reimbursement (for fractures and dislocations, only when the ad states that a doctor's consent is required), booking, holiday or evening treatment, home visits and parking. Insurance coverage is the fact AI uses most

"Can I use health insurance at a seikotsuin?" is a question AI gets often. According to the Japan Health Insurance Association, health insurance covers acute or traumatic bruises, sprains and strains (including pulled muscles) and fractures and dislocations. For fractures and dislocations, a doctor's consent is needed in advance except for emergency first aid. Simple stiff shoulders or muscle fatigue, use as a substitute for relaxation massage, pain or stiffness caused by illness (neuralgia, rheumatism, frozen shoulder, arthritis, herniated discs and so on), and injuries at work or while commuting (covered by workers' compensation instead) are not covered (checked November 11, 2026). Japan Health Insurance Association: using a judo therapist (Japanese)

State this line in your own words on your site and link to the official explanation. Writing "insurance covers any symptom" is listed in the guideline as an example of exaggeration. The association also says that from January 2027, people receiving treatment on many body parts over a long period may be moved to reimbursement payment, where they pay in full and claim later. Write changes like this with the official page at hand and a date next to them.

For self-paid treatments (those insurance does not cover), give each one a plain description, the typical duration, the price including tax and the possible risks. When you explain how you differ from unlicensed treatments such as seitai, avoid claiming to be better; stating facts such as "treatment is given by a licensed judo therapist" and "insurance covers the injuries listed above" is the safe approach.

Either is fine as long as your name and page text make it clear that this is a judo therapy clinic; there is no need to look like a hospital or medical clinic in your markup. The guideline asks clinics not to use names that could be mistaken for a hospital or medical clinic (for example "clinic" or "rehab").

Checklist before you publish

  1. Reception hours, days closed and last reception times are written as text and match across your site, Google Business Profile and booking sites.
  2. Which injuries insurance covers and does not cover is explained in line with the official guidance, including doctor's consent for fractures and dislocations.
  3. Each self-paid treatment shows a description, typical duration, price including tax and risks.
  4. No unverifiable claims or comparisons such as "guaranteed to cure", "No. 1 in ..." or "insurance covers any symptom".
  5. Before-and-after photos are not edited, and patient testimonials are not used as undisclosed advertising.
  6. Website copy is not reused as-is in search ads, flyers or social media ads (ads may carry only the items the law allows).
  7. Values in your structured data (name, address, hours, prices) match the page, and llms.txt lists your overview, treatments and fees, insurance explanation, directions and booking pages. What is llms.txt?

FAQ

Can a seikotsuin show its fees on its website?

Under the MHLW guideline of February 2025, a clinic's own website is in principle not treated as advertising, and websites are asked to explain self-paid treatments, their usual fees and risks. Avoid putting excessive emphasis on low prices. Ads such as search ads and flyers may carry only the items the law allows.

Can I create pages about stiff shoulders or back pain?

Health insurance covers traumatic bruises, sprains, strains, fractures and dislocations; simple stiff shoulders and muscle fatigue are not covered. If a page uses a symptom name, do not promise results and state correctly whether insurance applies. Keep symptom names and effects out of ads, and ask your public health center when unsure.

Which structured data type should I use?

MedicalBusiness, a kind of LocalBusiness, or plain LocalBusiness both work. What matters is that name, address, hours and prices match the page, and that the text makes clear the clinic is run by licensed judo therapists.
