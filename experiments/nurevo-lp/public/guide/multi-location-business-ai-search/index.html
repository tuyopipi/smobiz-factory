AI search for multi-location businesses: keep each location's hours, address and services from being mixed up

When a business has several locations, most wrong AI answers come from one location's facts being used for another. Give each location its own page with its facts in plain text (address, phone, hours, temporary closures, what differs there), tie it to the brand in structured data, and keep the same values in Google Business Profile and your other listings. Those three steps are the core of the work.

Written November 15, 2026 (JST). This guide is for any business with two or more locations or offices (restaurants, salons, clinics, shops, schools, estate agents) and for the agencies that build their sites. The company itself (legal name, founding date, leadership) is covered in About page AI search optimization, and the basics of one location's markup in local business schema and SEO plugins. This guide is about the problems that come from having many locations.

AI answers change by day and by wording. These steps make the information AI reads accurate; they do not guarantee that you will appear in an answer or in any position.

How AI answers go wrong for multi-location businesses

ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews read web pages, build an answer and show their sources. With one location, "the business's facts" are a single set. With several, the pages an AI reads contain several addresses, several sets of hours and several phone numbers. If the text does not make clear which value belongs to which location, the answer can be built from the wrong one.

The questions people ask split into questions about the brand and questions about one location:

  1. "Is the Umeda branch of [brand] open on Sundays?" (that location's hours and closing days)
  2. "Which [brand] locations have parking?" (facilities that differ by location)
  3. "Is the Shinjuku [brand] still open, or did it move?" (closures and moves)
  4. "Is there a [brand] near Kyoto Station?" (which location serves which area)
  5. "Can I get the same menu / treatment / service at every [brand]?" (services that differ by location)
  6. "Who runs [brand]?" (brand and operating company)

Four mistakes come up again and again: the flagship's hours given as every location's hours, a closed location described as open, the central booking number given as a location's phone, and a service only some locations offer described as available everywhere. Each becomes less likely once every location's facts are written on that location's own page.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special structured data for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual SEO basics apply (Google Search Central: AI features and your website). Multi-location work is the same: not a special way of writing for AI, but each location's facts published accurately, readably and with the same values everywhere.

Site structure: separate the brand page, the location list and the location pages

First decide which page answers which question. A single page that lists every location makes it easy for an AI to attach a paragraph to the wrong location.

Give each location a URL that will not change, such as /stores/osaka/umeda/. Renaming locations or URLs midway creates more mismatches with what AIs and other sites read before. If you must change one, redirect the old URL to the new one.

Do not create pages for areas where you have no location (pages that only swap the place name, "looking for X in [town]? Choose us"). They can also lead an AI to describe a location that does not exist.

Location pages often end up as the same text with only the name and address swapped. That is acceptable, but writing at least three facts that only apply to that location (facilities, which services it handles, busy times, directions) gives an AI something to tell the locations apart by.

Keep each location's FAQ on that location's page, separate from brand-wide questions: questions that can be answered with the location's name in them, such as "Is there parking at this location?" or "Can I get X at this location?"

Closures, moves and temporary closures

The wrong fact that lasts longest for multi-location businesses is a closed location. Deleting its page does not remove what other sites and AIs have already read. Clean up in this order:

  1. Do not delete the closed location's page straight away. Rewrite it as "This location closed on [date]" with links to nearby locations. For a move, redirect to the new location's page or state the new address clearly.
  2. Remove it from the location list and remove its structured data (do not keep publishing a closed location as a LocalBusiness).
  3. Update its Google Business Profile to show the closure or move.
  4. Search the rest of the site (footer, recruitment page, campaign pages, PDF flyers) for mentions.
  5. Ask other publishers (booking sites, portals, local directories) to update their listings.
  6. For a temporary closure, give dates and show it with specialOpeningHoursSpecification or a notice. Remove it when you reopen.

Structured data: tie each location to the brand

For several locations, structured data should make "which company, which location" readable by machines. The approach:

  1. Publish the head office (brand or operating company) once as an Organization on the home page or About page, with a fixed @id such as https://example.com/#organization.
  2. On each location page, publish exactly one LocalBusiness of the most specific type, with an @id unique to that location such as https://example.com/stores/osaka/umeda/#store.
  3. Point from each location to the head office with parentOrganization. schema.org marks the older branchOf as deprecated and recommends parentOrganization instead.
  4. Put the store code in branchCode, which schema.org itself describes as the "store code".
  5. If a location contains a separate section (schema.org's examples are a store with a pharmacy and a bakery with a cafe), connect it with department, which lets each section have its own hours.
  6. Put each location's values on its own page rather than listing every LocalBusiness on the location list. Do not put values in structured data that the page text does not show.

Google lists matching structured data to the visible page content as one of the basics and recommends keeping Business Profile information up to date (AI features and your website). Treat structured data as the answer key for what the location page already says.

Keep Google Business Profile and the site on the same values

When a location's Business Profile and its page disagree, neither people nor AIs can tell which is right. What helps most is one master sheet of every location's facts, from which the site, Google Business Profile and booking sites are all updated.

Multi-location checklist

  1. Every location has its own page, at a URL that will not change.
  2. Location names follow the same brand + place pattern everywhere.
  3. Each location page states that location's address, phone (direct or central), hours and closing days in text.
  4. Temporary closures and holidays have dates and are removed when over.
  5. Services common to all locations are written separately from those only this location offers.
  6. Closed or moved locations show a notice and are removed from the location list and structured data.
  7. Each location's LocalBusiness has its own @id and points to the head office with parentOrganization.
  8. Structured data values match the location page text.
  9. The site and Google Business Profile are updated from the same master sheet.
  10. Locations can be browsed by region, with links to each location page.
  11. You picked locations and checked questions with and without the name on different days, and recorded the results.

FAQ

Do we need a page for every location, or is one location list enough?

To answer questions about one location (its hours, its facilities), one page per location is more reliable. With only a list, an AI can easily attach a value to the wrong location. Even with few locations, give each one its own heading with its address and hours together.

If every location has the same hours, do we still need them on each page?

We recommend it. An AI may answer from the location page alone, and if the hours are not there it will use values from other pages or other sites.

Should we use branchOf or parentOrganization?

schema.org marks branchOf as deprecated and recommends parentOrganization. Point from each location's LocalBusiness to the head office Organization's @id with parentOrganization.

Should we delete a closed location's page straight away?

Turning it into a notice with the closing date and links to nearby locations, and removing it from the location list and structured data, communicates the closure better than deleting it. For a move, redirect to the new location's page or state the new address.

Our franchisees run their own sites and social accounts. How do we keep them consistent?

Treat the head office's master sheet as the source of truth, share the agreed spelling of names, addresses, phone numbers and hours with franchisees, and ask them to use the same values. Linking both ways, from the franchisee's site to the brand's location page and back, also makes the relationship clear.

We have too many locations for llms.txt. Should all of them be listed?

No. If the location list and your main location pages are there, the rest can be reached from them. In Nurevo AEO, the webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines filter can add a line for the location list.

Will these steps make every location appear correctly in AI answers?

That cannot be guaranteed; AI answers change by day and wording. These steps reduce the causes of one location's facts being mixed with another's and make the information AI reads accurate and consistent.
