Founder, expert and author profile pages for AI search: get people's roles, credentials and expertise read correctly

Whether an AI answers a question about a person correctly depends on whether that person's profile page states, in plain text, who they are, where they work and what they are an expert in. Put the name, role, organization, credentials, areas of expertise and career on one page; link to it from article bylines and from the leadership line of your About page; and publish the same values as Person structured data. Those three steps are the core. Inflating credentials or inventing authors backfires: Google calls fabricated creator profiles deception.

Written November 16, 2026 (JST). Google's "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" (last updated October 5, 2026) and "Profile page (ProfilePage) structured data" were checked the same day. This guide is for companies, shops and practices with pages about their founder, director, practitioners, teachers or writers, and for the agencies that build those sites. The company itself is covered in About page AI search optimization.

AI answers change by day and by wording. These steps make the information AI reads accurate; they do not guarantee that a person will appear in an answer or that rankings will rise.

What AI uses profile pages for

ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews read web pages when they answer and show them as sources. For a question about a person, the profile page on the site of the organization they belong to is the most direct material. When there is no such page, when it is only a photo and a name, or when the job title differs from page to page, the AI may build its answer from an old article or from someone else with the same name.

Questions about people tend to fall into a few patterns.

  1. "Who runs Sample Ltd and what is their background?" (the founder or CEO)
  2. "What is the director of Sample Clinic a specialist in?" (an expert's credentials and field)
  3. "Which accountant in Leeds is good with inheritance tax?" (found by expertise; no name in the question)
  4. "Who wrote this article, and can I trust it?" (the author)
  5. "How is Jane Doe connected to Sample Ltd?" (role, other positions, previous jobs)
  6. "How do I book a consultation with Dr Doe?" (contact and booking)

In a question like the third one, with no name in it, the AI first looks for a practice or shop and then adds who would handle the work. A profile page is material not only when someone asks by name but also when they search by expertise.

Whose page, and where

Sort the people on your site into four roles. Not everyone needs a long page; start with the people an AI is most likely to be asked about.

When the same person appears as founder, author and reviewer, keep one page and spell the name and title the same way everywhere. An AI may take two spellings for two people.

Writing the page: say who the person is in the first sentence

  1. Put the name, role, organization and field in the first sentence, e.g. "Jane Doe is the founder of Sample Ltd in Leeds and a chartered accountant specialising in small-business accounts and inheritance tax."
  2. Use the second sentence for facts that tell her apart from others: the year she qualified, the bodies she belongs to, the area she covers.
  3. Write credentials in full. Where a credential can be checked in a public register, you can say so, so readers can verify it.
  4. Put the career in a table or timeline, not an image or a PDF.
  5. Replace claims nobody can compare, such as "the industry's number one", with facts: years, numbers, fields.
  6. In regulated fields such as medicine and law, there are rules on how titles and qualifications may be advertised. For healthcare, see also AI search for clinics.

Bylines and reviewers

Show the author's name at the start or end of each article and link it to the profile page. A team byline is fine, but then the team page should say who writes and how.

When an outside expert checks the content, call it a review, separate from authorship, and state what was reviewed (for example "the tax calculations") and when. schema.org has reviewedBy for people who reviewed a page for accuracy, a different property from author. Do not list the author and reviewer together under author.

Google recommends telling readers how content was produced, including whether automation or AI was used. If an article was drafted with AI, say so where readers would expect it, along with the fact that a person checked it.

Markup example: ProfilePage and Person

A minimal example. Every value must also be in the text. Pointing worksFor at the same @id as your company node lets the person and the company be read as one pair.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "ProfilePage",
  "dateModified": "2026-11-16",
  "mainEntity": {
    "@type": "Person",
    "@id": "https://example.com/about/jane-doe/#person",
    "name": "Jane Doe",
    "jobTitle": "Founder and Chartered Accountant",
    "worksFor": { "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" },
    "description": "Chartered accountant specialising in small-business accounts and inheritance tax in Leeds.",
    "knowsAbout": ["Inheritance tax", "Small-business accounting"],
    "hasCredential": {
      "@type": "EducationalOccupationalCredential",
      "name": "Chartered Accountant",
      "credentialCategory": "Professional qualification"
    },
    "image": "https://example.com/images/jane-doe.jpg",
    "sameAs": ["https://www.linkedin.com/in/example"]
  }
}
</script>

The credential type EducationalOccupationalCredential can also carry the kind of credential (credentialCategory) and the body that recognizes it (recognizedBy). Put in sameAs only profiles the person controls and that clearly belong to them.

Profile page checklist

  1. The first sentence has the name, role, organization and field.
  2. Name and title are written the same way on the About page, bylines and staff pages.
  3. Credentials are written in full with the year or issuing body.
  4. Areas of expertise are specific and backed by cases or articles.
  5. Numbers have a date and say how they were counted.
  6. No invented people, AI-generated headshots or inflated credentials.
  7. Bylines link to the profile; reviews are separate from authorship, with scope and date.
  8. ProfilePage and Person markup match the text and are not duplicated.
  9. sameAs lists only accounts the person controls.
  10. Titles are fixed as soon as they change, and the last-updated date follows.
  11. You checked with questions that include the name, on different days, and recorded the results.

FAQ

Does a small company need a founder page?

A few lines with the name, role and background in the About page are often enough. If the founder writes articles, speaks or gets interviewed, or people search for them by name, a separate page keeps the facts in one place.

Are articles by people without formal credentials less likely to be used by AI?

Google does not say that lacking a credential is a problem in itself. What it says matters most is trust, and real experience (how many years, what kind of work) written as fact is good material. For health or money topics, you can also have an expert review the content and say so.

Can we publish under a pen name?

Yes, as long as you do not attach an invented background or credentials, and the pen name's page states in what capacity the person writes. Google calls fabricated creator profiles deception.

Which comes first, the author page or the ProfilePage markup?

The page. Markup puts the same facts as the text in a machine-readable form; do not put facts in markup that the text does not state.

Should a reviewer go under author?

Keep authors and reviewers apart. schema.org has reviewedBy for people who checked a page for accuracy. In the text, too, write "written by" and "reviewed by" separately.

Our CEO has changed. What should we update?

The About page, the CEO's profile page, founder or employee in the markup, bylines, and profiles elsewhere (social accounts, industry directories). Keep the former CEO's page with their role and dates, or point it to the new page. There is no fixed time for AI answers to catch up.

If we follow these steps, will AI always describe our people correctly?

That cannot be guaranteed; AI answers change by day and wording. These steps make the information AI reads accurate and consistent, so mix-ups are less likely.
