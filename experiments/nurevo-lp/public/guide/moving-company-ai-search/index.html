AI search for moving companies: getting your estimates, price conditions, cancellation fees and service area read correctly

For a moving company, AI search optimization mostly means answering, in the text of your own pages, what people ask an AI assistant before they book: "Is the estimate free, and does someone have to visit?", "Roughly how much for a one-bedroom, and what changes the price?", "Until when can I cancel or change the date for free?", "What happens if something breaks?" and "Do you cover my area?". Write cancellation and estimate rules the way your contract terms set them (in Japan, the standard moving contract terms published by the transport ministry), then publish the same values as structured data: the company as a schema.org MovingCompany, the area as areaServed and the free estimate as a Service.

Written 19 November 2026 (JST). The Japanese transport ministry and regional bureau pages, a Hyogo Prefecture consumer leaflet, news coverage of the certification mark and the top-ranking articles were read that day. schema.org types were checked that day in schema-dts 2.1.0. The Google and National Tax Agency pages reflect what we checked on 1 November 2026.

This guide is written for movers in Japan, where Nurevo is based, so the contract sections describe Japanese rules. If you work elsewhere, the same pattern applies to whatever terms and consumer rules govern moves in your country. AI answers change with the day and the wording, and nothing can guarantee you will appear. The aim is that AI does not get your facts wrong and has what it needs to recommend you. This is not legal advice.

What people planning a move ask AI

Moving questions combine place, timing, volume, money and worry: "Two of us are moving from [city] to [city] at the end of March. Roughly how much, and who should we use?", "Do I have to let them into my home for the estimate?", "How much will I pay if I cancel the day before?", "Is my fridge covered if it gets damaged?", "Which movers also take down the air conditioner?"

To answer, an AI needs your site to state in text the areas you cover, how estimates work and what they cost, what drives the price, cancellation and postponement fees, how to make a damage claim and the extra jobs you take on (packing, furniture assembly, air conditioners). If your prices say only "contact us for a quote", your area is a map image and your cancellation terms sit inside a PDF, the AI falls back on comparison sites and generic articles.

Google says there are no extra requirements or special structured data for AI Overviews and AI Mode: structured data that matches the visible text and an up-to-date Business Profile are the basics that matter. Google: AI features and your website. For a mover, the work is the same: get the facts written down and consistent, not a trick.

Write estimates and cancellation fees the way your terms set them

Japan's standard moving contract terms (標準引越運送約款), published by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, were revised with effect from 1 June 2018. The cap on cancellation and postponement fees became 50% of the fare and charges on moving day, 30% the day before and 20% two days before (previously 20% on the day and 10% the day before). The terms also began to apply to shared-truck moves, where one truck carries several households' belongings. MLIT: revision of the standard moving terms · Chugoku District Transport Bureau

A consumer leaflet from Hyogo Prefecture (February 2019) lists the main points of the revised terms: no estimate fee and no deposit at the estimate; the mover confirms any changes up to three days before the pick-up date in the estimate; damage or loss must be reported within three months of the move. It advises consumers to have the mover inspect in person rather than quote by phone or online only, and to check cancellation fees in advance. Hyogo Prefecture: moving troubles (PDF, Japanese)

Asked "how much if I cancel the day before?", an AI may answer with the general rule. If you follow the standard terms, say so and list the bands on your prices page and in your FAQ. If you are more generous (free until two days before, for example), state that clearly. Uploading the terms as a PDF alone leaves them hard to read for both people and AI.

Publish prices as worked examples with their conditions

A move's price depends on volume, distance, timing and the building, so you cannot list one fixed price. But "contact us for a quote" alone leaves an AI with nothing better than "it varies by company". Two or three worked examples, each with every condition and the month it was written, let both people and AI read "under these conditions, about this much".

  1. Conditions for each example: number of people and home size, volume (main furniture and appliances, number of boxes), distance (same city, same prefecture, out of prefecture), timing (month, weekday or weekend, time of day), the building (floor, lift or not, distance from where the truck parks), crew and truck size, and what is included (packing materials, floor protection, furniture assembly).
  2. Give amounts including tax with the month and year of the estimate. Update or remove old examples.
  3. For peak season, explain why prices rise (your own limits on crew and trucks, for example) and how booking early or being flexible on dates changes them. Use industry-wide demand figures only if you can check their source.
  4. Say when extra charges apply (more belongings on the day, hoisting, long carries) and how they are decided. The Hyogo leaflet notes that the estimate cannot be raised for the mover's convenience.

Service area, claims and signals of trust

Write your service area as prefecture and city names in text, not just a map image. Say whether you take long-distance or out-of-prefecture moves and whether they are handled differently. If you have several branches, give each its own page with its address, phone number and area. AI search for multi-location businesses

For claims, state the deadline (three months from the move under the standard terms), where to report, what to declare in advance and, if you carry insurance, what it covers. Any amounts you publish should match the policy.

One signal of trust in Japan is the Hikkoshi Anshin Mark (引越安心マーク). It comes from a certification scheme the Japan Trucking Association created in fiscal 2014 for movers it recognises as providing safe and reliable moving services. MLIT: certification of excellent movers (Japanese). Requirements include every branch handling moves being a certified safe operator (G-mark) or equivalent, following the standard terms and having a complaint-handling system. Kyushu District Transport Bureau (Japanese). According to December 2025 coverage, 41 movers were certified in fiscal 2025 (valid 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2028), and 348 movers with 1,673 branches held the mark. LOGISTICS TODAY, 22 December 2025 (Japanese)

If you hold a certification, write its name, the body that grants it and its validity period in text on your company page, not just as a logo. Do not use similar wording if you are not certified. Collect reviews on third-party places such as your Google Business Profile rather than padding your reputation with reviews you post on your own site.

Structured data: MovingCompany and areaServed

schema.org has a type for movers, MovingCompany, one of the home and construction business types (HomeAndConstructionBusiness) under LocalBusiness. The area you cover goes in areaServed, where prefectures or cities can be listed as AdministrativeArea. A free in-home estimate can be a Service with an Offer of 0.

Keep structured data identical to the page text. An Offer holds a single amount, so keep plan prices in the worked examples on your page and put only fixed amounts, such as the free estimate, in structured data.

Setting it up on a WordPress moving site

  2. In the setup wizard choose "Other", then enter MovingCompany in Settings > "Business type (schema.org type)". Do not choose "Professional services", which sets ProfessionalService.
  3. Check the company name, address, phone number, hours and description. In the description, briefly state your area, the kinds of move you take (single person, family, office) and how estimates work.
  4. Add services one by one (up to 20): in-home estimate (0 and its duration), online estimate, your main plans and extras such as air conditioners. Name, duration in minutes, price including tax with currency, category, booking URL and description are published as a Service, an Offer and a booking action (ReserveAction), and each gets a line in llms.txt. For plans whose price depends on conditions, leave the price empty and write the conditions in the description.
  5. Add your service area as areaServed with the developer filter webmcp_canary_business_node (code below; in a child theme's functions.php or a code snippets plugin). With an SEO plugin active, the property is merged into that plugin's business node, and values the plugin already sets win.
  6. Enter the questions people ask (estimate cost and method, cancellation and postponement fees, claims, extra charges on the day, payment methods, moves outside your area) as FAQ and place the Nurevo FAQ block (or the [nurevo_faq] shortcode). Questions only for your prices page can be set as that page's own FAQ in the Nurevo AEO panel of the block editor.
  7. Open /llms.txt and check that your prices, estimates, area, claims and company pages are listed. Pages for finished campaigns can be left out of llms.txt in the Nurevo AEO panel. Run the diagnosis on the AEO score screen and apply the one-click fixes.

Keeping comparison and quote sites consistent

AI reads quote-comparison sites, comparison articles and your Google Business Profile as well as your own site. If your name, address, phone, area or hours differ from place to place, the AI cannot tell which is right. Keep a list of where you are listed and bring each one in line with your site once or twice a year.

Prices and campaigns can differ between listing sites. Saying on your own site that "the written estimate is the reference price" and that "campaigns on listing sites follow each site's conditions" reduces the chance of an AI repeating an expired discount.

Checklist before you publish

  1. For each estimate method (in-home, online, phone): no fee, how long it takes, and that a written estimate is given.
  2. Your prices page has two or three worked examples with every condition, tax included and dated.
  3. The cancellation and postponement fee bands (moving day, day before, two days before) and when cancelling becomes free are written in text on the prices page and in the FAQ.
  4. The claim deadline and contact, and items to declare in advance, are stated.
  5. Your area is written as prefecture and city names and matches areaServed in your structured data.
  6. The business type is MovingCompany, and your name, address, phone and hours match across your site, structured data, Google Business Profile and listing sites.
  7. Any certification is written out with its name, granting body and validity period (and nothing you do not hold).
  8. No claims you cannot back up, such as "cheapest in the industry" or "guaranteed".
  9. llms.txt lists your prices, estimates, area, claims and company pages. What is llms.txt

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the pages are in order, write 5–10 questions a prospective customer might ask, combining area, timing, household size and worries (cancellation fees, claims, estimates). Ask them in ChatGPT and Perplexity and record whether your company appears by name or URL, whether prices and cancellation fees are described correctly, and whether the source cited is your site or a quote site. FAQ

Where should a moving company start with AI search?

Put worked price examples with their conditions on your prices page, write your cancellation and postponement fees and the claim deadline in text, and list your service area by city name. Then publish structured data with the MovingCompany type and make your Google Business Profile and listing sites match.

Won't publishing prices put us at a disadvantage?

Publishing worked examples with conditions, rather than a single price, avoids the impression that a quote will match exactly. Without any figures, AI may answer with numbers from comparison sites or generic articles. Always add the conditions and the month each example was written.

How should we write cancellation fees?

Under Japan's standard moving terms in force from 1 June 2018, the caps are 50% of the fare and charges on moving day, 30% the day before and 20% two days before. Write the values in the terms you use, and when cancelling becomes free, in text on your prices page and FAQ.

Do estimates have to be done in person?

It is not a rule, but a Hyogo Prefecture consumer leaflet advises people to have the mover inspect rather than rely on a phone or online quote alone. Explaining what each method (in-home, online, phone) can assess and how the written estimate is given helps AI explain the difference.

Which structured data type should a mover use?

schema.org MovingCompany. The service area goes in areaServed and a free estimate can be a Service with an Offer of 0. Keep the values identical to the page text.
