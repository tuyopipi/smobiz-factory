AI search for eye clinics: how to label insured, top-up and self-pay care so AI reads cataract, contact lens and myopia information correctly

The single most useful thing an eye clinic can do for AI search is to state, in text on each treatment page, whether the test or treatment is covered by insurance, partly covered, or self-pay, and what the patient pays themselves. One eye clinic often offers insured cataract surgery, multifocal lenses billed as a top-up on insured care, and self-pay refractive surgery or orthokeratology side by side, so AI tools mix them up easily. Then keep contact lens prescribing (the clinic) separate from lens sales (the shop), follow the medical advertising rules, and publish the same facts in your structured data, llms.txt and Google Business Profile.

Written November 17, 2026 (JST). The examples come from Japan, where Nurevo is based. The multifocal lens top-up scheme (a hospital notice), the approval of a low-dose atropine eye drop (Yakuji Nippo) and a clinic's patient leaflet, contact lens notices from Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency, the Government Public Relations Online and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW), and the schema.org types (schema-dts 2.1.0 type definitions) were checked that day. The MHLW medical advertising Q&A items are as checked on November 16, 2026. Outside Japan, insurance and advertising rules differ: check your own country's rules.

What patients ask AI about eye clinics

Eye care questions are not only about symptoms. They carry money and process. Family members ask too: adult children researching a parent's cataract surgery, parents researching a child's myopia. Six question types come up again and again.

Area × symptom"An eye clinic near X station that can see red eyes on Saturday"Hours by day, walk-ins or appointments, ages seen

In every case AI can only use a page where the conditions are written as text. A price list that is only an image, "please ask about fees", or a page that never says whether something is insured is unlikely to be cited.

Label each treatment: insured, top-up or self-pay

Cost is where AI gets eye clinics wrong most often. If "cataract surgery is covered by insurance" and "multifocal lenses cost extra" are mixed on one page, AI may say "multifocal lenses are covered" or "cataract surgery costs 300,000 yen", both inaccurate. Put the category near the top of each treatment page.

Treatment or serviceWhat to stateWording AI misreads
Cataract surgery (monofocal lens)That it is insured, day surgery or inpatient, number of pre-op tests and follow-up visitsListing it in the same table as multifocal prices
Cataract surgery with a multifocal lensThat it is selected (top-up) care, the extra lens cost paid on top of the insured fees (per lens, per eye, tax included), and that the clinic has notified the authorities"Self-pay, about X yen" as if the whole surgery were self-pay
Treatment to slow child myopiaName, ages, whether tests and drops are insured or self-pay, monthly cost, visit frequency, and that it does not stop myopia or restore vision"Cures myopia", "restores eyesight"
Orthokeratology, refractive surgery (ICL etc.)That it is self-pay if so, the fee (one or both eyes, whether tests and follow-ups are included), main risks and side effects, eligibility testsOnly "fees explained at consultation"
Contact lens exam and prescriptionSteps, time needed, what to bring, and where the lenses are paid forMixing clinic fees and shop prices

In Japan, cataract surgery using a multifocal lens has been "selected care" (senteiryoyo) since April 2020. Selected care is additional care the patient chooses; that part is fully self-paid, on top of the normal insured fees. One city hospital's notice lists extra costs of 234,300 to 272,800 yen per eye (tax included) depending on the lens, and states that the hospital has notified the regional health bureau. Fujisawa City Hospital: multifocal lens notice (PDF, Japanese). Costs vary by clinic and lens, so publish your own figure for each lens.

For child myopia, a low-dose atropine eye drop from Santen was approved on December 27, 2024 as Japan's first drug to slow myopia progression. Yakuji Nippo, January 9, 2025 (Japanese). One medical institution's patient leaflet says it is for ages 5 to 15, exams are insured, the drops are self-pay (5,000 yen including tax for 30 days), and everything becomes self-pay when combined with orthokeratology. Patient leaflet (PDF, Japanese). That is one institution's handling; write down exactly how your clinic handles it.

Self-pay surgery and treatment pages: fees and main risks on the same page

Under Japan's medical advertising rules, a website that patients look up themselves may explain things normally barred from advertising if certain conditions are met ("genteikaijo", lifting of limits). For self-pay care, the MHLW Q&A says the site must give the usual treatment content and fees and the main risks and side effects (listing one or two generic drawbacks is not enough, Q6-12), a contact point that answers questions (an appointments-only number or auto-reply does not count, Q6-10), and must present these clearly (Q6-11). For self-pay treatment using approved devices or drugs, it should also state that it is self-pay and give the standard fee. MHLW: Q&A on the medical advertising guidelines (PDF, Japanese)

This is also exactly what AI needs when asked about cost, risk or the number of visits. Refractive surgery, multifocal lens and myopia pages read well in this order.

  1. Treatment name, and whether it is insured, top-up or self-pay.
  2. Who it suits, who cannot have it, and what the eligibility tests are (without promising how well the patient will see).
  3. The process: eligibility tests, treatment day, number and timing of follow-up visits.
  4. Fees: one eye or both, tax included or not, whether tests, drops and follow-ups are included, which part is insured and which is self-paid.
  5. Main risks and side effects, and after-care instructions (driving, face washing, work) as your clinic's guidance.
  6. A contact point that answers questions (with hours), and who to call if there is a problem after treatment.
  7. The page's last-updated date. Change it when lenses or fees change.

Contact lenses: the clinic prescribes, the shop sells

In Japan, contact lenses, including coloured lenses with no vision correction, are classed as highly controlled medical devices. The Consumer Affairs Agency advises people to see an eye doctor first, get a prescription and agree how often to have check-ups (September 10, 2021). Consumer Affairs Agency: eye injuries from contact lenses (Japanese). According to Government Public Relations Online, the National Consumer Affairs Center's reporting line for doctors received 104 accident reports between August 2014 and December 2022, and more than half of the lenses had been bought online. Government Public Relations Online (Japanese).

On the shop side, an MHLW notice (July 18, 2012) says sellers should check whether the buyer has seen a doctor, recommend a visit if not, and record the name of the clinic. MHLW: handling at the time of contact lens sale (Japanese). When a patient asks AI "where can I get contacts?", it helps if your site makes clear that the clinic is where the exam and prescription happen.

  1. On the contact lens page, give the steps, time needed and what to bring (such as the box of the lenses in use, as your clinic's guidance) for first-time and returning patients.
  2. If there is a lens shop in or next to the clinic, say whether it is the same business or a separate one. If separate, give its own hours, phone number and prices.
  3. State in an FAQ, as clinic policy, whether you write prescriptions only and how you guide people who buy elsewhere.
  4. Give check-up intervals as your clinic's policy, not as a general rule.

Reviews and post-surgery results: where common AI advice collides with the rules

AI optimisation articles often recommend collecting reviews and showing results. For eye surgery and myopia treatment, take care.

Common adviceHow Japan's MHLW Q&A treats itWhat to do instead
Publish patient testimonialsTestimonials about the content or effect of treatment may not be published (Q1-18, Q2-9)Explain the facts in more detail: process, fees, risks, visits
Show that patients "see without glasses after surgery"Claims of treatment effect are restricted; before/after photos count as effect claims (Q2-8, Q3-14)Check what you may say in the MHLW case book and with your public health centre
"No. 1 in the area", "painless", "safe day surgery"Treated as examples of false, exaggerated or comparative claimsWrite only what numbers and facts support, such as surgery time or visit count
Put reviews into FAQs or review markupAs above; reviews the clinic requested or edited may also countMake FAQs factual: "Is it insured?", "When can I drive?"

More examples are in the MHLW case book (PDF, Japanese). You cannot control whether AI reads third-party reviews you did not request, so the dependable approach is to state facts accurately on your own site.

Structured data: the Ophthalmology type and the type for a lens shop

schema.org has separate types for an eye clinic and an optical shop (checked against the schema-dts 2.1.0 type definitions on November 17, 2026).

Type or propertyMeaningHow an eye clinic uses it
OphthalmologyDiagnosis and treatment of disorders of the eye and visual structures, including medical and surgical treatment. A MedicalBusiness, and so a LocalBusinessThe type for an eye clinic. It carries address, phone and opening hours directly
OptometricTesting visual acuity and prescribing corrective lensesFits optometry practices (common in the US). A physician-led eye clinic fits Ophthalmology
OpticianA store that sells glasses and similar devicesFor a separately run glasses or lens shop, not for the clinic
medicalSpecialtyThe medical field; Ophthalmology is one of its valuesInclude only what your pages describe
Service and offersA service and its priceRegister each self-pay treatment or top-up lens fee with the same price as the page

Google says no special structured data is needed for AI features such as AI Overviews, and that structured data should match what is visible on the page. Google: AI features and your website. Do not put prices only in structured data, do not use prices that differ from the page, and do not publish the clinic and a separate shop as one business.

Adding medicalSpecialty

Nurevo AEO does not output medicalSpecialty by itself. To add it, use the filter that edits the business structured data (webmcp_canary_business_node) in your theme's functions.php or a small plugin.

add_filter('webmcp_canary_business_node', function ($org, $settings) {
    // Add only the specialty your pages actually describe
    $org['medicalSpecialty'] = array('Ophthalmology');
    return $org;

Pre-publish checklist

  1. Every treatment and test page says whether it is insured, top-up or self-pay.
  2. The multifocal lens page gives the extra cost per lens (per eye, tax included) and says the insured fees are separate.
  3. The myopia page gives ages, what is insured and what is self-pay, the monthly cost and visit frequency.
  4. Self-pay surgery pages give fees, main risks and side effects, eligibility tests and a contact point that answers questions.
  5. The contact lens page gives steps, time needed and what to bring, and explains how the clinic and the lens shop relate.
  6. No testimonials, before/after photos, superlatives or comparisons in the text, FAQs or structured data.
  7. The price list is text, not only an image, and has a last-updated date.
  8. The structured data type is Ophthalmology, service prices match the page, and a separate shop is not mixed into the clinic's data.
  9. Name, address, phone and hours match across structured data, Google Business Profile and official medical directories.
  10. llms.txt lists the clinic information, price list, cataract, contact lens and access pages. What is llms.txt

FAQ

Where should an eye clinic start with AI search?

Label every treatment and test page as insured, top-up or self-pay. Then add fees, main risks and a contact point to multifocal lens and self-pay pages, and make your name, address, phone and hours match across structured data and Google Business Profile.

How should we present multifocal lens costs?

Say that it is selected (top-up) care, that the extra cost is paid on top of the insured fees, and give a table of amounts per lens with conditions such as per eye and tax included. In Japan, cataract surgery using a multifocal lens has been selected care since April 2020, with the extra part fully self-paid.

We have a contact lens shop attached. How do we handle structured data?

If the clinic and the shop are separate businesses, describe the clinic as Ophthalmology and the shop with its own type such as Optician, and do not merge them. In the page text, give each its own hours, phone number and prices.

Which structured data type should we use?

schema.org's Ophthalmology is the type for eye clinics; it is a MedicalBusiness and a LocalBusiness. Optometric fits optometry practices and Optician fits glasses shops. Matching the values to your page matters more than the type.

Will publishing surgery numbers or post-op vision results help AI pick us?

Any AI effect is unproven. Under Japan's medical advertising rules, claims of treatment effect and before/after photos are restricted. Check the conditions in the guidelines, the MHLW case book and with your public health centre, or your own country's rules.
