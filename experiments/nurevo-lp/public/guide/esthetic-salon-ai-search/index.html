AI search for esthetic and hair-removal salons: getting AI to read your course prices, refunds and the medical line correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for an esthetic or hair-removal salon comes down to one thing: making your own site able to answer the questions people ask AI before they sign a course contract. "What is the total?", "What do I get back if I stop halfway?" and "How is this different from medical hair removal?" Write each course's total price, number of sessions, expiry and payment options, your cooling-off and cancellation terms, and the fact that you are not a medical provider as text on your pages, then publish the same facts as BeautySalon structured data and in llms.txt. Bold claims about results do not make AI more likely to pick you.

Written November 21, 2026 (JST). Legal and government information is from Japanese prefectural, city and ministry pages, checked on the dates given in the text. Rules differ by country, so check the ones that apply to you. This article is not legal advice.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your salon's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your salon or rank it anywhere.

What people ask AI about esthetic and hair-removal salons

Hair salon questions are mostly about place and time: "a salon near the station open on Sunday". With esthetic and hair-removal salons, the worries people have before signing a contract become the questions. Courses are often prepaid, from hundreds to thousands of dollars, and the number of sessions, the expiry and the cancellation terms change what a client really pays.

So a menu name and a price are not enough for an AI to use your salon in an answer. It needs to read, as text on your site, how many sessions a course has, by when they must be used, what the total is, how a cancellation is calculated, and which payment options exist. Where that is missing, the AI guesses from reviews, comparison sites and general explanations.

Put the course contract rules on your pricing page

In Japan, esthetic contracts that run longer than one month and cost more than 50,000 yen are "specified continuous services" under the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions. Clients can cancel within 8 days of receiving the legally required documents (cooling-off), and related products sold as "needed for the treatment", such as supplements or underwear, are covered too. After that, they can still cancel for any reason within the contract period (Osaka Prefecture, updated November 13, 2026). Osaka Prefecture: cancelling a hair-removal contract (Japanese)

Most AI search articles for salons stop at "state your cancellation terms". What clients and AI want is how the calculation works. On your pricing page, rather than pasting the wording of the law, apply it to one of your own courses: "If you cancel a 6-session course of 33,000 yen (tax included) after 2 sessions, we refund the balance after deducting the 2 sessions you received and 10% of the remaining amount (no more than 20,000 yen)." How the per-session value is set (the contract rate or the regular price) depends on your contract, so write exactly what your contract says.

Four things are worth writing down: the cooling-off period and how to use it, how a mid-term cancellation is calculated and how long a refund takes, what happens to related products, and what happens when a course expires. Wording that reads as if it contradicts the law, such as "no cancellations" or "no refunds", misleads clients when an AI quotes it. Outside Japan, put your own local rules and your own terms in the same place.

Answer the prepayment and closure worry before it is asked

Clients have reasons to worry about paying a lump sum up front. In Japan, Tokyo Shoko Research counted 88 bankruptcies of esthetic businesses, including hair-removal salons, in 2023 (a record) and 87 in January to October 2024 (as reported by J-CAST on November 28, 2024). J-CAST (Japanese)

Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency asks people to check the contract documents for whether prepayments are protected before paying a large sum up front for esthetic, cosmetic medical and similar services, and to consider payment methods other than a lump sum. Consumer Affairs Agency: lump-sum prepayment (Japanese). Fukuoka City's consumer centre notes that "unlimited" and "lifetime guarantee" courses are often split into a paid period and a free period, and that refunds follow the contract's cancellation clause (July 10, 2025). Fukuoka City: hair-removal salon closures (Japanese)

What a salon can do is say it openly. If pay-per-visit or monthly payment is available, write it on the pricing page. If prepayments are protected, say how; if they are not, do not imply that they are. For "unlimited" or "guaranteed" courses, write the paid sessions and period separately from the conditions after the course becomes free. These are the facts an AI can point to when someone asks "Can I avoid paying everything up front?" or "What happens after the guarantee period?".

Be honest about the line with medical hair removal

In Japan, a 2001 notice from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (医政医発第105号) says that irradiating the hair root with a laser or other high-energy light to destroy the papilla and similar structures, whether or not the device is a medical one, is an act that a non-doctor may not perform as a business under Article 17 of the Medical Practitioners Act. MHLW notice 105, as posted by Naha City (Japanese PDF). Other countries draw this line differently, so check your own rules.

On a salon's pages, the starting point is not to use wording that reads as if you perform what that rule reserves for doctors. Phrases like "destroys the hair root", "the same results as a clinic" or "never grows back", quoted by an AI, misdescribe what your salon does. Instead, write what you do and what you do not: "This is not medical hair removal. If you would like treatment at a medical clinic, we will tell you so."

Many people ask AI whether salon or medical hair removal is better. A salon page that sets out the difference fairly is easier for an AI to use as a source in that comparison. If you are a clinic that offers medical hair removal, describe yourself as a medical provider, not a BeautySalon. AI search for dermatology clinics

Structured data: BeautySalon and a Service per course

schema.org has no type just for esthetic or hair-removal salons. Checked in schema-dts 2.1.0 on November 21, 2026, the subtypes of HealthAndBeautyBusiness are BeautySalon, DaySpa, HairSalon, HealthClub, NailSalon and TattooParlor. The closest type for an esthetic or hair-removal salon is BeautySalon; for a spa facility, DaySpa.

Publish each course as its own Service. Put the number of sessions and the expiry in the name, the total including tax in the Offer, and the payment options and a pointer to your cancellation terms in the description. Keep booking sites and your Google Business Profile in line

Esthetic and hair-removal salons are often listed on booking and coupon sites. AI reads those pages too, so if your site and a booking site show different sessions, totals or expiry for the same course, the AI cannot tell which is right. A common gap is a booking site that shows only an introductory price, with the regular course total written nowhere.

When you change prices or end a course, update your page text, your structured data (the registered services), your Google Business Profile and your booking sites on the same day. Give campaign prices an end date, and remove them when they end.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Each course has its total including tax, number of sessions and expiry (and when the expiry starts) written as text.
  2. Any costs beyond the total, such as shaving or late cancellation fees, are stated.
  3. The payment options (pay-per-visit, monthly, lump sum, finance) are stated.
  4. The cooling-off period and method, how a cancellation is calculated (with an example from your own course) and how related products are handled are stated.
  5. Whether prepayments are protected, and the paid and free periods of "unlimited" or "guaranteed" courses, are stated.
  6. The page says it is not medical hair removal and uses no wording that reads as a medical procedure (destroying the root, permanent results).
  7. The trial price and content, and that no same-day contract is required, are stated.
  8. The structured data type is BeautySalon (or DaySpa), and course prices in the Services match the page text.
  9. Sessions, totals, expiry and opening hours match across your site, booking sites and Google Business Profile.
  10. llms.txt lists your pricing and cancellation page and your directions page. What is llms.txt?

Check whether AI mentions you, and whether it gets you right

Once the facts are in place, ask AI what your clients would ask. As well as area-plus-condition questions ("salons in my city that offer pay-per-visit hair removal", "facials near the station with no hard sell") to see whether your name comes up, ask with your salon's name included ("What do I get back if I cancel?", "How is it different from medical hair removal?") and record whether the answer matches your pricing page. When an AI gets it wrong, usually nothing on your site answers that question, or your site and a booking site disagree.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time. FAQ

Where should an esthetic salon start with AI search?

Write each course's total including tax, sessions, expiry and payment options, plus your cooling-off and cancellation calculation, as text on your pricing page. Then publish the same facts as BeautySalon structured data and in llms.txt, and make them match your booking sites and Google Business Profile.

Won't publishing cancellation and refund terms lead to more cancellations?

Terms set by law are the client's rights whether you publish them or not. If they are not on your site, AI guesses from general explanations and other sources. Writing your own terms and a worked example correctly is the better way to avoid disputes caused by misunderstanding.

In Japan, what is the most a salon can charge on a mid-term cancellation?

For esthetic contracts over one month and over 50,000 yen: up to 20,000 yen before any service, and after services begin, the value of the services received plus the lower of 20,000 yen or 10% of the unused balance (per the Osaka and Hiroshima prefectural consumer centres). For a specific contract, check with a consumer centre (188 in Japan).

Can a salon page say "the same results as medical hair removal"?

Avoid it. In Japan, the health ministry's notice says that irradiating the hair root with strong light to destroy the papilla may only be done by doctors. Wording that reads that way misdescribes your treatment. State as fact that it is not medical hair removal, and what you do.

Which structured data type should an esthetic salon use?

schema.org has no esthetic or hair-removal type, so BeautySalon is the closest. Use DaySpa for a spa facility. A clinic offering medical hair removal should use a medical type, not BeautySalon.
