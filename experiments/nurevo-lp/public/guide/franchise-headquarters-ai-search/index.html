AI search for franchisors: get franchisee locations and franchise-recruiting pages read correctly

Two kinds of questions about a franchise brand reach AI: customers asking "is this location open on Sunday, and who runs it?" and prospective franchisees asking "what are the fees and the start-up cost, and what support do I get?". The franchisor's job is threefold: state each location's operator and what differs there in plain text, link locations to the brand in structured data without making franchisees look like subsidiaries, and publish dated figures with their assumptions on the recruiting page, kept identical to the figures published elsewhere.

Written November 17, 2026 (JST). This guide is for franchisor marketing teams and the agencies that build franchisor sites. The location-page basics shared with any chain (one page per location, closures and moves, keeping Google Business Profile in step) are in AI search for multi-location businesses. This guide covers what changes when another company (a franchisee) runs the location, and the questions prospective franchisees ask.

AI answers change from day to day and with the wording of the question. These steps make the source information AI reads accurate; they do not guarantee that you appear in AI answers or at any position.

Two kinds of franchise question reach AI

Unlike a company-owned chain, a franchise has two very different readers: people who use the locations and people who want to own one. They may type the same brand name and need completely different facts.

Who asksExample questionWhere the answer comes from
Customer (location named)"Is Sample Diner Riverside open on Sunday?"That location's page, Google Business Profile
Customer (no name)"Which diners near the river are open on Sunday?"Area, hours and category on location pages
Customer (problem)"Who do I contact about a refund from Sample Diner Riverside?"Operator and contact details on the location page
Prospect (brand named)"What are the franchise fee and royalty for Sample Diner?"The franchisor's recruiting page, franchise directories
Prospect (no brand)"Which food franchises can I start with little capital?"Directories, news articles, franchisor pages
Prospect (comparison)"How does the Sample Diner franchise compare with X?"Both recruiting pages, directories

For prospect questions, one study points to the weight of franchise directories. Branding Works, a Japanese company, published a survey on October 12, 2026 (the same question sent to the ChatGPT and Gemini APIs three times a day for nine days, 54 valid answers): 89% of ChatGPT's answers named no specific brand, and the most cited sources were Japanese franchise directory and portal sites (web-repo.jp 8.8%, entrenet.jp 7.7% and others). The most common angle was low start-up cost, at 35.2% (PR TIMES, in Japanese). It is a small study (one question, 54 answers) by a company that sells AI search services, and it covers Japan. Still, the working assumption for a franchisor is clear: AI reads the figures on directories, not only on your own site.

What to state differently on franchisee and company-owned location pages

Customers don't care who runs the location. But payments, refunds, bookings and complaints may be handled differently by each operator, and when AI mixes them up, customers contact the wrong place. On each location page, write the following so it is clear what applies to every location and what applies only here.

ItemExample wordingWhat to watch for franchisee locations
Operator"This location is independently owned and operated by Riverside Foods LLC, a Sample Diner franchisee."Use the registered company name. For company-owned: "Operated by Sample Diner"
Prices and menu"Prices may vary by location. See this location's prices."Separate the brand-wide menu from this location's prices and specials
Payment and loyalty"Payment methods accepted here"Separate the brand app or loyalty scheme from what this location takes
Bookings and enquiriesLocation phone, and what the brand's customer line handlesOne line on which questions go where
Refunds and complaintsContact and hoursDecide whether the franchisor or the location handles them
Jobs"Jobs at this location are offered by Riverside Foods LLC"Make clear the franchisee is the employer

Whether prices can differ by location depends on the franchise agreement and on competition law in your country, which limits how far a franchisor can set franchisees' resale prices. Check with your legal adviser (in Japan, the Japan Fair Trade Commission's franchise guidelines; this guide does not summarise them). The site should state only what has been decided.

Structured data: don't make a franchisee look like a subsidiary

In a company-owned chain, each location's LocalBusiness points to the company with parentOrganization. But schema.org defines parentOrganization as "the larger organization that this organization is a subOrganization of", and subOrganization as "a relationship between two organizations where the first includes the second, e.g., as a subsidiary". Point a franchisee's location at the franchisor with parentOrganization and it reads as part of the franchisor.

schema.org has no property for "franchise" or "franchisee" (checked against the schema-dts 2.1.0 definitions on November 17, 2026). This guide recommends the following split. It is our reading of the schema.org definitions, not a method Google prescribes.

  1. Publish the franchisor (the brand) once as an Organization on the home or About page with a fixed @id. From each location, point to it with brand ("the brand(s) maintained by an organization").
  2. For a franchisee-run location, set parentOrganization to the operating company (the franchisee) and give its legalName (the registered company name). The franchisor stays as brand.
  3. For a company-owned location, set parentOrganization to the franchisor's @id (as in the multi-location guide).
  4. Put the store code in branchCode and use the same number in your records, on the site and in Google Business Profile.
  5. Write the operator's name in the page text as well. Never put a value only in structured data.
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Restaurant",
  "@id": "https://example.com/locations/riverside/#store",
  "name": "Sample Diner Riverside",
  "branchCode": "0123",
  "url": "https://example.com/locations/riverside/",
  "telephone": "+1-555-000-0000",
  "address": { "@type": "PostalAddress", "streetAddress": "1 River St", "addressLocality": "Springfield", "addressRegion": "IL", "addressCountry": "US" },
  "brand": { "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" },
  "parentOrganization": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "Riverside Foods LLC",
    "legalName": "Riverside Foods LLC",
    "url": "https://riverside-foods.example/"
  }
}

Google asks for structured data to match what is visible on the page and says AI features need no special markup (AI features and your website, checked October 29, 2026). Structured data confirms the text; state the franchise relationship in a line of text first.

The recruiting page: publish the figures AI quotes, with dates and assumptions

When answering a prospect, AI reads your recruiting page together with figures on directories and in news articles. An old figure left anywhere can be quoted instead of the new one. Make your own page the newest and most detailed source, and keep outside listings on the same values.

ItemHow to write itAvoid
Start-up costBreakdown and total: franchise fee, deposit, training, fit-out, equipment; tax included or not"From $X" with no list of what is excluded
RoyaltyFlat or a percentage, and which sales it is calculated on"Lowest in the industry" with nothing to compare against
Term, renewal, early exitLength, renewal conditions, how exit costs are worked outA simplified version that differs from the agreement
Number of locationsFranchised and company-owned separately, with a date ("as of October 31, 2026")An undated "500 locations nationwide"
Unit economicsAssumptions (site, seats, average spend, staff cost), and whether the figure is actual or projectedSales or profit figures without assumptions
SupportTraining length, help before and after opening, what the franchisor's staff do"Full support" on its own
Territories and requirementsWhere you are recruiting, company or individual, experience neededImplying every region is open

Franchisors usually have to give prospects a legal disclosure document: in Japan under the Act on the Promotion of Small and Medium Retail Business (e-Gov, in Japanese), in the US the Franchise Disclosure Document. Check the required items in your own country's rules and keep the recruiting page's figures identical to that document (this guide does not list the legal items).

Prospects now arrive having researched the brand with AI, so franchisors are urged to make their materials readable and transparent (Franchising.com, November 30, 2025). Put the figures in the page text, not only in a PDF, and add the common questions (fees, capital, no experience, running it alongside another job) as an FAQ for that page only.

Franchisor, franchisee, agency: who updates what

In a franchise, information drifts mostly because several people edit the same value separately. Keep one location master owned by the franchisor, and fix the order in which every listing is updated from it.

InformationDecided byUpdated byChecked
Name format, logo, brand-wide menuFranchisorFranchisor (or agency)On every change
Location hours and closuresFranchiseeFranchisee tells franchisor → franchisor updates the siteMonthly and on change
Location prices and specialsFranchisee (within the agreement)Franchisor publishes on the siteOn change
Operator and contact detailsFranchiseeFranchisor updates the location pageOn contract change or resale
Google Business ProfileSet in the agreementWhoever the agreement namesMonthly
Recruiting-page figuresFranchisorFranchisor (and tells directories)Quarterly and on every revision

Whether the franchisor or the franchisee owns each Google Business Profile, and how to manage many locations together, follow Google's own conditions, which change; check the Google Business Profile Help Center and write the choice into the agreement or your operating rules. If franchisees run their own social accounts or sites, give them a short rule sheet: the name format, a link back to the brand's location page, and which figures they may publish.

When a franchisee sells its location to another company, change the operator, jobs and contact details on the same day. If the old operator's name stays, AI may keep describing the location as theirs.

If an agency maintains the franchisor's site, put this table into the maintenance scope so there is no doubt about who updates what. Agencies that support franchisors or franchisees monthly can join the partner program.

On WordPress (with Nurevo AIO)

If the franchisor's site runs on WordPress, Nurevo AIO (free) publishes the franchisor's facts in structured data and llms.txt, and location and recruiting pages are handled like this. Nurevo's settings describe one business per site, so enter the franchisor (the brand) there.

  1. Give each location one page and write its operator in the text (the company name for franchisees, "Operated by [brand]" for company-owned). Store the operator in a custom field and output both the text and the structured data from it.
  2. In Nurevo's business details, enter the franchisor's name, address, phone and URL. Don't enter one location's opening hours; they would read as every location's hours.
  3. Add a store node only on location pages with the developer filter webmcp_canary_schema_graph, in extra. The franchisor's @id is available as $bundle['org_id']; put it in brand. If the operator field is filled, parentOrganization is the operator; if empty, the franchisor (code below).
  4. On the recruiting page, enter questions for that page only (fees, capital, no experience) in the Nurevo panel of the editor and place the Nurevo FAQ block set to "This page's own questions". It is published as FAQPage on that page only.
  5. With Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO or Slim SEO, Nurevo adds only the missing facts to that plugin's structured data, and the store node is appended to its graph. It also runs alongside SEO SIMPLE PACK and VK All in One Expansion Unit (from 0.9.1). If a store-locator plugin already outputs per-location structured data, keep only one of the two.
  6. Open /llms.txt and check that the recruiting page and the location list appear. llms.txt lists the 30 most recently updated pages and posts; if they are missing, add lines with the webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines filter. Pages for closed territories or closed locations can be removed with "Don't list this page in llms.txt".
  7. If franchisees run their own WordPress sites, export the settings on the franchisor's site (Settings → Tools, or wp nurevo export), import them on the franchisee's site, and have them change only the location name, address, phone and hours. Keys, tokens, plan and connection are never exported.
// Add a store node only on location pages (values from the same custom fields the page shows)
add_filter('webmcp_canary_schema_graph', function (array $bundle) {
    if (!is_singular('store')) {
        return $bundle;
    }
    $id    = get_queried_object_id();
    $store = array(
        '@type'      => 'Restaurant',
        '@id'        => get_permalink($id) . '#store',
        'name'       => get_the_title($id),
        'url'        => get_permalink($id),
        'branchCode' => get_post_meta($id, 'store_code', true),
        'telephone'  => get_post_meta($id, 'store_tel', true),
        'brand'      => array('@id' => $bundle['org_id']), // the franchise brand
    );
    $operator = get_post_meta($id, 'operator_name', true); // e.g. Riverside Foods LLC
    if ($operator) {
        // Franchisee-run: the operating company is the parentOrganization; HQ stays the brand
        $store['parentOrganization'] = array('@type' => 'Organization', 'name' => $operator, 'legalName' => $operator);
    } else {
        // Company-owned: HQ runs the store
        $store['parentOrganization'] = array('@id' => $bundle['org_id']);
    }
    $bundle['extra'][] = $store;
    return $bundle;
});

The two kinds of question draw on different pages, so log them separately. Ask the same question to the same AI on different days, and don't judge from a single answer.

Franchisor checklist

  1. Each location page states its operator in text (company name for franchisees, "operated by the brand" for company-owned).
  2. Brand-wide information and location-only information (prices, specials, payment) are separated.
  3. One line says whether bookings, refunds and complaints go to the brand or the location.
  4. Franchisee store nodes link to the franchisor with brand; their parentOrganization is the operator.
  5. Company-owned store nodes link to the franchisor with parentOrganization.
  6. Store codes match across your records, the site and Google Business Profile.
  7. Recruiting-page costs show a breakdown, tax treatment and a date, and match the disclosure document.
  8. Location counts separate franchised and company-owned and carry a date; unit economics state assumptions and whether they are actual or projected.
  9. Figures on directories and other outside listings are corrected on every revision.
  10. There is a table of who updates what, and a same-day procedure for resales and closures.
  11. Customer and prospect questions were logged on different days.

FAQ

We have both franchisee-run and company-owned locations. Do the location pages need different layouts?

No. Use the same layout with an operator field: the company name for franchisees, "operated by [brand]" for company-owned. In structured data, give both brand = franchisor; set parentOrganization to the franchisor for company-owned locations and to the operator for franchisee locations. That expresses the difference.

Won't naming the franchisee hurt brand consistency?

Keep the location name and logo in the brand format and put the operator in one line lower on the page. Where refunds, complaints or hiring are handled by the franchisee, leaving the operator out leads customers and AI to the wrong place.

We set parentOrganization to the franchisor for every location. Is that a problem?

It may not cause visible problems, but by the schema.org definitions it describes an organization included in the franchisor, such as a subsidiary. For franchisee-run locations we recommend brand = franchisor and parentOrganization = the operating company.

If we publish our fees and start-up cost, won't we lose out in comparisons?

If you don't, AI may answer with figures from directories or old articles. Publishing a dated breakdown with assumptions on your own page reduces the chance of being compared on wrong figures. Decide which figures to publish together with your disclosure document.

A directory still shows our old figures. What can we do?

Send the listing owner the new figures with their date and ask for an update. On your recruiting page, show the revision date ("revised October 2026") so AI and people can tell which is newer.

A franchisee wants to do its own AI search work on its own site.

Give it a short rule sheet: use the same name, address, phone, hours and operator as the franchisor's location master, link to and from the brand's location page, and don't publish recruiting figures. If it uses Nurevo AIO on WordPress, importing the franchisor's exported settings and changing only the location's values keeps the sites aligned.

Will these steps make AI recommend our brand?

There is no guarantee. AI answers vary by day and wording, and directories appear to weigh heavily in prospect questions. These steps make the information AI reads accurate and reduce the mix-ups between locations, and between franchisor and franchisee.
