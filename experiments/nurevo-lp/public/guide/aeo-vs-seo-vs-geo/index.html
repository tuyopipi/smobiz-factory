AEO vs GEO vs SEO (plus LLMO and AIO): What Each Term Means, How Measurement Differs, and What to Do First

AEO, GEO, LLMO and AIO are all names for the same work: getting your business named, or your pages cited, inside answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and similar tools. In practice you can treat them as one thing. SEO is about ranking in a list of search results. The foundation is shared (pages that can be crawled and that state accurate facts); what differs is where results show up and how you measure them. Google itself says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special optimization beyond SEO best practices.

Written 23 November 2026 (JST). This is a full rewrite of our short October 2026 page, based on a fresh reading of 13 top-ranking Japanese and English articles and the primary sources. It is for business owners lost in the acronyms, and for web agencies that sell, or have been sold, "GEO" or "LLMO" services.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is asked. The steps here make the information AI reads more accurate; they cannot guarantee that you will appear in an AI answer or hold any position.

The terms at a glance: SEO, AEO, GEO, LLMO and AIO

Here is where each term comes from and what it aims at. Writers define them slightly differently, and there is no agreed standard definition.

AcronymStands forWhere the term comes fromWhat it usually means
SEOSearch Engine OptimizationGeneral term that spread with web search enginesRanking higher in the list of links on Google, Bing and other search engines
AEOAnswer Engine OptimizationBefore generative AI, it meant winning featured snippets and voice-assistant answersBeing named or cited inside AI answers
GEOGenerative Engine OptimizationNamed in a research paper first published in 2023 (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024)Having your content used and cited in generative AI answers
LLMOLarge Language Model OptimizationCommon in articles by Japanese SEO firms and agenciesGetting LLMs such as ChatGPT to understand and describe you correctly

Watch out for AIO, because it has two meanings. When someone offers "AIO optimization", ask whether they mean AI search in general or Google AI Overviews only. The answer changes where you measure the results (see below).

Are AEO, GEO, LLMO and AIO the same thing?

For practical purposes, yes. Ahrefs' Japanese blog says GEO, LLMO and AEO all refer to the same thing (Ahrefs: GEO is just SEO). A Japanese article on AIO versus LLMO notes that the definitions are not settled and then lists the same shared tactics for both (Sungrove).

Some English articles split them: AEO as being extracted as a direct answer, GEO as being summarized, synthesized and cited by generative AI (NeuronWriter). That distinction does not create separate work on your website, though. Whatever the label, the work comes down to three things: make your site readable to AI, make the facts on your pages accurate and consistent, and measure whether you actually appear.

Which word to use: whichever your customers and colleagues already use. In proposals and contracts, write down what will be fixed and what will be measured, and how, rather than the label. If the same work and deliverables are sold as separately priced "AEO", "GEO" and "LLMO" packages, compare what is actually in them.

As the table shows, AI search optimization adds to SEO rather than replacing it. A page that is not indexed, or that does not state its facts in the text, is unlikely to be used by either.

In short: how content is written can change visibility in a research setting; Google says no special work is needed; and raising an on-site score does not by itself get you mentioned. That is why measuring whether you actually appear is a required step, not an extra.

Where to start: priorities for a small business

Whatever the label, do these in order, because each one builds on the one before.

  1. Indexing and crawling: check in Search Console that your home page and key pages are indexed. Check that robots.txt, your CDN or your firewall does not block AI search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot (how to check)
  2. Facts on the page: state your business name, address, phone number, opening hours, prices, service area and what you offer in the text, not in images
  3. Structured data: publish those facts as JSON-LD, with the same values the page shows
  4. Consistency elsewhere: make your Google Business Profile, directory listings and social profiles match your website
  5. Pages that answer questions: answer what customers ask AI (how much it costs, whether booking is needed, where you are) with a clear heading and a short answer
  6. Measurement: pick questions that do not include your business name, ask them repeatedly under the same conditions, and record how often you are named or cited (how to measure)

Treat llms.txt as something to add after these six. Its effect is unproven, but it costs little and there is no official statement that it does harm (llms.txt explained).

FAQ

Should I say AEO or GEO?

Either. The work and deliverables are almost the same. English-language marketers use both GEO and AEO; in Japan, LLMO and AIO are common too. On your own pages and in proposals, mention the terms your readers are likely to search for.

If I do AI search optimization, do I still need SEO?

Yes. Most AI answers are built from pages that search crawlers and indexes have collected. Pages that are not indexed, or that do not state their facts, are unlikely to be used by AI either. Think of SEO as the foundation and AI search optimization as the layer on top.

Is LLMO about training data and GEO about search?

Some articles say so, but it is not an agreed definition. Real AI answers can draw on both search at answer time and training data, and the work you can do on your site (accurate facts, crawler access, consistency with other sites) is the same either way. Whether to block training crawlers is a separate decision from AI search.

Does AIO mean Google AI Overviews?

It is used both ways: AIO as short for AI Overviews, and AIO as AI Optimization. Ask which one is meant. If it is Google AI Overviews only, Search Console's generative AI performance report shows your impressions there.

How long does AI search optimization take to work?

There is no fixed timeline. AI answers change easily, and how quickly search crawlers re-read your pages differs between services. Measure the same questions under the same conditions every month and judge by the trend, not a single result.

Does the GEO paper's "up to 40%" apply to ChatGPT?

Not necessarily. The paper experimented with 10,000 research queries and a generative engine built for the study, and also tested Perplexity.ai, but each commercial AI search tool works and updates differently. Read it as evidence that writing choices can matter, and measure on your own site.
