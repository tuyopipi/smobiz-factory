How to write a booking page AI reads correctly: booking methods, cut-offs, cancellation rules, and how booking inside AI works

To get AI to answer booking questions correctly, pick one booking page on your own site and write, as HTML text rather than inside an image or an embedded booking widget: how to book (online, phone, messaging), whether booking is required and what happens to walk-ins, how late and how far ahead you take bookings, the cancellation deadline and fee, and the list of official places to book. As of November 2026, booking inside ChatGPT or Google's AI runs mainly for restaurants, through reservation platforms such as OpenTable. Your own booking page is where AI goes to read your booking rules whether or not you are on one of those platforms.

Written November 21, 2026 (JST). How to write opening hours is in opening hours and holidays, and prices in how to write a pricing page. This guide covers the booking page that salons, clinics, restaurants, studios, lodging and call-out services all have in common.

AI answers change by day, by wording and by service. The steps here make a page whose booking rules AI is less likely to misread, and show how to check; they do not guarantee you will appear in an answer or become bookable inside an AI.

More businesses ask how to become bookable in ChatGPT. For now, booking inside an AI runs through integrations with booking platforms. There is no tag you can put on your site that adds a booking button. The table lists what the official announcements say.

ChatGPT's reservations are built from lists of restaurants that booking platforms send to OpenAI. Each record is meant to carry the restaurant's own website URL and its listing URL on the platform (OpenAI: Restaurant reservation conversion spec, written for booking platforms).

So the booking button inside an AI depends on your booking platform. What a business controls is keeping its platform listings correct and writing its booking rules correctly on its own site. Even without a booking button, an AI asked "do I need to book?" or "is there a cancellation fee?" will answer from your site and from review and listing sites. The rest of this guide is about making that answer right.

This guide covers, for any business that takes bookings, what to write on the booking page, how to sort out several booking channels, structured data, WordPress steps and how to check the result.

Eight facts every booking page needs

Booking questions fall into roughly eight kinds. Give each a one- or two-sentence paragraph, or a table row, in the booking page's text.

FactExampleThe question AI gets
How to bookOnline 24 hours a day, by phone 10 am to 7 pm, by message (replies during opening hours)"How do I book at (business)?"
Whether booking is requiredBy appointment. Walk-ins welcome when we have space (expect a wait)"Can I just walk in?"
Cut-offUntil 8 pm the day before. Same-day by phone only"Can I book for today?"
How far aheadUp to two months ahead"Can I book for next month yet?"
DurationA haircut takes about 60 minutes"How long does it take?"
CancellationFree until the day before. Same-day cancellations are charged 50%. Cancel by phone"Is there a cancellation fee?"
Payment and depositsNo payment when booking. Pay on the day by cash, card or mobile payment"Do I have to pay in advance?"
Who it's for, what to bringFirst visit: arrive 10 minutes early to fill in a form. Children welcome"Is it OK for a first visit?" "Can I bring my kids?"

Make every sentence make sense on its own. AI sometimes lifts a single sentence to answer, so avoid phrases that lean on what's around them, such as "see above" or "click here". Weak: "Book here. Please let us know early if you need to cancel." Strong: "Online booking is open 24 hours a day, and phone booking from 10 am to 7 pm. Cancellation is free until the day before; same-day cancellations are charged 50% of the price."

Say what changes with and without a booking. If booked customers come first, write "Walk-ins are seen between booked appointments." If you are strictly by appointment, write "We can't see customers without a booking." That reduces answers that wrongly say "no booking needed".

Several booking sites: decide which channels are official

Salons and restaurants commonly take bookings in several places: their own system, booking sites, phone, messaging. AI passes on whatever booking channel it finds, so a booking site you've left or an old phone number can end up in the answer. Sort it out in this order.

  1. List every channel you take bookings through (your own system, booking sites, the Google Business Profile booking link, phone, messaging, social media DMs)
  2. Remove listings on channels you no longer use, or mark them "no longer taking bookings"
  3. On the booking page, write "We take bookings in these ways" and list only the channels you use
  4. If each service has its own booking URL, fix one official URL per service (a link straight to that service's booking screen beats a link to your home page)
  5. Use the same cancellation rules and cut-offs on your site and every booking site. Where they differ, say which rule applies to which channel
  6. Link from booking-site profiles and your Google Business Profile description back to your own booking page

Google Business Profile booking links have rules: the profile must be verified; the link must lead to a page dedicated to your business where the booking can be completed; social media, messaging apps, app stores and link shorteners aren't allowed; the page must open without a login or CAPTCHA and return 200 (Business links policies). You can also appear through a booking provider that works with Reserve with Google; Google says custom links you add yourself get no performance data (Bookings through a provider).

Don't write availability. Write how to check it

Write "Saturdays are usually quiet" or "no booking needed on weekdays" and an AI may keep using the sentence after things change. No AI service publishes how often it re-reads a page. Treat availability as something your booking system shows, and write how to check it instead: "See available times on the online booking screen. For same-day availability, please call."

If you mention busy times, phrase them as a stable pattern: "Weekend afternoons get busy, so we recommend booking." Making it look emptier than it is misleads people and AI alike.

Make it readable: check embeds, images and logins

Many booking systems show their screen as an embed from another site (an iframe) or through JavaScript. People can see it, but whether AI reads the text inside an embed as part of your page differs by service and isn't published. Write the booking rules outside the embed, in the booking page's own text. The booking form itself can stay embedded.

This command shows whether your booking page opens without a login and carries the rules in its HTML.

# Is the booking page public (200, no login) and are the rules in its HTML?
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" https://example.com/book/
curl -s https://example.com/book/ | grep -iE "cancel|walk-in|same day"
# If nothing prints, the rules live in an image, an embedded widget or JavaScript

OpenAI says that to be included in ChatGPT search, you should allow OAI-SearchBot to crawl your site and make sure your host or CDN allows traffic from OpenAI's published IP addresses (OpenAI Help). Check that bot protection isn't blocking the booking page in particular. For businesses that want AI agents to complete bookings for customers, Stripe suggests reviewing bot protections and considering WebMCP, but that is a further step that includes payments (Stripe).

Structured data: ReserveAction is fine to add, but don't expect much

schema.org has ReserveAction, the action of making a reservation, and you can use it to tie a booking URL to a service. But we found no official statement from ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI features that they use ReserveAction for booking. Google says AI features need no special structured data and that structured data must match the visible text (AI features and your website, checked 2026-11-18). Adding it does little harm, but it won't make a booking button appear.

If you add it, point each Service at its booking URL like this. It is the same shape Nurevo AIO publishes.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Service",
  "@id": "https://example.com/#nurevo-service-1",
  "name": "Haircut (wash and blow-dry included)",
  "provider": { "@id": "https://example.com/#business" },
  "additionalProperty": {
    "@type": "PropertyValue",
    "name": "duration",
    "value": 60,
    "unitCode": "MIN",
    "unitText": "minutes"
  },
  "offers": { "@type": "Offer", "price": "55.00", "priceCurrency": "USD" },
  "potentialAction": {
    "@type": "ReserveAction",
    "target": { "@type": "EntryPoint", "urlTemplate": "https://example.com/book/haircut/" },
    "result": { "@type": "Reservation", "name": "Haircut (wash and blow-dry included)" }
  }
}
</script>

The key is not to write a Reservation on its own. A bare Reservation says a booking already exists, which isn't true. As the result of a ReserveAction it says "this action produces a booking". Check the syntax with the Schema Markup Validator.

How to check: fix your booking questions and keep a record

To see whether your booking rules come across correctly, ask the same questions to the same AI on different days and record the answers. Don't judge from a single answer.

QuestionWhat to look at
"Do I need to book at (business)?"Is it right about appointments only vs walk-ins welcome?
"Can I book (business) for today?"Are the cut-off and the method (phone only, etc.) right?
"What is (business)'s cancellation fee?"Are the deadline and fee right? Is the source an old booking site?
"How do I book at (business)?"Is the channel it gives one you use now?
"Which (business type) in (area) can I book today?"Are you on the list for an unbranded question? Does a booking button appear (restaurants)?

Record the date, the AI service, the question, the answer and the source URL. If the source is an old booking site or blog post, fix that. Checklist before you publish

  1. There is one booking page, and it opens without a login
  2. How to book and when bookings are taken is written as text
  3. Whether booking is required, and what happens without one, is stated
  4. The cut-off (same day, day before) and how far ahead you book are stated
  5. The cancellation deadline, fee and how to cancel are stated and match the booking sites
  6. Only channels you use are listed; old ones are gone
  7. The page says how to check availability rather than stating availability
  8. The booking rules are in the page text, outside embeds and images
  9. The booking URLs in structured data point at the booking screens you use now
  10. Your Google Business Profile booking link points at the same channel

FAQ

What do I put on my site to get a Reserve button in ChatGPT?

Nothing on your site switches it on. OpenAI's help says the Reserve button only appears when ChatGPT can match the restaurant to a listing on a supported provider (OpenTable, Resy, Yelp). What you can do is write your booking rules correctly on your site and keep your booking platform listings accurate.

Will adding ReserveAction bring bookings from AI?

We found no official statement that it does. ReserveAction does little harm, but no benefit to bookings has been shown. Fix the booking rules in your page text first.

Our booking page is just an embedded widget with no text.

Whether AI reads the text inside an embed as part of your page differs by service and isn't published. The form can stay embedded; write the booking methods, cut-offs and cancellation rules as text above or below it.

Our booking site's cancellation rules differ from our own site's.

There's no telling which one an AI will quote. Make them the same if you can; if not, say which rule applies to which channel, for example "Online bookings can be cancelled free until the day before; bookings made through booking sites follow each site's rules."

Would writing our availability on the site help us appear in AI?

Availability changes, so writing it in the page text risks old information staying in AI answers. Write how to check availability, and let the booking system show the availability itself.

Will businesses other than restaurants become bookable inside AI?

In August 2025 Google said agentic booking in AI Mode would expand to local service appointments, starting in the U.S. Check Google's announcements for your country. What you can do now is keep your booking page and your Google Business Profile booking link accurate.

After we change the booking page, when will AI answers change?

No AI service publishes how long it takes. Check with the same questions a few days to a few weeks later. If an old booking channel still appears, check the source URL and fix that page.
