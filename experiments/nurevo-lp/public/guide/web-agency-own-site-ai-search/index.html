How a web design agency can make its own site easy for AI search to recommend

When someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "which web design agency should I hire in my city?", the AI searches the web and pieces an answer together from several pages: agency websites, directories, reviews and comparison articles. To be one of the candidates, write down where you work, what you build, what it costs and what you have built, in a form AI can read on your own site; make your listings elsewhere say the same thing; and check a fixed set of questions regularly. AI answers vary between days and runs, so nothing guarantees a mention, but an agency whose facts cannot be read is hard to include.

Written November 12, 2026 (JST). What Google, OpenAI and Perplexity say is based on their own documentation. Their guidance can change, so check the current pages too.

This guide is about winning clients for your agency, not about AEO for your clients. For the client-facing service, see how agencies can offer AI search optimization monthly.

How AI answers "which agency should I hire?"

ChatGPT search relies on a crawler called OAI-SearchBot, and OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI: overview of crawlers). Perplexity recommends allowing PerplexityBot (Perplexity: crawlers). For AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations, and that SEO best practices still apply (Google Search Central: AI features and your website).

In short, AI builds its answer from pages it could find and read. Your website is one of those sources, alongside directories, portals, reviews and social profiles you do not control. What you can do comes down to three things: (1) publish your facts on your own site in a readable form, (2) make outside listings say the same, and (3) measure the actual answers.

Since almost no agency blocks AI, the usual problem is not that AI cannot read your site. It is that, once it does, it still cannot tell where you work, what you build or what you charge. Note that the survey looked at home pages only.

The questions buyers ask, and the answers your site should hold

People who are about to hire an agency ask AI questions with conditions attached. For each kind of question, here is the fact AI needs and where to put it.

What these have in common: "get in touch" and "flexible solutions" give AI nothing to answer with. The more plainly you state numbers, place names and industries, the easier it is for AI to match you to a question with conditions.

Step 1: write your facts as page text

  1. About page: company name, address, service area (by city or region name), year founded, team size, who runs it, phone and email, business hours. If you serve the whole country, say so, and say whether clients need to visit.
  2. Services and pricing: for each plan, a price guide, what is included (number of pages, design, mobile, CMS, photography and so on) and a typical timeline, in a table. A worked example of a common project helps AI more than "from $X" alone.
  3. Case studies: for each one, the industry, location, what you built (type of site, number of pages), your part in it and the launch year. Get the client's permission before naming them or linking to the site; without it, write "a dental clinic in [city] (20 pages, WordPress)".
  4. Specialities: name the industries, client sizes and CMSs you are good at, linked to the case studies that prove it. Do not claim industries you have no work in; when AI finds your claims contradicted elsewhere, it trusts you less.
  5. FAQ: short, specific answers to what prospects really ask: price, timeline, maintenance, rounds of revisions, contract length, and who owns the domain and hosting.

Step 2: use structured data to say where you are and what you do

Structured data (schema.org JSON-LD) repeats the facts on your pages in a form machines read easily. For an agency, start with Organization, and add LocalBusiness if you have an office that receives visitors. schema.org also has a ProfessionalService type, but schema.org notes that the general type for local businesses was deprecated (schema.org: ProfessionalService).

On WordPress, the free versions of Yoast SEO, Rank Math and All in One SEO usually publish an Organization with a name, URL and logo, but often not the address or phone number. Add only what is missing, and never publish the same company twice. See adding local business schema to your SEO plugin. Google also asks that structured data match what is visible on the page (Google: LocalBusiness structured data).

Step 3: make sure you are not blocking AI crawlers

  1. robots.txt: check that OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Googlebot are not blocked. Only 1.3% of surveyed agencies blocked an AI bot, but a staging robots.txt left on the live site happens to agencies too.
  2. Security plugins, WAFs and CDN bot protection: check their settings for anything that stops AI crawlers outside robots.txt.
  3. Training crawlers are a separate decision: whether to allow crawlers that collect content for model training, such as GPTBot, is a different question from appearing in AI search. Decide it as company policy.

Step 4: put a summary and your key pages in llms.txt

llms.txt is a text file at /llms.txt that summarises your site and lists its key pages for AI. List a one-line description of the company, your service area, services and pricing pages, case studies by industry, FAQ and contact page. In the survey, 13.6% of agencies had one.

Google says you don't need new AI text files for its own AI features, and an llms.txt does not guarantee a mention. Treat it as a helpful extra, after your page text and structured data. See how to write llms.txt.

Step 5: make your listings elsewhere say the same thing

AI reads pages beyond your own site, so out-of-date listings put old addresses or discontinued services into its answers. Keep your company name, address, phone number, service area and main services identical in places like these:

  1. Google Business Profile (if you have an office)
  2. Agency directories, comparison sites and matching platforms
  3. Official social profiles, chamber of commerce and trade association member pages
  4. Company details on job sites, press releases

Do not write your own reviews or comparison articles, or have them written under a third party's name. Rules on undisclosed advertising differ by country; in Japan, stealth marketing has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations since October 1, 2023, and posts a business asks or instructs third parties to make count as that business's advertising (Consumer Affairs Agency: stealth marketing (Japanese)). Asking clients for honest feedback is fine; do not dictate what they say or make a reward conditional on it, and disclose the relationship.

Step 6: check a fixed set of questions once a month

Finally, pick about ten questions your prospects are likely to ask and, once a month under the same conditions, ask them in ChatGPT and Perplexity. Record whether you were named (a mention), whether your site was a source (a citation), and which page was cited. Answers change between runs, so ask each question more than once and do not judge from a single result. See how to measure AI visibility.

Then decide which step to go back to. If the sources are mostly directories, review your outside listings. If only competitors appear, review how clearly you state conditions such as service area and pricing.

Checklist

  1. The About page states your address, service area and contact details in plain text
  2. Pricing guides, what they include and typical timelines are published
  3. Case studies show industry, location and scope, with the client's permission
  4. Organization (and LocalBusiness if relevant) structured data includes address, phone and service area, and matches the page
  5. The SEO plugin or theme and your additions do not publish the company twice
  6. robots.txt, WAF and CDN do not block OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot or Googlebot
  7. llms.txt lists a company summary and your key pages
  8. Outside listings (Google Business Profile, directories, social profiles) state the same facts
  9. A fixed set of questions is recorded monthly under the same conditions

FAQ

Will a paid listing on a comparison site make AI recommend us?

We don't know. AI does cite comparison sites, but which ones it reads varies by question and by run, and we found no primary source measuring the effect of paid listings. If you list, make sure the listing states the same facts as your site.

Are we at a disadvantage if we don't publish prices?

For questions with a budget, such as "around $3,000", an agency with no price information is hard to match. Even if every project is quoted individually, a guide for a typical project and what the price includes helps both AI and people decide.

If we list a wide service area, will we appear in more places?

We don't recommend listing areas you do not actually serve. When your claims contradict your case studies or outside listings, AI is less likely to read you correctly. If you serve the whole country, say so, and name the places where you have work.

Will publishing lots of blog posts get us into AI answers?

Specific answers to prospects' questions matter more than volume. Start with pages that answer pre-hire questions with facts: price, timeline, maintenance and case studies.

How long until we see results?

There is no fixed time. How quickly AI picks up new information, and how its answers change, differs by service. Record the same questions monthly and judge by the trend.
