Original research and data pages: putting a citable "original" on your own site for AI search and journalists

To have original research or data cited by AI search, publish the original on your own site, not only in a press release, and state the sample, count, period, method and limits right next to the numbers. Research suggests that text with statistics and quotations is used more in generative answers, but no AI company publishes how it picks sources, and no method can promise a citation. Even a small business can produce data nobody else has: tallies of its own orders or enquiries, price records, or measured comparisons.

Written 25 November 2026 (JST). Research findings and figures were checked the same day against the papers (arXiv), a Search Engine Land article, Google's documentation, and the Japanese Consumer Affairs Agency's notices and press coverage.

AI answers change from day to day, each AI company decides which pages to cite, and the detailed criteria are not public. This guide is about publishing research correctly and in a form others can verify.

Do numbers and first-hand data get used in AI answers? Three studies

"Original data gets cited by AI" is a common claim, but the studies behind it come with conditions. Here are the three most useful.

StudySetupFindings
GEO (Princeton and others, KDD 2024)10,000 queries; an experimental pipeline where gpt-3.5-turbo answers from Google's top 5 results (also tested on Perplexity)Adding statistics raised the position-adjusted word count metric from 19.3 to 25.2, adding quotations to 27.2, citing sources to 24.6; keyword stuffing dropped it to 17.7. Up to about 40% improvement (up to 37% on Perplexity)
Kevin Indig (Search Engine Land)Gauge data: 301 pages cited across 316 prompts in 7 verticals, 1,075 citationsPrimary research was 8 pages (2.7%) but took 8.4% of citations (90), 11.3 per page vs 3.4 for others. But 75 of the 90 came from one cluster: cloud data warehouse benchmarks
Sprinklr researchers (SIGIR 2026)Six AI models, 252,000 trials comparing two documentsTopical relevance and list position mattered most for being cited first. Explicit prices and a recent timestamp also helped. Formatting-only edits had little effect

Sources: the GEO paper, Kevin Indig's article, What Gets Cited. GEO's results come from an experimental pipeline, not today's ChatGPT or Google AI Mode. Indig's article does not explain how prompts were chosen, and no date is shown on it.

Together they support only this much: (1) numbers, quotations and named sources in the text tend to be used more in answers; (2) primary research is strongest where people ask for a benchmark figure; (3) content that fits the question and a recent date matter more than formatting.

Google's own self-assessment questions for helpful content include whether the content provides original information, reporting, research or analysis (Google Search Central).

What marketers in Japan report

Interest in first-hand information is rising in Japan as well. In a PRIZMA survey (July 2026, 426 people), "primary information" was the measure marketers felt worked best for Google's AI Overviews at 43.5%, second to outside distribution at 52.8% (ASCII.jp, Japanese). Another survey (August 2026, 1,003 people) found primary information was the most common tactic in use, at 46.9% (TRUE MARKETING, Japanese).

Both come from companies that sell AI-search services, and the effects are self-reported. They do not show that publishing first-hand data gets you cited.

Five kinds of original data a small business can produce

You don't need to commission a big survey. You already hold data no other company has.

KindExamplesWatch out for
Tallies of your own workBreakdown of quote requests, busiest months for enquiries, share of repairs by faultAggregate so no individual can be identified; don't publish very small groups
Price and rate recordsMonthly purchase prices; asking rents in your area tracked for a yearSay what you recorded, where and when
Measurements and benchmarksTools or equipment tested under the same conditions; site speed comparisonsDescribe conditions and steps; be fair to other companies' products
Customer surveysPost-service satisfaction, reasons for choosing youState that only customers were asked; read the Japan rules section below before using it in a "No.1" claim
Re-tallied public dataGovernment statistics re-cut by region or industryName the original source and date retrieved, and say you processed it

In Indig's analysis, citations clustered on benchmark results. Look for questions in your trade that people want to compare but can't find numbers for ("how many days does X repair take?"), and a topic usually follows.

Ten things to put on the research page

A press release lives on the distribution service; the original of the numbers lives on your site. Always link from the release to this page.

ItemHow to write itWhy
TitleWhat, where, when, how many (e.g. "412 moving quote requests in X city, October 2026")Readers and AI can tell at a glance whether it fits the question
Key findingsOne number per sentence, with the base, period and source name in the same sentenceThe sentence still makes sense if quoted alone
About the studyPopulation, how it was selected, period, method, count (n)Lets readers and AI judge how much weight the number carries
Questions and definitionsThe exact question wording; what counted as "yes"Keeps the meaning of the number stable
Results tableA text table, not an image, with the base for each figureBoth AI and readers can read it
LimitationsWhat you didn't measure; where bias is likelyPrevents over-reading
OpinionClearly marked: "From here on, this is our view"Keeps facts and interpretation apart
Citation termsHow to credit, plus ready-to-use sentencesMakes it easy for others to cite correctly
Data fileCSV or similar, with column descriptionsOthers can verify
Dates and change logPublished, tallied, what was correctedResearch suggests recent dates help

Nurevo's own research pages use this structure. For example, our survey of Japanese web agencies' sites states 2,756 targets, 2,357 home pages reached and a tally date of 5 November 2026 in its method section, and keeps limitations, citation terms and our opinion under separate headings. It names no companies and publishes no figures for groups under 30 sites.

Rules when survey results become advertising claims (Japan example)

Advertising rules for survey-based claims differ by country. In Japan, using results in ads such as "No.1 in customer satisfaction" falls under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. Japan's rules are described here as one example; check the rules where you operate.

On 26 September 2024 Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency published a fact-finding report on No.1 claims (CAA, Japanese). According to press coverage, it was based on 368 ads, a web survey of 1,000 consumers and interviews with 15 advertisers, of whom only one knew which competitors had been compared (ECのミカタ, Japanese).

At the commissioner's press conference, a satisfaction survey that never checked whether respondents had used the product was given as a problem example; responsibility stays with the advertiser even when a research firm ran the survey, and advertisers were asked to let consumers see the basis (summary, Japanese). The Japan Fair Trade Commission's 2008 view — the claim must rest on an objective survey, and results must be cited accurately — still applies.

On 18 January 2022 the Japan Marketing Research Association issued a protest against "No.1 surveys" designed to produce a set result. Coverage listed tactics such as not disclosing the design, skewed samples, stopping once ahead, and splitting categories until a first place appears (J-CAST, Japanese).

  1. On the research page, state the population, who you compared against, period, method and count, whether or not you make a No.1 claim
  2. Say whether you asked users or asked non-users for impressions
  3. When numbers appear in ads or releases, link (or QR-code) to the research page
  4. Label paid placements as advertising (Japan has regulated stealth marketing since October 2023)

This guide is not legal advice. Check with a professional where needed before using results in advertising.

Structured data: should you use Dataset?

Google documents Dataset structured data for making datasets easier to find in Dataset Search; "a table or a CSV file with some data" is among its examples. Required properties are name and description (50 to 5,000 characters); creator, license, temporalCoverage, spatialCoverage, variableMeasured and distribution (download URL and format) are among the recommended ones (Google Search Central).

It is for Dataset Search, and no AI company has said it uses this markup in answers. Writing the method and tables as text on the page comes first; treat Dataset as an extra when you publish a CSV. Don't put anything in structured data that isn't visible on the page.

Steps in WordPress

  1. Create the research page as a Page with a short English slug such as /research-2026/. Use headings for key findings, about the study, results, limitations, opinion, citation terms, data and change log.
  2. Put results in a Table block as text. If you add a chart image, place a table with the same numbers below it.
  3. Upload the CSV to the Media Library (or your own server) and link it from the page, with column names and meanings in the text. If the data sits behind a form or email sign-up, neither AI crawlers nor journalists can read it.
  4. Link to the research page from the press release, blog posts and the company page. How to write the release is in Making press releases work for AI search.
  5. In Nurevo AEO (a free WordPress plugin), nodes you add to 'extra' through the webmcp_canary_schema_graph filter are not removed even alongside an SEO plugin (example below). The filter is not called on pages excluded from structured data. Since 0.9.1, using it alongside SEO SIMPLE PACK or VK All in One Expansion Unit does not duplicate structured data.
  6. Nurevo AEO's llms.txt lists up to 30 of the most recently modified published pages and posts. A research page left unedited can drop off, so to keep it listed, add a line with the webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines filter (second example).
  7. Afterwards, check the page source (application/ld+json) and /llms.txt to confirm they match the page. Get it on WordPress.org
// Add a Dataset node only on the research page (slug research-2026)
add_filter('webmcp_canary_schema_graph', function (array $bundle) {
    if (!is_page('research-2026')) {
        return $bundle;
    }
    $bundle['extra'][] = array(
        '@type'            => 'Dataset',
        'name'             => 'Moving quote requests in (region): October 2026 tally',

        'creator'          => array('@id' => $bundle['org_id']),
        'license'          => 'https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/',
        'temporalCoverage' => '2026-10-01/2026-10-31',
        'distribution'     => array(array(
            '@type'          => 'DataDownload',
            'encodingFormat' => 'text/csv',
            'contentUrl'     => 'https://example.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/research-2026.csv',
        )),
    );
    return $bundle;
});

Keep the research page in llms.txt

Separately from Dataset, this pins an entry for the research page in llms.txt.

// Keep the research page in llms.txt whatever its modified date
add_filter('webmcp_canary_llms_txt_lines', function (array $lines) {
    $lines[] = '## Research and data';
    $lines[] = '';
    $lines[] = '- [Moving quote requests, October 2026](https://example.co.jp/research-2026/): tally of 412 requests, with method, limits and CSV';
    $lines[] = '';
    return $lines;
});

How to check

  1. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode the question your research answers ("when is moving cheapest in (region)?") and record the answer and source URLs with the date.
  2. See which appears as a source — the research page, the release or coverage of it — and whether the numbers are quoted correctly. If they're wrong, rewrite the key-finding sentence more plainly.
  3. In Google Analytics 4, look at visits to the research page from AI services and CSV downloads. Track links and mentions with Search Console's links report and brand searches.
  4. AI answers vary by day, so ask the same questions on the same day each month. A method for a fixed question set is in How to measure AI visibility.

Pre-publication checklist

  1. The title says what, where, when and how many
  2. Each key-finding sentence includes the base, period and source name
  3. Population, selection, period, method, count and question wording are stated
  4. Results are in a text table, not only an image
  5. Limitations are written down
  6. Facts and our opinion are under separate headings
  7. Data is aggregated so no person or client can be identified; tiny groups are withheld
  8. If used for No.1 or ad claims, it says whether users were asked and who was compared
  9. The CSV and column descriptions are available without sign-up
  10. Citation terms and ready-to-use sentences are on the page

Repeat it every year

Running the same method every year (or month) gives you a number nobody else has: how it changed since last time. Give each year its own URL (/research-2026/, /research-2027/), keep earlier pages and link them to each other. If you change the method, say in the limitations what can't be compared.

FAQ

Will a research page get us cited by AI?

Not guaranteed. Research suggests text with numbers and sources is used more in answers, but source selection isn't public. Stating population, count, method and limits so others can verify is the starting point.

How many respondents do we need?

There's no fixed threshold. Stating the count and population honestly matters more than the number itself. Don't publish small groups sliced finely; note it in the limitations.

Isn't a press release enough?

Keep the original of the numbers on your own site. Distribution pages can't always be edited or removed and can't hold the full method and data. Link from the release to the research page.

Can we require an email sign-up to download results?

You can, for sales reasons, but AI crawlers and journalists can't read past a sign-up. At least publish the key findings, method and main tables as text on the page.

Can we run a survey so we can say "No.1 in satisfaction"?

In Japan that falls under the misleading-representations law; the Consumer Affairs Agency flagged surveys that never checked whether respondents had used the product and says the advertiser is responsible. Other countries have their own rules. State who was compared, the population and method, and check with a professional.

Can we use AI to analyse the data or draft the text?

Yes, but check every number against the source data and say in the method how AI was used so readers can judge. Google also recommends explaining how automation or AI was used.

Is Dataset structured data required?

No. It's for Google's Dataset Search, and no AI company says it uses it. If you publish a CSV, adding it with the same content as the page is reasonable.
