How agencies can offer AI search optimization (AEO) as a monthly service: scope, reporting and pricing

If you are an agency adding AI search optimization to your monthly services, treat the initial implementation as a one-off project and make the retainer about two things: keeping the business facts AI reads accurate, and checking a fixed set of questions in AI answers and reporting what you find. AI answers change from day to day, so contract for the work and the reporting, not for mentions or positions.

Written October 30, 2026. What Google, OpenAI and Perplexity say was checked in their own documentation that day. Their guidance can change, so check the current pages before you pitch.

This guide is a service design for agencies that already build, maintain or optimise websites. We do not quote market prices for agency AEO services: we found no reliable primary source for them.

What a retainer can cover, and what it cannot promise

AI search optimization (AEO, also called GEO) means making sure ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews and others can read correct facts when they answer questions about a business. What is AEO?

Google says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that SEO best practices still apply (Google Search Central: AI features and your website). In other words, AEO is not a separate trick: it is the steady work of publishing a business's facts accurately and in readable form. That is exactly what suits a monthly retainer.

AI answers, on the other hand, vary between days and between runs of the same question. Nobody can promise "you will appear in ChatGPT" or "number one in AI". In your proposal and contract, promise the work and the reporting, not the outcome.

Setup checklist

  1. Structured data: does the site publish the business name, address, phone number, opening hours and business type as schema.org JSON-LD? Google explains that LocalBusiness structured data can tell it about business hours, departments and more (Google: LocalBusiness structured data). More specific types such as HairSalon or Dentist are available too.
  2. Matches the page: does the structured data match the text visitors can see? Google also recommends keeping structured data consistent with visible text.
  3. AI crawler access: is robots.txt blocking the crawlers AI search uses? OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI: overview of crawlers), and Perplexity recommends allowing PerplexityBot (Perplexity: crawlers).
  4. Training crawlers are a separate decision: whether to allow crawlers that collect content for AI model training, such as GPTBot, is a different question from appearing in AI search. Let the client decide; the agency should not decide it for them.
  5. llms.txt: summarise the site and its key pages for AI at /llms.txt. Google says you don't need new AI text files for its own AI features, so present llms.txt as a helpful extra, not a promise. How to write llms.txt
  6. Duplicates with the SEO plugin: make sure the site is not publishing the same business twice next to what Yoast SEO or Rank Math already outputs. Adding local business schema to your SEO plugin

Every month

  1. Keep facts current: reflect changes to opening hours, closing days, prices, menus, staff and location on both the page and the structured data. Outdated answers from AI often start with outdated information on the site.
  2. Add new pages: when news, case studies or pricing pages are added, reflect them in llms.txt and internal links.
  3. Check a fixed set of questions: pick a few questions the client's prospects are likely to ask, such as "dentist open Saturday in Austin", ask them in AI platforms under the same conditions every month, and record whether the client is mentioned or cited.
  4. Look at Search Console: impressions and clicks from Google's AI features are included in the Search Console Performance report under the Web search type (Google Search Central). They cannot be split out on their own, so say so in the report.

Pricing: build it up from hours and tool costs

Rather than working back from what others charge, build the monthly fee up from monthly hours × your internal hourly rate + tool costs + reporting time. It is easier to explain and harder to lose money on. Log your hours for the first few months and check them against the estimate.

Tiers that differ by how much you measure, such as "upkeep only", "upkeep + monthly check" and "upkeep + ongoing measurement", are easy for clients to choose between. Having the client hold tool subscriptions in their own name, while you charge for your work and reporting, keeps the cost breakdown clear.

FAQ

Can an AI search optimization retainer guarantee results?

We advise against it. AI answers vary between days and runs of the same question, and no one can guarantee a mention or a position. Contract for the work, the reporting schedule and what the report covers, and present AI visibility as measured results.

Does it need to be separate from an SEO maintenance contract?

No. Google says SEO best practices apply to its AI features too, so much of the work overlaps. You can add "maintaining structured data, llms.txt and AI crawler rules" and "a monthly AI visibility check" to an existing maintenance plan. AEO vs SEO vs GEO

How many questions should we track?

There is no fixed number. Start with a few questions the client's prospects are likely to ask and check them under the same conditions each month. More questions mean higher monitoring costs and more reporting work.

Can we offer it for sites not built on WordPress?

Yes. Structured data, llms.txt and robots.txt work on any site. Nurevo covers WordPress with a free plugin (WordPress.org), other sites with a snippet, and businesses without a website with a hosted page.
