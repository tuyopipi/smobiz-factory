AI search readiness of Singapore web design agencies: 641 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 641 Singapore web design agencies' own websites. 38.8% (249 sites) had an llms.txt and 32.4% met all six AI-search items — both the highest among the 17 countries with 100+ sites in our survey (Japan: 13.4% and 10.4%). Singapore sites were also the most likely to name AI bots in robots.txt (22.0%), yet only 5.7% (31 sites) actually blocked one, no different statistically from other overseas countries.

Published 24 November 2026. Data aggregated 12 November 2026. Research and writing: Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社, Japan). Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, so we have an interest in this topic. This is the seventh of our country reports and the second in Asia after India (the others: the US, the UK, Australia, Canada and France).

Key findings

  1. Of 641 Singapore web agency websites, 38.8% (249) had an llms.txt and 32.4% met all six AI-search items — the highest of the 17 countries with 100+ reached sites. There was no statistically significant difference from India (36.6%, 29.9%; p=0.33, p=0.23), while Singapore was ahead of Australia (24.3%, 20.8%), the US (23.1%, 18.9%) and Japan (13.4%, 10.4%) (all p<0.001).
  2. The lead holds among WordPress sites: 46.3% of Singapore's WordPress agency sites had an llms.txt, against 32.4% of WordPress sites in all other overseas countries and 39.5% in India (p<0.001, p=0.007).
  3. Of the 546 sites with a robots.txt, 22.0% (120) named an AI bot — the most of the 17 countries — and 86 of them blocked none. 5.7% (31) actually blocked an AI bot, with no significant difference from India (5.8%), the US (4.3%) or other overseas sites (5.1%), but more than Japan (1.3%). 27 of the 28 sites blocking GPTBot still allowed OAI-SearchBot.

Methodology

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 974 agency sites listed as Singapore in our collection list, 641 home pages were reachable. This is not a sample of all Singapore businesses. For comparison we use India (1,554), the Philippines (241), Australia (1,719), the US (4,472), Japan (2,486) and all other overseas sites excluding Singapore (12,711) from the same survey, plus Asia-Pacific countries with 30+ reached sites.
  2. What we fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), between early November and 12 November 2026; aggregated on 12 November.
  3. How we judged: llms.txt counts if it returned status 200 with non-HTML text longer than 20 characters. JSON-LD means at least one block on the home page; "broken" means at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type means an @type containing Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress means wp-content/, wp-includes/ or a WordPress generator tag in the home page HTML. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt group that applies to each bot disallows the home page (/), using longest match. The base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" means blocking any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot. "Names an AI bot" means a User-agent line for any surveyed bot other than Googlebot and Bingbot.
  5. The "six items" are: (1) has an llms.txt, (2) has JSON-LD, (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type, (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot, (5) has a meta description, (6) has a canonical link. It is a simple count (0–6) for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 score.
  6. HTTP 202: 47 Singapore home pages answered with status 202 (7.3% of reached) — fewer than Australia (14.5%), the US (15.0%) or other overseas sites (11.3%), more than India (1.5%). A 202 page scores "no" on almost everything, which affects country comparisons. Among the 594 Singapore sites that answered 200, llms.txt was 41.9%, all six items 35.0% and the average 4.70 — slightly above India's 200 sites (37.2%, 30.3%; p=0.043, p=0.036) and above the US (27.2%, 22.3%; p<0.001).
  7. Statistics: differences were tested with chi-square tests; AI bot blocking and broken JSON-LD (small counts) with Fisher's exact test. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown.

Results: Singapore vs India, the Philippines, Australia, the US and Japan

Singapore had the highest llms.txt (38.8%), FAQPage (15.0%) and all-six (32.4%) shares of the regions compared. JSON-LD (80.8%), Organization-type (73.3%), meta descriptions (81.6%) and canonical links (82.2%) were close to India and the Philippines, and 6–14 points above Australia, the US and other overseas sites. The gap with India is small: llms.txt, all six and FAQPage showed no significant difference, and India was slightly higher on JSON-LD (84.4%, p=0.042). Singapore was ahead of the Philippines on llms.txt (31.5%, p=0.045).

Compared with Japan, Singapore had 2.9 times the llms.txt share (38.8% vs 13.4%), 3.1 times the all-six share (32.4% vs 10.4%) and 3.8 times the FAQPage share (15.0% vs 3.9%). Japan was higher on meta descriptions (90.7% vs 81.6%), robots.txt (91.8% vs 85.2%) and WordPress (92.3% vs 81.3%), and has very few zero-item sites (1.6%), so the gap in the six-item average (4.36 vs 4.05) is smaller than the gap in the all-six share.

Blocking by bot

For the 546 Singapore sites with a robots.txt, the share blocking the home page for each bot. Crawlers described as training crawlers (Bytespider, CCBot, GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Applebot-Extended, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended) were blocked by 4.6–6.0% — close to India (4.2–5.6%), slightly above Australia and the US (3.3–4.3%) and above Japan (1.0–1.2%). Bots used for search or to fetch pages when a user asks — OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot — were each blocked by one site (the same site); ChatGPT-User, Googlebot and Bingbot by none. What the blocking and naming look like (facts)

  1. 27 of the 28 sites blocking GPTBot did not block OAI-SearchBot.
  2. Of the 34 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots above, 25 used the same combination of seven: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended. Three blocked Bytespider only, two CCBot and Bytespider, one CCBot only, and three used other combinations. One of the 34 also blocked the search bots OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot.
  3. 120 sites (22.0% of those with a robots.txt) named an AI bot — more than other overseas sites (11.2%), India (15.0%), the US (8.8%) and Japan (2.5%) (all p<0.001). 86 of them (15.8% of sites with a robots.txt) named AI bots but blocked none of them from the home page (other overseas: 5.8%).
  4. 62 of those 86 sites (72.1%) also had an llms.txt, against 40.4% of sites whose robots.txt names no AI bot (p<0.001). This survey does not check whether robots.txt or llms.txt was written by hand or generated by a plugin.
  5. 11 of the 34 sites blocking an AI bot also had an llms.txt.
  6. 92.1% of robots.txt files included a Sitemap line.

Structured data types and broken JSON-LD

The most common JSON-LD types on Singapore home pages were WebSite (71.5%), Organization (69.3%), WebPage (61.6%), ImageObject (60.2%) and SearchAction (59.3%), a profile close to India's. Compared with the US, Singapore had more PostalAddress (40.7% vs 24.3%), ContactPoint (29.2% vs 12.5%), FAQPage (15.0% vs 6.2%) and AggregateRating (12.2% vs 4.6%). Compared with Japan almost every type was more common; only BreadcrumbList was about the same (41.7% vs 43.4%).

Of the 518 sites with JSON-LD, 4.1% (21) had a block that does not parse as JSON — the highest share of the 17 countries, above the US (1.6%) and other overseas sites (1.7%) (p<0.001), with no significant difference from India (2.9%, p=0.24). This survey does not record which part is broken.

WordPress and SEO plugins

81.3% (521) of Singapore agency sites run WordPress — more than Australia (76.0%) and the US (74.5%), fewer than India (89.0%) and Japan (92.3%). The WordPress sites had llms.txt 46.3%, JSON-LD 95.6%, all six 39.3% and an average of 5.15. The 120 non-WordPress sites had llms.txt 8 (6.7%), JSON-LD 16.7% and an average of 0.93, but they include all 47 sites that answered 202 (WordPress cannot be detected on a 202 page). Among the 73 non-WordPress sites that answered 200: llms.txt 11.0%, JSON-LD 27.4%, average 1.49.

Among WordPress sites alone, Singapore's llms.txt share (46.3%) was still higher than other overseas (32.4%, p<0.001), India (39.5%, p=0.007) and the US (30.1%), and so was its all-six share (39.3% vs 27.4%, p<0.001, and vs India 32.7%, p=0.006). So the difference is not explained by the WordPress share alone.

Yoast SEO was detected on 50.3%, Rank Math on 23.0% and AIOSEO on 6.0% of WordPress sites — similar to the UK and Australia. llms.txt shares were AIOSEO 64.5% (20 of 31), Yoast SEO 46.9%, Rank Math 46.7% and no plugin detected 38.3%. SEOPress (1 site) is below 30 and not shown.

.sg vs .com

We split Singapore agency sites by the end of the host name: of 641 reached, 50 were .sg (including .com.sg), 413 .com and 178 other — .com is the majority (.com.sg and .sg have 25 sites each, under 30, so they are combined). JSON-LD was higher on .com (83.1% vs 64.0%, p=0.001); llms.txt (39.7% vs 26.0%) and all six (33.4% vs 20.0%) were also higher on .com, but not clearly significant (p=0.060, p=0.055). The .sg group is small (50). This survey cannot tell why (client base, how sites are built, and so on).

Distribution of the six items

In Singapore, five items (37.3%) and six items (32.4%) together made up 69.7%. Of the 239 sites meeting five items, 207 (86.6%) were missing only the llms.txt (then Organization-type 19, robots.txt 9). Zero items accounted for 13.9% (89 sites), 44 of which answered 202. Japan has very few zero-item sites (1.6%) but a thick middle at three to four items (30.7% combined; Singapore 12.6%).

The median llms.txt length was 88 lines in Singapore (249 files), 80 in India, 64 in the US and 93 in Japan. Length says nothing about quality.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not all businesses. Country is as recorded in our collection list, which is not a representative sample of all agencies in Singapore; it may include regional headquarters based in Singapore and Singapore-facing sites of agencies from other countries. Per-bot counts and the smaller Asia-Pacific samples are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for the home page (/) only; partial-directory blocks and firewall or CDN blocking are not counted. For llms.txt we recorded presence and line count, not quality.
  3. Each site was fetched once; the 333 unreachable sites are excluded. The 47 sites that answered 202 may have served something other than the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were identified from HTML strings and may be misclassified if traces are removed or several plugins are installed. Structured data injected later by JavaScript is not counted.
  5. Figures are from the 12 November 2026 aggregation. The survey is re-run weekly, so later figures may differ.

How to read this (opinion)

This section is our interpretation. Singapore agency sites have the basics in place about as often as India and the Philippines, and lead the 17 countries on llms.txt and on meeting all six items. Because the lead holds among WordPress sites, it may reflect more than plugin adoption — perhaps a tendency to try new conventions on their own sites first — but this survey does not test cause. The many robots.txt files that name AI bots without blocking them may suggest that many agencies are writing deliberate AI bot rules. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are Singapore agencies ahead on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt (38.8%) and meeting all six items (32.4%), Singapore was the highest of 17 countries. The difference from India was not statistically significant (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

How does Singapore compare with Japan?

Singapore had 2.9 times Japan's llms.txt share (38.8% vs 13.4%), 3.1 times the all-six share (32.4% vs 10.4%) and 3.8 times the FAQPage share (15.0% vs 3.9%). Japan was higher on meta descriptions and robots.txt.

Do many Singapore sites block AI crawlers?

5.7% of sites with a robots.txt (31 sites), no different statistically from India, the US or other overseas sites. 22.0% name AI bots in robots.txt, but most of those do not block them. Only one site blocked search bots.
