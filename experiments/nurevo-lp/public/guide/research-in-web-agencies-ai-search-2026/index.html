AI search readiness of Indian web design agencies: 1,554 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 1,554 Indian web design agencies' own websites. 36.6% (569) had an llms.txt — 2.7 times Japan (13.4%) and well above the US (23.1%) and the UK (21.5%). 29.9% met all six AI-search items we checked, second only to Singapore among the 17 countries with at least 100 sites in our survey. 5.8% of sites with a robots.txt blocked an AI bot, and 70 of the 72 sites blocking GPTBot still allowed OpenAI's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot.

Published 2026-11-21. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic. This is the fourth of our country reports (after the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia). A Hindi version is also available.

Key findings

  1. 36.6% (569) of 1,554 Indian web agency websites had an llms.txt — 2.7 times Japan (13.4%) and higher than the US (23.1%), the UK (21.5%) and all overseas sites outside India (25.0%) (all p < 0.001). It was not statistically different from Singapore (38.8%, p = 0.35).
  2. 29.9% (464) of Indian sites met all six AI-search items we checked, with an average of 4.53. Among the 17 countries with 100+ sites, India ranked second (after Singapore) on both llms.txt and all six items. JSON-LD appeared on 84.4% of home pages and FAQPage on 14.5% (US 6.2%).
  3. Of 1,406 Indian sites with a robots.txt, 5.8% (81) blocked an AI bot (Japan 1.3%, US 4.3%). 70 of the 72 sites blocking GPTBot allowed OAI-SearchBot. Only 7 sites (0.5%) blocked any of the four bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 2,316 agency sites listed as Indian in our collection list, 1,554 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of Indian businesses in general. For comparison we use the same survey's US (4,472), UK (942), Singapore (641), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites outside India (11,798).
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: only 23 Indian home pages answered with status 202, compared with 671 in the US and 140 in the UK. Such pages fail almost every item and pull the US and UK figures down. Comparing HTTP 200 sites only, India still leads the US on llms.txt (37.2% vs 27.2%) and on all six items (30.3% vs 22.3%) (both p < 0.001).
  7. Statistics: differences between India and other groups were tested with chi-square tests. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown.

Results: India compared with the US, the UK, Singapore and Japan

India was above the US and the UK on almost every item. The largest gaps were llms.txt (36.6% vs 23.1% in the US), JSON-LD (84.4% vs 69.0%), Organization-type markup (76.3% vs 59.4%) and FAQPage (14.5% vs 6.2%). India and Singapore were close on most items, with no statistically significant difference on llms.txt, all six items or FAQPage. Compared with Japan, India was higher on everything except meta descriptions (82.9% vs 90.7%) and robots.txt (90.5% vs 91.8%): 2.7 times on llms.txt and 2.9 times on all six items. India also had the highest share of schema.org microdata (27.7%) of the groups compared.

Blocking by bot

Share of the 1,406 Indian sites with a robots.txt that block each bot from the home page. Crawlers generally described as training crawlers (Bytespider, CCBot, GPTBot, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot, Applebot-Extended, anthropic-ai) were blocked by 4.2–5.6% — a little above the US and the UK, about the same as Singapore, and four to five times Japan. Among bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks, PerplexityBot was blocked by 6 sites, ChatGPT-User by 4, OAI-SearchBot by 2 and Claude-SearchBot by 1. Two sites blocked Googlebot and one blocked Bingbot. India's overall AI-bot blocking rate (5.8%) was slightly higher than the US (4.3%, p = 0.04) and not different from the UK, Singapore or other overseas sites.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. 70 of the 72 sites blocking GPTBot (97.2%) did not block OAI-SearchBot. The figure was 119 of 128 in the US, 28 of 28 in the UK and 27 of 28 in Singapore.
  2. Of the 93 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots above, 52 (55.9%) used the same combination: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all seven together (US 58.5%, UK 77.1%, Singapore 73.5%). The next most common was Bytespider alone (11 sites).
  3. 7 sites (0.5% of those with a robots.txt) blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
  4. 119 sites (8.5% of those with a robots.txt; US 4.4%) named an AI bot in robots.txt but blocked none from the home page.
  5. 31 of the 81 sites blocking an AI bot (38.3%) also had an llms.txt (US 34.0%).

Structured data types

In India, WebSite (76.9%) and Organization (72.7%) were the most common @types in home page JSON-LD, both the highest of the groups compared. Types that state an address or contact details — PostalAddress (35.6%) and ContactPoint (25.0%) — as well as Person (24.7%) and AggregateRating (10.9%) were 1.3 to 2.4 times as common as in the US and the UK, and close to Singapore. We did not examine what the AggregateRating values refer to or where the ratings come from. Of Indian sites with JSON-LD, 2.9% (38) had at least one block that did not parse.

WordPress and SEO plugins

89.0% (1,383) of Indian agency sites ran WordPress — more than the US (74.5%) and the UK (73.4%), and close to Japan (92.3%). WordPress sites had llms.txt 39.5%, JSON-LD 92.5% and a 6-item average of 4.95; non-WordPress sites (171, of which 23 answered 202) had llms.txt 13.5%, JSON-LD 18.7% and an average of 1.17. Comparing WordPress sites only, llms.txt was 39.5% in India, 30.1% in the US and 14.0% in Japan (India vs US p < 0.001).

Among WordPress sites, Yoast SEO was detected on 42.7%, Rank Math on 29.9% and AIOSEO on 6.9%. Rank Math's share is higher than in the US (18.0%), the UK (20.7%) and Australia (22.8%). llms.txt adoption: Rank Math 46.1%, AIOSEO 45.8%, Yoast SEO 37.5%, no plugin detected 31.9%. We did not check whether the llms.txt was generated by a plugin or added by hand. SEOPress (4 sites) is below our 30-site threshold and not shown.

Distribution of the six items

Seven in ten Indian sites met five (40.5%) or all six (29.9%) items. Only 7.5% met none — fewer than the US (22.1%) and the UK (23.6%), where many zeros are HTTP 202 pages. Japan has a thicker middle (3–4 items, 30.7% combined) where India has 16.1%.

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the sample is not representative of all Indian agencies. Counts such as per-bot blocking are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites (762) are excluded. The 23 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. Indian agency sites carry more llms.txt files, structured data and FAQPage markup than US or UK ones, which suggests they are quick to adopt new AI-search items. The high share of WordPress, and of plugins such as Rank Math that can publish an llms.txt, may be part of the reason (this survey does not test cause). AI bot blocking is a minority practice, and where it happens it is almost always limited to training crawlers. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision, but sites that want to appear in AI search may want to confirm they are not blocking search crawlers by accident. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are Indian agencies ahead on AI search readiness?

Among the 17 countries we compared, yes. India ranked second after Singapore on llms.txt (36.6%) and on meeting all six items (29.9%), above the US (23.1%, 18.9%) and Japan (13.4%, 10.4%) (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

Isn't the gap with the US just HTTP 202 pages?

They explain part of it, but comparing HTTP 200 sites only, India still leads on llms.txt (37.2% vs 27.2%) and all six items (30.3% vs 22.3%) (p < 0.001).

Do many Indian sites block AI crawlers?

5.8% of sites with a robots.txt — a little more than the US (4.3%) and Japan (1.3%), but a small minority. Most blocking targets training crawlers: only 7 sites (0.5%) blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
