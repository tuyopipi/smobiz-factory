Reviews, testimonials and AI search: the rules agencies must follow, a compliant request system, and testimonial pages that work

When AI recommends a business, it draws on ratings and review text from Google Maps and review sites. What an agency can do is build a system that asks every customer for a review in the same way, publish on-site testimonials that say whose experience it was, when and for what, and keep three things out of client sites: incentives for reviews, asking only happy customers, and marking up the business's own testimonials as star ratings in structured data.

Written 26 November 2026 (JST). Google's review snippet guide (last updated 17 September 2026), Google Maps' prohibited content policy, the US FTC rule and its Q&A, and Japan's stealth marketing guidance were all read on the same day.

This guide is for agencies that look after local businesses' websites, Google Business Profiles or local SEO. Better reviews do not guarantee that a client appears in AI answers. Answers change from day to day and with wording, and no AI company has published how it weighs reviews. What follows is how to get genuine reviews in a way that respects platform rules and the law, so that AI and people read accurate facts.

Asking for reviews: what is allowed and what is not

Most of the reviews AI reads live on Google Maps and review sites. Break the rules there and you lose more than a few reviews: the business listing itself can be restricted. Show the client this table and get agreement before you build anything.

AllowedNot allowedBasis
Ask every customer, with the same messageAsk only satisfied customers; skip unhappy onesGoogle Maps policy (no selective solicitation of positive reviews)
Ask for an honest account, good and badOffer discounts, gifts or points for a review, or for changing or removing a negative oneGoogle Maps policy (no incentives); FTC rule 465.4 bans compensation conditioned on sentiment
Staff mention it politely at the end of a visitAsk customers to name a staff member; let staff, former staff or relatives postGoogle Maps policy (no requested content, no conflicts of interest); FTC rule 465.5 on insider reviews
Reply to negative reviews calmly, with facts and what you didThreaten legal action to get a review removed; hide bad reviews while presenting the rest as all reviewsFTC rule 465.7 (review suppression)
Make it clear when a post was requested by the businessPresent a commissioned post as an ordinary customer's viewFTC rule; Japan's stealth marketing rule (since 1 October 2023)

Google says that when a business violates its fake engagement policy, it may, besides removing reviews, stop the profile receiving new reviews for a period, hide existing reviews for a period, and show customers a warning that fake reviews were removed (Google Maps prohibited content, Business Profile restrictions). In other words, the material AI uses to describe the business can disappear all at once.

In the US, the FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials took effect on 21 October 2024. It bans fake reviews, compensation conditioned on a particular sentiment, undisclosed reviews by officers and managers and their relatives, company-controlled review sites posing as independent, review suppression, and fake social media indicators. The FTC's Q&A says advertising agencies, PR firms, review brokers and reputation-management companies are not immune, while generalized requests to all customers to leave a review are exempt. Its December 2025 warning letter template cites civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation (FTC announcement, FTC Q&A, warning letter template).

In Japan, since 1 October 2023 it has been a violation of the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations to publish advertising that consumers cannot recognise as advertising. The Consumer Affairs Agency says this includes content a business asks or instructs third parties to post, review posts included; a person's own voluntary impressions are not covered, and the party regulated is the business (Consumer Affairs Agency, Japanese). Rules differ by country, so check the ones that apply to each client.

Build the request system

  1. Pick one moment to ask, such as the day of the appointment or the day after the booking confirmation. Send it through a system so that every customer gets it, rather than leaving staff to decide who to ask.
  2. Put the review link from the Google Business Profile dashboard in the confirmation email, as a QR code on the receipt, and on a short "Thanks for visiting" page on the site.
  3. Use wording like the example below: ask for an honest account, offer nothing in return, and say that posting or not changes nothing. Leave out any line asking people to mention a staff member.
  4. Reply in the business's name with facts and what was done. For negative reviews, separate what you apologise for from what is factually wrong, and give a contact. Report a review to Google only when it breaks the policy (off-topic, personal attacks and so on).
  5. Where reviews keep asking the same thing (parking, the full price, how to book), answer it in the site's text and FAQ. AI reads both reviews and the site; an answer on the site leaves less to guesswork based on reviews alone.
Subject: Thanks for choosing (business name)

Hi (name),

Thank you for using (service) last week.
If you have a minute, we'd appreciate a review on Google Maps.
Tell it as it was: what worked and what we could do better.

(link to your Google review form)

Whether or not you post makes no difference to your price or to how we treat you.

(Business name, your name)

Writing the testimonial page

Testimonials on a business's own site are, for AI and for people alike, voices the business chose to show. Say so openly and give readers what they need to judge them. We recommend recording the following for each one.

[Customer] Office worker, 40s (Springfield)
[Permission] Confirmed by email, 12 October 2026 (initials only, no photo)
[Service] Bathroom remodel, September 2026, four days on site
[In their words] (as sent to us; only typos corrected)
[From us] Prices depend on bathroom size; this job was $X including tax
[Thank-you] No gift was given / We gave a $X voucher as thanks for this testimonial

Get permission before publishing, including how the person is named (full name, initials or age group only) and whether a photo is used, and record the date and method. Don't over-polish the wording, and never edit in a way that changes the meaning. Put prices and timeframes, with their conditions, under "From us" so a number in a testimonial does not travel without its context.

If you gave a thank-you gift, say so, so readers can weigh the testimonial themselves. For reviews posted on Google Maps or other third-party platforms, incentives are not allowed at all, as described above.

Regulated sectors have their own rules. In Japan, for example, patient testimonials are listed as a prohibited form of advertising on medical websites (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare case guide, Japanese). For clinics, legal, financial and property clients, check the advertising rules for their sector and country before building a testimonial page.

Structured data: no stars for your own testimonials

Google's review snippet guide says star ratings for LocalBusiness and Organization are only for sites that collect reviews about other businesses or organizations. Reviews a business publishes about itself on its own site are "self-serving" and not eligible, and ratings from other websites should not be included in the aggregate (Google: review snippet structured data). Google introduced the rule in September 2019; a third-party review widget embedded by the business also counts as reviews the business controls (Google Search Central blog).

Many themes and review plugins add aggregateRating to the business node. Leaving it there is not penalised, but the stars won't show, and the site keeps publishing a rating that has nothing to do with what visitors see. When you audit a site, use the snippet below to find pages whose structured data contains ratings, and remove those attached to the business or organization.

Products are different: an online shop can mark up ratings that genuinely belong to that product. Nurevo AEO (a free WordPress plugin) never adds a rating to the business node. How to check

  1. Pick three to five decision-stage questions ("best-reviewed (service) in (area)?", "what do reviews say about (business)?"). Once a month, ask the same questions in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode.
  2. Log, with the date, whether the business was named, whether the rating and review summary match its current reviews, and whether the source was Google Maps, a review site or the business's own site.
  3. If a summary is out of date or wrong, answer the facts in the site's text and FAQ, and reply to recurring misunderstandings in reviews with the facts. There is no way to edit an AI answer directly.

Checklist

  1. Every customer gets the same review request
  2. No incentive is offered for a review, or for changing or removing a negative one
  3. Nobody is asked to name a staff member; staff and relatives don't post
  4. Requested posts are not presented as ordinary customers' views
  5. Negative reviews get factual replies
  6. Each testimonial records permission, date, service and any gift
  7. Sector advertising rules (medical, legal, financial) have been checked
  8. No self-serving star rating in the business's structured data
  9. Questions that reviews keep raising are answered in the site's text and FAQ
  10. Answers and sources for the chosen questions are logged monthly

FAQ

Do more reviews make AI mention a business more often?

Studies find that brands with review profiles appear in AI answers more often, but that is a correlation, and no AI company has published a threshold. Rather than chasing a number, build a system that keeps fresh reviews coming from every customer.

Can we give a discount to customers who leave a review?

Not on Google Maps: offering discounts, products or other incentives in exchange for a review is prohibited, even if you don't ask for a positive one. Ask every customer for an honest review, with nothing in return.

Can we send the Google review link only to people who rated us highly in a survey?

No. Selectively asking for positive reviews is against Google Maps policy. You can run a survey, but the review request must go to everyone in the same way, whatever they answered.

The client wants star markup on the testimonial page.

Reviews a business publishes about itself on its own site aren't eligible for Google's review stars, so the stars won't show. Write the testimonials well as page text and let ratings come from Google Maps and review sites.

Can you get a bad review removed?

You can report reviews that break the policy (off-topic, personal attacks, conflicts of interest), but not a review simply because it is negative. Reply with facts and what was done, and answer recurring misunderstandings on the site.

Is the agency liable if it only wrote the review request?

Under the FTC rule, the FTC says agencies are not immune. In Japan the stealth marketing rule regulates the business (the advertiser), but as the party designing the system, agree with the client that requested posts will never be passed off as ordinary customers' views.

If we fix reviews, will AI recommend the client?

There is no guarantee. AI answers change by day and wording, and how reviews are weighed is not published. What you can do is make sure genuine, policy-compliant reviews keep coming and that they don't contradict the facts on the site.
