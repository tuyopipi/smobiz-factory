AI search for on-location photographers: getting AI to read your service area, travel fees, location permits and rain policy correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for an on-location photographer comes down to writing, as text on your site, the three things people ask AI before they book: how far you travel, what it costs in total, and what happens if it rains. Name the towns you cover instead of writing "and surrounding areas", give one worked example of the total including travel, say who arranges and pays for location permits, and state your rain, postponement and cancellation terms with dates and percentages. If you have no studio, keep your home address off the web: set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business and put your area in your site's structured data with areaServed.

Written November 25, 2026 (JST). The Google Business Profile rules were checked on Google's help pages on November 25, 2026. The park-permit and cancellation-fee examples are from Japan (Tokyo wards and Japanese consumer law), because that is where we checked them; rules differ by country, city and venue, so check the ones that apply where you shoot. AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your terms readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention you or rank you anywhere. If you run a studio, see AI search for photo studios.

What people ask AI before booking an on-location photographer

People looking for an on-location photographer usually start with the occasion (a family session, a newborn shoot, an engagement, a company headshot day) and the place, then narrow the list by three things: will you come to them, what is the total, and what happens if the weather turns. Unlike a studio, where the customer comes to you, the place and the travel terms are at the centre of every answer.

Write your service area as named towns, with the travel fee for each

"Anywhere in the metro area" or "free travel to nearby areas" leaves AI unable to answer "Will they come to [town]?" List the towns or districts, and give the travel fee for each band as an amount. For places outside your bands, say how travel is calculated rather than only "ask us"; a clear rule lets AI mention you with the right condition.

Put the days and times you can shoot (some places allow weekdays only, sunrise or golden-hour sessions), and how you travel (car or public transport, whether you need parking) on the same page, so AI can answer with the conditions attached.

No studio? Set up Google Business Profile as a service-area business

If you travel to customers and do not receive them at your own address, Google Business Profile treats you as a service-area business. Google's guidelines say such businesses should hide their address from customers, and give a plumber running the business from home as the example. A business that travels to customers and has no storefront with clear signage is allowed one service-area profile. Google: Guidelines for representing your business

You can set up to 20 service areas, by city, postal code or other areas. A radius is not allowed, and the overall area should not extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from where you are based (Google adds that larger areas may be appropriate for some businesses). Changes can take up to 48 hours to show. Google: Manage your service areas

  1. If you do have a space with permanent signage where you receive clients, you are a hybrid business: enter both the address and the service areas. A place without signage does not count as a storefront.
  2. P.O. boxes, and virtual-office addresses you do not actually work from, are not eligible.
  3. Use your real business name as it appears on your site and cards. Adding keywords such as "[Town] Family Photographer Newborn" is not permitted and can get the profile suspended.
  4. Make the areas on your site and the service areas on your profile the same. Change one without the other and AI cannot tell which to trust.

Give one all-in example: session + travel + images

Buyer-side pricing guides break an on-location quote into time on site, travel, the number of images and editing, and the usage rights. @SOHO: on-location photography prices (Japanese). Write the same items for each package as text, and add one worked total for a common case so AI can answer "How much including travel?" If local law requires tax-inclusive pricing for consumers, as Japan does, show the tax-inclusive amount.

What goes on your site is not a summary of the law but your own practice. For example: "We follow the rules of each park, shrine or venue. Where a location needs a permit, we check and apply in advance, and the location fee is passed on to you at cost; we will tell you the amount before you book." Religious sites, shopping centres and wedding venues have their own rules, which we did not check for this article. Confirm each with its manager and put only what you have confirmed on your site.

Rain, rescheduling and cancellation: dates and percentages

Outdoor sessions get moved for rain and for sick children; that cannot be avoided. "Contact us if it rains" does not let AI answer "Can we reschedule for free if it rains?" Write when you decide, how many free reschedules there are and by when, and what costs what.

Dates and percentages are examples. Write your real terms.
Decide and writeExample wording
Rain callWe email you by 6 pm the day before to confirm or reschedule
Rain rescheduleUp to two free reschedules for rain, within 3 months of the original date
Light rainIn light rain we can move to a covered spot or to your home
Client cancellationFree up to 8 days before; 30% of the session fee 7–2 days before; 50% the day before or on the day
IllnessA child's fever on the day is treated as a reschedule (once)

Cancellation fees can be limited by law. In Japan, Article 9(1)(i) of the Consumer Contract Act voids the part of a cancellation fee that exceeds the "average damage" the business suffers from cancellations of that type of contract (as quoted in a Consumer Affairs Agency study-group paper). Consumer Affairs Agency study-group paper (PDF, Japanese). The agency began a study group on cancellation fees in December 2023, where a survey was reported in which about 6 in 10 people who had cancelled something in the past year were unhappy about paying the fee. ECのミカタ, December 26, 2023 (Japanese). Base your percentages on what a cancellation actually costs you (the bookings you turned away, preparation), and ask a professional if you are unsure. Other countries have their own consumer rules.

Put the same wording on both your pricing page and your booking page. For booking pages, see booking pages and AI search.

Portfolio and client photos: add words, and get consent

  1. Caption gallery images with the occasion, place, season and time of day (for example, "Family session, Example Park, November, 3 pm"). AI and search engines understand a photo mostly from the text around it and its alt attribute.
  2. Before you publish a client's photos on your site or social media, confirm in writing or by email whether you may, where, and whether faces should be blurred, and keep the record. A line on the booking page such as "We may ask to use some images in our portfolio; saying no does not change the price" prevents most disputes.
  3. You may ask clients for reviews, but do not make them conditional on a star rating or offer something in return. On marketplaces, follow their review rules too.

Structured data: there is no photographer type, so use LocalBusiness with areaServed

schema.org has no dedicated type for a photographer or a photography business (Photograph is a CreativeWork type for the work itself). Publish the business as LocalBusiness and put your area in areaServed, described as "the geographic area where a service or offered item is provided" and available on LocalBusiness (Organization), Service and Offer (checked in schema-dts 2.1.0 on November 25, 2026). If you do not publish your home address, leave address out.

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    {
      "@type": "LocalBusiness",
      "@id": "https://example.com/#organization",
      "name": "Example Photo",
      "description": "On-location family photographer based in Example City. Family, newborn and milestone sessions in parks, at venues or at your home. No studio. No travel fee within Example City and North County; $40 elsewhere in the county.",
      "url": "https://example.com/",
      "telephone": "+1-000-000-0000",
      "areaServed": [
        { "@type": "City", "name": "Example City" },
        { "@type": "AdministrativeArea", "name": "North County" },
        { "@type": "AdministrativeArea", "name": "Example County" }
      ]
    },
    {
      "@type": "Service",
      "name": "On-location family session (60 minutes)",
      "provider": { "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" },
      "areaServed": "Example City and North County (no travel fee)",
      "offers": {
        "@type": "Offer",
        "price": "350",
        "priceCurrency": "USD",
        "description": "Session fee $350 including tax. At least 60 edited images delivered online within 14 days. Travel included within Example City and North County. Location permit fees, where a location needs one, are extra."
      }
    }
  ]
}

Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special markup. Google: AI features and your website. Treat structured data as a machine-readable copy of the area and prices in your page text, and keep the two identical.

Pre-publish checklist

  1. Your area is written as named towns, with a travel fee amount for each band.
  2. How travel and lodging are charged outside your bands is written.
  3. Each package states session fee, length, image count and deadline, and extras (tax-inclusive where required), with one total example.
  4. Who checks and applies for location permits, and who pays the fee, is written.
  5. The rain call time, number and window of reschedules, and cancellation fees by date use the same wording on the pricing and booking pages.
  6. Portfolio images have a short caption (occasion, place, season) and alt text.
  7. The booking page says you will ask before publishing a client's photos.
  8. If you keep your home private, the address appears nowhere: not on the site, in the structured data or on your profile.
  9. The area on your site matches your Google Business Profile service areas and marketplace coverage.
  10. llms.txt lists your pricing, area, booking and FAQ pages. What is llms.txt

Check whether AI mentions you, and whether it is right

Once your pages are in order, put the questions clients would ask to AI. Check whether your name comes up for area-plus-occasion questions such as "on-location family photographer in [town]" or "photographer who shoots at [park]", and also ask with your name, such as "What is their travel fee?" or "What happens if it rains?", to see whether the answers match your site. Check too that AI does not recommend you for places outside your area.

FAQ

Where should an on-location photographer start with AI search?

First, write your area as named towns with a travel fee for each band. Then add a total example for each package and your rain, rescheduling and cancellation terms to your pricing page as text. After that, match your Google Business Profile service areas to your site, and publish the same facts in structured data and llms.txt.

I work from home. Will AI ignore me if I do not show an address?

No address is needed to answer area questions if your site names the towns you cover. Google's guidelines ask service-area businesses that do not receive customers to hide their address. Show your area with areaServed on the site and in structured data, and keep your home address off everything.

How many areas can I list?

Your site has no limit, but list only places you actually travel to. Google Business Profile allows up to 20 service areas, within about 2 hours of driving from your base, and does not allow a radius.

Do I need a permit to shoot in a park?

It depends on who manages the park. In Tokyo, for example, Suginami ward requires an application for business social media or magazine photos but not for personal snapshots, and Setagaya ward requires one for any commercial shoot. Whether a paid family session counts is not stated, so ask the manager before you shoot.

What cancellation percentage should I charge?

There is no set figure. In Japan, the Consumer Contract Act voids the part of a cancellation fee above the average damage such cancellations cause; other countries have their own rules. Base your fees on real costs, write the dates and percentages on your site, and ask a professional if unsure.
