AI search for insurance agencies: getting AI to read your carriers, your licence, how you recommend and how you work correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for an insurance agency comes down to writing, as text on your own site, the facts AI needs to answer one question well: which insurers does this agency represent, in what role, and how does it choose what to recommend. That means the list of carriers you are appointed with, whether you are captive or independent, that you are a separate business from those carriers, your licence number where the law asks for it, the basis on which you narrow down and recommend policies, the conditions behind any premium example, and how a consultation and a claim work. Much of what agencies publish goes through carrier or compliance review, so make these changes inside that process, then put the same facts in your Google Business Profile and structured data.

Written November 27, 2026 (JST). We read the California statute and California Department of Insurance notice, and Japan's Financial Services Agency pages, on November 27, 2026. Insurance advertising and licensing rules differ by country and state, so check the ones that apply to you with your carriers and your compliance adviser. This article is not legal advice.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention you or rank you anywhere.

What people ask AI about insurance agencies

People who ask AI for an insurance agent do not only want the closest office. They want to know which companies an agent can quote, whether the recommendation is biased toward one carrier, and whether they can ask questions without buying. AI can only answer those questions if your role and your way of choosing are written down somewhere it can read.

These are illustrative examples for this article, not measured question frequencies.

Conductor (updated August 13, 2026) studied more than 3.6 million citations of insurance sites in US AI answers from January to May 2026, across more than 178,000 prompts. AI Mode accounted for 43.9% of the citations, Perplexity 33.6%, ChatGPT 11.9%, Gemini 6.9%, Copilot 2.1% and Claude 1.6%. The prompts behind the citations were mostly about understanding insurance (33.2%), price (17.6%) and comparison (9.5%). In Google's AI Overviews, 53.3% of cited pages were outside the organic top 10, and product and policy pages were the most cited page type. These figures are for insurance brands and aggregators, not small agencies, but they point the same way: pages that explain, price and compare are the ones AI cites.

State your role and the carriers you represent

AI easily confuses an agency with the carrier it sells for, especially when the agency carries the carrier's brand. Put your role in one sentence on the home page and in full on your About page.

Examples only. Write what matches your own appointments and contracts.
What to writeExample
That you are an agencyWe are an independent insurance agency. We are a separate business from the insurance companies we represent.
Captive or independentCaptive: the one carrier you represent and that you do not quote others. Independent: how many carriers, by line.
The carrier listEach carrier's full legal name, grouped by line, with the date the list was last updated
LicenceYour agency and producer licence numbers in the states where the law asks for them (see the California example below)
Shared brandsIf other offices use the same name, that each is a separate business and may represent different carriers

Keep the number of carriers the same on your site, your Google Business Profile and any directory listings. When the numbers differ, AI cannot tell which is right. If you run several offices, see AI search for multi-location businesses.

Licence numbers: the California example

Some states set out where a licence number must appear. California Insurance Code section 1725.5 requires licensees to print their licence number on business cards, written price quotations and print advertisements distributed in the state, in a type size at least as large as the phone number, address or fax number shown, or 12-point (8-point on business cards), whichever is larger. California Insurance Code §1725.5

Since January 1, 2023 (SB 1242), the licence number must also appear in emails that involve an activity requiring a licence, next to or on the line below the person's or organization's name. The California Department of Insurance says the National Producer Number, or a link to a licence list, does not replace the licence number. California Department of Insurance notice

The statute text we read does not name websites. Even so, putting the licence number in your site footer and on your About page, in the same format as your emails, gives AI one consistent fact to repeat. Check your own state's rules before deciding what is required.

Explain how you compare and recommend

Japan offers a useful model for writing this down. The Financial Services Agency's inspection checklist asks agencies that represent two or more insurers to show an overview of comparable products that fit the customer's needs, to explain clearly why they recommend one, to explain objective criteria (such as product features or premium level) when they narrow the choice, and, when the narrowing is not objective, to explain the reason, including a capital tie to one insurer or business policy. FSA checklist, III.1.⑫

Whatever your jurisdiction, a short "How we choose a policy for you" page lets you give AI and customers the same answer. It is the only place AI can find an answer to "does this agent push one company?"

Our summary of the FSA checklist items (III.1.⑫(vii)). Japan published a further revision on August 28, 2026, which we have not read.
How you recommendWhat to explainExample wording
Show the options that fit, the customer choosesAn overview of the options and why you suggest them"We ask about your needs, then show the quotes from our carriers that fit them."
Narrow by objective criteriaThe criteria and the reason"Among policies with the same coverage, we show the three lowest premiums."
Narrow for business reasonsThe reason itself"We are owned by [carrier], so we mainly offer its policies."

Avoid "best" rankings and "No. 1" claims unless you can state what they are based on. Japan's FSA guidelines list showing figures not based on objective facts, and showing only part of a comparison, as examples of misleading comparisons, and ask that comparisons state the policy term, coverage, underwriting conditions and premium along with how the premium was calculated. FSA guidelines on life insurance solicitation

Put the conditions in the same sentence as the premium

AI often lifts a single sentence from a page. If you write "Auto insurance from $45 a month" and put the conditions in a footnote, the AI answer may keep only the price. When you give a premium example, put the coverage, limits, deductible, the driver or property details and the date in the same sentence.

  1. Weak: "Renters insurance from $12 a month." (conditions in a footnote)
  2. Better: "With [carrier], a renter in [ZIP] with $20,000 of personal property cover and a $500 deductible paid $12 a month as of [date]; your price depends on your details and the carrier's underwriting."
  3. Set a date to review every example. When rates change and the old figure stays on the page, AI repeats the old figure.
  4. Do not write "guaranteed savings" or "anyone qualifies". Japan's FSA checklist, for example, flags any display that leads customers to think a policy is far better or far more favourable than it is.

Working AEO changes through carrier and compliance review

Many carriers review the marketing their agents publish, and many agencies keep an approval record. Japan's FSA checklist, for example, asks insurers to run a legal check on solicitation material in advance and to manage material made by agents as well. Adding FAQs or rewriting descriptions for AI is new marketing copy, so plan it inside the same process.

  1. Separate agency facts (name, address, hours, consultation steps, staff, the carrier list) from policy descriptions (coverage, premiums, comparisons). Ask your carriers which of these they need to approve.
  2. FAQs, structured data descriptions and llms.txt descriptions are also read by customers and AI. Reuse approved page text in them instead of writing new claims. Whether carriers review these files varies, so ask.
  3. Link to each carrier's own pages for detailed policy terms, and keep your pages to who you serve and how you choose.
  4. Keep any approval codes or disclosures your carriers require exactly as specified.
  5. Log the date, the changed text and the approval for each change. It also helps when you later need to work out which version an AI answer came from.

Describe the consultation, your people, your office and after-sale help

  1. Fees (if free, say so, and whether reviews are always free), how long a meeting takes, how to book, and whether you meet by phone, video or in person.
  2. The steps: we ask about your needs, compare the options that fit, explain why we recommend one, confirm the application matches what you asked for, and give you the policy documents.
  3. That a review does not oblige anyone to buy or switch, and how you follow up (only what you actually do).
  4. Staff profiles: name, licence number where required, the lines they write, years in insurance, and any designations, shown as private designations, not as a licence. See AI search for person profile pages.
  5. After the sale: who to contact for changes, accidents and claims (you or the carrier's claim line), and your hours.
  6. How to complain, and to whom.

Align your Google Business Profile and directory listings

  1. Use your real business name in your Google Business Profile. Adding a city or service to the name ("Example Insurance | Cheap Auto Insurance [City]") is not allowed by the guidelines. Google: Business Profile guidelines
  2. Keep categories, hours and the number of carriers in your description the same as your site.
  3. If you appear on comparison or agent-finder sites, keep your name, address, carriers and consultation options the same as your site.
  4. Rules apply to asking for reviews. Before tying a reward to a review, read reviews, testimonials and AI search.

Structured data: InsuranceAgency plus Service

schema.org has an InsuranceAgency type, a kind of FinancialService and so of LocalBusiness (checked in schema-dts 2.1.0 on November 27, 2026). Put your office facts in InsuranceAgency and your consultations and quotes in Service. knowsAbout can list the lines you write and areaServed where you are licensed and work.

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    {
      "@type": "InsuranceAgency",
      "@id": "https://example.com/#organization",
      "name": "Example Insurance Group (example)",
      "description": "Independent insurance agency in Example City. We represent 9 home and auto carriers and 4 life carriers, and we are a separate business from each of them. California license No. 0000000.",
      "url": "https://example.com/",
      "telephone": "+1-000-000-0000",
      "address": {
        "@type": "PostalAddress",
        "addressRegion": "CA",
        "addressLocality": "Example City",
        "streetAddress": "123 Example St"
      },
      "openingHours": "Mo-Fr 09:00-17:00",
      "areaServed": { "@type": "State", "name": "California" },
      "knowsAbout": ["Auto insurance", "Homeowners insurance", "Life insurance", "Commercial insurance"]
    },
    {
      "@type": "Service",
      "name": "Home and auto insurance quotes",
      "serviceType": "Insurance quote and review",
      "provider": { "@id": "https://example.com/#organization" },
      "description": "We compare quotes from the carriers we represent and explain why we recommend one. Coverage is subject to each carrier's underwriting."
    }
  ]
}

Keep structured data descriptions identical to your page text. Leave premiums out of structured data and explain them in the page text with their conditions, so an old or unconditioned price is not left behind.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Your home and About pages say you are an agency and a separate business from the carriers.
  2. Captive or independent is stated, with the carrier list by line and an update date.
  3. The number of carriers matches across your site, Google Business Profile and directories.
  4. Licence numbers appear where your state requires them, in the same format everywhere.
  5. A "how we choose" page explains your criteria and how you give the reason for a recommendation.
  6. Premium examples carry their conditions and date in the same sentence; no rankings without a stated basis.
  7. Fees, the consultation steps, meeting options and "no obligation" are written down.
  8. Who to contact for changes, claims and complaints is written down.
  9. FAQ, structured data and llms.txt text matches the approved page text, and you have checked how your carriers treat it.
  10. Structured data uses InsuranceAgency plus Service, and llms.txt lists the main pages. What is llms.txt?

Check whether AI mentions you, and whether it is right

Once your pages are in order, ask AI what customers would ask. Check whether you are named for questions such as "independent insurance agent in [city] for home and auto", then ask with your name: "Which carriers does [agency] represent?", "Is [agency] part of [carrier]?", "Can I get a free policy review?" Compare the facts in the answers with your site. When something is wrong, find the page and sentence it came from and fix that.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time, and keep a record. FAQ

Where should an insurance agency start with AI search?

First, say on your home and About pages that you are an agency, whether you are captive or independent, and which carriers you represent. Then add a page on how you choose and recommend policies, your consultation steps and who handles claims. After that, align the same facts in your Google Business Profile and structured data (InsuranceAgency).

Do new FAQs for AI need carrier approval?

Copy that describes policies often goes through carrier or compliance review. Which items need approval differs between carriers, so ask yours. Reusing approved page text in FAQs, structured data and llms.txt is the safest approach.

Can we publish a "best insurance" ranking?

Only if you can state what the ranking is based on. Comparisons built on figures without an objective basis, or on part of the picture, are listed as misleading in Japan's FSA guidelines. Writing your criteria and the reason for each recommendation is a better approach.

Is AI search worth it for a captive agent?

Yes. State that you are captive and name the carrier, so AI can answer "which company is this agent with?" and "can they quote others?" correctly. A clear role may make it more likely you are named for questions that fit you.

Should premiums go in structured data?

We do not recommend it. Premiums depend on personal details and change with rate filings. Put agency and consultation facts in structured data, and give premium examples in the page text with their conditions and date.

Will this guarantee ChatGPT recommends us?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your role, carriers, way of recommending and consultation process easy for AI to read, and keep checking whether it is mentioned and correct.
