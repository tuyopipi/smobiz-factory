When AI states wrong facts about your business: find the cause, then fix it in the right order

There is no way for a business to edit an AI's answer directly. What you can fix is the information the AI reads when it writes that answer. Record the error, trace its sources, then fix things by cause: your own website first, then listings such as your Google Business Profile, then third-party pages. After that, report the answer and re-ask the same questions to see whether it changed.

Written November 12, 2026 (JST). The Google and Perplexity details were checked that day on their official help pages, linked below. Screens and policies change, so check the current official pages as well.

This guide is for factual errors: wrong opening hours, being described as closed, being credited with services you do not offer, or being mixed up with a business of the same name. AI answers change every time a question is asked, and no fix can guarantee that an AI will always describe you correctly. The goal is to make the facts AI reads consistent, so errors become less likely.

The key idea: fix what the AI reads, not the answer

Search-based AI such as ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews searches the web when it gets a question and writes its answer from the pages it finds. Perplexity says each answer includes numbered citations that link to the original sources so you can verify them (Perplexity Help Center). Google states plainly that AI responses may include mistakes (Google Search Help).

So most errors have one of two causes: some page somewhere can be read that way, or the fact the answer needs is not written down anywhere. In the first case you fix the source page; in the second you publish the correct fact clearly. Feedback buttons help, but if the source stays out of date the same error can keep coming back.

Step 1: Record the error (do not judge from one answer)

Start by writing down what is wrong, on which AI, and for which question. Answers vary even for the same question, so ask two or three times on different days and note whether the error appears every time or only sometimes. This record is also your baseline for checking later whether the fix worked.

Step 2: Follow the sources and sort the cause into one of six types

Open the source URLs you recorded and look for where the wrong statement is written. Perplexity numbers its sources, which makes them easy to trace, and Google's AI Overviews show links beside or below the answer. If none of the sources contains the error, that is a clue too. Use the table to decide what kind of cause it is and where to fix it.

Also check whether AI can read your site at all. If robots.txt blocks AI search crawlers, your correct pages may not be used as sources while old third-party pages still are. OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers (OpenAI: overview of crawlers).

Step 3: Make your own site the most reliable source

Whatever the cause, the foundation is having the correct facts in one clear place on your own site. When an AI compares several pages, a clear official page makes it more likely that yours is the one used (it is not a guarantee).

  1. On your About or Location page, write out in text your legal name, address, phone number, opening hours (including closing days, public holidays and seasonal exceptions), service area and main services. Do not put them only in an image.
  2. Show a "last updated" date on the page and change it whenever hours or prices change.
  3. Clean up pages that keep old facts alive. Remove finished promotions, old price lists and maps from before a move, or add a note at the top such as "This offer ended in March 2026".
  4. If you have closed, moved or paused trading, say so clearly on both the home page and the About page. For a temporary closure, give the reopening date.
  5. If you are confused with a namesake, write facts that tell you apart: "Smith Dental in Springfield, Illinois (founded 2009, general dentistry). We are not connected with Smith Dental Group in Ohio."
  6. If AI says you offer a service you do not, answer it in your FAQ: "We do not offer X. We do offer Y." AI sometimes fills gaps with guesses, so "we don't do this" is a fact worth publishing.

Step 4: Where to look on WordPress

On WordPress the same fact is often output from several places. To avoid fixing the text while the structured data or llms.txt still says the old thing, check each of these in turn. Google asks that structured data match what is visible on the page (Google Search Central: AI features and your website).

Step 5: Fix listings and third-party pages

If your Google Business Profile is verified, you can correct it with Edit profile from your profile in Google Search or Maps. Google says it reviews changes before they go live on your profile (Google Business Profile Help). Make opening hours, temporary closures, address, phone number and website URL match your site exactly. Google says there are no special optimizations for its AI features and that the usual basics matter, such as keeping your Business Profile information up to date (Google Search Central).

Booking sites, directories and trade association lists are corrected through their own dashboards or contact forms. For third-party articles and review sites you cannot edit, ask the publisher, stating what is wrong, what it should say and why, with a link to your official page as evidence. Requests are not always granted, so keep making your own site clearer (step 3) at the same time.

Step 6: Report the answer to each AI

In Google's AI Overviews, choose the thumbs down icon below the overview, then Report a problem, pick the category that fits and submit. Google says the feedback you send includes your most recent search query and its results (Google Search Help), so send it right after searching the question that produced the error, so it is clear which answer you mean.

ChatGPT and Perplexity also let you rate answers, but at the time of writing we could not find on their official pages a dedicated channel for businesses to request corrections to facts about themselves. Treat reporting as something to do when you notice an error, and put most of the effort into fixing the sources in steps 3 to 5.

Step 7: Re-ask the same questions

After fixing, ask the same questions on the same AI in the same mode as in step 1, and put the records side by side. How long changes take depends on when the AI or search engine re-reads your pages, and none of the official pages we read gives a set period. Pick an interval, such as every one or two weeks, record in the same format, and look for "2 out of 3" becoming "0 out of 3", or for the source changing from an old page to yours.

For Google, the Generative AI performance report in Search Console shows how often your pages appear in AI features (How to see AI Overviews in Search Console). Those are impressions, though: they do not tell you whether the answer was correct. Check the content with your recorded answers.

What not to do

  1. Do not hide text meant only for AI, or publish structured data that differs from the page. Google asks that structured data match the visible content.
  2. Do not write fake reviews or misleading comparison pages. You would be replacing one error with another, and it may break consumer protection rules.
  3. Do not block every AI crawler in robots.txt. The error will not go away, and your correct pages may stop being read. Training crawlers and search crawlers have different names, so if you block anything, choose by purpose, for example training crawlers only.
  4. Do not decide that a fix worked, or failed, from a single answer. Always check several times under the same conditions.

Checklist

  1. Recorded the error with date, AI, exact question, answer, source URLs and how often it appeared
  2. Opened the sources and sorted the cause into one of the six types
  3. Wrote the correct facts in text on the About or Location page, with a last updated date
  4. Removed or marked as ended old announcements, price lists and maps
  5. Made structured data (SEO plugin and theme) and llms.txt match the page text
  6. Confirmed robots.txt does not block AI search crawlers
  7. Cleared caches and checked the new values on the live page
  8. Made your Google Business Profile and main listings match
  9. Asked publishers of third-party pages to correct them, with an evidence URL
  10. Reported the wrong answer in Google's AI Overviews
  11. Scheduled a re-check with the same questions every one or two weeks

FAQ

Can I ask the AI company to correct facts about my business?

At the time of writing we could not find, on the companies' official pages, a way for a business to have an answer rewritten on request. Google's AI Overviews have a Report a problem option, but it is not a guaranteed fix. The main lever is correcting what the AI reads: your website, your Business Profile and your listings.

How long does a fix take to show up?

It depends on when the AI or search engine re-reads your pages, and no official page gives a set period. Pick an interval, such as every one or two weeks, re-ask the same questions and record the results.

My site is correct but the AI is still wrong. Why?

Usually the source is a third-party page (an old listing, a namesake business) or the fact the answer needs is not written on your site. Open the source URLs to find the cause, and check your structured data and robots.txt too.

Will adding llms.txt fix the error?

llms.txt is a helper file that tells AI your site's essentials and key pages; on its own it may not change the answer. What matters is that page text, structured data and listings all give the same correct facts.

AI says we are closed. What should I do first?

Check the status and hours on your Google Business Profile, then state clearly on your home page and About page that you are open, with your location. Also check whether news of a namesake closing is being used as a source.

Is the fix different for ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google?

The basics are the same: fix the sources. What differs is how you check. Perplexity's numbered citations make sources easy to trace; Google offers Report a problem and impressions in Search Console. To appear in ChatGPT search at all, robots.txt must not block OAI-SearchBot.
