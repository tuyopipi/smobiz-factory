AI search readiness of US web design agencies: 4,472 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 4,472 US web design agencies' own websites. 23.1% (1,032) had an llms.txt — higher than Japan (13.4%) and in line with the UK (21.5%), Canada (22.9%) and Australia (24.3%). 4.3% of sites with a robots.txt blocked at least one AI bot, and of the 128 sites blocking GPTBot, 119 did not block OpenAI's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot.

Published 2026-11-17. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimization (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic. This is the first of our country reports.

Key findings

  1. 23.1% (1,032) of 4,472 US web agency websites had an llms.txt, about 10 points more than Japan (13.4%, 333 of 2,486); the difference is statistically clear (p < 0.001). Excluding the 671 home pages that answered HTTP 202, the share is 27.2%.
  2. JSON-LD structured data was almost identical (US 69.0%, Japan 69.4%), but 18.9% (846) of US sites met all six AI-search items we checked — about 1.8 times Japan's 10.4%.
  3. Of 3,404 US sites with a robots.txt, 4.3% (147) blocked an AI bot (Japan 1.3%). Blocking targeted mainly training crawlers — CCBot and Bytespider (4.1% each), GPTBot (3.8%) — while search and retrieval bots such as OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot were blocked by 0.3% (9–11 sites) each.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 6,646 agency sites listed as US in our collection list, 4,472 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of US businesses in general. For comparison we use the same survey's Japan (2,486), UK (942), Canada (908), Australia (1,719) and all overseas sites outside the US (8,880).
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: 671 US home pages answered with status 202 (none in Japan). None of them had JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they may have returned a bot-check or other interim page. We therefore also show figures excluding them.

Results: the US compared with Japan and other English-speaking markets

US figures were close to the UK, Canada and Australia. Compared with Japan, the US was higher on llms.txt, FAQPage and "all six items", and lower on meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt. The lower US values are partly due to the 671 HTTP 202 pages, which fail almost every item; excluding them, meta description is 80.0%, canonical 87.4% and robots.txt 88.8%, much closer to Japan.

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. Of the 128 sites blocking GPTBot, 119 (93.0%) did not block OAI-SearchBot — they keep out the training crawler but let the search crawler in.
  2. Of the 159 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots above, 93 (58.5%) used the same combination: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all seven together.
  3. 149 sites (4.4% of those with a robots.txt) named an AI bot in robots.txt but blocked none from the home page — they either allow it by name or restrict only other paths.
  4. 55 of the 159 sites blocking an AI bot (34.6%) also had an llms.txt.

WordPress and SEO plugins

74.5% (3,333) of US agency sites ran WordPress (87.7% excluding HTTP 202). WordPress sites had llms.txt 30.1%, JSON-LD 91.4% and a 6-item average of 4.8; non-WordPress sites (1,139, of which 671 answered 202) had llms.txt 2.5% and an average of 0.28. Among WordPress sites, Yoast SEO was detected on 56.4%, Rank Math 18.0% and AIOSEO 7.1% — the reverse of Japan, where AIOSEO leads (35.2%) ahead of Yoast (14.4%).

llms.txt adoption by SEO plugin: AIOSEO 53.4%, Rank Math 38.2%, SEOPress 33.3%, Yoast SEO 28.0%, no plugin detected 18.7%. We did not check whether the llms.txt was generated by a plugin or added by hand.

Distribution of the six items

The US had more sites at both ends than Japan: 0 items (22.1%) and all 6 (18.9%). Most of the zeros are HTTP 202 pages; excluding them, 0 items falls to 8.8%. Japan has a thicker middle (3–4 items, 30.7% combined) and 10.4% with all six. On Japanese sites with five items, the missing one is usually llms.txt (score distribution study).

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the sample is not representative of all US agencies.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites are excluded. The 671 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. US and Japanese agency sites use structured data at almost the same rate; the gap is in llms.txt and in having everything in place. In the US data, nearly every site that blocks training crawlers still lets search crawlers in. Whether to block either is each site owner's decision, but sites that want to appear in AI search may want to confirm they are not blocking search crawlers by accident. None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are US agencies ahead of Japan on AI search readiness?

On llms.txt (US 23.1%, Japan 13.4%) and on meeting all six items (18.9% vs 10.4%), yes. JSON-LD is about the same (69.0% vs 69.4%), and Japan is higher on meta descriptions and canonical links (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

Do many US sites block AI crawlers?

4.3% of sites with a robots.txt, more than Japan (1.3%) but still a small minority. Most blocking targets training crawlers; only 0.3% block OAI-SearchBot.

What is the HTTP 202 issue?

671 US home pages answered with status 202 and showed no JSON-LD or WordPress markers, so they may have returned an interim page. We show figures with and without them.
