Make product sizes, colours and specs readable by AI: spec tables, variants and WooCommerce

People who ask an AI about products add conditions: "is this 90 cm shelf available in white?", "I'm 170 cm, should I get the M or the L?". What an AI can use to answer is a text spec table in the page body, with units and the measuring method, and variant data that gives each colour and size its own ID, URL, price and stock. Keep the same values in three places, the page body, the structured data (ProductGroup) and your product feed, and AI answers are less likely to mix them up. WooCommerce's built-in structured data does not output colour or size, so the fastest wins are the text tables and the variant URLs.

Written November 28, 2026 (JST). Google's and OpenAI's documentation and the WooCommerce 11.3.1 source code were read on the same day.

This guide is for people who sell products in several colours, sizes or volumes on their own online shop, and for the agencies that build and run those shops. Shop-wide facts (returns, shipping, stock) are in AI search for online shops, and applying for ChatGPT shopping feeds is in how products get into ChatGPT shopping. This page is only about the sizes, colours and specs inside one product page.

Product questions come with conditions

Shoppers rarely stop at "a good linen shirt". They add a condition, and the AI looks for a fact on the page that meets it. If that fact is not written as text, it cannot be part of the answer. Here are typical conditions by product type and the facts each one needs.

Product typeConditional questionFacts the page needs in text
ClothingI'm 170 cm and want a relaxed fit. M or L?Garment measurements per size, a height guide, the model's height and size worn, material
ShoesDo you have a wide fit in UK 8?Size labels in the system you sell in, width, which sizes are in stock
Furniture and storageA white bookcase under 90 cm wide?Outer and inner dimensions, colour name, weight, whether assembly is needed
Phone cases and partsWhich case fits the iPhone 17?The exact compatible models, and the ones it does not fit
Cosmetics and foodA refill pack of at least 300 mL?Volume with the unit, whether it is a refill, ingredients

In every case, the answer can change with the colour or size. If only the white is out of stock, or the L costs more, each variant has to carry that difference in a readable form.

Keep the same values in three places: page, structured data, feed

Product facts live in three places. Different AI search systems read different ones, so one is not enough, and when the three disagree nobody can tell which is right.

PlaceWhat it holdsMain readers
Page bodySpec table, size chart, colour names, descriptionAI search crawlers that read pages, and people
Structured data (JSON-LD)ProductGroup and one Product per colour or size (SKU, GTIN, price, stock)Google Search and AI systems that read the page
Product feedThe product list sent to Merchant Center or OpenAIGoogle Shopping, and ChatGPT shopping if you applied

On February 20, 2024 Google announced support for grouping variants in a ProductGroup, with one Product per variant under hasVariant (Search Central blog). variesBy can list color, size, suggestedAge, suggestedGender, material and pattern, and each variant needs its own sku or gtin (product variant structured data).

OpenAI's product feed spec says the same thing in feed form: item_id is unique per item or variant, and variants conditionally need group_id, listing_has_variations=true on every variant row, and a variant_dict such as {"color":"Black","size":"10"}. Dimensions take at least two of length, width and height plus a unit, and weight takes a unit. It also says not to invent an MPN to replace a missing GTIN (OpenAI product feed spec).

How to write the spec table and size chart

Put the spec table in the page body as a text table, not an image. AI crawlers cannot be relied on to read numbers inside a picture. Use this example and its rules.

[Spec table example: put it in the page body as a text table]
Field              Example                                  Rule
Size (garment)     M: length 72 cm, chest width 58 cm       Measured flat; say how, under the table
Size (fit guide)   M: height 165–175 cm                     Separate from garment measurements; label it a guide
Model              178 cm tall, wearing L                   Give both the size worn and the build
Material           55% linen, 45% cotton                    Same wording as the care label
Outer / inner      Outer W 90 x D 30 x H 180 cm             Furniture: outer and inner on separate rows
Weight             3.2 kg (unit only)                       Say what the weight includes
Compatible models  iPhone 17, iPhone 17 Pro (exact models)  Never "latest models"
Volume             50 mL                                    Always with the unit
Colour name        Navy                                     Same name in images, options and JSON-LD

The key is to keep measurements and guidance apart. "M fits 165–175 cm" is your advice; "M is 58 cm across the chest" is a measured fact. AI answers sometimes lift a single sentence, so label guidance as a guide and it is less likely to be repeated as a promise.

State the measuring method (laid flat, body measurements, outer or inner) in one line under the table. The same word "width" gives different numbers under different methods, and they cannot be compared. Use the same colour name in image descriptions, the option selector and the structured data. If the selector says "Dark blue" and the markup says "Navy", a machine may not know they are the same colour.

Variants: ID, URL, price and stock

Give every colour and size these four things.

  1. Its own SKU, and a GTIN (UPC, EAN, JAN) where you have one. Never reuse the parent's value
  2. A URL that opens with that variant selected. For single-page variant pickers, Google expects query parameters (such as ?color=…) to preselect a variant, and the preselected variant must show the right image, price and availability and be addable to the cart
  3. Its own price and stock, so that "white in M is sold out" is readable
  4. Colour and size names that match across page, selector, markup and feed

Some checklists, such as Salesforce's, advise avoiding URLs like ?color=blue. That advice is about duplicate pages. Google's guidance for single-page variants is to keep one canonical URL and let query parameters select the variant. Point the canonical at the parent product page and use the query URLs in the feed and in offers.url, and both are satisfied.

Google recommends putting product markup in the initial HTML, and says markup generated with JavaScript may make Shopping crawls less frequent and less reliable. Even when picking a colour changes the price on screen, include every variant's values in the first HTML response.

Structured data example (ProductGroup)

Here is a shirt marked up after Google's example. Add one Product per colour and size inside hasVariant.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "ProductGroup",
  "name": "Linen-blend camp collar shirt",
  "description": "55% linen, 45% cotton. Machine washable camp collar shirt.",
  "url": "https://shop.example.com/product/linen-shirt/",
  "brand": { "@type": "Brand", "name": "Example Works" },
  "productGroupID": "LS-100",
  "variesBy": ["https://schema.org/color", "https://schema.org/size"],
  "hasVariant": [
    {
      "@type": "Product",
      "name": "Linen-blend camp collar shirt, Navy, M",
      "sku": "LS-100-NV-M",
      "gtin13": "4900000000000",
      "color": "Navy",
      "size": "M",
      "material": "55% linen, 45% cotton",
      "image": "https://shop.example.com/img/ls-100-nv.jpg",
      "offers": {
        "@type": "Offer",
        "url": "https://shop.example.com/product/linen-shirt/?attribute_pa_color=navy&attribute_pa_size=m",
        "price": "58.00",
        "priceCurrency": "USD",
        "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock"
      }
    }
  ]
}
</script>
* The GTIN, URL and price are placeholders. Use your own values, keep them
  identical to what the page shows, and add one entry per variant.

Check it in the Rich Results Test to confirm the variants parse without errors. Search Console's Product snippets and Merchant listings reports also show variant validation. Structured data does not make an AI mention a product. It puts the same facts as your text table into a form machines cannot misread.

What WooCommerce outputs today

Reading the WooCommerce 11.3.1 source (class-wc-structured-data.php), the built-in structured data for a variable product is as follows.

Since WooCommerce 11.0.0, a URL whose query identifies exactly one variation gets that variation's price (10.9.0 does not do this). If the query matches more than one variation, for example because of an "Any" variation, the price range stays.
URLWhat WooCommerce outputsWhat it does not output
Product page, no queryProduct with a price range (AggregateOffer: lowest, highest, number of variations), or an Offer if every variation costs the samePer-variant price and stock, color, size, material, ProductGroup
URL with every attribute in the query (e.g. ?attribute_pa_color=navy&attribute_pa_size=m)An Offer at that variation's price, the variation's SKU, GTIN and MPN, and inProductGroupWithID pointing at the parent SKUcolor, size, material, ProductGroup

The same ?attribute_… query also preselects the variation in the product page's dropdowns (wc-template-functions.php). So on WooCommerce 11 or later, variant URLs in this form give matching prices in your feed and markup.

To get colour and size into the markup you need more than core. Yoast's developer docs say the paid Yoast WooCommerce SEO outputs a ProductGroup for variable products and lists each variation under hasVariant (Yoast ProductGroup). A feature request asking WooCommerce's Google feed extension to use ProductGroup instead of separate products has been open since November 12, 2024 (feature request). Before adding a plugin, check what already outputs Product so the same product is not marked up twice.

WordPress (WooCommerce) steps

  1. Under Products → Attributes, create colour and size as global attributes rather than custom per-product ones, named the way customers say them
  2. On the product's Attributes tab, tick "Visible on the product page" and "Used for variations". Visible attributes appear as text on the Additional information tab
  3. On the Shipping tab, enter weight and dimensions (length, width, height). These also appear on the Additional information tab
  4. On the Variations tab, give each colour and size its own SKU, GTIN, price and stock, and use the description to note what differs ("L has a longer body")
  5. Add the size chart and spec table to the description as a text table (the Table block), with the measuring method under it
  6. Open a variant URL such as ?attribute_pa_color=navy&attribute_pa_size=m and confirm the selector, image, price and stock all switch to that variant

Check AI answers by naming a colour or size

Asking with your shop or product name does not tell you whether the conditional facts are being read. Test with the kind of question a customer would actually ask.

  1. For each main product, write three conditional questions ("a white bookcase under 90 cm wide", "a relaxed linen shirt for someone 170 cm tall", "a folio case that fits the iPhone 17")
  2. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode, and record the answer, the cited URLs and any numbers in a table
  3. Check that dimensions, prices, stock and compatible models match the page, and that no value from another colour or size has crept in
  4. After fixing, ask the same questions again two to four weeks later and record the results

AI answers change by day, wording and location, so do not judge from a single run. Ask the same question several times and keep the record. Checklist

  1. The spec table and size chart are text tables in the body, not only images
  2. Measurements and fit guidance are separate, and guidance is labelled as a guide
  3. The measuring method (laid flat, outer or inner) is stated under the table
  4. Compatible models are listed by exact model, never "latest models"
  5. Colour names match across selector, image descriptions, markup and feed
  6. Each colour and size has its own SKU, and a GTIN where available
  7. A query URL shows that variant's image, price and stock
  8. Price and stock in the markup match the page (checked in the Rich Results Test)
  9. No two plugins output a Product for the same product
  10. Three conditional questions are written down and AI answers are recorded

FAQ

Is a size chart image enough?

Keep the image for people, but put the same values in a text table in the body too. AI search crawlers cannot be relied on to read numbers inside an image.

Will adding ProductGroup get my products into AI answers?

No markup guarantees that. Structured data puts the facts from your page into a form machines cannot misread. Fix the text spec table and per-variant price and stock first.

Can WooCommerce output ProductGroup on its own?

Not in WooCommerce 11.3.1: the built-in markup outputs neither ProductGroup nor colour or size. You need a plugin that supports ProductGroup, such as Yoast WooCommerce SEO, and should check that no other plugin outputs a second Product.

Should each colour have its own page?

Google documents both single-page and multi-page variant setups. Either works. What matters is that every colour and size has its own ID, a URL that opens with it selected, and the right price and stock.

What if a product has no GTIN?

GTIN is optional. A distinct SKU for each colour and size is enough to tell them apart. OpenAI's spec says not to invent an MPN to fill a missing GTIN.

If I give a height guide, will AI say the size is guaranteed to fit?

It might. Put the guide on its own line, label it as a guide, and list the garment measurements and measuring method next to it so the measured numbers are read too.
