Wikipedia and Wikidata for AI search: should your company have an article, and what to do if it doesn't qualify

Wikipedia is one of the sources ChatGPT cites most. But a company article is only appropriate when the company has been covered by reliable sources independent of it (notability), editing your own article is strongly discouraged, and paid editing must be disclosed. For most small and mid-sized businesses, the dependable move is not getting a Wikipedia article: it is making your own site's about page and structured data the place where the correct facts live, and, if an article already exists, fixing errors through the proper process.

Written November 24, 2026 (JST). The Wikipedia rules were checked that day against the Japanese Wikipedia guideline on organization notability, the English Wikipedia conflict-of-interest guideline and the Wikimedia Foundation's paid-contribution rules. The Wikidata notability page could not be fetched, so its criteria are summarized from secondary sources.

Having a Wikipedia article does not guarantee that AI answers will mention your company or rank it higher. AI answers change daily, and how sources are chosen is not published. This guide covers the decisions and steps for getting your company's facts across to AI correctly.

What you can take from them: Wikipedia stands out as a ChatGPT source, matters much less in Google's AI features and Perplexity, and its share can swing within months. Neither "a Wikipedia article is all you need" nor "without Wikipedia you can't appear" holds.

These studies count whether a Wikipedia page was shown as a source. The page shown is usually a general article about a topic or place, not a company's own article.

Does your company qualify? A quick test

The Japanese Wikipedia guideline on organization notability says companies and organizations are notable when they have been covered by secondary sources. Those sources must be reliable and independent of the company, and thin coverage needs several independent sources (Japanese Wikipedia: notability (organizations)). Notability does not mean "famous" or "important". It means independent publications have taken notice.

Most local small businesses do not meet the bar with one feature in the local paper. An article created without enough coverage is likely to be nominated for deletion, which costs time and credibility.

Rules to know before writing it yourself or hiring someone

Even when a company might qualify, writing its own article is very hard to do within the rules. Three rules matter most.

  1. No promotion: the Japanese guideline states that promotional activity is prohibited by Wikipedia's official policy (Japanese Wikipedia: notability (organizations)).
  2. Conflict-of-interest editing is strongly discouraged: the English guideline asks people connected to a subject not to edit its article directly and to propose changes on the article's talk page. New articles are meant to go through Articles for Creation review. It also says plainly that once an article about your company exists, you have no right to control its content (Wikipedia: Conflict of interest).
  3. Paid editing must be disclosed: the Wikimedia Foundation's Terms of Use require anyone paid to contribute to any Wikimedia project to disclose their employer, client and affiliation, on their user page, the article's talk page or in the edit summary. The amount paid need not be disclosed. The rule took effect on June 16, 2014 (FAQ on paid contributions). Individual projects can adopt an alternative disclosure policy by consensus, so check the rules of the language edition you are dealing with.

Some services offer to "create your Wikipedia page" or "guarantee" it. Whether an article stays is decided by Wikipedia's volunteer community, so no vendor can guarantee it. If you do hire someone, check at least the points below.

If an article already exists: how to fix errors

  1. List each error (old address, departed executives, discontinued businesses) and public evidence of the correct fact: registry records, news articles, published financial notices. Your own website alone is weak evidence.
  2. Do not edit the article. On its talk page, state that you are connected to the company and list the passage, the correct fact and the source. English Wikipedia offers an {{edit COI}} template for these requests.
  3. If nothing changes, do not repeat the request or edit from another account. Wait for other editors to decide.
  4. Record what was corrected and when, and confirm that your own about page states the same facts.

Some tools compare an existing article with competitors' and suggest improvements, such as Adobe LLM Optimizer's Wikipedia analysis, which requires an existing article and notes that edits are made on Wikipedia itself (Adobe Experience League). Any suggestions still have to go through the process above.

What Wikidata is: a lower bar than Wikipedia, but not a promotion channel

Wikidata is Wikimedia's structured-data database. Each thing is an item with an ID starting with Q, holding a label, description and aliases, plus facts stated as claims such as inception date, headquarters location and official website. Because an item can sometimes exist without a Wikipedia article, many guides suggest Wikidata to companies that don't qualify for Wikipedia.

Wikidata has its own notability policy (Wikidata:Notability). We could not fetch the original page this time, so this is a summary from secondary sources: an item should meet at least one of three criteria. (1) It links to a page on a Wikimedia project such as Wikipedia. (2) It refers to a clearly identifiable entity that can be described using serious, publicly available references. (3) It fulfills a structural need, making other items' statements more useful. Read the original before creating anything.

  1. Paid-contribution disclosure applies to Wikidata too: the Terms of Use cover all Wikimedia projects.
  2. Items referenced to public records and press coverage hold up better than items referenced only to the company's site.
  3. Search first by company name, English name and former names, so that you don't create a duplicate.
  4. No AI company has published how much it uses Wikidata. That creating an item makes you appear in AI answers has not been shown.

How Google's knowledge panel fits in

Google says knowledge panels are created automatically by its algorithms when enough information is available on the open web. It describes the sources as publicly available information, licensed data and content owners themselves; Wikipedia is not named on that help page (How Google's Knowledge Graph works).

If your company has a panel, search for it on Google, choose "Claim this knowledge panel" and sign in to an official YouTube, Search Console, X (Twitter) or Facebook account to verify. You can then suggest changes. Not every panel can be claimed yet (Get verified on Google). Anyone can report errors through the panel's Feedback link.

The only one any company can reliably fix today is its own site. Whether or not you have a Wikipedia article, the foundation is putting the correct facts on your site, where the other three can find them.

What you can do on your own site without Wikipedia

  1. State your legal name, name in other languages, former names, founding date, address, representative and business lines as text on your about page. See about pages for AI search.
  2. Add Organization structured data to the home page or the about page. Google says no property is required and recommends placing it on the home page or a single page that describes the organization.
  3. In sameAs, list URLs on other sites that hold information about your company. Include only official accounts you control and pages that definitely refer to you. Add a Wikidata item or Wikipedia article only if it exists and is correct.
  4. When newspapers or trade publications cover you, list the outlet, date and link on a press or media page. It helps AI and Wikipedia editors alike find independent sources.

How to check

  1. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode "What is (company)?" and "When was (company) founded?", and log the answers and cited URLs with the date. If something is wrong, note whether the source is Wikipedia or another site.
  2. Search Wikipedia for your name, former names and names in other languages. Check whether an article exists, what it says, and whether it mixes you up with a namesake.
  3. Search Wikidata the same way. If there is an item, check the official website, founding date and location.
  4. AI answers change from day to day, so ask the same questions on the same day each month. Measuring AI visibility explains logging a fixed question set.

When a client asks your agency to "get us on Wikipedia"

Writing a client's Wikipedia article is paid editing: it requires disclosure, and creating the article directly is discouraged. Usually it works better to go through the notability test with the client's coverage, and if it falls short, propose cleaning up the about page and structured data and building a press page instead. That is more dependable and easier to explain.

FAQ

Can my company appear in ChatGPT without a Wikipedia article?

Yes, that is possible. ChatGPT cites Wikipedia often, but company facts are also read from your own site, news coverage, review sites and other pages. Studies find Wikipedia's share even smaller in Google's AI features and Perplexity.

Can an employee write our company's article?

It is not recommended. English Wikipedia strongly discourages conflict-of-interest editing and asks for talk-page proposals instead. If it is part of someone's job, it is also paid editing and the employer must be disclosed.

Will lots of press releases make us notable?

No. Press releases are primary sources from the company and don't count. You need independent publications covering the company itself.

Can we register our company freely on Wikidata?

Not freely. Wikidata has a notability policy, including being describable with serious, publicly available references, and the paid-contribution disclosure rules apply.

Our Wikipedia article has an error. Can we fix it?

Don't edit it directly. On the article's talk page, say you are connected to the company and propose the correct fact with a source. Other editors decide.

Do we need Wikipedia to get a Google knowledge panel?

Google's help says panels are created automatically when there is enough information on the open web and does not say Wikipedia is required. If a panel exists, you can claim it and suggest changes.

Can we put our Wikipedia URL in sameAs?

Yes, if your article exists and is accurate. Don't add general topic articles, a namesake's article, or items that don't exist.
