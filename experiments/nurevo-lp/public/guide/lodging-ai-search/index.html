AI search for hotels, inns and vacation rentals: how to get AI to read your check-in times, amenities and booking correctly

AI search optimization (AEO) for a hotel, inn, guesthouse or vacation rental comes down to publishing, on your own site, the facts that let an AI answer conditional questions without guessing: "a family-friendly hot-spring inn", "a hotel near the airport with free parking where I can check in after 11pm". Write your check-in and check-out times, amenities, room types, how your prices are set, directions and how to book, both in page text and in schema.org LodgingBusiness or Hotel structured data, and keep the same values on your booking-site (OTA) listings and Google Business Profile.

Written November 10, 2026 (JST). Information from schema.org, Google and Japanese government agencies was checked on each official page on the dates given in the text.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your property's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your property or rank it anywhere.

What travelers ask AI, and what AI uses to pick a place to stay

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews where to stay, the AI reads what it can find on the web and builds a shortlist. Lodging questions are rarely about place alone. They combine conditions: "an inn within 30 minutes of Kyoto Station with a private open-air bath", "somewhere I can stay with my dog and have dinner in the room", "a hotel near the airport that takes late-night check-ins", "a hotel with a wheelchair-accessible bath". Times, amenities, who the place suits and how to get there all come into it.

To answer, an AI needs each condition written down as text somewhere it can check. If your amenities list is only an image, your latest check-in time appears nowhere, or your pet policy just says "please contact us", the AI has to rely on booking sites, review sites or guesswork. If a booking site's information is out of date, your property may be described with the old facts.

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special schema.org structured data needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that the usual basics still matter, such as making structured data match the visible text (checked November 1, 2026). Google: AI features and your website. For lodging, too, the work is not a trick: it is publishing your property's facts correctly, with the same values everywhere.

Your own site and your OTA listings: why one is not enough

Most properties are listed on booking sites (online travel agencies, or OTAs) such as Booking.com and Expedia. OTA pages are detailed and AI sometimes uses them as sources. Your own site still matters because it is the only place where you can describe your property in your own words, in the most detail and most accurately.

A simple way to think about it: your site is where you explain reasons and conditions; OTAs and Google Business Profile are where you check that the same values appear.

The facts to publish, and the structured data that goes with them

In structured data, a place to stay is described as a schema.org LodgingBusiness. LodgingBusiness is a kind of LocalBusiness, and its more specific types are Hotel, Resort, Hostel, BedAndBreakfast, Motel, Campground and VacationRental. LodgingBusiness itself has properties such as checkinTime (the earliest someone may check in), checkoutTime, amenityFeature, petsAllowed, numberOfRooms, availableLanguage, starRating and audience (checked November 10, 2026, schema.org V30.1). Google's local business documentation lists name and address as required and asks you to use the most specific sub-type possible (checked October 29, 2026). Google: Local business structured data

Property names checked on schema.org on November 10, 2026. Google's required and recommended properties are the ones in its local business documentation; not all of these are Google requirements.

schema.org's guide for hotels separates the lodging business itself, the accommodation guests stay in (an Accommodation such as a HotelRoom) and the offer it is sold under. It also notes that amenityFeature does not say whether a feature is free or charged. schema.org: Hotels. If parking or breakfast costs extra, put the amount in your page text.

Show prices the way local rules require. In Japan, for example, the National Tax Agency requires prices shown to consumers in advance to include consumption tax, whatever the medium (checked November 1, 2026). National Tax Agency: total price display (Japanese). Room rates change with date, party size and plan, so rather than putting a fixed price in structured data, explain the basis in your text, such as "per person, two sharing a room" or "local lodging tax not included". That is harder to misread.

Google also documents structured data for hotel prices, but that is for checking price accuracy in Google's hotel products (Hotel Center) (checked November 10, 2026). Google: Hotel price structured data. It is not a requirement for appearing in AI answers.

Vacation rentals and private lodging

For private lodging, a registration or notification number is one of your facts too. In Japan, for example, the Japan Tourism Agency says private-lodging (minpaku) booking platforms must confirm the operator's name, address and notification number before listing a property, must not list a property they cannot confirm, and must show the number on the listing (checked November 10, 2026). Japan Tourism Agency: preventing listings of illegal properties (Japanese). Rules differ by country and city, so check yours. Showing the same number, name and address on your own site as on the platforms makes it easier for AI and travelers to confirm it is the same place.

Whole-house rentals and holiday homes have their own structured data type, VacationRental. Maximum guests, the number of beds or futons, whether there is a kitchen or washing machine, how keys are handed over, and house rules for neighbors (noise, rubbish) are all things travelers ask AI about. If no staff are on site, also publish an emergency contact and the hours it is answered.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Check-in start and latest times and the check-out time match across your site, OTAs and Google Business Profile.
  2. Your amenities are written as text, not only shown as an image, and paid ones (parking, breakfast) state the price or terms.
  3. Pets, children, accessibility and food allergies are covered with conditions: what you can and cannot do.
  4. The structured data type fits the property, such as Hotel, Resort, Hostel or VacationRental, not a generic Organization.
  5. Prices are shown as local rules require, with the basis stated: per room or per person, and how local lodging taxes are handled.
  6. For private lodging, the registration number, name and address are the same on the platforms and on your site.
  7. llms.txt lists your overview, rooms, amenities, directions, FAQ and booking pages. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, ask AI the questions travelers would ask (area plus condition plus purpose) and see whether your property comes up by name or URL: "a family-friendly inn in [hot-spring town]", "a hotel near [station] with free parking". With questions that include your property's name, also check that what the AI says (check-in times, amenities) is right. FAQ

Where should a hotel or inn start with AI search?

Write your check-in and check-out times, amenities, rooms, conditions for pets and children, directions and how to book correctly as text on your own site. Then publish the same facts as Hotel (or another lodging type) structured data and in llms.txt, and make sure your OTA listings and Google Business Profile have the same values.

If I am on OTAs, do I still need to fix my own site?

OTA pages can be AI sources too, but their fields are fixed, so details such as shuttle conditions or how to book a private bath often do not fit. Your own site is where you can describe your property in the most detail. Keeping both in agreement is the safest approach.

Which type should a traditional inn use, Hotel or LodgingBusiness?

schema.org has no type for traditional Japanese inns. Choose the closest type, such as Hotel or Resort for a hot-spring stay, or keep the general LodgingBusiness if none fits. Say in your text and description what kind of place it is.

The paid plans add an always-current ruleset (Standard, $20/month) and AI visibility measurement in beta (Pro, from $99/month). Nurevo AEO on WordPress.org

Will this guarantee my property appears in ChatGPT?

No. AI answers change, and no tool or technique can guarantee a mention or a position. What you can do is make your facts easy for AI to read and keep checking whether you appear and whether you are described correctly.
