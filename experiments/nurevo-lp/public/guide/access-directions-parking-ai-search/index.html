How to write your directions, access and parking page so AI search gets it right

To get AI to give correct directions and parking information, write these on your directions page as HTML text, not only as a map or an image: your full address (including suite or floor), the nearest station or stop with the exit and walking time (and how you measured it), landmarks to the entrance, and parking (whether you have it, how many spaces, where, and the cost). Then use one address format everywhere — your site, Google Business Profile and directories — and put the same address and your entrance's coordinates in your structured data. If you have no parking, say so: "We have no parking; please use the public garage on Example Street." That leaves AI less room to guess.

Written November 21, 2026 (JST). Opening hours are covered in How to publish hours and closures and booking in How to write your booking page. This guide is for any business people visit — shops, clinics, studios, offices, guesthouses — and focuses on one thing: getting AI to say correctly how to reach you and whether you can come by car.

AI answers change with the day, the wording of the question and the service. The steps here make your access details harder for AI to misread and show you how to check; they don't guarantee that you will appear in an answer or that it will be correct.

What visitors actually ask AI comes with conditions: "Is there parking near (business)?", "How far is it from (station)?", "Can I get there with a stroller?" AI answers by pulling sentences from your directions page, so anything not written as text is more likely to be filled in from other sites or by guessing.

Eight things to put on your directions page

Put these on your location page or a separate "Directions" page. If you embed a map, still write the same details as text: AI may not be able to read what's inside the map.

Bad: "Near the station! Parking available. See the map." Good: "4-minute walk (about 300 m) from the north exit of Springfield Station. 123 Example Street, Suite 300. The entrance is down the passage on the left of the building. Two reserved spaces behind the building (7 and 8); when they're full, use the public garage on Oak Street."

"Parking available" alone doesn't say how many spaces, where, whether it's free or whether it's a partner car park. For a partner car park, give its name, address and the discount terms (for example "one hour free with a purchase over $20"). When the terms change, update the page that day.

Walking times: decide how you measure, and say so

Walking times are easy to get inconsistent. Japanese real-estate advertising has a fixed rule: road distance along the route, 80 m per minute, any remainder rounded up to a full minute, with no allowance for traffic lights or slopes (the enforcement rules of Japan's fair competition code for real-estate advertising, as explained in Mitsubishi Jisho House Net's glossary, Japanese). Shops and clinics aren't bound by it, and other countries have no common rule, but picking one rule and sticking to it keeps your site and directory listings from disagreeing.

  1. Decide the start and end points ("from the north exit gates to our building entrance")
  2. Measure the walking distance along the route in a map app and convert it with your rule. Adding the distance ("about 300 m") lets both AI and people check it
  3. At large stations with several exits, name the closest exit and add one line for people arriving from another
  4. Check your directory listings and Google Business Profile description for a different number, and make them match

Only write "1 minute from the station" if you really are at the exit. An exaggerated walking time shows up in reviews as a mismatch.

Use one address format everywhere

AI services are generally thought to combine your site, Google Business Profile, directories and social profiles to decide whether they describe the same business; how they match them isn't published. When the address is written differently from listing to listing, the listings may not be linked, or an address without the suite or floor ends up in answers. Decide these five things and use them on every listing.

  1. Abbreviations: "Street" or "St", "Suite" or "Ste". Either is fine; pick one
  2. Characters: plain hyphens, consistent numbering, no decorative symbols
  3. Building name, suite and floor. If the building is renamed, update every listing that day
  4. Whether to include the state or region (always include it in structured data)
  5. If you also publish an address in another language, write it so it is recognisably the same place (include the building name)

After a move, put the new address on your directions page with one line such as "We moved on (date); our previous address was …". Simply deleting the old page leaves the old address on other sites unconnected to your site, and AI may keep giving it.

Set parking and accessibility attributes in Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile has "attributes", shown on Google Search, Maps and other Google services. Some attributes you edit yourself; others may be filled in from customers who visit. Accessibility attributes include a wheelchair-accessible entrance, restroom, seating, elevator and wheelchair-accessible parking — choose Yes for the last one if you have a parking spot reserved for people with accessibility needs. Google says review of an edit usually takes about 10 minutes but can take up to 30 days (Manage your business attributes, checked November 21, 2026). Which attributes you see depends on your business category.

How Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode use profile attributes isn't published. But the profile is the information shown in Google Maps and Search, so make it say the same as your site. Details attributes can't express — number of spaces, cost, partner terms — go in your page text and the profile description.

Structured data: address, coordinates, map and amenities

In Google's LocalBusiness structured data documentation (updated September 2026), name and address are required and geo (latitude and longitude) is recommended. The examples split the address into PostalAddress streetAddress, addressLocality, addressRegion, postalCode and addressCountry, and the coordinates must have a precision of at least five decimal places. Take the coordinates from a map app at your entrance: for directions, the door people use is more useful than the middle of the building.

Google's documentation doesn't mention them, but schema.org also has hasMap (a URL to a map of the place), amenityFeature (amenities written as LocationFeatureSpecification name/value pairs) and publicAccess (whether the place is open to public visitors). In our research we found no official statement that Google or any other AI service uses hasMap or amenityFeature. Adding them does no harm, but treat them as a restatement of what your page text already says.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Your address, with suite or floor and postal code, is written as HTML text
  2. The nearest station or stop, line, exit, walking time and distance are written, and you decided where you measured from and to
  3. A route with two or three landmarks, plus the entrance and floor, is written
  4. Parking — whether you have it, spaces, location, cost, partner terms — is written; if you have none, it says so
  5. Bicycles, motorbikes, accessibility and strollers are covered
  6. The address is written the same way on your site, Google Business Profile, directories and social profiles
  7. Google Business Profile attributes (parking, accessibility) match your site
  8. The address and coordinates (at least five decimal places) in structured data match the page text
  9. No page, PDF or blog post still shows an old address or a car park you no longer use
  10. You chose your questions and recorded AI's answers on the day and two to four weeks later

FAQ

We embed a map. Do we still need to write directions as text?

Yes. Whether AI can read an embedded map or a directions image differs by service and isn't published. Treat the map as help for people, and write the station, exit, walking time, entrance and parking in the page text.

We have no parking. Should we just leave it out?

Saying "We have no parking" is safer. If you say nothing, AI may answer from other sites or guess. Add where to park nearby and how to come by public transport.

How should we work out walking time?

There is no universal rule. Japan's real-estate advertising standard (road distance, 80 m per minute, round up) is one clear option. Whatever you choose, say where you measured from and to, and add the distance.

Do we need coordinates in structured data to appear in AI answers?

Not necessarily. In Google's documentation geo is recommended and only name and address are required. Whether ChatGPT or Perplexity use coordinates isn't published. Fix the page text and address format first, then add coordinates taken at your entrance.

Should we add hasMap or amenityFeature?

You can, but they aren't in Google's documentation and we found no official statement that any AI service uses them. Add them as a restatement of the map page and parking details already on your page.

We moved, and AI keeps giving our old address.

Update your site, Google Business Profile, directories and social profiles on the same day, and keep one line on your directions page with the move date and the previous address. Check the URLs AI cites; if they're old pages or PDFs, fix or remove them. Update times aren't published, so ask again a few weeks later.

Can I include the suite and floor in Nurevo's Address field?

Yes, please do. The field is published as typed in streetAddress and on the llms.txt "Address" line. Use the same format as your page and Google Business Profile.
