AI search readiness of Canadian web design agencies: 908 agency websites (November 2026)

We fetched the home page, robots.txt and llms.txt of 908 Canadian web design agencies' own websites. 22.9% (208) had an llms.txt — level with the US (23.1%), the UK (21.5%) and Australia (24.3%), and 1.7 times Japan (13.4%). 18.9% met all six AI-search items we checked, exactly the US figure. 3.5% of sites with a robots.txt blocked an AI bot, and all 19 sites blocking GPTBot still allowed OpenAI's search crawler, OAI-SearchBot.

Published 2026-11-22. Data aggregated 2026-11-12. Research by Nurevo AEO (Bestie.合同会社), Japan. Nurevo sells AI search optimisation (AEO) tools and a WordPress plugin, and so has an interest in this topic. This is the fifth of our country reports (after the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia and India). A French version is also available.

Key findings

  1. 22.9% (208) of 908 Canadian web agency websites had an llms.txt. That is not statistically different from the US (23.1%, p = 0.95), the UK (21.5%, p = 0.52) or Australia (24.3%, p = 0.45), and is 1.7 times Japan (13.4%, p < 0.001).
  2. 18.9% (172) of Canadian sites met all six AI-search items we checked — the same as the US — with an average of 3.58. Among the 17 countries with 100+ sites, Canada ranked 12th on llms.txt, in the same middle band as the US, the UK and Australia.
  3. Of 660 Canadian sites with a robots.txt, 3.5% (23) blocked an AI bot (US 4.3%, Japan 1.3%). All 19 sites blocking GPTBot allowed OAI-SearchBot, and only one site (0.2%) blocked any of the four bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks.

Method

  1. Population: web design agencies' own websites. Of 1,335 agency sites listed as Canadian in our collection list, 908 home pages could be reached. This is not a sample of Canadian businesses in general. For comparison we use the same survey's US (4,472), UK (942), Australia (1,719), Japan (2,486) and all overseas sites outside Canada (12,444).
  2. Fetched: each site's home page, /robots.txt, /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt, once each (User-Agent: NurevoSurvey/1.0), early November to 12 November 2026; aggregated 2026-11-12.
  3. Classification: llms.txt = HTTP 200, not HTML, more than 20 characters. JSON-LD = present on the home page; "broken" = at least one block that does not parse as JSON. Organization-type = an @type of Organization, LocalBusiness, Corporation or ProfessionalService. WordPress = the home page HTML contains wp-content/ or wp-includes/, or a WordPress generator tag. SEO plugins were identified from strings in the home page HTML.
  4. AI bot blocking: whether the robots.txt rules that apply to each bot disallow the home page (/) (longest match); the base is sites with a robots.txt. "Blocks an AI bot" = blocks any of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot or OAI-SearchBot.
  5. The six items: (1) has an llms.txt; (2) has JSON-LD; (3) has an Organization or LocalBusiness type; (4) has a robots.txt that blocks none of GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and OAI-SearchBot; (5) has a meta description; (6) has a canonical link. The count (0–6) is a simple measure made for this survey, not Nurevo's 0–100 product score.
  6. HTTP 202: 122 Canadian home pages (13.4% of those reached) answered with status 202, a similar share to the US (15.0%), the UK (14.9%) and Australia (14.5%). Such pages fail almost every item. Among the 786 Canadian sites that answered HTTP 200, 26.5% had an llms.txt, 21.9% met all six items and 78.6% had JSON-LD — again not different from US HTTP 200 sites (27.2% and 22.3%; p = 0.73 and 0.85).
  7. Statistics: differences between Canada and other groups were tested with chi-square tests. Groups with fewer than 30 sites are not shown.

Results: Canada compared with the US, the UK, Australia and Japan

Canada was within one to three percentage points of the US, the UK and Australia on almost every item. None of llms.txt, all six items, JSON-LD or AI bot blocking differed significantly from those three countries. FAQPage (8.0%) was slightly higher than in the US (6.2%, p = 0.048). Compared with all overseas sites outside Canada (llms.txt 26.6%, all six 22.0%), Canada was a little lower on llms.txt and all six items (p = 0.016 and 0.034). Compared with Japan, Canada had 1.7 times the llms.txt share and 1.8 times the share meeting all six items, with about the same JSON-LD share (68.1% vs 69.4%, p = 0.47). Japan was higher on meta descriptions (90.7% vs 67.3%), canonical links (84.6% vs 71.3%) and robots.txt (91.8% vs 72.7%); differences in the share of HTTP 202 and non-WordPress sites are part of that gap.

Where Canada stands among 17 countries

The 17 countries (Japan included) with at least 100 reached sites, ranked by llms.txt. Canada was 12th on llms.txt, tied with the US and the Netherlands at 18.9% on all six items, and 14th on the six-item average (3.58). Its AI bot blocking rate (3.5%) was the fourth lowest, after Japan (1.3%), France (1.9%) and Ireland (3.3%). Singapore, India, Brazil, Spain and the Philippines lead; the English-speaking US, UK, Canada and Australia sit together in the middle. Blocking by bot

For the 660 Canadian sites with a robots.txt, the share blocking the home page for each bot. Bots described as training crawlers (CCBot, Bytespider, Google-Extended, GPTBot, Applebot-Extended, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai) were blocked by 2.4–3.5%, slightly below the US, the UK and Australia and two to three times Japan. Among bots used for search or for fetching pages when a user asks, a single site blocked both ChatGPT-User and PerplexityBot; no site blocked OAI-SearchBot or Claude-SearchBot. No site blocked Googlebot or Bingbot. Canada's AI bot blocking rate (3.5%) did not differ significantly from the US, the UK or Australia (p = 0.38, 0.31, 0.25) and was higher than Japan's (p < 0.001).

What the blocking looks like (facts)

  1. All 19 sites blocking GPTBot did not block OAI-SearchBot. In the US it was 119 of 128, in the UK 28 of 28 and in Australia 55 of 56.
  2. Of the 25 sites blocking any of the 11 AI bots above, 15 (60.0%) used the same set: GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider and Applebot-Extended, all blocked together (US 58.5%, UK 77.1%, Australia 64.6%).
  3. One site (0.2% of sites with a robots.txt) blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
  4. 41 sites (6.2% of sites with a robots.txt; US 4.4%) named an AI bot in robots.txt without blocking the home page for any of them.
  5. 8 of the 23 sites blocking an AI bot (34.8%) also had an llms.txt (US 34.0%).

Structured data types

The most common types in Canadian home page JSON-LD were WebSite (64.8%), WebPage (57.8%) and Organization (56.9%); every type was close to the US, UK and Australian figures. Compared with Japan, Canada was higher on WebPage, SearchAction and ImageObject (types WordPress SEO plugins commonly output), PostalAddress (26.2% vs 16.4%), ContactPoint (14.1% vs 6.5%) and GeoCoordinates (11.0% vs 3.7%). BreadcrumbList was about the same as Japan (41.4% vs 43.4%). Only 1.1% (7) of Canadian sites with JSON-LD had a block that did not parse as JSON — the lowest of the groups compared.

WordPress and SEO plugins

71.6% (650) of Canadian agency sites run WordPress, close to the US (74.5%) and the UK (73.4%) and below Japan (92.3%). WordPress sites had llms.txt 30.8%, JSON-LD 93.2% and a six-item average of 4.87; non-WordPress sites (258, of which 122 answered 202) had llms.txt 3.1%, JSON-LD 4.7% and an average of 0.35. Even among the 136 non-WordPress sites that answered HTTP 200, only 5.9% (8) had an llms.txt and 8.8% (12) had JSON-LD. Comparing WordPress sites only, llms.txt was 30.8% in Canada and 30.1% in the US (p = 0.77), both above Japan (14.0%, p < 0.001).

Yoast SEO was detected on 53.7% of Canadian WordPress sites, Rank Math on 21.4% and AIOSEO on 7.1% — a mix similar to the US (56.4%, 18.0%, 7.1%). The llms.txt share was 41.3% with AIOSEO, 36.7% with Rank Math, 30.1% where no plugin was detected and 27.2% with Yoast SEO. This survey does not tell whether an llms.txt was generated by a plugin or placed by hand. SEOPress (3 sites) is under 30 and not shown.

.ca versus .com

We split Canadian agency sites by the end of their domain name: of 908 reached, 301 were .ca, 499 .com and 108 other. .com sites were slightly ahead on llms.txt (24.4% vs 20.3%) and all six items (20.0% vs 16.3%), but neither difference was statistically significant (p = 0.20 and 0.22). JSON-LD was higher on .com (70.3% vs 62.8%, p = 0.03), and the share answering HTTP 202 did not differ (12.6% vs 14.6%, p = 0.49). Among HTTP 200 sites only, llms.txt was 23.7% on .ca and 28.0% on .com (p = 0.26).

Distribution of the six items

In Canada, five items (36.2%) and six items (18.9%) together made up 55.2% of sites, and 24.3% met none. The shape is almost the same as in the US, the UK and Australia. Most zero-item sites are those that answered HTTP 202 (122 sites, 13.4% of those reached), where we may not have seen the real page. Japan has very few zero-item sites (1.6%) and a thicker band at three to four items (30.7% combined, Canada 14.1%).

Limitations

  1. The population is web design agencies' own websites, not businesses in general. Country comes from our collection list, and the sample is not representative of all Canadian agencies. We did not break results down by province or by language (English or French) because too few sites have a known location or language. Counts such as per-bot blocking are small numbers.
  2. Only the home page was examined. robots.txt was judged for / only; partial blocking and blocking by firewalls or CDNs were not counted. For llms.txt we checked only whether it exists, not its quality.
  3. One fetch per site; unreachable sites (427) are excluded. The 122 HTTP 202 responses may not reflect the real home page.
  4. SEO plugins were detected from HTML strings; sites that hide plugin markers or run several plugins may be misclassified.
  5. Part of this aggregation reuses fetches from the 5 November run.

Our reading (opinion)

What follows is our opinion, not a finding. On llms.txt, structured data and AI bot handling, Canadian agency sites look almost identical to those in the US, the UK and Australia, which may reflect English-speaking agencies using much the same tools (WordPress with Yoast SEO, Rank Math and similar plugins); this survey does not test cause. AI bot blocking is a minority practice, almost always limited to training crawlers — only one site also blocked search crawlers. Which bots to block is each site owner's decision. Non-WordPress sites rarely had an llms.txt or structured data (5.9% llms.txt even excluding HTTP 202). None of this guarantees appearing in AI answers (in another of our studies, on-site signals showed no relationship with appearance).

FAQ

Are Canadian agencies different from US agencies on AI search readiness?

Hardly. llms.txt was 22.9% in Canada and 23.1% in the US, both had 18.9% meeting all six items, and AI bot blocking (3.5% vs 4.3%) did not differ significantly (November 2026, agencies' own websites).

How does Canada compare with Japan?

Canada had 1.7 times Japan's llms.txt share (22.9% vs 13.4%) and 1.8 times its share meeting all six items (18.9% vs 10.4%). JSON-LD was about the same (68.1% vs 69.4%), while Japan was higher on meta descriptions, canonical links and robots.txt.

Do many Canadian sites block AI crawlers?

3.5% of sites with a robots.txt (23 sites) — a small minority. Almost all of it targets training crawlers: only one site blocked any of OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot.
