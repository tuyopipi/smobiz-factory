Redesigning a Site Without Losing AI Search Citations: What Agencies Do Before, On and After Launch

To keep AI search citations through a redesign: before launch, inventory the URLs AI answers cite and the AI-facing output (structured data, llms.txt, robots.txt); map every old URL to its new URL with a one-to-one, server-side permanent redirect (301 or 308); on launch day, check that the new host does not block AI crawlers and that no old URLs remain in the structured data or llms.txt; then measure before and after with the same questions. Google publishes its redirect rules, but we could not find any official explanation of how ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude handle redirects. That is exactly why launch day should leave the site in a state where any crawler, reading any old or new URL, reaches the same facts.

Written November 19, 2026 (JST). This guide is for web agencies, SEO firms and WordPress care companies that redesign client sites, swap themes or CMSs, move to HTTPS, change domains or merge sites. If you are ending a contract and handing the site to someone else, see Ending or Handing Over AEO Work. If AI visibility has already dropped after a launch, see Why AI Visibility Drops.

AI answers change from day to day and with the wording of the question. These steps keep cited pages and facts from disappearing or contradicting each other during a redesign. They do not guarantee that the site will appear in AI answers afterwards, or how quickly.

First, name the kind of change

Google separates site moves with URL changes from moves without them, such as changing hosts (Google Search Central: How to move a site). The same split decides how much AI search work a project needs.

Change of Address scope is from the Search Console help ([Change of Address tool](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9370220?hl=en)): it is not for HTTP to HTTPS, path changes within a site, www vs non-www, or host changes with the same URLs.

Google recommends changing only one thing at a time. If the design, URLs, domain and copy all change on the same day, there is no way to tell which change moved the AI answers. When budget or timing forces a single launch, at least agree with the client that URLs and factual content will not change on the same day. It makes the result much easier to explain later.

Before launch, part 2: build the URL map and redirects

Google documents redirects in detail (Redirects and Google Search, How to move a site). The essentials:

  1. Use server-side permanent redirects (301 or 308) wherever possible. Google states that 301 and other permanent redirects don't cause a loss in PageRank.
  2. 302, 303 and 307 are treated as temporary and do not signal that the target should be canonical. If the move will not be reverted, use a permanent redirect. Use JavaScript redirects only when server-side or meta refresh redirects are impossible.
  3. Send each old URL straight to the new URL with matching content. Redirecting many old URLs to an unrelated page, such as the home page, may be treated as a soft 404.
  4. Return 404 or 410 for content that no longer exists.
  5. Avoid chains. Googlebot follows up to 10 hops, but Google recommends redirecting directly to the final target (if that is impossible, fewer than 5 hops, ideally 3 or fewer).
  6. Keep redirects as long as possible, generally at least a year. Give each new URL a self-referencing canonical, and update internal links, sitemaps and hreflang to the new URLs.
  7. Expect temporary ranking fluctuation; a medium-sized site can take a few weeks or more before the new URLs replace the old ones.
# Apache (.htaccess): a page URL changed (one rule per page, straight to the target)
Redirect 301 /service.html https://www.example.com/services/
Redirect 301 /company/access.html https://www.example.com/access/

# Apache: the domain changed (paths stay the same)
RewriteEngine On
RewriteCond %{HTTP_HOST} ^(www\.)?old-example\.com$ [NC]
RewriteRule ^(.*)$ https://www.example.com/$1 [R=301,L]

# nginx: the domain changed (server block of the old domain)
return 301 https://www.example.com$request_uri;

Give the URL map these columns: old URL, new URL, status (301/404/410), cited by AI (yes/no), and the facts on the page. For every row that was cited, confirm the new page carries the same facts (prices, hours, address).

For ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude, we found no official documentation on how they handle redirects or when they switch a citation from the old URL to the new one (as of November 19, 2026). A single permanent redirect that follows Google's rules means any AI crawler that reads an old URL still lands on the page with the same content.

WordPress steps (with Nurevo AEO)

On WordPress, this order leaves the fewest gaps. Where Nurevo AEO (the free plugin on WordPress.org) is installed, its behaviour is noted too.

  1. If the new site is built in a separate environment, export the settings on the old site (Nurevo AEO menu, Settings > Tools, or wp nurevo export) and import them on the new one. Business details, FAQ, services and output switches move across. Keys, tokens, the plan and the connection do not, so a connected site must be reconnected on the new install. Per-page settings (pages left out of llms.txt or structured data, page-specific FAQ) are not in the export; if the database is not moved, set them again by hand.
  2. After a domain change, replace the old domain left in the database with wp search-replace. Look at the counts with --dry-run first, and take a backup before running it.
  3. If Nurevo AEO's "URL" setting is empty, the organization URL and @id in the structured data and the URL line in llms.txt use the WordPress site address, so they follow the new domain. If the old domain was typed in, it keeps being published: change it to the new domain or clear it. Service URLs and booking URLs are typed values too, so fix them one by one.
  4. Nurevo AEO builds the page list in llms.txt from up to 30 published pages and posts, most recently updated first, using their current permalinks. Change the permalinks and the list follows.
  5. Nurevo AEO adds its AI crawler rules to the virtual robots.txt WordPress generates. If the new host has a physical robots.txt in the document root, that file is served instead and the rules do not appear. If you use a physical file, put the same rules in it.
  6. When the theme or SEO plugin changes: alongside Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO or Slim SEO, Nurevo AEO adds the missing facts inside their structured data; next to a theme's own schema it prints its own graph and leaves out what is already there. After the switch, use the Rich Results Test to confirm one business entity per page.
# 1. Does the old URL reach the new one in a single permanent redirect? (shows every hop)
curl -sIL https://old-example.com/service.html | grep -i -E "^HTTP|^location"

# 2. AI-facing output on the new site
curl -s https://www.example.com/robots.txt
curl -s https://www.example.com/llms.txt | head -20
wp nurevo status

# 3. Is the old domain still in the database? (look at the counts with --dry-run first)
wp search-replace 'https://old-example.com' 'https://www.example.com' --dry-run

# 4. Move the settings from the old site or staging (keys, tokens and the plan are not included)
wp nurevo export --file=nurevo-settings.json   # on the old site / staging
wp nurevo import nurevo-settings.json          # on the new site

In the curl -sIL output, a single permanent redirect looks like this: the first status is 301 (or 308), the location is the new URL, and the last status is 200. Fix anything that returns 302 or shows more than one location. Changing the user agent in curl does not test a WAF that decides by IP range; for that, check the host or CDN logs for blocked AI crawlers.

What to put in the quote and the contract (for agencies)

  1. List "AI search inventory (cited URLs, structured data, llms.txt, robots.txt, fact pages)" and "URL map" as their own line items. Folded into design work, they are the first thing cut when time runs short.
  2. Define a post-launch check period (for example, redirect and log checks for one month after launch, and a question run in month three).
  3. Decide who pays for the old domain, and until when. Keeping redirects for a year or more means keeping the old domain and its redirect setup.
  4. State that AI answers change, so the redesign does not guarantee appearing in AI answers or any position, and describe how before and after will be measured and reported.
  5. Change facts such as prices or hours only after client approval, and on a different day from the launch.

Redesign checklist

  1. The kind of change (same URLs, paths, HTTPS, domain, merge) and what changes at once are agreed with the client.
  2. URLs cited in AI answers and the pages that carry facts are inventoried.
  3. Pre-launch baselines are recorded: the question results and the Search Console numbers.
  4. One-to-one 301 (or 308) redirects from old to new URLs are in place and resolve in a single hop.
  5. Pages with no matching content return 404 or 410; nothing is mass-redirected to the home page.
  6. The new site's robots.txt, host, CDN and WAF do not block AI search crawlers.
  7. No old URLs remain in structured data or llms.txt, and each page has one business entity.
  8. Internal links, sitemaps, canonicals and hreflang point to the new URLs.
  9. For a domain change, the Change of Address tool is used and the old domain and redirects are kept for a year or more.
  10. After launch, the same questions are measured before and after, including citations of the old domain.

FAQ

Will a redesign remove our site from AI answers?

Not necessarily. But when a cited URL starts returning 404, a table of facts is dropped, or the new host blocks AI crawlers, the site becomes less likely to be used as a source. A pre-launch inventory and launch-day checks prevent most of this.

Do 301 and 302 differ for AI search too?

Google documents 301 and 308 as permanent and 302, 303 and 307 as temporary. We found no official documentation from ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude, so for a move you will not revert, a 301 or 308 is the safe choice.

How long should redirects stay in place?

Google recommends as long as possible, generally at least a year. For domain changes, the Change of Address help says to keep redirects for at least 180 days, longer while Google Search still sends traffic to them, and to keep paying for the old domain for at least a year.

What should we do with llms.txt?

If it is a hand-made file, rewrite it with the new URLs or regenerate it. If a plugin such as Nurevo AEO builds it, the list follows the new permalinks, but hand-typed values such as the organization URL may still hold the old domain, so check them.

Does changing only the theme affect AI search?

With unchanged URLs no redirects are needed, but the new theme may print or drop structured data, and tables of hours or prices may be rebuilt differently. Check that structured data and on-page facts match the old site.

After launch, AI keeps showing old URLs or old information. What now?

First confirm that the old URL reaches the new one in a single 301 and that the new page carries the same facts. Then keep recording the same questions on different days. When AI answers switch over is not published, so keeping the facts right is the most reliable lever.

When merging sites, what happens to the pages being merged?

Redirect each one with a 301 to the page with the closest content. If nothing matches, return 404 or 410. Redirecting them all to an unrelated page such as the home page may be treated by Google as a soft 404.
