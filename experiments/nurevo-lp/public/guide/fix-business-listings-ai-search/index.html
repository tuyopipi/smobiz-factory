How to fix your business details on listings and comparison sites that AI answers draw on

When an AI answer shows your old address, hours or phone number, the thing to fix is not the AI but the listings it cites. Put one fact sheet on your own site, find the listings by reading the answer's sources and searching your old details, then correct them in order: Google Business Profile, map apps, your industry's directories and comparison sites. In the studies so far, listings made up around four in ten AI citations.

Written 27 November 2026 (JST). The listing sites' help pages and terms, and the studies, were read at the links in the text on that date. Screens and steps change, so check each site's help before you start.

AI answers vary by day and by wording. No AI company publishes how long a corrected listing takes to show up, and nothing in this guide guarantees that an AI will show your details correctly. You are fixing what the AI reads, and you check the effect over weeks.

Three things follow. First, in every study your own website and listings together make up a large share of what AI cites. Second, each AI leans on different listings, and that changed between 2024 and 2026 (ChatGPT and Yelp are the example). Third, there is no single list that is enough; the reliable way is to check which listings AI actually cites for your business type and area.

On what stale data does, Birdeye (updated 17 March 2026) reports that some AI tools showed closed clinics and restaurants as open. It gives no rate.

Step 1: Write one fact sheet before you fix anything

Put the correct facts in one place first. If staff write the name three ways, listings end up under three names and AI has a harder time telling they are the same business. The sheet is internal, but the same facts go on your location page and About page, and you point listing owners to that page as proof.

Fact sheet (example)   Last updated: 2026-11-27   Owner: ___
Official name : Example Cafe Downtown (not "Example Cafe DT" or "Example Café")
Address       : 123 Example St, Suite 200, Example City, CA 90000
Phone         : (000) 000-0000 (bookings and questions)
Hours         : Tue-Sun 11:00-20:00 (last order 19:30); closed Mondays
Website       : https://example.com/ (location page https://example.com/downtown/)
Booking       : https://example.com/book/ (phone also accepted)
Planned change: from 2027-01-10, open 11:00-21:00
Old details   : former address 45 Old Rd (moved 2025-03); former phone (000) 111-1111 (disconnected)

The "Old details" line matters most: your former address, phone and name are the search terms for finding stale listings in step 2. For hours, holidays and temporary closures, see AI search and business hours.

Step 2: Find the listings AI actually uses

You may be listed on hundreds of sites, but you do not need to fix them all. Find the ones likely to feed AI answers first.

  1. Ask the AI: put a question with your name ("What day is Example Cafe Downtown closed?") and one without it ("Cafes open on Mondays in downtown Example City?") to ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google (AI Overviews and AI Mode). Copy every source URL the answer shows.
  2. Search your old details: search Google for your former address, phone number or name in quotes (for example "(000) 111-1111"). Every page that comes up is a stale listing an AI may read.
  3. Check the map apps: search your name in Google Maps, Apple Maps and Bing Maps. Look for duplicates, closed pins and the pre-move location.
  4. Check your industry's directories: Yelp, Tripadvisor and OpenTable for restaurants and travel, Healthgrades or similar for clinics, BBB and Angi for services, and so on.
  5. Make a table: one row per listing with the URL, the wrong field, who can fix it (you, the site, an author) and whether it appeared as an AI source.

Fix them in this order: listings that appeared as AI sources, then Google Business Profile and map apps, then your industry's main directories, then other listings and articles. Answers vary from run to run, so ask two or three times on different days and give priority to sources that repeat.

Step 4: A request template for site owners and authors

For listings you cannot edit, send a request by email or the site's contact form. Owners get many requests, so one message that says which page, which field, the correct value and where to check it saves them time.

Subject: Correction request: Example Cafe Downtown (Example City)

Hello [site name] team,

I'm [name], the owner of Example Cafe Downtown. Thank you for listing us.
Some of our details on the page below are out of date. Could you correct them?

Page: https://[listing URL]
Corrections:
- Address   listed: 45 Old Rd  ->  correct: 123 Example St, Suite 200 (we moved in March 2025)
- Closed day listed: Wednesday  ->  correct: Monday

You can check these on our official location page: https://example.com/downtown/
or by calling the business line, (000) 000-0000.

Thanks for your help.
[Name], Owner, Example Cafe Downtown
info@example.com

Always include the page URL and "listed / correct" pairs. A short reason (we moved, the number changed) helps the owner see the old value is stale. Thank them for the listing, and do not mix in other asks such as removal or ranking.

If you hear nothing, send one follow-up after two or three weeks. If it is still not fixed, mark it "requested, not fixed" in your table and move on. Do not keep resending or submit the same edit under different accounts.

The order for moves, renames, closures and duplicates

With big changes, fixing things in the wrong order mixes old and new details. Use this order.

On WordPress: make your site the most reliable source

Corrected listings drift back if your own site is out of date. In BrightLocal's study, business websites were over 40% of citations. Put these four things on your site.

  1. The fact sheet's details as text on the location or About page (not only in an image), with a last-updated date.
  2. Moves, renames and closures stated in one sentence on the location page itself, not only in a news post.
  3. The same name, address, phone and hours in your LocalBusiness structured data and llms.txt. In Nurevo AEO, what you enter in the business details screen is published in both the structured data and /llms.txt, so the wording stays the same in one place.
  4. The listing pages you control in sameAs (code below). Nurevo AEO's business details form has no sameAs field, so add it with the developer filter webmcp_canary_business_node.
// Add the listing pages you control to the business node as sameAs
// (not pages you do not manage, and not articles about you)
add_filter('webmcp_canary_business_node', function (array $node, array $settings) {
    $node['sameAs'] = array(
        'https://www.instagram.com/example_cafe/',
        'https://www.yelp.com/biz/(your claimed page)',
        'https://www.tripadvisor.com/(your claimed page)',
    );
    return $node;
}, 10, 2);

Only list official pages you manage in sameAs; leave out articles and listings you have not claimed. From 0.9.1, Nurevo AEO can run alongside SEO SIMPLE PACK and VK All in One Expansion Unit without duplicating structured data.

What not to do

  1. Use bulk submission tools that create new listings for the same business (more duplicates, and the stale ones stay).
  2. Create a new Google Business Profile when you move (change the address on the existing one).
  3. Write your name or phone differently on different sites (follow the fact sheet).
  4. Try to fix answers by typing corrections into the AI over and over (feedback buttons exist, but stale sources keep the error alive).
  5. Write reviews or articles yourself to bury the old details.

Checklist

  1. Fact sheet written, including former address, phone and name
  2. Location page states the same facts as text, with a last-updated date
  3. Asked ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google questions with and without your name, and copied the source URLs
  4. Searched your old address and phone in quotes and listed the stale pages
  5. Google Business Profile name, address, phone and hours match the sheet
  6. Checked Google Maps, Apple Maps and Bing Maps for duplicates and old pins
  7. Corrected the industry listings you can manage yourself
  8. Sent requests with URL, field, correct value and proof for the rest
  9. Structured data and llms.txt match the sheet; sameAs lists only pages you manage
  10. Set a date two to four weeks out to re-ask the same questions, and made the record table

FAQ

Will contacting the AI company fix a wrong answer?

You can report an answer with the feedback buttons, but AI answers draw on listings and your own site. If those stay stale, the same error can keep coming back. Fix the sources first and use reports as a supplement.

How many listings do I need to fix?

Not all of them. Start with listings that appear as AI sources, then Google Business Profile and map apps, then your industry's main directories. Pages found by searching your old details go into a table and get requests in turn.

Does ChatGPT read Google Business Profile?

In BrightLocal's 2026 study of the US, UK and Australia, Google Business Profile was not a top ChatGPT source, while Yelp was used heavily. In Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google Business Profile was the most cited source. Results in other countries have not been published.

Do I need to claim a listing to fix it?

To edit it yourself, usually yes. Without claiming, Google Maps lets anyone signed in suggest an edit, and many sites have a contact form for wrong details. Claimed listings are faster to keep current.

The comparison site will not fix my details. What now?

Send one follow-up after two or three weeks. If nothing changes, record it and move on. Meanwhile, keeping the correct facts on your site and Google Business Profile gives AI other sources with the right details.

How long until AI answers reflect the fix?

No AI company publishes a timeframe, and some listing sites review edits first. Re-ask the same questions every two to four weeks and record what changes.
