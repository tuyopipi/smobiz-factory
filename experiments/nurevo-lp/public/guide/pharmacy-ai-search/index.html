AI search for pharmacies and drugstores: how to get AI to read your hours, pharmacist availability and home visits correctly

AI search optimization for a pharmacy comes down to answering, on the page and down to the day and the minute, the questions people now ask AI: are you open now, is a pharmacist on duty (can I buy pharmacist-only medicines), will you fill a prescription from this clinic, can I get advice out of hours, and will you deliver or visit at home. Then you publish the same facts consistently in structured data, llms.txt, your Google Business Profile and any official pharmacy directory you report to. In Japan that directory is the national medical information site (Navii), fed by the pharmacy function reports every pharmacy files with its prefecture.

Written November 15, 2026 (JST). The Tokyo and Saitama pages on Japan's pharmacy function reporting system and schema.org's types (as defined in schema-dts 2.1.0) were checked that day. Examples of rules come from Japan; check the rules where you practise.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your pharmacy's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your pharmacy or rank it anywhere.

What people ask AI about pharmacies

People look for a pharmacy on the way back from a doctor, or when someone falls ill in the evening or at the weekend. So the questions carry times and conditions: "a pharmacy near [clinic] open after 7pm", "where can I fill a prescription on Sunday", "a drugstore with a pharmacist on duty right now", "a pharmacy that delivers medicine to my parent's home", "a pharmacy where I can ask in English".

For an AI to name your pharmacy in answer to one of these, it has to be able to read, as text, that you meet the condition. A page with opening hours only in an image, no difference between store hours and dispensing hours, or home visits described only as "please ask us" is hard to use for a conditional question.

We read the top-ranking pages on this topic in Japanese and English. Most stop at general advice: update your Google Business Profile, collect reviews, add structured data. Almost none cover matching your site to the official reports a pharmacy already files, how to write the hours when no pharmacist is present, a concrete WordPress setup, or how to check whether you appear. This guide fills those gaps.

Match the official pharmacy reports (Japan: Navii)

In Japan, Article 8-2 of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act requires each pharmacy to report its functions to its prefecture (the pharmacy function information system). According to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, reports have been filed online through G-MIS since January 2024 and published on the national medical information site, Navii, since April 2024. Tokyo Metropolitan Government (Japanese) / Navii (Japanese)

There is an annual report of the situation as of 31 December (in Tokyo, usually filed between early January and late February), and some items must be reported promptly whenever they change. Those items include opening days, opening hours, hours when advice is available outside opening hours, whether the pharmacy displays health support pharmacy status, whether it holds community-cooperation or specialist-cooperation certification, and whether there are hours with no pharmacist present. Saitama Prefecture's page adds suspension, reopening and closure to that list. Saitama Prefecture (Japanese)

This is useful for AI search. The things AI is asked about a pharmacy (how late it is open, whether advice is available after hours, whether a pharmacist is there, whether it is certified) are almost exactly the items a pharmacy already reports. If the reported values and your website disagree, neither AI nor the patient can tell which is right. Make it a rule to change the report, the website, your Google Business Profile and the sign on the same day. Outside Japan, apply the same idea to whichever official register or health-service directory lists your pharmacy.

How to write "no pharmacist on duty" and out-of-hours advice

A drugstore can be open while no pharmacist is on duty. If an AI is asked for "a shop where I can buy a pharmacist-only medicine now" and your page only lists store hours, it may say yes at a time when you cannot sell it.

An example: "Store open 9:00-22:00, every day. Prescriptions Mon-Fri 9:00-19:00 (last prescription 18:45), Sat 9:00-13:00. No pharmacist on Tuesdays 19:00-22:00: we cannot dispense or sell pharmacist-only medicines then. Out-of-hours advice: registered patients can call 0X-XXXX-XXXX from 22:00 to 9:00." Put store, dispensing, pharmacist and out-of-hours information in one place (the number is a placeholder).

Separate rules govern pharmacist-only medicines and what an online pharmacy shop must display, and they keep changing. Check the current law and your regulator's guidance for the exact items.

Advertising rules apply to text written for AI

In Japan, medicine advertising falls under the advertising provisions of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act and the Ministry of Health's standards for appropriate advertising of medicines. Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (Japanese). FAQ, structured data and blog posts written to be picked by AI are covered the same way. Most countries have comparable rules.

  1. Do not promise results or safety ("always works", "no side effects"), and do not use superlatives or comparisons ("the best in town").
  2. In symptom articles, do not say "for this symptom, take this medicine". Say when to see a doctor, and what you can help with at the pharmacy.
  3. Do not list prescription-only medicines like products with prices.
  4. Do not put customer comments in FAQ or review markup as evidence that something works.

If you are unsure, ask your regulator or check your professional body's guidance. For the clinic side, see AI search for primary care and paediatric clinics.

Structured data: the Pharmacy type and useful properties

schema.org has a Pharmacy type, described as "A pharmacy or drugstore" (checked in the schema-dts 2.1.0 type definitions on November 15, 2026). Pharmacy is a kind of MedicalBusiness and carries the properties of both LocalBusiness and MedicalOrganization. A drugstore can use Pharmacy rather than Store.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Opening hours are written as text, not only in an image, with closed days and holiday handling.
  2. Dispensing hours and the last time to hand in a prescription are listed separately from store hours.
  3. If there are hours with no pharmacist, the days, times and what cannot be done then are stated.
  4. Whether out-of-hours advice is available, the number and the hours are stated.
  5. How to hand in or send a prescription ahead, and the area, recipients and request method for home visits, are stated.
  6. Certifications and designations are stated only as they actually are.
  7. Name, address, phone and hours match across the site, structured data, Google Business Profile and the official pharmacy directory.
  8. No promises of results, no superlatives or comparisons, and no customer comments used as evidence in FAQ or structured data.
  9. llms.txt lists your hours, access, prescription and home-visit pages. What is llms.txt

FAQ

Where should a pharmacy start with AI search?

Write store hours and dispensing hours separately as text, and add when no pharmacist is present and whether advice is available out of hours. Then make those values match your structured data, Google Business Profile and the official pharmacy directory.

Should a drugstore use Store or Pharmacy in structured data?

If you sell medicines and have a pharmacist, Pharmacy is the better fit. schema.org describes Pharmacy as "A pharmacy or drugstore", and as a kind of LocalBusiness it holds address and hours directly. That the values match your page matters more than the type.

What happens if the official directory and our website disagree?

AI and patients cannot tell which to trust. In Japan, opening days and hours, out-of-hours advice and pharmacist absences are items a pharmacy must report promptly when they change. Updating the report, the site and your Google Business Profile on the same day prevents the mismatch.

Isn't it better not to mention hours with no pharmacist?

It is safer to mention them. Otherwise an AI may say people can buy pharmacist-only medicines at a time when they cannot, and they arrive disappointed. In Japan, whether there are hours without a pharmacist is also a reported item.

Can we write articles on symptoms and the medicines that treat them, to get picked by AI?

Medicine advertising is regulated (in Japan, by the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act and the Ministry of Health's advertising standards). Avoid promising results or using superlatives; focus on when to see a doctor and what the pharmacy can help with. Ask your regulator if unsure.
