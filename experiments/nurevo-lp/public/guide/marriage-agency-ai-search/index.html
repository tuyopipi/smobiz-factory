AI search for marriage agencies and matchmakers: how to get AI to read your total fees, what counts as a match, member numbers and cancellation rules correctly

AI search optimization for a marriage agency or matchmaking service comes down to answering, in plain text on your own pages, the questions singles now ask AI: what it costs in total from joining to a match, when the success fee is due, how many members there really are, and what you get back if you quit early. Agencies define a "match" differently and count members differently, so if you do not state your own definitions, AI fills the gap from other agencies or comparison sites. Then you publish the same facts consistently in structured data, llms.txt, your Google Business Profile and the comparison sites or networks that list you.

The legal examples come from Japan, where marriage agencies (kekkon soudanjo) are a large industry; check the rules where you operate.

This guide is for marriage agencies and matchmakers, whether counselor-led, database matching or online. Dating apps and wedding venues raise different questions and rules, so they are not covered (for venues, see AI search for wedding venues). AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your agency or rank it anywhere. What singles ask AI

Questions about matchmakers stack age, area, how the service works and cost. For example: "a matchmaker in [city] for a woman in her late thirties with a busy job", "an agency that accepts divorced men and offers online consultations" or "no success fee and about $X for a year". For an AI to name your agency, the text on your site has to show that you meet those conditions.

Google says there are no additional requirements or special structured data for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that ordinary SEO basics, structured data that matches the visible text and an up-to-date Business Profile are what matter. Google: AI features and your website. For a matchmaker too, the work is not a trick; it is writing down, accurately, the amounts and definitions people use to compare.

Price the whole journey, not just the monthly fee

Agency costs are split into a registration or joining fee (often shown together as an "initial fee"), a monthly fee, a fee per introduction or first date, a success fee, and extras such as profile photos or one-to-one coaching. Comparison articles line agencies up on these items, but what an "initial fee" includes varies. If only the monthly fee is prominent, AI can present it as the whole cost. For each plan, show these items and an example total in a table.

In Japan, prices shown to consumers in advance must include consumption tax, on any medium. National Tax Agency: total price display (Japanese). Wherever you operate, say whether tax is included.

If you offer plans with and without a success fee, show totals over the same period so AI can say which is cheaper under which conditions.

Define "a match" in your own words

Agencies define a match differently. One Japanese comparison article lists definitions ranging from "starting to date with marriage in mind", "entering an exclusive relationship" and "both confirming they intend to marry" to "getting engaged" and "leaving the agency because of dating, engagement or marriage", and notes that each agency also counts its results differently. Minna no Kekkon Soudanjo: agency ranking, October 27, 2026 (Japanese)

If you do not publish yours, AI may answer with another agency's, for example that the success fee is due at engagement. Near your fees and success-fee explanation, state these four things:

  1. The point your agency counts as a match (for example, "when both members confirm they intend to marry and leave the agency together").
  2. When the success fee becomes due, and the payment deadline.
  3. What happens if the relationship ends after a match (for example, the fee to rejoin).
  4. If you publish a success rate: what was counted (the definition), the denominator, the period and the date. For example: "Of N members active from April 2025 to March 2026, M left with a match as we define it."

A bare "X% success rate" gets compared with other agencies' figures that use different definitions and denominators. With the definition and denominator in the same paragraph, an AI quoting the figure is more likely to pass the conditions on with it.

Separate your own members from the network's

Many Japanese agencies belong to a federation, a network of agencies that share member profiles, and comparison articles note that an agency's member count may include the network's members. Minna no Kekkon Soudanjo (Japanese). Shared networks exist elsewhere too. If your site gives only the network figure, AI can present it as the size of your agency.

Publish cooling-off, early cancellation and pause rules

In Japan, matchmaking contracts that run over two months and cost over 50,000 yen are "specified continuous services" (tokutei keizokuteki ekimu teikyo) under the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions. Clients can cancel under cooling-off within eight days of receiving the legally required document, and after that can cancel at any time within the contract period for any reason. The charge for early cancellation is capped: 30,000 yen before the service starts, and after it starts, the price of services already received plus the lower of 20,000 yen or 20% of the unused amount. Osaka Prefecture: cancelling a matchmaking contract, updated May 14, 2024 (Japanese). Hiroshima Prefecture's consumer centre gives the same caps and notes that the contract amount includes joining fees and related goods the contract requires. Hiroshima Prefecture: early cancellation rights (Japanese)

People considering an agency ask AI about this. On your fee page or a contract-terms page, state at least the following:

  1. The cooling-off period and how to give notice.
  2. How to cancel early and how the refund is calculated (prepaid monthly and initial fees). Do not set terms beyond the legal caps.
  3. Pausing: for how many months, fees while paused, and whether introductions stop.
  4. Any difference in procedure or fees between leaving with a match and leaving for other reasons.
  5. The documents you give before signing, and where to raise a complaint (your own contact and, in Japan, the consumer hotline 188).

For detailed rules, such as what the contract documents must contain, check the latest information from the Consumer Affairs Agency or your consumer authority.

Sign-up documents, certifications and testimonials

Answer the trust questions in your text too: "do I need to prove I'm single?" and "is this agency legitimate?". List the documents you require at sign-up (such as a certificate of single status and photo ID, whatever you actually ask for) and how you check them.

Osaka Prefecture describes two private third-party certifications for matchmaking services in Japan: "IMS certification" for internet-based services (run by the NPO Marriage Partner Introduction Service Certification Organization) and the "Maru-teki Mark CMS" (run by the NPO Japan Life Design Counselor Association). It says they check information given to users, clear contracts and cancellation, identity checks and proof of single status, and a complaints desk. Osaka Prefecture: using matching services safely, updated May 29, 2026 (Japanese). If you hold a certification, give its official name, the body that runs it and the year you obtained it. Never display a mark you do not hold.

When you publish testimonials from couples, get their consent and do not hide rewards or the fact that something is an advertisement. In Japan, since October 1, 2023, advertising that hides that it is advertising (stealth marketing) is an unfair representation under the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations. Consumer Affairs Agency: stealth marketing (Japanese). A US study of dating apps also found Google AI Overviews citing forum posts heavily: reddit.com in 25 of 41 overviews. 5W: Dating App AI Visibility Index 2026. You cannot rewrite what others post, so stating your fees and definitions clearly on your own site is the surest way to reduce misunderstandings.

Structured data: there is no matchmaker type

schema.org has no type for marriage agencies or matchmakers. ProfessionalService has been deprecated because it was confused with Service. schema.org: ProfessionalService. The sensible shape is a LocalBusiness with your free consultation and plans linked as a Service, with the price as an Offer.

Pre-publish checklist

  1. Who you serve, where you meet clients (office or online) and the areas you cover are stated.
  2. Each plan shows joining and registration fees, the monthly fee, introduction fees, the success fee and extras, broken down, with tax treatment.
  3. Two or three example totals for typical lengths are shown, including one with no match.
  4. Your definition of a match and when the success fee is due sit next to your fees.
  5. Any success rate has its definition, denominator, period and date in the same paragraph.
  6. Your own member count and the network's are shown separately, each with a date.
  7. Cooling-off, how early cancellation is settled, pause rules and a complaints contact are stated.
  8. Sign-up documents and any certification's official name, body and year are stated. Testimonials have consent and do not hide that they are advertising.
  9. Name, address, phone and fees match across your site, structured data, Google Business Profile and network or comparison listings.
  10. llms.txt lists your fee, match-definition, cancellation and free-consultation pages. What is llms.txt?

Checking whether you appear in AI answers

Once the facts are in place, write five to ten questions a single person would ask, combining age, area, situation (divorced, busy job and so on), budget and how they want to meet people. Put them to ChatGPT and Perplexity and record whether your agency appears by name or URL, whether fees and your definition of a match are stated correctly, and whether the source cited is your site or a comparison site. FAQ

Where should a marriage agency start with AI search?

Write, as text and tables on your pages, each plan's joining, registration, monthly, introduction and success fees with an example total, your own definition of a match, your own member count separated from any network's, and your cancellation and pause rules. Then make your name, address, phone and fees match across structured data, your Google Business Profile and comparison listings.

Do we really need to publish our definition of a match?

Yes. Agencies define a match differently, and that changes when the success fee is due. Without your own definition on the page, AI may answer with another agency's. Put the point you count as a match and when payment is due next to the success fee.

Can we publish our network's member count?

Yes, but keep it separate from your own member count, with the network's name, source and date. If only the network figure appears, AI may present it as the size of your agency.

How should we describe early cancellation?

In Japan, matchmaking contracts over two months and over 50,000 yen are specified continuous services: there is an eight-day cooling-off period and early-cancellation charges are capped (30,000 yen before the service starts; after it starts, the price of services received plus the lower of 20,000 yen or 20% of the unused amount). Within those limits, state how you settle a cancellation on your page, and check the rules where you operate. This article is not legal advice.

Which structured data type should a matchmaker use?

schema.org has no matchmaker type, and ProfessionalService is deprecated. Publish the agency as a LocalBusiness and link your free consultation and plans as Service with Offer.
