AI search for pet grooming salons: getting AI to read your breed-based prices, acceptance rules, registration and the line between grooming and veterinary care

AI search optimization (AEO) for a pet grooming salon comes down to making your own site able to answer what owners ask AI, such as "How much is a toy poodle groom near me?" or "Which groomers take senior dogs?" Write your prices and times by breed and weight, which dogs and cats you can accept, what you do if a pet becomes unwell during grooming, and (in Japan) your animal business registration as text on your pages, then publish the same facts as LocalBusiness and Service structured data and in llms.txt. Words that belong to a vet, such as "cures skin disease" or "tartar removal", make a salon harder to trust, for AI and for owners alike.

Written November 23, 2026 (JST). The legal and government information comes from Japanese sources (the Ministry of the Environment, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, prefectural guides and the Japan Veterinary Medical Association), as cited in the text. Rules differ by country and region, so check the ones that apply to you. This article is not legal advice.

AI answers change from day to day and with how a question is phrased. The steps here make your salon's facts readable to AI. They do not guarantee that an AI will mention your salon or rank it anywhere. Veterinary clinics are not covered here.

What owners ask AI about grooming salons

Unlike a hair salon for people, the first question about a grooming salon is usually "Will they take my pet?" Breed, weight, age, temperament (biting, fear) and health conditions all change which salons can help. When a salon's site states these conditions, AI can use them as evidence. When it does not, AI guesses from reviews and general advice, or names another salon whose conditions are clear.

Registration you must display (Japan)

In Japan, under the Act on Welfare and Management of Animals, a business that holds customers' animals counts as the "keeping" (hokan) category of a Type 1 animal handling business and must register with the prefecture or city. The Ministry of the Environment lists "grooming businesses (when they hold the animal)" as an example, alongside pet hotels and pet sitters. Ministry of the Environment: animal handling businesses (Japanese). Whether grooming done on the spot with the owner present is covered is a question for your local office.

Registered businesses post a sign at the premises and show set items in their advertising. Hiroshima Prefecture's guide lists eight items and says websites and social media count as advertising, not only newspapers and flyers. Hiroshima Prefecture (Japanese). Saitama Prefecture's guide lists the same items and says registration lasts five years and lapses if not renewed. Saitama Prefecture, Sayama health office (Japanese)

The items in the Hiroshima and Saitama guides, grouped into six rows for a website. Follow your local office's guide for the exact wording. When you renew and the number or dates change, update your site, social media and booking sites on the same day.

The line from veterinary care: what a salon can do and what not to write

In Japan, only a veterinarian may treat animals as a business. The Ministry of Agriculture's FAQ says a grooming salon may not use a shampoo intended to treat disease (washing the coat is fine), that removing tartar with a scaler is veterinary treatment requiring a vet's judgement and skill, and that tartar cannot be removed by brushing (updated December 12, 2024). MAFF: FAQ on small animal veterinary care (Japanese)

A 2019 document from the Japan Veterinary Medical Association lists nail trimming, ear cleaning, clipping (grooming), shampooing, expressing anal glands and tooth brushing as not veterinary treatment, while injections, anaesthesia, giving medicine and diagnosing disease are. JVMA, April 15, 2019 (Japanese). So a salon's normal menu can stay on the page, as long as it is worded carefully.

These rewrites are this article's recommendation, not an application of the law. Check doubtful wording with your local office.

Eight facts to put on your price and menu pages

  1. Prices by breed and weight including tax: breed, weight, service and total together, such as "Toy poodle under 5 kg, full groom, $70". List cat prices separately.
  2. Extra charges: matting, large dogs, dogs that bite, add-ons (spa bath, tooth brushing) and their amounts.
  3. Time and pick-up: typical time per service, how you tell owners the pet is ready, pick-up time rules.
  4. Acceptance: weight limits, whether you take cats, how you handle age and health conditions, vaccination and flea/tick requirements (your own policy).
  5. If a pet becomes unwell: when you stop, how you contact the owner, how you get the pet to a nearby vet and who pays.
  6. What the salon does not do: diagnose or treat illness, give medicine, remove tartar.
  7. Booking, cancellation and pick-up service: how to book, cancellation fee, pick-up area and fee, parking.
  8. Registration and groomers: your registration display (above) and each groomer's experience and exact certificate names (for private certificates, the issuing body).

Many salons post their price list only as an image. Text inside images may not be read by AI, so write it as text too. See AI search for pricing pages for price pages in general and business hours and holidays for closing days.

Structured data: neither a medical type nor a human beauty type

schema.org has no type for grooming salons. Checking schema-dts 2.1.0 on November 23, 2026, the closest is PetStore (described as "A pet store"), and there is no grooming type. For a salon that mainly grooms and boards, use LocalBusiness and say what you do in the description and in each Service. If selling food and supplies is your main business, PetStore fits.

Two types are best avoided. VeterinaryCare is a medical type (MedicalOrganization) for veterinary clinics. BeautySalon and HairSalon sit in the same group as human hair and nail salons (HealthAndBeautyBusiness), so AI may mistake you for a salon for people.

Publish each menu item by breed, weight and service as a Service, and describe what is included and how long it takes. Entered in Nurevo AEO, it looks like this (values are examples).

Setting it up on a WordPress grooming salon site

  1. Install and activate Nurevo AEO (free) from WordPress.org. Keep your SEO plugin, such as Yoast SEO, Rank Math, All in One SEO or SEO SIMPLE PACK: structured data is not duplicated.
  2. In the setup wizard, choose "Other". Once you enter an address, phone number and hours, the salon is published as LocalBusiness. If you mainly sell supplies and food, choose "Retail shop" and type PetStore in the "Business type (schema.org type)" field in Settings. Do not choose "Beauty salon" or "Clinic or dental".
  3. In the business description, write "We are not a veterinary clinic: we do not diagnose or treat illness or remove tartar", and, in Japan, your registration category and number.
  4. Add each menu item as a service (up to 20). Put breed, weight and service in the name, the price including tax (numbers only), the currency, the booking URL, and in the description what is included, how long it takes and which pets should talk to you first. It is published as a service, an offer and a booking action, and listed line by line in llms.txt.
  5. Add FAQs (acceptance, time, what happens if a pet is unwell, tartar, cats, pick-up) and place the Nurevo FAQ block (or the [nurevo_faq] shortcode) on your price page. See AI search for FAQ pages.
  6. Open /llms.txt and check that your price, acceptance, salon information (registration) and booking pages are listed. Run the diagnosis on the AEO score screen and fix what is missing.

Keep Instagram, booking sites and Google Business Profile in step

Many grooming salons find clients through Instagram photos and booking sites. AI reads those too, so if a booking site shows different prices, or an old post still says "tartar removal" or "good for skin disease", AI may keep using the old information after you fix your site.

Keep prices, hours, closing days and acceptance rules the same on your site, booking sites and Google Business Profile. In Japan, social media counts as advertising for the registration display, as Hiroshima's guide says, so add it to your Instagram profile too. When you change prices or renew your registration, update everything on the same day.

Checklist before you publish

  1. Prices by breed, weight and service, and cat prices, are written as text.
  2. Extra charges (matting, large dogs, add-ons) are explained.
  3. Time per service and how you tell owners the pet is ready are written.
  4. Which dogs and cats you accept (weight, age, health, vaccinations) is written.
  5. What you do if a pet becomes unwell, and how you get it to a vet, is written.
  6. No vet words such as "cures", "tartar removal" or "examination".
  7. In Japan, the registration display (name, address, category, number, date, expiry, responsible person) is on every page or the salon information page as text.
  8. The structured data type is LocalBusiness (PetStore if retail is your main business), not VeterinaryCare or BeautySalon.
  9. Prices, hours and the registration display match across your site, booking sites, Google Business Profile and Instagram.
  10. llms.txt lists your price, acceptance, salon information and booking pages. What is llms.txt

Check whether AI shows you, and whether it is right

Once the pages are in order, ask AI what owners would ask. Check whether you are named for local, condition-based questions such as "toy poodle grooming price in [city]" or "grooming salon for senior dogs near [station]", and also ask with your salon's name, such as "Do they take cats?" or "How long does it take?", and record whether the answers match your site. If AI describes your salon as if it were a vet, or says you remove tartar, look for old wording on your site, booking sites or old posts.

AI answers change between runs, so ask the same questions several times, spread over time. FAQ

Where should a grooming salon start with AI search?

Write your prices and times by breed and weight, which pets you accept, and what you do if a pet becomes unwell as text on your pages, and (in Japan) put the animal business registration display in your footer or salon information page. Then publish the same facts as LocalBusiness and Service structured data and in llms.txt, and make them match your booking sites and Google Business Profile.

Do I have to show my registration number on my website?

In Japan, a registered Type 1 animal handling business shows its registration number and other set items in its advertising, and Hiroshima Prefecture's guide says websites and social media count. Check the details with your local office. Showing it also gives AI evidence when someone asks whether your salon is registered.

Can a grooming salon advertise "tartar removal"?

Avoid it. Japan's Ministry of Agriculture says removing tartar with a scaler is veterinary treatment and that brushing cannot remove tartar. Call your menu item "tooth brushing (plaque care)" and advise seeing a vet about tartar.

Can I list a medicated shampoo?

Washing the coat is fine, but the Ministry of Agriculture says a grooming salon may not use shampoo intended to treat disease. Do not write "cures skin disease". Write that pets with skin symptoms should see a vet first.

Which structured data type should a grooming salon use?

schema.org has no grooming type, so use LocalBusiness and publish your menu as Services. Use PetStore if selling supplies and food is your main business. Do not use VeterinaryCare (veterinary clinics) or human beauty types such as BeautySalon.
